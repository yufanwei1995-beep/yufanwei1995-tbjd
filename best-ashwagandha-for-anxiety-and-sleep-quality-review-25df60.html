What to Look For When Buying Ashwagandha

Not all ashwagandha is created equal. Here's the short version of what I learned after reading 40+ studies and testing these products on myself.

1. Standardized Extract vs. Raw Powder

Raw root powder has inconsistent levels of withanolides (the active compounds). You might get 1% or you might get 0.1%. Standardized extracts guarantee a specific percentage. Look for KSM-66 (5% withanolides) or Sensoril (8% withanolides). These are the two most clinically studied forms on the market.

2. Dosage That Actually Works

Clinical trials use 300-600 mg of KSM-66 or Sensoril daily. Anything less than 250 mg of standardized extract is likely a waste of money. Conversely, more is not better. Taking 1200 mg+ can cause digestive upset and afternoon fatigue.

3. Withania Somnifera vs. Withania Coagulans

Make sure the label says Withania somnifera. Some cheap brands use Withania coagulans, which is a different species with weaker effects on cortisol. This is a common trick in budget supplements.

4. Third-Party Testing

Supplements are not FDA-regulated. Look for brands that publish Certificate of Analysis (CoA) from labs like NSF or Informed Choice. This ensures you're getting what's on the label and no heavy metals.

5. Delivery Form Matters Less Than You Think

Capsules, gummies, and tinctures all work. The extract quality matters more than the form. Gummies are convenient but often have added sugar. Capsules are the most cost-effective. Tinctures absorb faster but taste terrible.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does ashwagandha take to work for anxiety?

Most clinical trials show significant effects after 4-6 weeks of daily use. Some people feel a difference in 1-2 weeks, but the full cortisol-lowering effect takes about a month. Don't expect a Xanax-like immediate effect. This is a cumulative adaptogen, not a fast-acting sedative.

Can I take ashwagandha every day?

Yes, for up to 3 months. After 12 weeks, take a 2-week break. This prevents tolerance and resets your system. Some research suggests long-term daily use without breaks can lead to thyroid suppression in rare cases.

What's the best time of day to take it?

For sleep: take it 1 hour before bed, especially if using Sensoril. For anxiety: take it in the morning with breakfast. KSM-66 can be taken either time. Avoid taking it late afternoon unless you want to feel sleepy at 7 PM.

Are there any side effects?

The most common are digestive upset (nausea, diarrhea) and drowsiness. Less common: lowered blood pressure and blood sugar. If you're on thyroid medication, diabetes meds, or blood pressure drugs, talk to your doctor first. Pregnant women should avoid ashwagandha entirely.

Is ashwagandha addictive?

No. It doesn't act on the same brain receptors as benzodiazepines or alcohol. You won't experience withdrawal symptoms when you stop. Some users report a "rebound" of anxiety for a few days after quitting, but it's mild compared to pharmaceutical options.
