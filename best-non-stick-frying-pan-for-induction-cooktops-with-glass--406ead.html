Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Induction cooktops are brutal on cookware. They heat the metal directly, which means your pan needs a thick, magnetic base. Here’s what matters:

  • Base Thickness: Look for a base that is at least 3mm thick. Induction heat causes rapid expansion, and thin pans (under 2.5mm) will warp into a dome shape within months. The De Buyer has a 3.5mm base. The GreenPan has a 3mm base. Avoid anything thinner.
  • Magnetic Compatibility: Not all stainless steel is magnetic. Bring a magnet to the store. If it doesn't stick hard, it won't work on induction.
  • Glass Lid Fit: Many pans ship with glass lids that don't sit flush. A gap means steam escapes and your food cooks unevenly. Check the rim—it needs to be perfectly flat. The All-Clad lids have a silicone seal that fixes this issue.
  • Non-Stick Coating Type: You have three choices: PTFE (Teflon), Ceramic, or textured. PTFE is the most slippery but degrades above 500°F. Ceramic is eco-friendly but dies faster. The HexClad is a hybrid—stainless steel with a non-stick layer—which handles high heat better.
  • Handle Material: On induction, the pan body gets hot, but the handle should stay cool. Stainless steel handles are oven-safe but get hot. Silicone-coated handles are cooler but can't go above 350°F. The Scanpan has a silicone grip that stays cool for 15 minutes of cooking.

FAQ

Will any non-stick pan work on induction?

No. The pan must have a magnetic base. If a magnet sticks to the bottom, it will work. Pure aluminum and most ceramic pans won't work unless they have a magnetic plate fused to the base.

Why do pans warp on induction?

Induction heats the metal directly and fast. Thin aluminum expands and bends. Always choose a pan with a base of 3mm or thicker.

Can I use metal utensils on these pans?

Only the HexClad is metal-safe. All others require silicone, wood, or plastic utensils. Metal will scratch PTFE and ceramic coatings.

How long do non-stick coatings last?

PTFE coatings last 2-3 years with proper care. Ceramic lasts 6-12 months. The HexClad hybrid is the only one that lasts 5+ years.

Is a glass lid really necessary?

It helps with monitoring cooking without losing heat. But the seal matters more than the material. A poorly fitting glass lid is worse than a good metal lid.
