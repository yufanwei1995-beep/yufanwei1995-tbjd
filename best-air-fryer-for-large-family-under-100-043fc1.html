Buyer's Guide: What to Look For in a Large Family Air Fryer Under $100

Don't get tricked by marketing fluff. Here's what actually matters when you're feeding a crew.

Basket Size: 5.8 Quarts Minimum

For 4-6 people, you need at least 5.8 quarts. A 5.8-quart basket fits a whole 4-pound chicken or 2 pounds of fries. Smaller baskets (3-4 quarts) mean cooking in batches — the opposite of convenient.

Wattage: 1500-1700 Watts

More power means faster cooking and better browning. All models in this review have at least 1500 watts. Over 1700 watts is rare under $100, but 1500-1700 is sufficient for most foods.

Presets vs. Manual Controls

Presets (chicken, fries, steak) are helpful if you're new. But they're just timers and temps — you can set those yourself. Don't pay extra for a dozen presets you'll never use.

Ease of Cleaning

Look for a nonstick basket that's dishwasher safe. Square baskets are easier to scrub than round ones. Avoid models with exposed heating elements on top — they trap grease.

Temperature Range: 170°F to 400°F

You need at least 400°F for crispy fries and chicken wings. A low end around 170°F is nice for dehydrating or reheating leftovers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I fit a whole chicken in a 5.8-quart air fryer?

Yes. A 4-pound chicken fits in most baskets over 5.5 quarts. For a 5-pound bird, go with the Chefman (6.3 quarts).

Are square baskets better than round ones?

Square baskets are easier to clean and use counter space more efficiently. Round baskets often cook slightly more evenly, but the difference is small.

Do I need 12 presets?

No. Most people use 3-4 presets regularly. The presets are just timed temperatures — you can set those manually. Don't pay extra for a dozen presets you won't touch.

Can I use parchment paper in an air fryer?

Yes, but only with holes. Use perforated parchment paper designed for air fryers. Solid paper blocks airflow and can cause uneven cooking or fire risk.
