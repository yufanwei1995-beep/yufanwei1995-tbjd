I've spent the last decade testing patio lighting. I've hung, staked, and zip-tied just about everything. This year, I ran a six-month side-by-side test on my own covered pergola to settle the debate: string lights or solar stakes? Here's what actually matters.

What to Look For: The Buyer's Guide

Before you buy anything, understand your pergola's specific conditions. A covered pergola changes everything compared to an open deck.

Light Coverage & Beam Angle

String lights with G40 bulbs (about 1.5 inches in diameter) cast light in a 360-degree sphere. That means light bounces off your pergola's ceiling and fills the space evenly. Solar stake lights, by contrast, typically throw light downward in a 120-degree cone. You'll get bright pools of light on the floor but dark shadows above eye level.

Battery vs. Hardwired vs. Solar

String lights come in three flavors: plug-in (120V), battery-operated, and solar. For a covered pergola, plug-in string lights are the workhorse. The cover protects the outlet and cords from rain, so you don't need the weatherproofing you'd demand in an open area. Battery-operated strings last 6–10 hours per charge on AA batteries, but you'll be swapping batteries weekly if you use them nightly.

Solar stake lights have a built-in panel on top of the stake. Critical issue: covered pergolas block direct sunlight. If your pergola roof is solid (metal or polycarbonate), the solar stakes won't get enough sun to charge fully. You'll get 2–3 hours of dim light instead of the advertised 8 hours. If your pergola roof is open slats or lattice, solar works fine.

Lumens & Color Temperature

Don't buy based on bulb count alone. Look at total lumens. A 48-foot string of G40 bulbs pushes about 270 lumens total (roughly 5.6 lumens per bulb). That's warm and dim — perfect for dining, terrible for reading. Solar stake lights range from 10 to 100 lumens per head. For a 10x10 pergola, aim for 1,500–2,000 total lumens for functional light, or 500–800 for pure ambiance.

Color temperature matters more than people think. Warm white (2700K) makes skin look healthy and food appetizing. Cool white (5000K+) feels like a parking lot. Most quality string lights are warm white. Many cheap solar stakes are cool white — check the specs before buying.

Weather Resistance

Your pergola covers the lights, but it's not a sealed room. Humidity, wind-blown rain, and temperature swings still hit everything. Look for IP65 rating minimum on string lights. That means dust-tight and protected against water jets. Solar stakes should be IP65 as well, but check the battery compartment seal — that's where water gets in first.

Installation Time & Permanence

String lights take 20–30 minutes to hang. You'll need hooks or zip ties on each rafter. Solar stakes take 5 minutes — just push them into the ground. But stakes are semi-permanent; they're easy to trip over and annoying to mow around. String lights are more of a set-and-forget solution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I mix string lights and solar stakes on the same pergola?

Yes, and it's actually the best setup. Use plug-in string lights hung from the rafters for ambient glow. Place solar stakes at the perimeter corners to light the ground and define the space. The combination gives you both overhead warmth and floor-level visibility. Just make sure the color temperatures match — buy warm white for both, not one warm and one cool.

How do I hang string lights without damaging my pergola?

Use stainless steel screw hooks (1.5-inch size) screwed into the rafter undersides. They're removable and leave only a tiny pilot hole. For metal pergolas, use zip ties around the beams. Don't use nails — they pull out under the weight of the cord. Space hooks every 4–5 feet to prevent sagging.

Will solar stakes work if my pergola has a solid roof?

No, not reliably. A solid polycarbonate or metal roof blocks 90% of the sunlight. Solar stakes need 4–6 hours of direct sun to charge fully. If your roof is solid, get plug-in lights or look for a solar string with a remote panel (like the Toodour) that you can mount in a sunny spot nearby.

How many string lights do I need for a 10x10 pergola?

Get a 48-foot string with at least 24 bulbs. That gives you roughly one bulb per 2 square feet, which is the sweet spot for ambiance. If you want brighter light for reading or cooking, add a second string or swap to larger S14 bulbs.

What's the lifespan difference?

Plug-in LED string lights last 20,000–30,000 hours — that's 10+ years of nightly use. Solar lights have rechargeable batteries that fade after 2–3 years. When the battery dies, the whole unit is typically e-waste. Over a 10-year period, plug-in string lights are cheaper even though they cost more upfront.

The Bottom Line

For a covered pergola, buy plug-in string lights. The Brightown G40 set is the best value — reliable, warm, and bright enough for dinner parties. Solar stakes only make sense if your pergola has open slats that let sunlight through, and even then, they're a secondary light source, not the main event. The one exception: if you have zero outlets nearby and can't run an extension cord, get the Toodour solar string with the remote panel. Otherwise, spend the $35 on string lights and skip the solar headache.
