What to Look for in a Multivitamin for Women Over 50 With Iron Deficiency

Not all iron is created equal. Here's what actually matters.

1. Form of Iron

Ferrous sulfate is cheap but harsh — it's the main cause of nausea and constipation. Look for ferrous bisglycinate (also called iron glycinate) or iron carbonyl. These are gentler on the stomach and absorb well even with food. If you see "ferrous fumarate," it's fine, but expect more GI upset.

2. Dosage Matters — But Not the Way You Think

The RDA for postmenopausal women is only 8mg because you're not losing blood monthly. But if you're deficient, you need more. Look for 8-18mg in a multivitamin. If your deficiency is severe, you'll need a standalone iron supplement (45-65mg) — a multivitamin alone won't fix that. Get your ferritin tested first.

3. Vitamin C Is Non-Negotiable

Iron absorption is poor on its own. Vitamin C dramatically improves uptake. Some brands include 60-100mg in the formula. If they don't, take your multivitamin with a glass of orange juice.

4. Don't Take Iron With Calcium

Here's the catch: calcium blocks iron absorption. Many women's 50+ formulas pack 500mg of calcium and iron in one pill — that's counterproductive. If your multivitamin has both, take it at opposite times of day, or choose a formula that separates them (some do a morning iron pill and evening calcium pill).

5. The Menopause Extras

After 50, you need more B12 (for brain health), Vitamin D (for bones — aim for 800-1000 IU), and Magnesium. Skip the "herbal blends" and "energy complexes" — they're marketing filler.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I just take an iron supplement instead of a multivitamin?

Yes, if your deficiency is severe. But most women over 50 also need B12, vitamin D, and magnesium, which a standalone iron pill won't provide. A multi with 18mg of gentle iron is the smarter baseline.

How long until my iron levels improve?

With 18mg daily, expect 4-8 weeks to see ferritin rise. Re-test your blood after 3 months. If ferritin hasn't improved, you need a higher dose (65mg) under medical supervision.

Should I take iron with food?

Iron bisglycinate can be taken on an empty stomach — that's its advantage. Ferrous sulfate (like Nature Made) must be taken with food to avoid nausea. Never take iron with dairy, coffee, or tea — they block absorption by up to 60%.

What's the difference between iron deficiency and anemia?

Iron deficiency means low ferritin (stored iron). Anemia means your hemoglobin is also low. A multivitamin can fix deficiency. Anemia needs a prescription-strength iron supplement or IV iron therapy. Get a blood test before buying anything.
