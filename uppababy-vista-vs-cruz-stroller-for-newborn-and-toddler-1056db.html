You are staring at two strollers that look almost identical from across the store. Both have the UPPAbaby logo. Both have all-terrain wheels. Both cost more than your first car. The difference between the UPPAbaby Vista V2 and the Cruz V2 is not about which is "better"—it's about which is better for you.

This guide is for parents who are trying to decide between these two premium strollers. I've spent the last three months pushing both around the city, through parks, in and out of car trunks, and through narrow coffee shop doors. Here is the honest breakdown.

What to Look For in a Newborn-to-Toddler Stroller

Before we pit these two against each other, let's talk about what actually matters. You are going to use this thing every day for three to four years. Here is the checklist I use when testing:

  • Newborn Readiness: Does it lie flat or come with a bassinet? Newborns can't sit up, so this is non-negotiable.
  • Weight and Fold: A 30-pound stroller is a backbreaker when you're lifting it into an SUV. Test the fold with one hand.
  • Ride Quality: Does the suspension absorb bumps, or does your baby's head bobble? This matters for naps.
  • Storage: Can you fit a diaper bag and groceries? Check the basket dimensions, not just the stated weight capacity.
  • Toddler Lifespan: Does the seat have a high weight limit? A toddler who hits 40 pounds will outgrow a flimsy seat quickly.
  • The "Grow" Factor: Do you plan on having another kid in the next two years? This is the biggest deciding factor between the Vista and Cruz.

UPPAbaby Cruz V2 Review

The Cruz V2 is the "single-child" stroller in UPPAbaby's lineup. It's designed to be nimble, lighter, and simpler. This is the one I recommend to 80% of the parents I talk to.

Push and Maneuverability: The Cruz V2 has a narrower frame than the Vista. This makes a huge difference in tight aisles and crowded sidewalks. The push is buttery smooth—the front wheels swivel effortlessly, and the all-wheel suspension eats up uneven pavement. You can steer it with one finger while holding a coffee in the other hand.

Newborn Use: The seat itself does not lie completely flat. It reclines to about 170 degrees, which is technically "newborn safe" but not ideal. I recommend buying the Mesa Infant Car Seat (which clicks straight in) or the Bassinet accessory ($180). The bassinet is sleep-safe, so you can use it for overnight sleep at grandma's house too.

Toddler Life: The seat holds up to 50 pounds. That gets most kids to age 4 or 5. The leg room is generous, and the seat is tall enough that your toddler won't look like a giant in a go-kart. The canopy is massive—it has a peek-a-boo window and extends down to cover the entire seat in sun.

Trading Space: The basket holds 25 pounds. It's big enough for a diaper bag plus a small grocery haul. The fold is a one-handed, two-step process. It's not as compact as an umbrella stroller, but it fits in the trunk of a sedan without removing the wheels.

The Catch: This is a stroller for ONE child. You cannot attach a second seat, a ride-along board is the only option for a toddler when a new baby arrives. If you have twins or Irish twins, this doesn't work.

UPPAbaby Vista V2 Review

The Vista V2 is the "grow with your family" stroller. It's a tank. It's heavy. But it's the only stroller in this comparison that can turn into a double.

Push and Maneuverability: The Vista is heavier (27 lbs vs. 22 lbs for the Cruz) and wider. You feel the difference immediately. It's still a good push—the suspension is plush and the ride is stable—but it's not as agile. Turning around in a narrow shop aisle is a three-point maneuver. You will bump doorframes.

Newborn Use: The Vista V2 comes with a bassinet included in the box. That's a $180 value. The bassinet is genuinely safe for overnight sleep and clips onto the frame with a satisfying click. It also has a "toddler seat + bassinet" mode from day one, meaning you can have a newborn in the bassinet and a toddler in the lower seat. This is the only stroller in this class that does this without buying a full second stroller.

Double Mode: When baby #2 arrives, you buy the RumbleSeat (~$180) and attach it to the lower position. The weight limit for the lower seat is 35 lbs. This works well for a 2-year-old and a newborn. But a 3-year-old will feel cramped down there. The upper seat holds 50 lbs, the lower holds 35 lbs. Also—the cargo basket shrinks to almost nothing when the second seat is in use. Plan on using the stroller's back pocket for your stuff.

Toddler Life: The seat is wider and deeper than the Cruz. Big toddlers are more comfortable here. The leg rest adjusts nicely, and the harness is easy to click with one hand. The ride is stable—you feel like you're driving a luxury SUV compared to the Cruz's sporty sedan feel.

The Catch: It's heavy. Lifting this into a trunk is a workout. It's also bulky when folded—it takes up a lot of cargo space. And the full double setup makes the front wheels feel light and sometimes wobbly on uneven terrain.

UPPAbaby Ridge (The Alternative)

If you live in a rural area or love hiking, the Ridge is the third option. It's an all-terrain stroller with air-filled tires and a hand brake. It's not in the same "city" category as the Vista and Cruz—it's a different beast.

Pros: It handles gravel, dirt, and grass like a dream. The hand brake is a lifesaver on hills—you won't feel like you're doing a leg press every time you stop. It has a hand brake, which neither the Vista nor Cruz has.

Cons: It's 26 lbs and massive. It doesn't fold as flat as the Cruz. It's not as car-friendly. And it's $150 more than the Vista. This is a "second stroller" or a "primary stroller for farm families" kind of purchase.

I'm including it here only to say: if you're comparing the Vista and Cruz, the Ridge is not your answer. It's a different category.

UPPAbaby Minu V2 (The Travel Option)

The Minu is the lightweight travel stroller. It folds down to the size of a carry-on bag and weighs 16.9 lbs. This is NOT a newborn stroller without the bassinet attachment, but it's a great "second" stroller to pair with the Cruz or Vista.

Pros: It fits in airplane overhead bins. It has a one-hand, one-step fold. It's the easiest to live with day-to-day if you're a public transit user.

Cons: The seat is lower and smaller. It doesn't handle rough terrain as well. It's not a full-featured stroller—you're trading features for portability.

If you buy the Cruz or Vista, consider the Minu for travel. If you buy the Minu as your only stroller, you'll be disappointed in the newborn phase.

The Hidden Costs Nobody Talks About

The stroller is the cheap part. Here's what you'll actually spend:

  • Infant Car Seat Adapter: $80-$100. You need this to click the car seat in. Not included.
  • Bassinet (if you buy Cruz): $180. The Vista includes it.
  • RumbleSeat (for Vista double mode): $180.
  • Ride-Along Board (for Cruz or Vista): $120. Great for a 3-year-old who can't sit anymore.

Budget an extra $200-$400 on top of the base price.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Vista V2 worth the extra money over the Cruz V2?

Only if you plan to have a second child within 3 years. If you're done after one, the Vista is just a heavier, bulkier stroller that costs more. The Cruz is the better single-child stroller.

Can I use the Cruz V2 from birth?

Technically, yes. The seat reclines to a near-flat position. But I don't recommend it for long periods. Buy the bassinet or use the car seat. Newborns need a flat surface for healthy spine development.

Which is better for tall toddlers?

The Vista V2. The seat is deeper and the leg room is more generous. A tall 4-year-old will look cramped in the Cruz but comfortable in the Vista.

Can I run with these strollers?

No. Neither the Cruz nor the Vista has a fixed front wheel or hand brake. For jogging, you need a dedicated jogging stroller like the Thule Urban Glide 2 or BOB Gear Revolution Flex.

Do they fit in a Mini Cooper trunk?

The Cruz V2 fits in a compact sedan trunk. The Vista V2 does not—it will barely fit in a mid-size SUV. The Minu V2 is the only one that fits in a truly tiny trunk.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the UPPAbaby Cruz V2 if you have one child or plan a 4+ year gap between kids. It's lighter, easier to push, easier to store, and $200 cheaper once you factor in accessories. Buy the UPPAbaby Vista V2 if you're having a second baby within 3 years or want the bassinet included. The extra weight and bulk are a fair trade for not buying a second stroller. Skip the Ridge unless you're an off-road family, and treat the Minu V2 as a travel accessory, not a primary stroller. In 2026, the Cruz is the smarter buy for most families, but the Vista is the only one that grows with you.
