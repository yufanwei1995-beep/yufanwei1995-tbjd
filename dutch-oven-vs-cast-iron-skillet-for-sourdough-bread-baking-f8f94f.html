What to Look For When Buying

Before we get into the specific models, here’s what actually matters for sourdough.

Heat Capacity and Thermal Mass

Cast iron’s superpower is its specific heat — roughly 0.46 kJ/kg·K. That means it absorbs a ton of heat energy and releases it slowly. When you drop a cold, wet dough into a screaming-hot vessel, that heat transfers fast. You get maximum oven spring in the first 15 minutes. A thin steel pan or ceramic dish just can’t deliver that same punch.

Lid Seal and Steam Trap

Sourdough needs steam in the first 15 minutes. The steam keeps the crust soft so the dough can expand. A Dutch oven traps steam from the dough’s own moisture. A skillet doesn’t — you need to add water or ice cubes to create the same effect. That’s the biggest difference between these two tools.

Size and Dough Capacity

Standard sourdough recipes use 500g of flour, which makes a 1kg loaf. That needs a 5.5 to 6-quart Dutch oven or a 10-inch+ skillet. Smaller gear means you’re baking batards that look like dinner rolls.

Weight and Handling

This is the part nobody mentions in reviews. A 6-quart Dutch oven weighs 13-15 pounds empty. You’re lifting it in and out of a 500°F oven twice per bake. If you have wrist issues or arthritis, that’s a real problem. Skillets are lighter and have longer handles, which makes them easier to manage.

Preheating Time

Both need at least 45 minutes to fully preheat in the oven. The Dutch oven takes a bit longer because there’s more mass. Plan your bake schedule around this — you can’t shortcut it.

2. Lodge 10.25-Inch Cast Iron Skillet (Model L10SK3)

Price: $25-$35 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: Batch bakers and beginners

This is the budget champion. At under $30, it’s a third of the price of most Dutch ovens. But here’s the catch: you need a method to create steam. I used the “ice cube trick” — preheat the skillet, drop in a few ice cubes, then add the dough and cover loosely with a stainless steel bowl.

Results: Good, but not great. The crust was thinner and less crackly. The ear was less pronounced. The crumb was still open and airy, but the oven spring was about 15% less than the Dutch oven. The skillet’s lower walls mean less steam stays trapped around the dough.

Where the skillet shines is volume. I can fit two skillets side by side in my standard oven, which means two loaves at once. That’s a massive time saver if you bake for a family or sell at a farmers market.

3. Le Creuset 5.5-Quart Round Dutch Oven (Model 5.5QT)

Price: $380-$450 | Rating: 4.2/5 | Best for: Aesthetic lovers and no-seasoning crowd

This is the enameled option. The interior is smooth ceramic, so no seasoning required and no sticking issues. It also comes in 20+ colors, so it doubles as a serving dish.

But for sourdough specifically, I found it underperforms compared to bare cast iron. The enamel coating reduces thermal conductivity slightly — my oven spring was consistently 5-8% less than the Lodge bare iron pot. The lid seal is also less tight because the enamel creates a slightly uneven rim.

Honestly, you’re paying for beauty and ease of cleaning, not performance. If you already own one, use it. But don’t buy it just for bread.

4. Victoria 12-Inch Cast Iron Skillet (Model VIC-12)

Price: $30-$40 | Rating: 4.0/5 | Best for: Extra-large loaves and deep-dish bakes

The bigger sibling to the Lodge skillet. At 12 inches, it fits a 1.5kg dough comfortably — great for giant country loaves. The handle is longer and stays cooler, which is a nice safety touch.

In testing, the larger surface area meant the dough spread out more, creating a flatter, wider loaf rather than a tall boule. If you like artisan-style flatbreads, this is great. If you want a tall, round sourdough, this isn’t your tool.

It’s also harder to cover. My standard stainless bowl didn’t fit, so I had to use a sheet pan as a lid, which let steam escape. Results were the weakest of all four — the crust was chewy, not crackly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a skillet without a lid?

Yes, but you need a steam source. Put a metal pan with water on the bottom rack, or spritz the dough with water before baking. The crust won’t be as shiny, but it’ll still work.

Do I need to preheat the Dutch oven?

Absolutely. Cold cast iron will make your dough stick and kill the oven spring. Preheat for 45 minutes at 500°F minimum.

Which is easier to clean?

The enameled Le Creuset wins by a mile — just wipe it out. Bare cast iron needs scrubbing and re-oiling. The skillet is easier than the Dutch oven because there’s no deep well to reach into.

Can I bake sourdough in a skillet without parchment?

Technically yes, but you’ll hate yourself. The dough will stick to the bare iron. Use parchment paper — it’s cheap and saves your sanity.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Lodge 6-Quart Dutch Oven if you bake one loaf at a time and want the best possible crust and oven spring. It’s the closest thing to a professional bread oven at a home price. Buy the Lodge 10.25-Inch Skillet if you’re on a tight budget, bake multiple loaves, or have wrist issues. With the ice cube trick, you’ll get 85% of the Dutch oven’s results for half the price. Skip the Le Creuset unless you care more about colors than crust, and skip the Victoria unless you’re baking massive flatbreads. Your sourdough will thank you.
