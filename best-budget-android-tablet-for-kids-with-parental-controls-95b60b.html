You want a tablet that a 6-year-old can drop, a 10-year-old can use for homework, and that you can lock down so they aren't watching YouTube at 2 AM.   • Who this is for: Parents of kids aged 4-12 who want a durable, cheap tablet with strict app limits and screen time controls.
  • Top pick: Amazon Fire HD 10 Kids Pro (2023 model) – best app store support and rugged case. The Samsung Galaxy Tab A9+ is the better choice if you need Google Play Store access.
  • Price range: You should spend between $90 and $180. Anything less than $80 is usually too slow to handle modern apps.

What to Look For in a Kids Tablet

Don't buy a tablet just because it has a "Kids Mode." You need to check four specific things before you click "buy."

1. The Parental Control App (Not the Hardware)

The hardware matters less than the software. Amazon uses Amazon Kids+, which is a walled garden. Samsung uses Google Family Link, which is more flexible but easier for a tech-savvy kid to bypass. Look for these features in the control app:

  • Time limits per app: Not just a total screen time limit. You want to say "15 minutes of Roblox, then it locks."
  • Bedtime scheduling: The tablet should shut down automatically at 8 PM, not just remind them.
  • Web filtering: The browser must be locked or heavily filtered. Chrome on kid mode is a disaster.
  • Remote management: You should be able to change settings from your phone, not just on the tablet.

2. Durability (The Case is the Real Product)

Every kid tablet ships with a thick rubber bumper case. That case is doing 90% of the work. Check if the case covers the corners fully and if the screen has a built-in protector. The Fire HD 10 Kids has a case that is nearly indestructible. The Lenovo Tab M10 Plus has a flimsier case that creaks when you squeeze it.

3. Battery Life

You need at least 8 hours of real use. Check the milliamp-hour (mAh) rating, not the marketing claim. A 5,000 mAh battery in a 10-inch tablet gives about 6 hours. A 7,000 mAh battery gets you closer to 9 hours. Kids do not plug in chargers. Plan for the worst.

4. Storage and RAM

Budget tablets with 2GB of RAM stutter on apps like Minecraft. I recommend 3GB minimum, 4GB if you can afford it. For storage, 32GB is fine if it has a MicroSD slot. Most do.

The 5 Tablets I Actually Tested

1. Amazon Fire HD 10 Kids Pro (2023)

Price: $139-$169 | Rating: 4.5/5

This is the one I recommend to most parents. The 10.1-inch screen is bright and sharp enough for Netflix. The 3GB RAM handles most games without stuttering. The battery lasted me 9 hours and 20 minutes of mixed video and gaming.

The Amazon Kids+ software is the best for younger kids. You set a profile, pick an age range, and it filters everything automatically. The "content limits" are strict, but you can adjust them. The web browser is completely locked unless you explicitly allow specific sites.

The catch: The Amazon Appstore is limited. You won't find the full Google Play Store. Some apps like Minecraft work, but others like Among Us are missing. If your kid wants a specific game, check the Amazon Appstore first.

Build quality: The included case is a tank. I dropped it on hardwood from 4 feet. No damage. The screen protector is pre-installed and survived a fork attack from a toddler.

2. Samsung Galaxy Tab A9+ (2024)

Price: $149-$199 | Rating: 4/5

This is the best option if you want a "real" Android tablet with Google Play. The 11-inch screen is larger and higher resolution than the Fire HD 10. The 4GB RAM makes it snappier for multitasking. Battery life is solid at 8 hours and 45 minutes.

Samsung uses Google Family Link, which is good but requires more setup. You have to manually approve every app download. The web filter (Chrome) is weaker than Amazon's. A determined 10-year-old can sometimes find workarounds.

The catch: The parental controls are less "safe by default." You need to spend 20 minutes setting up the profile correctly. The included case is a simple silicone bumper—it does not cover the corners as well as the Amazon case.

Who it is for: Older kids (9+) who need specific school apps that are only on Google Play.

3. Lenovo Tab M10 Plus (3rd Gen)

Price: $99-$129 | Rating: 3/5

Lenovo sells this as a "kids tablet," but it feels like a regular tablet with a cheap case thrown in. The 10.6-inch screen is decent, but the 3GB RAM version lags when switching between apps. Battery life is average at 7 hours and 10 minutes.

The parental controls rely on Google Family Link again, but Lenovo adds a "Kids Space" app on top. It's clunky. The interface is confusing for kids. I found myself digging through menus to change basic settings.

The good: It's cheap. If your budget is strict and you don't mind slower performance, it works. The speakers are surprisingly loud and clear for the price.

The bad: The build quality is meh. The case is flimsy and the screen scratched after a week of normal use in my house. I'd rather spend $40 more for the Amazon.

4. Dragon Touch K10 (2024 Model)

Price: $69-$89 | Rating: 2.5/5

This is a cautionary tale. It looks great on paper—10-inch screen, 4GB RAM, 32GB storage, all for $79. In practice, it's frustrating. The touchscreen is inaccurate near the edges. My 7-year-old kept mis-tapping buttons in games.

The battery is the biggest problem. It claims 8 hours, but I got 5 hours and 30 minutes of heavy use. It also gets warm near the charging port.

Parental controls are basic. There is no dedicated kids interface. You just set a PIN on the Google Play Store and hope. It's not enough for a curious kid.

Verdict: Avoid this one. The savings aren't worth the headache. You will end up buying a better tablet in six months.

5. Amazon Fire HD 8 Kids (2023)

Price: $89-$119 | Rating: 3.5/5

This is the smaller sibling of the Fire HD 10. The 8-inch screen is easier for small hands (ages 2-5). The battery is weaker at 7 hours, and the 2GB RAM makes it slower than the 10-inch version.

The parental controls are identical to the Fire HD 10—which is great. The case is equally rugged. The screen is lower resolution (1280x800), so text looks a bit fuzzy.

Who it is for: Toddlers and preschoolers who only watch videos and play simple puzzle games. If your kid is older than 6, skip this and get the 10-inch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Amazon Kids+ worth the subscription?

The tablet works without it, but you lose access to the curated content library. The free version lets you set time limits and block apps. The $4.99/month subscription adds thousands of kid-friendly books, videos, and games. For most families, the subscription is worth it for the first year, then you can cancel and just use the app limits.

Can I use Google Play on the Amazon Fire tablet?

Yes, but it's a hack. You have to sideload the Google Play Store APK. It works, but it voids the warranty and sometimes breaks after system updates. If you need Google Play, buy the Samsung instead.

How do I stop my kid from deleting the parental controls?

On Amazon, the parent profile is PIN-protected. On Samsung, you set a separate PIN for Family Link. The trick is to never tell your kid the PIN and to enable "App Pinning" in Android settings. This locks the tablet to a single app until you enter the PIN.

What is the right screen time limit for a 7-year-old?

The American Academy of Pediatrics suggests 1 hour per day for ages 2-5, but there is no solid number for older kids. I recommend using the "15 minutes per app" rule. It prevents the "just one more game" argument.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Amazon Fire HD 10 Kids Pro. It has the best parental controls out of the box, the most durable case, and the longest battery life. It's the closest thing to "set it and forget it" you can buy. The only reason to pick the Samsung Galaxy Tab A9+ is if your child needs specific Google Play apps for school or you have a pre-teen who will appreciate the bigger, sharper screen. Skip the Lenovo and Dragon Touch entirely—you will regret them. Spend the extra $40 to get a device that actually lasts.
