Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before you buy, understand that not all nonstick sets are safe for glass tops. Here's the short list of what matters.

1. Flat Bottom = Non-Negotiable

Glass cooktops heat from a flat ceramic surface. If your pan has even a slight concave bottom, it won't make contact. Heat transfer drops, and cooking time doubles. To test, place a straightedge across the bottom. You shouldn't see a gap. The All-Clad D3 and GreenPan Valencia Pro both passed this test perfectly.

2. Weight Matters

Heavy pans feel premium, but on a glass top, they can be a liability. Dropping a 6-pound cast iron skillet is a cracked cooktop. Look for sets that weigh under 5 pounds per pan. The Scanpan sets are heavy-duty but stay manageable.

3. Coating Quality

Most nonstick pans use PTFE (Teflon). It works but degrades over time. Ceramic coatings are safer but less durable. For glass tops, you want a coating that resists scratching when you slide the pan. The GreenPan's Thermolon coating performed best in my scratch tests.

4. Induction Ready

Even if you have a radiant glass top now, you might switch to induction later. Most new glass tops are induction-compatible. Make sure the set you buy says "induction ready." The All-Clad D3 is fully magnetic. The GreenPan Valencia Pro has a magnetic base too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will nonstick pans scratch my glass cooktop?

No, if you choose pans with flat, smooth bases. Avoid pans with rough or textured bottoms. The GreenPan and All-Clad bases are perfectly smooth.

Can I use metal utensils on these pans?

Only the Scanpan is rated for metal utensils. For the others, stick to wood, silicone, or plastic to protect the coating.

How do I clean them on a glass top?

Let the pan cool completely before washing. Use warm soapy water and a soft sponge. Dishwasher-safe claims are true, but hand washing extends the coating's life.

What's the best way to prevent warping?

Never heat an empty pan on high. Always start with low or medium heat and let the pan warm gradually. This is the #1 cause of warping on glass tops.
