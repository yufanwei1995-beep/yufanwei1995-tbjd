What to Look For in a Multi-Cat Camera Feeder

Before you spend money, understand what actually matters when you have more than one cat. Most single-cat feeders are useless here. Here's the checklist.

1. Cat Identification Technology

This is the #1 feature. You need either RFID tags (small tags that hang on the collar) or built-in microchip readers that detect your cat's implanted chip. Without this, any cat can eat any food. The feeder door stays locked until the correct cat approaches.

Tradeoff: Microchip readers are cleaner (no collar tag needed) but cost $40-60 more. RFID tags are cheaper but can be lost if the collar breaks. Both work fine — just pick based on your budget.

2. Camera Quality and Field of View

You want at least 1080p resolution and a wide-angle lens (100° or more). A 720p camera might show you that food was dispensed, but you won't see which cat ate it or if your cat is looking unwell. Night vision is non-negotiable — cats are most active at dawn and dusk.

3. Portion Control and Scheduling

Look for feeders that allow 4-6 meals per day with 1/8 cup (or smaller) increments. For multi-cat homes, you also need the ability to set different portion sizes for different cats. Some feeders let you assign each cat's RFID tag to a specific portion. That's the gold standard.

4. Food Capacity

For multiple cats, you need at least 4 liters (about 1 gallon) of dry food capacity. Anything smaller means refilling every 2-3 days, which defeats the purpose. If you have more than 3 cats, look for 6+ liter hoppers.

5. App Reliability and Alerts

The app needs to send push notifications when food is dispensed, when the hopper is low, and when a cat eats. You also want two-way audio so you can call your cat if needed. Test the app before buying — read recent reviews on the App Store or Google Play.

6. Power Backup

If the power goes out, most feeders stop working. Look for models with battery backup (AA batteries or built-in rechargeable) that kicks in automatically. The best ones run 30-60 days on battery alone.

7. Cleaning Difficulty

The food hopper and bowl should be dishwasher safe or at least easy to hand-wash. If the feeder has lots of nooks and crannies, grease and kibble dust will build up. Look for a smooth hopper interior and a removable bowl.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a single-cat feeder with multiple cats?

Only if your cats don't steal food. If one cat is on a prescription diet and the other isn't, you need a feeder with cat identification. Otherwise, the feeder is just a fancy bowl.

Do RFID tags bother cats?

Most cats ignore them within 24 hours. They weigh about 5 grams — less than a collar bell. If your cat is sensitive, try the PetSafe microchip model instead.

How long does the battery backup last?

The Petlibro lasts about 30 days on 4 AA batteries. PetSafe lasts 45 days. The others don't have battery backup, so a power outage means manual feeding.

Can I set different portion sizes for different cats?

Yes, on the Petlibro and PetSafe. You assign each cat's tag/chip to a specific portion size in the app. The SureFeed also does this, but the tiny hopper makes it impractical.

Will the camera work if I'm in a different country?

Yes, as long as you have Wi-Fi and the app installed. All these feeders use cloud-based video streaming.
