  • Who this is for: Frequent flyers, business travelers, and anyone who wants to survive a long-haul flight without engine roar drowning out their podcasts.

Plane cabins are loud. The constant drone of jet engines sits around 75-85 decibels. That's not just annoying; it's fatiguing. You land exhausted before you even start your trip. Noise-cancelling headphones solve this. But the big question is: do you want the big comfy earmuffs or the tiny buds that disappear into your ears?

I've tested both styles on dozens of flights. Red-eyes, short hops, and 14-hour marathines. Here's the honest breakdown of over-ear vs in-ear noise cancelling headphones for flying, with specific product recommendations that actually hold up.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters at 35,000 Feet

Before we get to specific products, you need to understand the four pillars of a good flying headphone. Skip this and you'll buy something that looks good on paper but fails in the air.

1. Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) Quality

Not all ANC is created equal. The best systems use multiple microphones to sample the cabin noise and generate an inverted sound wave. You want at least 6 microphones for solid performance. Look for products from Sony, Bose, or Apple. They've spent years perfecting this tech. Cheap brands claim "hybrid ANC" but it's often just a marketing term for a single mic that barely does anything.

2. Battery Life

A transatlantic flight is roughly 7-8 hours. A transpacific flight can be 13-15 hours. You need a battery that outlasts your journey. Over-ear models with 30+ hours are common now. In-ear models typically top out around 8-10 hours, but the charging case extends that significantly. Always check if the product supports fast charging—a 5-minute charge that gives you an extra hour is a lifesaver on tight connections.

3. Comfort for Long Wear

This is where over-ear wins, hands down. Clamping force matters. Headband padding matters. Earcup depth matters. If the earcups press on your ears or the headband creates a hot spot, you'll be miserable by hour three. In-ear models have improved, but some people still get ear fatigue or soreness after a few hours. If you have small ear canals, in-ear might be painful. If you wear glasses, over-ear can press the arms into your temples.

4. Portability and Storage

In-ear buds live in a small case that fits in your pocket. Over-ear cans need a dedicated pouch or case in your carry-on. This sounds obvious, but it changes how you travel. If you're a minimalist one-bag traveler, in-ear is the way. If you have space and want comfort, over-ear wins.

5. Connectivity and Multipoint

You'll want to switch between your phone and laptop without re-pairing. Multipoint Bluetooth lets you connect to two devices simultaneously. This is standard on premium models now. Also check if the headphones have a wired option for when the airline's entertainment system calls. Not all do.

Individual Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

The king of over-ear ANC. Sony's fifth generation flagship is the benchmark every other headphone is measured against.

The noise cancellation is genuinely spooky. The V1 processor and eight microphones handle low-frequency engine rumble effortlessly. I flew from New York to London and didn't hear a single announcement until I turned on transparent mode. The cabin noise vanished. My playlist sounded clear and detailed at 60% volume.

Comfort is exceptional. The earcups are deeper than previous models, so my ears never touched the inside. The headband distributes weight well—no hot spots after 6 hours. They weigh 250 grams, and you do feel them, but the plush padding makes it a non-issue.

Battery life is rated at 30 hours with ANC on. I got 28.5 hours in my testing. The 3-minute quick charge gives 3 hours of playback, which is perfect for a delayed flight. The carrying case is flatter than the XM4's, making it easier to slide into a backpack sleeve.

The downsides? They don't fold flat. The case is still bulky. And at $400, they're expensive. But if you fly more than a few times a year, they're worth every penny.

Specs: 250g, 30hr battery, USB-C fast charge, Bluetooth 5.2, multipoint, LDAC support.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones

Bose invented consumer ANC in the 1980s. The QuietComfort Ultra is the culmination of decades of refinement. It's also the only over-ear that rivals Sony in noise cancellation.

The ANC here handles voices better than Sony. If you're sensitive to chatty neighbors, Bose's algorithm does a slightly better job. The engine rumble cancellation is equal to the Sony. The "immersive audio" mode adds spatial processing, but it's a gimmick for most people and drains the battery faster.

Comfort is the best in the business. The earcups are like memory foam pillows. At 254 grams, they're slightly heavier than the Sony, but the clamping force is gentler. I wore these for a 9-hour flight to Tokyo and forgot they were on.

Battery life is 24 hours, which is 6 hours less than the Sony. The case is also bulky. But there's a wired mode for airline entertainment systems, and the microphone quality for calls is better than Sony's.

Priced at $429, they're more expensive. The question is whether the slightly better voice cancellation and comfort justify the $30 premium. For many, the answer is yes.

Specs: 254g, 24hr battery, USB-C, Bluetooth 5.3, multipoint, immersive audio mode.

The noise cancellation is shockingly good for the size. It's about 85% as effective as the over-ear version. On a flight, the engine roar becomes a distant hum instead of a loud drone. Voices are still slightly audible, but they're muffled enough to ignore.

Comfort is the tricky part. The earbuds come with multiple tip sizes and a stability band. I found the right combination after a few tries. Once dialed in, they're comfortable for about 3 hours. Battery life is 6 hours with ANC on, and the case adds 18 more. The case is small—about the size of a lighter—and fits easily in a jeans pocket. This is the biggest advantage over over-ear models.

The touch controls are intuitive. Tap once to pause, twice to skip, and hold to cycle ANC modes. The "aware" mode lets you hear announcements without removing the buds.

At $299, they're pricey for earbuds. But if you hate the bulk of over-ear headphones, this is the best flying companion you can get.

Specs: 6hr battery (24 with case), Bluetooth 5.3, multipoint, wireless charging, IPX4 water resistance.

Sony WF-1000XM5

Sony's flagship earbuds are the direct competitor to Bose. They offer slightly better sound quality but slightly worse ANC.

The sound signature is warm and detailed. Bass is punchy without overwhelming mids. Vocals are clear. If you care about music quality over pure silence, these earbuds are better than the Bose.

ANC performance is close to Bose but not equal. The engine rumble is handled well, but there's a slight high-frequency hiss that Bose eliminates better. On a plane, you'll notice the difference in the first minute.

Comfort is similar to Bose. The earbuds are smaller and lighter than the previous generation, which helps. I got 3.5 hours before needing a break. The foam tips are comfortable but wear out faster than silicone.

Battery life is rated at 8 hours with ANC. I got 7.5. The case provides two additional charges. Fast charging gives 1 hour from 3 minutes.

At $248, they're $50 cheaper than Bose. If you're on a budget and want great sound with good ANC, this is the pick. If ANC is your priority, spend the extra money on Bose.

Specs: 8hr battery (24 with case), Bluetooth 5.3, multipoint, wireless charging, IPX4 water resistance.

ANC is decent but not premium. It reduces engine noise by about 60-70%. You'll still hear a low hum, but it's manageable. Voices are clearly audible, which might be a pro or con depending on your perspective.

Comfort is good. The earcups are plush and the headband is well-padded. At 263 grams, they're slightly heavier than the Sony, but the weight distribution is fine. I wore them for a 4-hour flight without discomfort.

Battery life is the standout feature. 40 hours with ANC on. That's better than every premium headphone on this list. A 5-minute charge gives 4 hours of playback.

Sound quality is surprisingly good. Bass is boosted, which some people love and others find muddy. The included EQ in the app lets you adjust the sound profile.

The build quality is plastic and feels less premium. The ANC isn't on the level of Bose or Sony. But for a flight or two a year, the savings are worth it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use noise cancelling headphones on a plane?

Yes. ANC headphones are safe to use on planes. They don't interfere with aircraft systems. You can use them during taxi, takeoff, and landing as long as you're not wearing them during safety demonstrations when the crew needs your attention. Most airlines allow Bluetooth devices in flight mode.

Do I need to turn off noise cancelling on a plane?

No. ANC is passive—it uses microphones and speakers, not wireless transmission. You can keep ANC on even when Bluetooth is off in airplane mode. If your headphones have a wired mode, you can use them with the airline's entertainment system while ANC continues to work.

Are in-ear or over-ear better for sleeping on a plane?

Over-ear is generally better for sleeping. The big earcups block out light and provide a cushion for your head against the window. In-ear buds can work but may hurt if you sleep on your side. If you're a side sleeper, consider in-ear buds with a low profile—they'll press less into your ear.

Why do my noise cancelling headphones make my ears hurt?

This is the "pressure" sensation. ANC creates a vacuum-like feeling by canceling low-frequency waves. It goes away after a few minutes. If it persists, your headphones might have too much clamping force (over-ear) or the earbud tips might be too big (in-ear). Try a different size.

Can I use noise cancelling headphones with the airline's entertainment system?

Yes, but you'll need a wired connection. Most premium headphones come with a 3.5mm cable. Some airlines have dual-prong jacks, so you'll need a dual-prong adapter. In-ear buds with a detachable cable work too, but they're rare now—most are wireless-only.

Bottom Line

For most people, the over-ear Sony WH-1000XM5 is the best all-around flying headphone. It has the best ANC, long battery life, and comfort for long flights. If you fly more than once a month, the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones are a better choice if you value voice cancellation and call quality. For travelers who hate bulky headphones, the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds are the top in-ear pick—they're 85% as good as the over-ear version in a pocketable form. If you fly rarely and want to save money, the Anker Soundcore Life Q30 gets you 80% of the experience for 20% of the price. Skip the Sony WF-1000XM5 unless you're a Sony fan—the Bose earbuds have better ANC for flying.
