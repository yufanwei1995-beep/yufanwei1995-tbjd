This is for anyone who throws actual pool parties — not just someone who wants a speaker for the shower. You need something that survives splashes, screams, and direct sunlight.   • Who this is for: Pool owners, beach-goers, and anyone who needs music loud enough to overcome splashing without killing the speaker.
  • Our top pick: Ultimate Ears BOOM 3 — best balance of 360° sound, durability, and floatability. The JBL Charge 5 wins on bass but sinks like a rock.
  • Price range: $80 to $180. You don't need to spend over $200 for a killer pool speaker.

What to Look For in a Pool Party Speaker

Don't buy on looks alone. Here's what actually matters when the speaker is six inches from the water.

IP Rating: Don't Get Fooled by "Waterproof"

IP67 means fully dustproof and can survive submersion in 1 meter of water for 30 minutes. IPX7 is the same but no dust rating. IPX6 means water jets but NOT submersion. For pool parties, you want IP67 or IPX7. Anything less is a risk. The JBL Charge 5 has IP67, which is solid. But here's the catch: it floats like a brick. If it falls in the deep end, you're diving for it.

Floatability: The Game Changer

This is the difference between a bathroom speaker and a pool speaker. If it floats, you can toss it in the water and it becomes part of the fun. If it sinks, you're either tethered to the edge or risking your phone's life in the deep end. The Ultimate Ears BOOM 3 floats. The JBL Charge 5 does not. That alone changed my ranking.

Battery Life: Real Hours vs Marketing Hours

Manufacturers rate battery life at 50% volume. Pool parties run at 90%+. Expect 50-60% of the claimed battery life at party volume. The JBL Charge 5 claims 20 hours. I got 11 hours of actual screaming-loud playback. That's still good, but know the real numbers.

Bass Response: Chest-Thumping vs Tinny

Outdoor spaces eat bass. A speaker that sounds punchy in your living room will sound thin by the pool. You need either a passive radiator (like the Charge 5) or a larger driver. Don't trust small speakers with "Bass Boost" buttons — physics wins.

Charging Port Protection

This is the weak point on most speakers. If the rubber flap over the USB-C port isn't sealed properly, chlorine water gets in and kills the board. Check the flap tension before buying. The JBL Charge 5 has a tight flap. The Anker and Tribit speakers have flimsier covers.

The 5 Speakers I Tested

1. JBL Charge 5 — The Bass King That Sinks

Price: $149-$179 | Rating: 4/5

The Charge 5 is the benchmark for a reason. It has a 30W driver with a passive radiator that delivers real, room-filling bass. At 100% volume, it stays clear with zero distortion — something cheap speakers can't handle. The PartyBoost feature lets you pair two JBL speakers for stereo, which is huge for a pool deck.

But it sinks. I dropped it in the shallow end and had to fish it out with a net. The IP67 rating means it survived fine, but it's not fun. It's also heavy at 2.1 lbs (960g) — you need a solid surface. The fabric mesh picks up sunscreen and pool chemicals, and it doesn't clean off easily.

Verdict: Best for a stationary setup on a table, not for tossing around.

2. Ultimate Ears BOOM 3 — The Pool Party Champion

Price: $129-$149 | Rating: 5/5

This is the one I'd buy again. It floats. You can throw it into the deep end and it bobs around playing music. The 360° sound means it sounds identical from every angle — no "sweet spot" to fight over. The cylindrical shape fits in a cup holder, and the fabric is replaceable if it gets gross.

Bass isn't as deep as the Charge 5, but it's punchy enough for poolside. The "Magic Button" on top is gimmicky — it just cycles playlists on Apple Music. Battery life is 15 hours claimed, I got 8.5 at full volume. It's IP67 rated, drops don't hurt it, and the chemical-resistant fabric is a win.

Verdict: The best overall for actual pool use. Floatability wins.

3. Anker Soundcore Motion Boom — The Budget Bruiser

Price: $79-$99 | Rating: 4/5

This thing is a tank. It has twin 20W drivers and a passive radiator that produces shockingly good bass for the price — honestly close to the Charge 5. It also has a strap so you can hang it from an umbrella or a fence. IP67 rated, and it floats (barely, with the strap on top).

The downsides: it's bulky at 2.4 lbs (1.09kg) and the Bluetooth range is weak — I lost connection at 30 feet with a phone in a pocket. The app is clunky but you don't need it. Battery claims 24 hours, I got 13 at party volume. That's the best runtime of the group.

Verdict: Best budget pick. Buy this if you're not ready to spend $150.

4. Sony SRS-XG300 — The Heavyweight With Lights

Price: $149-$199 | Rating: 3.5/5

Sony's mid-range party speaker has a handle that doubles as a stand, X-Balanced drivers, and a built-in light show that syncs to the beat. The sound is clear and loud with decent bass, but not as impactful as the Charge 5. IP67 rated, but it sinks — no floatation help here.

Battery life is 25 hours claimed, 14 actual at high volume. The lights drain it faster. The handle is genuinely useful for carrying it around a crowded pool deck, and the fabric is water-repellent. The problem is the price — at $199, it's $50 more than the Charge 5 for less bass.

Verdict: Only buy this if you want the light show. Otherwise, the JBL is better value.

5. Tribit StormBox Blast — The Loudest, Ugliest Option

Price: $129-$159 | Rating: 3.5/5

The StormBox Blast is a 90W monster that is obnoxiously loud — louder than the Charge 5 by a noticeable margin. It has a subwoofer and two tweeters, and it sounds phenomenal outdoors. It also has a built-in handle and a massive 30-hour claimed battery (I got 16 hours). IPX7 rated, but again, it sinks.

The tradeoff: it's gigantic. It weighs 5.2 lbs (2.36kg) — this is not a "grab and go" speaker. It looks like a cooler. The app is useless, and the EQ presets are limited. If you're hosting a party for 50 people, this is the one. For a casual day by the pool, it's overkill.

Verdict: The volume king for big groups, but too bulky for most.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I leave the JBL Charge 5 in the pool overnight?

No. IP67 means it survives a 30-minute dunk, not a 12-hour soak. Chlorine degrades the rubber seals over time. Take it out when you're done.

Is the UE BOOM 3 loud enough for a party?

For 10-15 people, yes. For 30+, no. It maxes out around 90dB, which is fine for a backyard, but the Tribit or JBL will go louder.

Does the Anker Motion Boom really float?

Yes, but barely. It bobs with the strap side up. Don't expect it to stay perfectly upright like the BOOM 3.

How do I clean sunscreen off these speakers?

Use a damp microfiber cloth with mild soap. Do NOT use alcohol or acetone — it eats the fabric and rubber. The JBL Charge 5's fabric is the hardest to clean. The UE BOOM 3's fabric wipes clean easiest.

Bottom Line

Buy the Ultimate Ears BOOM 3 if you want the best all-around pool speaker — it floats, sounds great, and survives everything. Buy the JBL Charge 5 if you prioritize bass above all else and plan to keep it on a table away from the water. Buy the Anker Motion Boom if you're on a budget and need 90% of the performance for 60% of the price. Skip the Sony unless you need the lights, and skip the Tribit unless you're DJing a block party.
