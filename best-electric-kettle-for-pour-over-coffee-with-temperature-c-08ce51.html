Quick Summary

You're here because you're tired of under-extracted, sour coffee or over-extracted, bitter cups. The culprit is almost always water temperature. A good electric kettle with temperature control fixes this instantly.

  • Who this is for: Home brewers who own a V60, Chemex, or Kalita Wave and want repeatable, precise brews.
  • Our top pick: The Fellow Stagg EKG is still the benchmark for a reason. It's fast, precise, and the gooseneck spout is a dream.
  • Price range: You'll spend between $80 and $200 for a quality variable-temperature gooseneck kettle. Anything cheaper usually means flimsy build or sloppy temperature control.

What to Look For in a Pour-Over Kettle

Don't buy the first pretty kettle you see. Here are the specs that actually matter for pour-over.

Temperature Accuracy and Stability

You want a kettle that hits your set temp and holds it. Look for a thermostat rated to within ±1°F (0.5°C). The Stagg EKG does this. Many cheaper kettles overshoot by 5-10°F before settling, which ruins your first pour. Also check how the kettle maintains temp. Most use a "hold" mode. Make sure it doesn't blast heat and cycle wildly, which causes temperature swings.

Gooseneck Spout Design

This is the heart of a pour-over kettle. A thin, curved spout gives you a slow, controlled stream. You need this for the circular, even saturation of coffee grounds. Look for a spout that doesn't drip after you finish pouring. A sharp, clean cut-off is a sign of good design. Wide spouts are for tea, not coffee.

Heating Speed and Power

Nobody likes waiting. Most quality kettles have 1200W to 1500W heating elements. The Fellow Stagg EKG hits 1500W. That brings 1 liter from room temp to boiling in about 3-4 minutes. Cheaper 1000W kettles can take 6-7 minutes. That's a long time when you're making coffee before work.

Capacity and Weight

Standard capacity is 1 liter (0.9L usable). That's enough for 2-3 cups of pour-over. If you're only making one cup, a smaller 0.6L kettle is easier to handle and heats faster. But remember, a lighter kettle is easier to control. Heavy kettles cause hand fatigue and shakier pours.

Build Quality and Materials

You're heating water that touches your coffee. Get food-grade 304 stainless steel. Avoid plastic interiors. Check the lid seal — a loose lid lets steam escape and makes temperature control worse. A counterbalanced handle keeps the kettle stable when full.

The 4 Kettles We Tested

I spent three weeks using these kettles side-by-side. I tested temperature accuracy with a digital probe, pour control with a V60, and heating speed. Here's the honest breakdown.

1. Fellow Stagg EKG

The benchmark. Still the best overall.

The Stagg EKG is the kettle everyone recommends, and for good reason. The 1500W heater brings 1 liter to boiling in just over 3 minutes. Temperature control is dead accurate — my probe showed it holding 205°F within 1 degree for a full 10-minute hold. The gooseneck spout is precise without being annoyingly slow. Bottom line: If you want the best and don't mind the price, this is it. It's the one I reach for daily.

2. OXO Brew Gooseneck Kettle with Variable Temperature

The smart-value pick. Almost as good, costs less.

OXO's offering is the Stagg's main rival. It also has 1500W of power and accurate temperature control. The key difference is the "smart" features. You can set custom presets and adjust the hold time from 1 to 30 minutes. The handle is comfortable and well-balanced. The spout is good, though slightly less refined than the Stagg's — pours are a touch less precise at very slow flow rates.

The build is solid, but the plastic base feels slightly cheaper than Fellow's. The display is clear, but the buttons are a bit small. One annoyance: the "hold" function defaults to on, so you have to turn it off if you just want to boil and stop. It's a minor quibble.

Bottom line: For $50 less than the Stagg, you get 95% of the performance. This is the smart buy for most people.

3. Bonavita Variable Temperature Gooseneck Kettle (BV382510V)

The budget veteran. Still relevant, but showing its age.

The Bonavita has been the budget champion for years. It does the basics well: accurate temperature settings, a decent gooseneck spout, and a 1200W heater. It's reliable and gets the job done. The base has a simple dial and hold function.

But compared to the newer competition, it feels dated. The 1200W heater is slow — almost 5 minutes to boil a full pot. The temperature display is a simple LCD that's hard to read in low light. The spout is serviceable but drips slightly after you finish pouring. The build quality is decent but the plastic handle feels less sturdy than the metal ones on the Fellow and OXO.

Bottom line: If you're on a strict budget, this works fine. But spend the extra $30 and get the OXO if you can.

4. Hario Buono Variable Temperature Kettle (BV120H)

A beloved spout, but the electronics are lacking.

Hario makes the original Buono, a stovetop favorite. This electric version has the same iconic, ultra-thin gooseneck spout — it's the most precise of any kettle here. For very slow, controlled pours, nothing beats it. The build is all stainless steel and feels great.

However, the temperature control is the weak point. It uses a simple dial with no digital display. You have to set it by feel, and it's not very accurate. My probe showed it overshooting by as much as 8°F from the dial setting. The 1200W heater is also slow. And the base is basic — no hold function to speak of.

Bottom line: Only buy this if you're a purist who values the spout above all else and don't mind fiddling with temperature guesswork.

Frequently Asked Questions

What temperature do I use for pour-over coffee?

Start with 205°F (96°C). This is the sweet spot for most light and medium roasts. If your coffee tastes bitter, drop to 200°F. If it tastes sour and under-extracted, bump it up to 208°F. Dark roasts do better at 195-200°F to avoid burning them.

Is a temperature-controlled kettle worth it?

Yes, if you care about consistency. Boiling water is 212°F, which is too hot and will scorch your grounds, causing bitterness. Without temp control, you're guessing. The kettle is the single biggest upgrade to your pour-over setup after a good grinder.

Can I use these kettles for tea?

Absolutely. Green tea needs 175°F, white tea 180°F, and black tea 205°F. The variable temperature settings make these kettles perfect for any hot beverage. Just don't use the gooseneck spout to pour into a teacup — it's slower than a regular kettle for that purpose.

How long do these kettles last?

With proper care (don't boil dry, descale every 3-6 months), a quality kettle should last 5+ years. The heating elements are robust in the Fellow and OXO. The Bonavita and Hario have more basic electronics that might fail sooner.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Fellow Stagg EKG if you want the best performance and don't mind spending $180. It's fast, accurate, and a joy to pour with. For everyone else, the OXO Brew is the smarter choice. It costs less, heats just as fast, and holds temperature just as well. The spout is slightly less refined, but I'd bet most people can't tell the difference. Skip the Bonavita unless you're really pinching pennies. And only get the Hario if you're a spout fanatic who values pour control over everything else. Whichever you choose, you'll finally stop guessing your water temperature.
