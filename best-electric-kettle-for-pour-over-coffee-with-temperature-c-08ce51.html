What to Look For in a Pour Over Kettle

Not all electric kettles are the same. Here's what actually matters when you're brewing coffee, not tea.

Temperature Accuracy Matters More Than You Think

Water at 212°F (100°C) scorches coffee grounds. You want water around 200°F (93°C) for light roasts, and 195°F (90°C) for dark roasts. A kettle that holds temperature within +/-2°F is fine. If it drifts by 5-10°F, your coffee will taste different every single morning.

Gooseneck Spout Design

The whole point of a gooseneck is control. A narrow, curved spout gives you a slow, steady stream. This lets you saturate the coffee grounds evenly without splashing. Look for a spout that starts dripping slowly and doesn't glug.

Hold Temperature Feature

Do you brew one cup or four? If you're just making a single V60, you don't need a kettle that holds temp for an hour. But if you brew multiple cups or drink tea too, a 30-60 minute hold function is worth the extra cash.

Capacity and Weight

Most kettles are 0.9L or 1L. That's enough for 2-3 cups of pour over. Heavier kettles (like the Fellow) feel solid but can be tough to pour when full. Lighter plastic ones are easier to handle but often lack precision.

Build Quality and Safety

Look for 304 stainless steel interiors. Avoid plastic that touches the water. Check for auto shut-off. A kettle that boils dry and doesn't shut off is a fire hazard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need temperature control?

Yes, if you're serious about pour over. Boiling water (212°F) will make your coffee bitter. Precise temp control is the easiest upgrade you can make to your brew.

What temperature should I use for pour over?

Light roasts: 205°F (96°C). Medium roasts: 200°F (93°C). Dark roasts: 195°F (90°C). Start there and adjust to taste.

Can I use these kettles for tea?

Absolutely. Green tea needs 175°F, black tea needs 205°F. All the kettles above handle that range easily.

Is a gooseneck kettle worth it?

If you're using a regular kettle, you're pouring too fast and too erratically. A gooseneck gives you the control to saturate grounds evenly. That's the difference between a good cup and a great one.

How long does it take to boil?

All the kettles I tested took between 2.5 and 4 minutes to boil a full capacity of water. The Fellow was the fastest at 2 minutes 45 seconds.
