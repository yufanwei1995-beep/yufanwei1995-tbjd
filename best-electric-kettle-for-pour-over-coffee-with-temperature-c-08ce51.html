If you are serious about pour-over coffee, the kettle is not a accessory—it is the instrument. A standard pot boils water to 212°F, which scorches delicate coffee oils and turns your $20 bag of beans bitter. What to Look for in a Pour-Over Kettle

Not all "gooseneck" kettles are equal. Here is what actually matters.

Temperature Accuracy & Stability

Setpoint accuracy is the headline feature. A good kettle holds water within ±1°F of your target. Cheaper ones swing ±5°F or more, which ruins light roasts. Look for a PID controller—that's the brain that maintains temperature. The Stagg EKG Pro uses one, and it shows in the cup.

Heating Speed

If you brew daily, a 5-minute wait is annoying. Most 1200W kettles boil 1L in 5-6 minutes. The Bonavita is slower at 6-7 minutes. The Oxo is faster at 4.5 minutes. Consider how much time you have in the morning.

Pour Flow Control

The gooseneck spout's shape dictates your flow. A thin, curved spout (like the Stagg) gives you a pencil-thin stream for precise bloom pours. A wider spout (like the Cosori) is more forgiving but less precise. This is the difference between a perfect spiral and a chaotic splash.

Capacity & Size

Most kettles hold 0.9L to 1L. That's enough for two 12oz mugs. If you brew for a crowd, look for 1.2L+ models. Also check the base footprint—some are huge. The Stagg has a small base, which is great for cramped counters.

Hold Temperature Feature

This keeps water at your set temp for up to 60 minutes. Critical if you brew multiple cups or do a slow pour-over ritual. The Bonavita holds for 60 minutes. The Cosori only holds for 30. Know your habits before buying.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need a gooseneck kettle?

Yes, if you want consistent pour-over. A regular kettle dumps water too fast, causing channeling (uneven extraction). The gooseneck's narrow spout gives you control over flow rate, which is the #1 variable after grind size.

What temperature should I use for pour-over?

For light roasts, use 205°F. For medium roasts, 200°F. For dark roasts, 195°F. Boiling water (212°F) is too hot—it over-extracts and tastes bitter.

Is temperature control worth the extra cost?

If you drink coffee daily, yes. A $60 kettle with no temp control will make inconsistent cups. A $130 kettle with temp control will make the same great cup every day. Over a year, that's pennies per cup.

Can I use these for tea?

Yes. Most have preset temps for green (175°F) and black (200°F) tea. The Cosori and Oxo have dedicated tea presets.
