This review is for anyone who feels like a balloon after eating. You finish a meal, your belt gets tight, and you spend the next three hours burping or dealing with embarrassing gas. You've tried probiotics, you've tried eating slower, and nothing has fixed it.

Digestive enzymes break down the food you eat into smaller pieces so your gut doesn't have to ferment it (which is what causes gas). Buyer's Guide: What to Actually Look For

Not all enzyme supplements are the same. Most cheap ones just have one or two enzymes and call it a day. Here's what matters when you're buying for bloating specifically.

1. Look for Alpha-Galactosidase (AG) — This is the Big One

This enzyme breaks down complex carbs in beans, broccoli, cabbage, and grains. Those carbs (raffinose and stachyose) are the #1 cause of gas. If a product doesn't have AG, it's not going to help with veggie bloat. Look for it in the ingredient list.

2. Lipase for Fatty Meals

If you bloat after pizza, burgers, or fried food, you need lipase. This enzyme breaks down fats. Many cheap formulas skip it because it's expensive to source. A good product has at least 1,000 FIP units of lipase per serving.

3. Protease Blend for Protein

Meat-heavy meals require protease. But here's the thing — you want a blend of different proteases (DPPIV, DPP4, etc.) because single proteases only work in specific pH ranges. The good products have 4-5 different types.

4. Lactase for Dairy

If milk or ice cream makes you gassy, you need lactase. Most enzyme blends include it, but check the dose. You need at least 3,000 ALU units to break down a normal serving of dairy.

5. Delivery Format Matters

Capsules are fine, but look for enteric-coated capsules if you have a sensitive stomach. They dissolve in the small intestine instead of the stomach, which prevents the enzymes from being destroyed by stomach acid. Chewable tablets work faster if you forget to take a pill before eating.

6. The "When" Is as Important as the "What"

You have to take these right before or during your first bite. If you take them after you finish eating, you've missed the window. The enzymes need to mix with the food as it enters your stomach.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take digestive enzymes every day?

Yes. Your body produces its own enzymes, but taking supplemental ones doesn't stop your natural production. It's like taking a multivitamin — it just tops you up. Most people use them daily with heavier meals.

Why didn't probiotics fix my bloating?

Probiotics change your gut bacteria over weeks. Enzymes work immediately — they break down the food before bacteria can ferment it. If you want immediate relief, enzymes are the answer. If you want long-term gut health, use both.

Are these safe if I have IBS or SIBO?

Generally yes. In fact, many IBS sufferers find relief with AG and protease enzymes. But if you have SIBO, be careful — some enzyme blends contain ox bile or HCl which can irritate an inflamed gut. Start with half a dose and see how you feel.

How fast do they work?

You'll feel the difference within 20-40 minutes of your meal. If you don't notice anything within an hour, either the dose is too low or the enzymes aren't matching your specific food triggers.
