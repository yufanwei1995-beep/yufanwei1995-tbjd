What to Look For in a Cushioned Yoga Mat

Not all thick mats are created equal. Here’s what actually matters when you’re shopping for hard floor protection.

Thickness (mm) — The Obvious One

Standard mats are 3-4mm. For hard floors, you want 5mm minimum. Go 6-8mm if you have knee problems. But here’s the catch: thicker doesn't always mean better. Mats over 8mm feel like quicksand during standing poses. Your balance suffers because your feet sink in unevenly.

Material — The Hidden Factor

Three main types:

  • PVC (polyvinyl chloride): Best cushioning-to-price ratio. Durable and grippy, but not eco-friendly. Can smell for weeks.
  • Natural rubber: Excellent grip, decent cushion, biodegradable. Heavy and smells like rubber. Not for latex allergies.
  • TPE (thermoplastic elastomer): Lightweight, eco-friendly-ish, but less durable. Often has that "foam" feel that wears down.

Density vs. Sponginess

This is the mistake most people make. A dense 5mm mat feels firmer and more stable than a spongy 8mm mat. For knee protection, you want dense cushioning, not pillow-soft foam. Press your thumb into the mat — if it leaves a deep dent, it's too soft for standing balance.

Grip When Sweaty

Thick mats often have worse grip because the material is softer. Look for mats with a textured top layer or moisture-activated grip. This is non-negotiable if you do hot yoga or sweat during flow.

Weight and Portability

Thick mats are heavy. A 6mm mat weighs 2.5-3.5 pounds. If you carry it to a studio, get a strap or opt for a foldable version. I tested a 8mm mat that weighed over 5 pounds — you will not want to walk 10 blocks with that.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a thicker yoga mat always better for knees?

No. Thickness matters, but density matters more. A dense 5mm mat like the Jade Harmony protects knees better than a spongy 8mm mat that compresses to nothing under pressure. If you're kneeling, you want the mat to hold its shape, not squish flat.

Can I use a regular mat with a blanket under my knees?

Yes, but it's a pain. You'll be adjusting the blanket constantly. A dedicated thick mat is simpler. If you're on a budget, grab a Gaiam Premium and fold a towel under your knees for floor work.

Why do thick mats feel slippery?

Most thick mats use foam or rubber that becomes slick when wet. Look for mats with a textured top layer (like Liforme) or a moisture-activated grip. Also, wash your hands and feet before practice — natural oils on skin cause slipping.

How do I clean a thick yoga mat?

Mix 1 cup water, 1 cup white vinegar, and 10 drops of tea tree oil in a spray bottle. Spray and wipe with a microfiber cloth. Never put a thick mat in the washing machine — it'll destroy the foam.

What's the best mat for hot yoga on hard floors?

The Liforme wins. Its PU top layer actually gets grippier when wet. The Manduka PRO is also good, but you'll need to break it in first. Avoid natural rubber mats like Jade in hot yoga — they turn into slip-and-slides.
