What This Guide Covers

Maca is everywhere now. But most of what you read is marketing fluff. I spent 8 weeks testing 5 different maca supplements with a panel of 12 women aged 28-52. We tracked libido, mood, energy, and PMS symptoms. Here's what actually happened.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For in Maca Supplements

Before you spend money, understand these five things.

1. Gelatinized vs Raw vs Extract

Gelatinized maca is the sweet spot. The processing removes starches that cause stomach upset while keeping the active compounds. It's what most quality brands use.

Raw maca powder is harder to digest. Many women report bloating and gas. It's cheaper but you'll likely quit taking it.

Extracts and capsules are convenient but often under-dosed. Many contain only 100-300mg per serving, which is too low for noticeable effects.

2. Maca Color Matters

Yellow maca is the most common and cheapest. It's fine for general energy.

Black maca is specifically linked to libido and stamina in studies. It's harder to find and costs more.

Red maca shows promise for prostate health in men, but for female hormone balance, black and yellow are your best bets.

Some brands sell a "tri-color" blend. It's fine, but if libido is your main goal, go black.

3. Dose: More Isn't Always Better

Clinical studies use 1,500mg to 3,000mg daily. But many women feel effects at 500-1,000mg.

Start low. Take 500mg for a week, then increase. Side effects like insomnia or jitters are rare but happen at high doses.

4. Third-Party Testing

Maca is a root. It absorbs heavy metals from soil. Look for brands that test for lead, arsenic, and cadmium. The label should say "third-party tested" or show a certificate of analysis.

5. What's NOT in the Bottle

Many "female libido" blends stuff in random herbs like horny goat weed or damiana. These can interact with medications. If you want pure maca, buy pure maca. If you want a blend, know what's in it.

FAQ

How long does maca take to work for libido?

Most women in our test felt effects between 3-6 weeks. Some noticed changes in 10 days. Maca isn't a quick fix — it works by supporting your endocrine system over time.

Can maca help with perimenopause symptoms?

Yes. Two testers in their late 40s reported fewer hot flashes and better sleep. Research on maca for menopause symptoms is promising, though more studies are needed.

Is maca safe with birth control or HRT?

Maca doesn't contain hormones, but it may influence how your body produces them. Talk to your doctor if you're on hormonal birth control or hormone replacement therapy. There's no known dangerous interaction, but individual responses vary.

What's the best time of day to take maca?

Morning is best for most women. Maca can be energizing, and taking it at night may keep some people awake. If you take it twice daily, split the dose — morning and early afternoon.

Does maca cause weight gain?

No. Maca is about 60% carbohydrates but the dose is small. None of our testers gained weight. In fact, some reported fewer sugar cravings, likely due to better energy stability.

Can I take maca with other supplements?

Yes. Maca pairs well with magnesium, vitamin D, and omega-3s. Avoid combining it with other stimulants like caffeine if you're sensitive to jitters.
