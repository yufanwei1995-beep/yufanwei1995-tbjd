What to Look For in a Resistance Band Set

Before we get into the reviews, you need to understand the difference between the two main types of bands: loop bands and tube bands with handles.

Loop bands (sometimes called "booty bands") are continuous circles of latex. Great for glute work, lateral walks, and some upper body moves. They don't come with handles, so you're gripping the band itself.

Tube bands have a cord with a handle on each end. They feel more like a cable machine. You can clip them to a door anchor, step on the middle, or attach them to a bar. Better for pressing, rows, and curls.

Here are the four things that actually matter:

  • Material quality: Look for 100% natural latex, not TPE or rubber blends. Latex lasts longer and provides better resistance. TPE is cheaper but degrades faster.
  • Resistance range: A good set should have at least 5 levels of resistance, from about 10 lbs up to 50 lbs per band. Combine multiple bands for heavier lifts like squats or deadlifts.
  • Accessories: Door anchors, ankle straps, and handles are essential. If a set skips these, you're paying more later to buy them separately.
  • Carrying case: For travel, the case needs to be compact but still hold everything without forcing you to cram the bands in. A drawstring bag is fine. A bulky zippered case is not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you build muscle with resistance bands?

Yes, if you use enough resistance. The key is progressive overload — you need to increase the resistance over time. A set like the WODFitters with up to 200 lbs combined resistance is plenty for most people. The research backs this up: a 2019 study in the Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research found that resistance bands produce similar muscle growth to traditional weights when the exercises are performed to failure.

How much weight do resistance bands add?

It depends on the band and how far you stretch it. A 50 lb band doesn't feel like a 50 lb dumbbell at the start of a movement. It feels like 50 lbs only when fully stretched. The resistance curve is exponential, meaning it gets harder as you extend the band. This is actually a benefit for exercises like chest presses, where you're weakest at full extension.

Can I use resistance bands for pull-ups?

Yes, but you need a specific type. You'll want a long loop band that you can attach to a pull-up bar and put your foot or knee in. The Serious Steel and WODFitters sets both work well for this. The band provides assistance, reducing your body weight by 20-50 lbs depending on the band thickness.
