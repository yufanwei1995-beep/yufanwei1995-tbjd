What to Look For in a Chew Toy for a Power Chewer

Before you spend money, stop looking at the cute colors and start looking at the engineering. Here are the five things that matter most, based on my testing.

1. Material Hardness (Durometer)

This is the most ignored spec. Rubber is measured on a Shore A scale. Cheap toys are often 40-60 Shore A, which feels soft and is easy to shred. Durable toys should be 80 Shore A or higher. The Goughnuts we tested are around 90-95 Shore A. That's the difference between a rubber band and a truck tire. If the manufacturer doesn't list the durometer, be suspicious.

2. Shape and Geometry

A toy with thin protrusions (like legs or spikes) creates stress points. That's where the tear starts. Look for toys with a solid, uniform body. A simple ring or a solid donut shape distributes bite force evenly. Toys with handles or knobs are for fetch, not for chewing. If you give a power chewer a toy with a weak handle, the handle is the first thing to go.

3. The "Give" Factor

Here's the counterintuitive part. The hardest toys aren't always the best. A 100% solid nylon or hard plastic toy can crack your dog's teeth. I've seen it happen. The best toys have a slight amount of "give" to absorb the shock of the bite. This is why high-quality natural rubber (like Goughnuts) beats hard plastic. It's tough enough to resist punctures but soft enough to protect the teeth.

4. Warranty

This is the clearest signal of confidence. If a company offers a lifetime replacement warranty on a chew toy, they know their product is good. Goughnuts and West Paw both do this. If a brand has no warranty, they are betting that you will be buying a replacement from them in a month anyway.

5. Size

Bigger is always better. A toy that is too small can be choked on or can be chewed down to a swallowable size. The general rule is that the toy should be larger than your dog's mouth. Do not buy a "large" toy if you own a 100 lb German Shepherd. Buy the "extra large" or "giant" option.

Pro-Tip: Never leave your dog unattended with a toy that has been chewed down to half its original size. Even the best toys need to be retired eventually. A Goughnut that is down to the red inner layer has done its job. Throw it away.

The 5 Best Durable Dog Toys for Aggressive Chewers

I tested these with a team of three power chewers: a 90 lb German Shepherd, a 70 lb Pit Bull mix, and a 50 lb Beagle that thinks he's a 150 lb wolf. Here are the results.

1. Goughnuts Indestructible Black Donut

This is the gold standard. It is a solid ring of natural rubber that is nearly impossible to puncture. The trick is the safety indicator: it has a red inner layer. If your dog chews deep enough to see the red, Goughnuts will replace it for free under their lifetime warranty. You just pay shipping.

In my tests, the German Shepherd (who has destroyed 100+ toys) spent three months working on this thing. He made surface marks, but he never got close to the red layer. The shape is brilliant. The ring geometry means there are no edges to grab onto and rip. It also bounces unpredictably, which keeps the dog engaged.

The downside is the price and the weight. It's heavy. This is not a fetch toy. It is a dedicated chew toy, and it costs around $35. But if you calculate the cost-per-day of ownership, it's the cheapest toy you will ever buy.

2. West Paw Zogoflex Jive

West Paw makes a material called Zogoflex, which is a proprietary thermoplastic elastomer. It's flexible, floatable, and tough. The Jive is a wavy disc shape that is great for games of tug and fetch, but it also stands up to serious chewing. It has a bit more "give" than the Goughnuts, making it slightly easier on teeth, but it is still extremely resistant to punctures.

This one is also backed by a lifetime warranty through the "Love It Guarantee" program. If your dog destroys it, they send you a new one. In my tests, the Pit Bull managed to chew a tiny nick in the edge after a month, but it never compromised the structure. It is also dishwasher safe, which is a nice bonus for hygiene.

It is not as indestructible as the Goughnuts. If you have a truly relentless chewer, the Goughnuts is tougher. But the Jive is more versatile for playtime.

3. KONG Extreme

You knew this one was coming. The KONG Extreme is the black version of the classic red KONG. It is made from a tougher, more durable rubber compound than the standard red one. It is great for stuffing with treats and peanut butter, which is a huge distraction tool for chewers.

Here is the honest truth: It is not indestructible. The Extreme is more durable than the red, but a dedicated power chewer can still shred the top off of it. The shape, with the narrow waist and the hollow center, creates a weak point. I have seen Pit Bulls rip the base off in a week. However, it is still one of the best "interactive" toys because of the stuffing capability.

Use it as a mental enrichment tool, not as a long-term chew toy. If your dog just wants to destroy things, get the Goughnuts. If your dog needs a job to do, the KONG Extreme wins.

4. Benebone Real Flavor Wishbone

If your dog is a "shredder" rather than a "crusher," this is your toy. Benebone is made from food-grade nylon and is flavored with bacon or chicken. It is incredibly hard. The Wishbone shape allows your dog to get a good grip and really work their back teeth.

The good news: Dogs love the flavor. It keeps them engaged for hours. The bad news: It is very hard. There is zero "give." I would not recommend this for aggressive chewers who bite down hard enough to hurt themselves. It is better for dogs that like to gnaw. Also, the nylon can get sharp edges as it wears down. You need to inspect it regularly and sand down any rough spots.

This is a good budget option at around $15, but it is not a "lifetime" toy. It will wear down over a few months, and that is by design. It is meant to be chewed, not to survive forever.

5. Ruffwear Gnawt-A-Rock

This is a newer entrant that surprised me. It is a medium-density foam that is coated in a tough rubber shell. It looks like a rock, and it feels like one too. It is incredibly lightweight, which makes it great for fetch and for dogs that like to toss toys around.

It is not as tough as the Goughnuts. The outer shell can be punctured by a dedicated chewer, and once the foam core is exposed, it is game over. However, for dogs that are "medium" aggressive chewers, this is a fantastic option. It is also completely non-abrasive, so it is safe for teeth and gums.

The main selling point is the bounce. It bounces wildly and randomly, which is great for burning energy. For a dog that gets bored easily, this is a winner. For a dog that wants to sit and destroy one thing for an hour, look elsewhere.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the strongest dog toy material?

Natural rubber that is vulcanized and rated at 90+ Shore A durometer is the strongest. Nylon is also very strong, but it lacks the shock absorption of rubber and can be dangerous for teeth. Tires and hard plastics are too dangerous and should be avoided.

Why does my dog destroy every toy?

Boredom and anxiety are the top reasons. A dog that is left alone for hours will take out their frustration on a toy. The second reason is that they simply enjoy the sensory feedback of tearing things apart. If it is anxiety, a chew toy is a band-aid. You need to address the root cause with more exercise and mental stimulation.

Are "indestructible" toys actually indestructible?

No. That word is a marketing term. There is no toy that a 150 lb Mastiff with a steel jaw cannot eventually break. The goal is to find a toy that lasts a long time, not forever. Look for toys with strong warranties. If a company is willing to replace it, they are confident in their product.

Can these toys hurt my dog's teeth?

Yes, if you buy the wrong one. Hard nylon and plastic can cause slab fractures (cracked teeth). This is a painful and expensive vet bill. That is why I prioritize rubber toys with a slight "give" over hard plastic. If you can't dent the toy with your fingernail, it might be too hard for your dog.

The Bottom Line: Which One Should You Buy?

If you have a true power chewer that eats everything, buy the Goughnuts Indestructible Black Donut. It is the only toy on this list that survived my testing group without significant damage, and the lifetime warranty makes it the best value in the long run. It is expensive to buy, but it is the last toy you will need to buy for a while. If your dog needs more interactive play, get the West Paw Jive as a secondary option. Skip the KONG Extreme if your dog is a shredder, and only buy the Benebone if you know your dog is a gentle gnawer. Match the toy to your dog's style, and you will save money and save your furniture.
