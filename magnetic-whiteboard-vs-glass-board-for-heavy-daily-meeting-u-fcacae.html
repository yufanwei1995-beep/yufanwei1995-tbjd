What to Look For: The 5 Things That Matter

Before you spend money, understand the core differences. Steel (magnetic) boards and glass boards look similar from across the room. They perform completely differently under daily abuse.

1. Marker Ghosting (The #1 Killer)

Ghosting is the faint leftover ink that won't wipe off. Glass boards win here — the non-porous surface won't absorb ink. But cheap glass boards can develop "ghosting" if you use the wrong markers. Steel boards need a quality porcelain or enamel coating. Dry-erase paint over MDF? Skip it. That's a desk toy, not a meeting room tool.

2. Magnet Strength

If you pin up papers, charts, or sticky notes, magnetic hold matters. Steel boards hold strong magnets. Glass boards are magnetic too — but only with specific glass-magnet combinations. Most glass boards use a steel backing plate, so magnets work. But thick glass adds distance between the magnet and the steel, so you need stronger magnets. Test with your actual magnets before buying.

3. Surface Durability & Scratch Resistance

Heavy daily use means pens, erasers, and occasional aggressive pointing. Glass is scratch-resistant but can chip or crack if hit hard. Steel boards dent and scratch but keep working. For a room with heavy traffic, steel is more forgiving. For a clean, premium look, glass wins — just don't throw a chair at it.

4. Maintenance & Cleaning

Daily use means daily cleaning. Glass wipes clean in one pass. Steel boards need more effort — especially if you let ink sit overnight. Both need periodic deep cleaning with isopropyl alcohol or specialized cleaner. Avoid abrasive pads on glass; they'll leave micro-scratches that trap ink.

5. Installation & Weight

Glass boards are heavier. A 4x6 glass board weighs 50-60 lbs. Steel boards weigh 30-40 lbs. Make sure your wall can support it. Both need proper anchors — don't trust a drywall screw. Consider a mobile stand if the room gets rearranged.

FAQ: Answers to What You're Actually Wondering

Does glass board ghosting happen?

Not if you use dry-erase markers and clean daily. Glass is non-porous, so ink sits on the surface. Steel boards with cheap paint will ghost after weeks. Stick with porcelain or enamel-coated steel.

Can I use regular magnets on glass boards?

Yes, but the hold depends on the gap between magnet and steel backing. Thin glass (4mm) holds standard fridge magnets. Thicker glass (6mm) needs neodymium magnets. Test before you buy a pack of 100.

What markers should I use for heavy daily use?

Expo low-odor fine-point markers are the standard. Avoid permanent markers — they'll ruin any surface. For glass, use liquid chalk markers if you want color options, but wipe them off within 24 hours.

How often do I need to deep clean?

Daily wipe with a whiteboard eraser or dry cloth. Weekly deep clean with isopropyl alcohol (70% or higher) on a microfiber cloth. Don't use abrasive cleaners — they'll eat the coating on steel and scratch glass.

Which surface is better for video calls?

Glass reflects light, causing glare on camera. Steel boards with a matte finish (like Quartet) are better for video — less reflection, more readable text. If you do heavy video, pick steel or a smart board.
