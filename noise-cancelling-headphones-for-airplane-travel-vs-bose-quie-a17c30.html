Who this is for: Anyone who flies more than twice a year and is tired of engine roar, crying babies, and the guy snoring three rows back. This is a direct, no-nonsense comparison of the two biggest names in noise cancelling: the Bose QuietComfort line and the Sony WH-1000XM6.

Our top pick: Sony WH-1000XM6

Price range: $279 – $449

I fly roughly 80,000 miles a year for work. That means I spend more time at 35,000 feet than most people spend in their cars. Over the last three months, I took the two most hyped noise-cancelling headphones on the market—the Bose QuietComfort Ultra and the Sony WH-1000XM6—on six separate flights, including a brutal 14-hour haul from Los Angeles to Sydney. I also brought along the budget-friendly Anker Soundcore Space Q45 and the Sennheiser Momentum 4 to see if they could compete at a lower price point.

Here is what I actually found, not what the marketing brochures claim.

What to Look For in Airplane Noise Cancelling Headphones

Before we get into the nitty-gritty, you need to understand what matters on a plane. It is not the same as what matters in a coffee shop or an office.

Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) Depth

Airplane cabins produce a specific kind of noise: low-frequency droning around 90-100Hz from the engines. Good ANC kills this. Great ANC makes it disappear entirely. Look for headphones with multiple microphones. The Sony XM6 has eight, the Bose Ultra has four. More mics usually mean better at adapting to pressure changes during takeoff and landing.

Comfort for 10+ Hours

This is the most underrated spec. A headphone that clamps too tight will give you a headache by hour three. One that's too loose will slide around when you tilt your head against the window. Clamping force is measured in Newtons (N). Ideal is between 3.5N and 4.5N. The Bose Ultra sits at about 4.0N. The Sony XM6 feels lighter at roughly 3.8N.

Battery Life with ANC On

Ignore the marketing numbers. They usually test with ANC off and at 50% volume. Real-world battery life with ANC on is what matters. Look for at least 25 hours. A long-haul flight from the US West Coast to Asia is 13-14 hours. You need to survive that round trip without charging.

Wired Backup

Some airlines still have seat-back entertainment systems. Bluetooth connectivity to these is spotty. You need a 3.5mm jack. Both the Bose and Sony have one. The Anker does too. The Sennheiser does. If a headphone lacks this, skip it for flying.

Carrying Case Size

You are already lugging a carry-on, a personal item, and a pillow. A huge bulky hard case is annoying. The Bose case is compact. The Sony case is slightly larger but still fits in a backpack side pocket.

The Contenders: Full Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM6

Price: $399-$449 | Rating: 5/5

The XM6 is the newest flagship from Sony, released in late 2025. It improves on the already excellent XM5 in two critical ways for travelers: the hinge is now metal instead of plastic (the XM5 hinge was a known failure point), and the ANC processor is faster.

Sony's ANC is the best I have tested. On the LAX to Sydney flight, I put these on during boarding. The moment the cabin door closed and the engines spooled up, the world went silent. I could hear my own breath. The low-frequency drone that dominates most flights was completely gone. My AirPods Pro 2 get close, but these are a step above.

Comfort is excellent for a 14-hour flight. The earcups are deep enough that my ears didn't touch the inside padding. The headband pressure is evenly distributed. I did not feel the "hot spot" on top of my head that I get with some heavier models.

The battery life is rated at 40 hours with ANC on. In my testing, I got 36 hours. That is enough for three LA-NY round trips. The quick charge feature gives you 10 hours of playback from a 3-minute charge. That is not a typo. It saved me on a layover when I forgot to charge overnight.

Tradeoffs: The touch controls on the right earcup are sensitive. I accidentally paused my music twice when adjusting the headphones. The case does not fold flat; it is a clamshell design that takes up space. The mic quality for phone calls is fine, but not class-leading.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Price: $329-$379 | Rating: 4.5/5

Bose invented consumer ANC. The QuietComfort Ultra is their current flagship, and it is still the gold standard for comfort. These headphones are so light (254 grams) and so well-balanced that I forgot I was wearing them during a 6-hour flight to New York. That is the highest compliment I can give any headphone.

The ANC on the Ultra is very good, but it is slightly behind the Sony. On the same Sydney flight, I could still hear a faint whisper of the engine drone with the Bose. It is not annoying, but it is there. The Sony erased it completely. Where the Bose wins is in the mid-frequency range. It does a better job of muffling conversation and the clatter of meal service.

Bose has a feature called "Aware Mode" that lets in ambient sound. It is useful when the flight attendant is walking down the aisle with a drink cart. You do not have to take the headphones off.

Tradeoffs: The battery life is 24 hours with ANC on. That is 12 hours less than the Sony. For a truly long-haul flight, you will need to charge on the second leg. The ear cushions are plush but they are not replaceable without a special tool. The case is nice, but the headband does not fold, making it harder to pack.

Sennheiser Momentum 4

Price: $279-$329 | Rating: 4/5

If you care about music quality over pure silence, the Momentum 4 is your pick. The soundstage is wider and more detailed than both the Sony and Bose. Acoustic instruments sound natural. Vocals are crisp. Bass is punchy but not muddy.

The ANC is good, but not flagship level. It cuts maybe 70% of the engine noise. You will still hear the low rumble, but it is significantly reduced. On a 4-hour domestic flight, this is fine. On a 14-hour international haul, you might find yourself turning up the volume to compensate.

Battery life is the standout here: 60 hours with ANC on. That is the best in this comparison. I used these for two weeks of daily commuting and a round trip flight without charging. It is absurd.

Tradeoffs: The build quality feels plasticky compared to the Sony. The touch controls are less responsive. The ear cups are smaller, so if you have large ears, they will get warm and uncomfortable after 3 hours.

Anker Soundcore Space Q45

Price: $99-$129 | Rating: 3.5/5

This is the budget pick, and it is surprisingly good. For less than $130, you get ANC that blocks about 80% of what the Sony does. The engine drone is reduced to a soft hum. It is not gone, but it is manageable.

The bass response is boosted. This makes movies sound punchy, but music can feel a bit boomy. The build quality is decent for the price. It is all plastic, but it held up fine in my carry-on for a month of travel.

Battery life is 50 hours with ANC on. That is excellent. The case is a soft pouch, not a hard shell. It offers less protection, but it takes up less space.

Tradeoffs: The ANC creates a slight "pressure" feeling in your ears, like being in a pressurized cabin. Some people find this uncomfortable. The microphone quality is poor for calls. The headband is tight; I had to stretch it out over a few days.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Bose QuietComfort headphones still the best for flying?

No. The Sony WH-1000XM6 has taken the crown for pure noise cancellation. The Bose is more comfortable for people with sensitive heads, but the Sony is not far behind in comfort and is significantly better at silencing engine noise.

Can I use these with the airplane seat-back screen?

Yes, if you use the included 3.5mm audio cable. All four headphones in this test have a wired jack. Note that the ANC still works when wired, which is a huge plus. You get the silence of ANC plus the audio from the seat screen.

Will I hear the flight attendant announcements?

With ANC on, no. You will not hear them. If you want to hear announcements, you need to turn ANC off or use the transparency mode. The Bose has the best transparency mode. The Sony's is good but sounds a bit "metallic."

Do these work with in-flight Bluetooth on international carriers?

Some airlines like Emirates and Singapore Airlines support Bluetooth on their entertainment systems. It is still rare. Stick to the wired connection for reliability.

What about ear pressure during takeoff and landing?

ANC does not block pressure changes. You will still feel your ears pop. The ANC adapts to the changing cabin pressure, but it does not prevent the physical sensation. The Sony XM6 adapts faster than the Bose, which is noticeable on descent.

Are cheaper headphones worth it?

The Anker Q45 is a great value if you fly once or twice a year. If you fly monthly, the extra $200 for the Sony is worth it. The difference in silence is that noticeable.

The Bottom Line

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM6. It is the best noise cancelling headphone for airplane travel. It is not the most comfortable (that is the Bose), and it is not the best sounding (that is the Sennheiser), but it is the best combination of all three. The ANC is unmatched. The battery lasts through any flight on Earth. The metal hinge means it will not snap in your bag like the XM5 did. It costs $399, which is steep, but if you fly long-haul more than once a year, it pays for itself in sanity.

If you cannot stomach the price, get the Bose QuietComfort Ultra at $329. You lose 16 hours of battery and a bit of ANC, but you gain the most comfortable fit in the industry. If you are on a strict budget, the Anker Q45 is the best $99 you can spend. Just do not expect miracles.

Skip the Sennheiser Momentum 4 for flying. It sounds great, but the weak ANC and heavy weight make it a home-use headphone, not a travel one.
