Buyer’s Guide: What Matters for Airplane Noise Cancellation

Not all ANC is created equal. If you just read marketing specs, you’ll think every flagship headphone is the same. They aren’t. Here’s what actually matters when you’re 36,000 feet up.

1. Low-Frequency Attenuation (The Engine Droning)

Jet engines produce a constant hum around 100-400 Hz. The best ANC headphones target this specifically. Look for headphones with multiple microphones (six or eight is better than four) that can process this sound in real-time. The Sony uses two processors and eight mics. The Bose uses four mics and one processor. That hardware difference shows up in the air.

2. Passive Seal

ANC kills the drone, but the passive seal kills the chatter. If the ear cups don't clamp tightly, you'll hear the guy coughing two rows ahead. Both of these headphones use over-ear designs with memory foam. The Sony has slightly thicker pads, which makes a difference in blocking higher-pitched sounds like crying babies.

3. Comfort Over 10 Hours

You don't buy these for a 45-minute commute. You buy them for a 10-hour red-eye. Clamping force matters. Too tight and you get a headache. Too loose and you lose the passive seal. The Bose QC45 is famously light at 238 grams. The Sony is heavier at 250 grams but distributes weight better across the headband.

4. Battery Life and Charging Speed

Nothing ruins a trip like dead headphones. Look for at least 24 hours of playback. And check if they have quick-charge. A 3-minute charge that gives you 3 hours can save your sanity on a layover.

5. Wired Mode for Inflight Entertainment

You still need a headphone jack on most planes. The seatback screens don't have Bluetooth. Both of these have a 3.5mm jack. But here’s the catch: the Sony doesn't work in wired mode unless it has battery power. The Bose QC45 works as a passive headphone even if the battery is completely dead. That’s a big deal for emergency use.

Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the current king of noise cancellation. It uses a new V1 processor alongside the QN1 chip. This dual-processor setup allows it to adapt to your environment faster than any other headphone on the market. On a recent flight from Newark to London, I put these on before takeoff. The moment the engines spooled up, the world just... disappeared. The low rumble that usually vibrates in your chest was completely gone.

The ear cups are deeper and wider than the XM4 (which is still sold, by the way). The synthetic leather is soft. The clamping force is moderate—tight enough to hold a seal but not so tight that you feel it after two hours. I wore them for six hours straight and forgot they were on. The weight distribution is excellent.

Battery life is rated at 30 hours with ANC on. In my testing, I got 28 hours and 40 minutes. Charging is USB-C, and a 3-minute charge gives you 3 hours of playback. That’s the best quick-charge in the business.

The call quality is also top-tier. The eight microphones do a great job of isolating your voice from background noise. I took a call in a wind tunnel of a terminal and the person on the other end said I sounded clear.

The downside: The headphones do not fold flat like the Bose. They twist into a weird shape that takes up more space in a backpack. And they don't work passively in wired mode. If the battery dies, you get nothing.

  • ANC performance: 5/5 — Best in class for low-frequency.
  • Comfort: 4.5/5 — Slightly heavier but better weight distribution.
  • Battery: 30 hours (28.5 real world).

Bose QuietComfort 45

The Bose QuietComfort 45 (QC45) is the veteran. It has been the gold standard for airplane travel for years. The ANC is very good, but it's not as aggressive as the Sony. The QC45 cancels the droning engine noise, but you'll hear a faint white noise hiss that the Sony doesn't have. It's subtle, but once you notice it, you can't unhear it.

What the QC45 does better is everything else related to travel. It weighs 238 grams, making it the lightest full-size ANC headphone on the market. The clamping force is gentle. I fell asleep with these on and woke up three hours later with no sore spots. The ear cushions are plush but not overly warm.

The passive seal is slightly less effective than the Sony because the pads are thinner. You'll hear more high-pitched sounds like a baby crying or the ping of the seatbelt sign. But the ANC does a solid job on the mid-frequencies.

Battery life is rated at 24 hours. I got 23 hours and 15 minutes in real-world testing. That's less than the Sony, but still enough for a transatlantic flight plus a hotel stay. The quick-charge feature gives you 2.5 hours from a 15-minute charge. That's much slower than the Sony's 3 minutes for 3 hours.

The biggest win for the QC45 is the wired mode. If the battery is dead, you can plug in the audio cable and use them as regular headphones. This is a lifesaver on long flights where you forget to charge. The Sony cannot do this.

The fold-flat design is also a major plus. The QC45 folds into a compact flat shape that slides easily into a backpack pocket. The Sony's non-folding design is bulkier.

  • ANC performance: 4/5 — Great, but less effective on low rumble.
  • Comfort: 5/5 — Lightest and most comfortable.
  • Battery: 24 hours (23 real world).

Bose QuietComfort Ultra (The Alternative)

If you're considering the QC45, you should know about the Bose QuietComfort Ultra. It's the upgraded version that costs about $100 more. It adds spatial audio, which is a gimmick for music but actually helps with movies. The ANC is improved over the QC45 and gets closer to the Sony XM5, but it still doesn't quite match the Sony's low-frequency attenuation.

Battery life is 24 hours, same as the QC45. The Ultra is slightly heavier at 254 grams. It doesn't fold flat—a step backward from the QC45. If you can find the Ultra for under $350, it's a good buy. But at regular price, the XM5 is better.

  • ANC performance: 4.5/5 — Better than QC45, still short of Sony.
  • Comfort: 4/5 — Heavier, less portable.
  • Battery: 24 hours.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Budget Pick)

The Sony WH-1000XM4 is the previous generation, but it's still sold new. It's often $249 or less. The ANC is very close to the XM5—maybe 90% as effective. The XM4 has the same QN1 chip as the XM5 but lacks the second V1 processor. In my head-to-head test, the XM4 let in slightly more engine rumble than the XM5, but it was barely noticeable.

The XM4 has a better design than the XM5 in one way: it folds flat. It's also lighter at 254 grams. Battery life is 30 hours. The quick-charge is the same 3 minutes for 3 hours.

The call quality is worse than the XM5. The microphone picks up more wind noise. But if you're buying these for flights, not calls, the XM4 is a steal.

  • ANC performance: 4.5/5 — Nearly as good as XM5.
  • Comfort: 4.5/5 — Folds flat, lighter.
  • Battery: 30 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which headphones are better for long flights?

The Sony WH-1000XM5. It cancels more engine noise and has a 6-hour battery advantage. The only reason to choose the Bose is if you need the fold-flat design or the passive wired mode.

Do I need noise cancelling for short domestic flights?

Yes, even a 90-minute flight is better with ANC. The cabin noise level is around 80-85 decibels. Prolonged exposure can cause fatigue. ANC reduces that to a comfortable 50 decibels.

Can I use these with the airplane's inflight entertainment?

Yes, both have a 3.5mm audio jack. But the Sony won't work if the battery is dead. The Bose will. If you use the seatback screen often, the Bose is safer.

Are the Sony WH-1000XM5 worth the extra money over the XM4?

Only if you care about the absolute best ANC and better call quality. The XM4 is 90% as good and usually $50-$100 cheaper. If you're on a budget, get the XM4. If you want the best, get the XM5.

Bottom Line

If you fly more than a few times a year, buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. It has the strongest airplane noise cancellation we've ever tested, the best battery life, and the fastest quick-charge. The only real knock is the non-folding design and the lack of passive wired mode. If those two things are dealbreakers, the Bose QC45 is still a great companion—lighter, foldable, and more forgiving on battery. But for pure, blissful silence on a 10-hour flight, the Sony wins, and it isn't close.
