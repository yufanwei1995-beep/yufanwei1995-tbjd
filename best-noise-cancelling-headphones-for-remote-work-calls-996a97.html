What to Look for in Headphones for Calls (Not Just Music)

Most headphone reviews focus on music. That's useless for calls. Here's what matters when your paycheck depends on sounding clear.

1. Microphone Quality (The Obvious One)

Not all mics are equal. Look for multi-mic arrays with AI-based noise suppression. The best headsets filter out keyboard clatter, dog barks, and street noise without cutting your voice. Some use bone conduction sensors (like the Sony WH-1000XM6) that pick up your voice through your jawbone when you speak.

2. Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) for You

You need ANC that kills background noise for you so you can hear the other person. But too much ANC can make you feel disconnected from your environment. Look for adjustable ANC or a transparency mode.

3. Sidetone (Hear Yourself Talk)

This is a dealbreaker. Sidetone pipes your own voice back into the headphones at a low volume. Without it, you'll shout because you can't hear yourself. Apple's AirPods Pro do this well. Many cheap headphones don't.

4. Battery Life + Quick Charging

Nothing worse than a dead headset mid-call. Look for at least 20 hours with ANC on. Quick charging (10 minutes = 3+ hours) is a lifesaver.

5. Wired vs. Wireless

Wireless is convenient, but Bluetooth latency can cause a delay on calls. If you're on Zoom or Teams often, a headset with a USB-C dongle (like the Poly Voyager Focus 2) gives lower latency and better stability.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use gaming headsets for work calls?

Sure, but most gaming headsets have heavy bass and poor mic noise filtering. They're built for game chat, not professional calls. Stick with the ones above.

Do I need a dongle or is Bluetooth fine?

Bluetooth is fine for most calls. But if you're on Zoom/Teams all day, a dongle gives lower latency and no dropouts. The Poly Voyager Focus 2 and Jabra Evolve2 85 include dongles.

What is sidetone and why do I need it?

Sidetone lets you hear your own voice in the headphones. Without it, you'll speak too loudly because you can't hear yourself. Most modern headsets have it, but check before buying.

How much should I spend?

For occasional calls, $80-150 is fine. For daily calls in noisy places, spend $250-400. The difference is night and day for the person on the other end.
