What to Look For in a Hard-Water Detergent

Not all detergents are created equal when it comes to mineral-heavy water. Here’s what actually matters:

Enzymes and Builders

Look for detergents with protease (breaks down protein like egg and meat) and amylase (breaks down starches like pasta and potatoes). The real kicker for hard water is the builder—usually sodium citrate or a phosphate alternative. Builders soften the water inside the machine so the surfactants can actually work. If a product doesn't list enzymes or a builder, skip it.

Glass Protection

Hard water etches glass over time, leaving a permanent cloudy look. Detergents with a glass shield or corrosion inhibitor are worth the extra cost if you run the machine daily. Check the label for these specific terms.

Form Factor: Pacs vs. Gel vs. Powder

Pacs are convenient but expensive. Gels are weak—they wash off the dispenser too fast in hard water. Powder is actually the best value because you can adjust the amount based on your water hardness and soil level. That said, the high-end pacs we tested outperform any powder for stubborn residue.

Rinse Aid Interaction

Even the best detergent won't fix everything if you skip rinse aid. It helps water sheet off glasses instead of beading and drying into spots. Use a separate rinse aid like Finish Jet-Dry alongside any detergent—it's a force multiplier.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use a rinse aid with these detergents?

Yes. Always. Rinse aid helps water sheet off dishes, so minerals don't dry onto the surface. Our tests showed even the best detergent left fewer spots when we used a rinse aid. It costs pennies per load.

Why is my dishwasher leaving white film even with good detergent?

Check your water softener if you have one. If it's out of salt, that's your problem. If not, you might need to run a dishwasher cleaner (like Affresh) to remove scale buildup inside the machine. Hard water deposits can clog the spray arms.

Are pods better than powder for hard water?

Pods are more convenient and better at removing food residue because they include pre-wash enzymes. Powder gives you control over dosing but requires more manual effort. For hard water, pods win on effectiveness; powder wins on price.

Can I use less detergent if my water is soft?

Yes. If your water is under 5 grains per gallon, you can cut the dose in half. Overdosing is a common cause of film on dishes.
