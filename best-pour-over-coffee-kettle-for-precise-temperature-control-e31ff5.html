What to Look For in a Pour-Over Kettle

Before we get to the reviews, here's the short list of what actually matters. Ignore the marketing fluff.

Temperature Accuracy and Stability

You want a kettle that hits your target temp (usually 195-205°F for light roasts) and holds it there. Look for a stated accuracy of ±1°F. Cheaper models drift by 5-10 degrees, which wrecks extraction. The probe placement matters too — a probe in the base reads the heating element, not the water. The best kettles use a secondary probe or a smart algorithm to compensate.

Flow Rate Control

The gooseneck's curve and spout opening dictate your pour. A 2.5-3mm spout is the sweet spot — fast enough for a bloom, slow enough for a precise spiral. Kettles with a wide, open spout (like some cheap stainless models) dump water too fast. You lose control, and you get channeling in the coffee bed.

Heating Speed

Nobody wants to wait 10 minutes for water to boil. A good electric kettle heats 1 liter from tap temp to 212°F in under 4 minutes. Stovetop kettles are slower and require a thermometer, which defeats the purpose of precision.

Build Quality and Ergonomics

You'll hold this thing daily. A heavy, unbalanced kettle is a wrist killer. Look for a comfortable, heat-resistant handle and a body that doesn't get scalding hot to the touch. Also check the lid — a loose-fitting lid is a steam burn waiting to happen.

FAQ

Do I really need temperature control?

If you brew light roasts, yes. Those beans need water between 200-205°F to extract properly. Boiling water (212°F) will make them taste bitter and flat. For dark roasts, you can get away with a stovetop kettle and a thermometer, but you're still guessing.

What's the ideal spout size?

Look for a gooseneck with a 2.5-3mm opening. Anything wider is hard to control. Anything narrower is too slow and frustrating.

Can I use these on an induction stove?

Only the Hario Buono works on induction, and only if you buy the specific induction-compatible version. The electric kettles are self-contained — just plug them in.

How long does a kettle last?

With daily use, a good electric kettle should last 3-5 years. The Hario Buono, if you don't drop it, can last a decade or more.
