Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before we get to the reviews, here are the five things that actually matter for seniors. Ignore the marketing fluff.

1. Grip Thickness and Texture

Arthritis hits the hands first. A 1.5-inch-thick dumbbell handle is brutal for a 70-year-old with osteoarthritis. Look for contoured grips (like the Bowflex) or textured rubber. Resistance bands win here because they don't require a crushing grip — you loop them around your wrist or foot.

2. Resistance Curve

Dumbbells have a constant load. The hardest part is the start of the lift. Bands have variable resistance — harder at the top, easier at the bottom. For seniors, this is a blessing. It protects the shoulder joint at the vulnerable starting position.

3. Joint Impact

Dumbbells create compressive force on the spine and knees. Bands do not. If you have osteoporosis or herniated discs, bands are the only safe option. If you have healthy joints, the compressive force from dumbbells actually helps build bone density — which is critical after 60.

4. Setup and Fuss Factor

If you need a bench, a rack, and 20 minutes to set up, you won't do it. Bands roll up and fit in a drawer. Adjustable dumbbells (like the PowerBlock) take 10 seconds to change weight but require floor space. Simple wins.

5. Drop Safety

Dropping a 30-pound dumbbell on your foot at 65 is a broken bone. Dropping a band? Nothing. If you live alone, this is a non-negotiable safety factor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can resistance bands build enough muscle after 60?

Yes, if you use the right resistance. A 70-year-old can build meaningful strength with 10-15 lbs of resistance. The key is progressive overload — you have to increase resistance every 4-6 weeks. The Bodylastics set is best for this because you can stack bands.

Are dumbbells better for bone density?

Yes. Weight-bearing exercise stimulates bone remodeling. Bands do not. If you have osteopenia or osteoporosis, you need at least 8-10 lbs of dumbbell work for 20 minutes, 3 times a week. Combine that with bands for joint-friendly mobility work.

Can I use bands if I have had rotator cuff surgery?

Yes, but start with the lightest band (2 lbs) and only do external rotation exercises. Stop if you feel sharp pain. Bands are actually the standard rehab tool for rotator cuff recovery because they provide resistance without joint compression.

How heavy should dumbbells be for a 65-year-old woman?

Start with 5 lbs for shoulder work, 8 lbs for bicep curls, and 10 lbs for squats and deadlifts. If you can't complete 12 reps with good form, go lighter. If you can do 15 reps easily, go up 2-3 lbs.

Do I need a bench to use dumbbells?

No. For seniors, standing exercises are better because they improve balance. But for chest presses, you can lie on the floor or use a yoga mat. Skip the bench — it's a fall risk getting on and off.
