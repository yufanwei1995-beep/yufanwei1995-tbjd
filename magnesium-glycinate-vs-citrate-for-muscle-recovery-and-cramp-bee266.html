Magnesium Glycinate vs Citrate: What to Look For

Before you buy, you need to understand the difference. It's not just about the magnesium — it's about what the magnesium is attached to.

The "Carrier" Matters

Magnesium is a mineral. It needs a carrier molecule to be absorbed. Glycine and citric acid are those carriers. Glycine is an amino acid that also acts as an inhibitory neurotransmitter. This means it calms your brain and nerves. Citric acid is a weak organic acid found in citrus fruits — it helps push magnesium into your cells faster.

Absorption and Bioavailability

Both forms absorb well. Studies show glycinate has a slightly higher absorption rate, but the real difference is in how your body handles it. Citrate pulls water into the intestines — that's why it helps with constipation. But that same mechanism causes the dreaded "magnesium runs" if you take too much. Glycinate doesn't pull water, so it's gentler on the stomach.

Dosage and Elemental Magnesium

This is where most people screw up. A label might say "Magnesium Glycinate 500mg" — but that's the total compound weight. The actual elemental magnesium is usually only 10-15% of that. For cramps, you want 200-400mg of elemental magnesium per day. Check the supplement facts panel for "Elemental Magnesium" not the total weight.

Timing

Glycinate is best taken 1-2 hours before bed. The glycine crosses the blood-brain barrier and lowers core body temperature, which signals your body it's time to sleep. Citrate is better taken in the morning or after a workout — it absorbs faster and won't make you drowsy.

FAQ: Your Magnesium Questions, Answered

Can I take both glycinate and citrate?

Yes, but it's usually overkill. If you want to try it, take citrate in the morning post-workout and glycinate at night. This gives you fast relief during the day and sustained recovery at night. Just keep total elemental magnesium under 400mg to avoid digestive issues.

How long until I see results for cramps?

Most people see a reduction within 5-7 days of consistent use. It takes time for magnesium to replete your muscle stores. If you're severely deficient, it could take 2-3 weeks. Don't expect overnight miracles.

Will magnesium make me sleepy during the day?

Glycinate can make you drowsy — that's why it's best at night. Citrate won't make you sleepy. If you need daytime relief, stick to citrate or a lower dose of glycinate (under 100mg).

What about magnesium oxide?

Avoid it for cramps. Oxide is the cheapest form and only about 4% is absorbed. It's fine for constipation but useless for muscle recovery.
