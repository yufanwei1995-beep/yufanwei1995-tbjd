Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Sourdough

Before you click buy, understand these four factors. They make or break your loaf.

1. Material and Heat Retention

Bare cast iron (Dutch oven) has massive thermal mass. It takes 30+ minutes to preheat fully, but once hot, it holds heat like a brick wall. This gives you a powerful "oven spring" — the initial burst of steam that makes the dough rise tall. Enameled cast iron (French oven) has the same thermal mass but the enamel coating adds a layer of insulation, making heat transfer slightly less aggressive. In my tests, bare cast iron gave a 10-15% taller rise on the same dough recipe.

2. Steam Trapping

You need a tight-fitting lid. A loose lid lets steam escape, and you'll get a matte, thick crust. The Lodge Combo Cooker has a shallow skillet that sits on top like a lid, creating an almost perfect seal. The Le Creuset French oven has a heavy, tight-fitting lid but the cast iron rim can wick heat away, causing slight condensation drips. For sourdough, a combo cooker design is superior to a traditional pot shape.

3. Ease of Loading

This is the biggest hidden trap. With a standard 5.5-quart Dutch oven, you have to lower your dough into a deep, screaming-hot pot. You will burn your forearms. A combo cooker (shallow base) or a bread oven (wide, low profile) lets you just drop the dough in. Trust me — after 20 loaves, this matters more than brand name.

4. Maintenance and Durability

Bare cast iron requires seasoning and will rust if you leave it wet. Enameled cast iron is dishwasher-safe (though you shouldn't) and never rusts, but the enamel can chip if you drop it. For bread baking, the high heat (450°F+) is fine for both, but acidic dough scraps left on bare iron can strip seasoning. You'll need to wipe the pot clean after each bake.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a French oven for sourdough?

Yes, absolutely. A French oven (enameled cast iron) works great for sourdough. The enamel doesn't affect the bread's flavor. The only downsides are the deep shape (harder to load dough) and the slightly lower heat transfer compared to bare iron.

Why is my bread sticking to the bottom?

This happens if your dough is too wet or if the pot isn't hot enough. Preheat your pot for at least 30 minutes at 450°F. Dust the bottom with cornmeal or rice flour before loading the dough. If using bare cast iron, make sure the seasoning is intact.

Do I need to preheat a French oven?

Yes. Always preheat any cast iron pot for 30-45 minutes before baking. This ensures the pot is radiating heat from all sides, which is what creates the oven spring. Skipping this step results in a flat loaf.

What's the difference between a Dutch oven and a bread cloche?

A Dutch oven is a deep, multi-purpose pot. A bread cloche (like the Challenger) is a shallow pan with a domed lid, designed specifically for bread. The cloche allows for easier loading and better steam management. For sourdough, a cloche is technically superior.

Can I bake sourdough in a stainless steel pot?

You can, but it won't work well. Stainless steel doesn't retain heat like cast iron. The crust will be pale and thin, and the oven spring will be weak. Stick to cast iron for best results.
