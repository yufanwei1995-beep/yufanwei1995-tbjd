Buyer's Guide: What to Look For in a Van Power Station

Capacity (Watt-hours)

This tells you how much total energy the battery holds. For van life, don't go below 1000Wh unless you're just charging phones. A 12V fridge pulls about 30-60Wh per hour (720-1440Wh per day). Laptops add 50-100Wh. Lights, fans, and water pumps add another 100-200Wh. Aim for 2000Wh minimum for weekend trips, 4000Wh+ for full-time living.

AC Output (Wattage)

This determines what appliances you can run at once. A standard mini-fridge pulls 50-100W. An induction cooktop pulls 1500-1800W. A microwave pulls 800-1200W. If you want to cook or use power tools, get at least 1800W continuous output. Most van lifers are fine with 1500W.

Recharge Speed

Solar charging is standard, but speed varies. A 200W solar panel on a good day gives you about 1000Wh in 5-6 hours. Some stations like EcoFlow's X-Stream tech can recharge from AC in under 2 hours — a lifesaver when you're at a coffee shop or friend's house. Check the max solar input wattage.

Weight & Size

You'll move this thing. If you're in a small van, every inch matters. The Jackery Explorer 2000 Pro weighs 43 lbs and is 18x12x12 inches — manageable for one person. The EcoFlow Delta Pro Ultra weighs 115 lbs — you'll want a dolly or a second person. Balance capacity with portability.

Battery Chemistry

Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) is the gold standard for van life. It lasts 3000-5000 charge cycles vs. 500-1000 for older lithium-ion. It handles temperature swings better and won't catch fire. Skip any station that doesn't use LiFePO4.

Ports & Connectivity

Minimum: 2-3 AC outlets, 2-4 USB-A, 1-2 USB-C (preferably 100W for laptop charging), 12V car port. Bonus: wireless charging pad, app control, and expansion capability (add extra batteries).

Frequently Asked Questions

How many watt-hours do I need for van life?

For weekend trips: 1000-1500Wh. For full-time living with a fridge, laptop, and lights: 2000-4000Wh. If you want to run AC, a microwave, or power tools: 4000Wh+.

Can I charge a power station while driving?

Yes, if you have a 12V car outlet or a dedicated DC-to-DC charger. Most power stations support 12V input (8A or 10A). It's slow — about 100-150Wh per hour of driving. For faster charging, install a DC-to-DC charger that can deliver 30-50A.

Is LiFePO4 worth the extra cost?

Yes. LiFePO4 lasts 3000-5000 cycles vs. 500-1000 for Li-Ion. If you use one cycle per day, LiFePO4 lasts 8-14 years. Li-Ion lasts 1-3 years. The cost difference is $200-400. Worth it.

Can I run a 1500W space heater on these?

Yes, if the station has at least 1500W continuous output. The Anker 757 and Goal Zero 1500X can. The Jackery 2000 Pro and Bluetti AC200L can. The EcoFlow Delta Pro Ultra handles 3600W — no problem.

Do I need solar panels?

Not if you drive daily or have access to shore power. But solar gives you freedom. A 200W panel adds 800-1000Wh per day on average. For full-time off-grid, 400W is the sweet spot.
