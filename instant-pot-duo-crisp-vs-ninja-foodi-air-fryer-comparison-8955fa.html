Instant Pot Duo Crisp vs Ninja Foodi: The Short Answer

Both machines do the same trick—pressure cook, air fry, bake, broil, and dehydrate. But they execute it differently, and those differences matter.

The Ninja Foodi uses a separate air fryer lid that stores on the back of the unit. The Instant Pot Duo Crisp uses a single lid with a built-in heating element and fan. That sounds like a minor detail, but it changes how you cook, how loud the machine gets, and how hard it is to clean.

I tested both over three weeks. I made roasted chicken, crispy tofu, french fries, stew, yogurt, and even a cheesecake. Here’s what I found.

Bottom line up front: The Ninja Foodi FD401 crisps food noticeably better and its separate lid is easier to handle. The Instant Pot Duo Crisp is quieter and costs less, but its lid is heavy and its air frying is more finicky.

Buyer’s Guide: What to Look For

Before you pick a side, understand what you're actually paying for.

Lid Design

This is the biggest difference. The Ninja Foodi has two lids: a pressure cooking lid and a separate air fryer lid. The air fryer lid is light and comes off easily. The Instant Pot Duo Crisp has one heavy lid (about 6 pounds) that flips open. It has a fan and heating coil built into it.

The single lid means you can't lift it off and set it aside. You have to work around it when stirring food. The Ninja's removable lid makes stirring and shaking food much easier.

Air Frying Performance

Ninja's air fryer fan is more powerful. It circulates air faster and gets food crispier. The Instant Pot's fan is slower and the heating element sits lower, so food cooks more unevenly. You'll need to shake the basket more often with the Instant Pot.

Capacity and Size

Both come in 6-quart and 8-quart sizes. The 6-quart fits a 4-pound chicken max. The 8-quart fits a 6-pound bird. But remember: the air fryer basket is smaller than the pot. Ninja's 8-quart has a 5-quart air fryer basket. The Instant Pot's 8-quart air fryer basket holds about 4 quarts.

Controls

Ninja uses simple dials and buttons. The Instant Pot has a digital display with more preset buttons. The Ninja is easier to program for beginners. The Instant Pot gives you more control over pressure levels and temperature, but the menu is cluttered.

Cleanup

Ninja's air fryer basket and crisper plate are nonstick and dishwasher safe. The Instant Pot's air fryer basket is also nonstick, but the lid is not. You have to wipe down the heating element and fan on the Instant Pot lid by hand. Grease splatter collects there, and it's a pain to clean.

Price

The Instant Pot Duo Crisp is usually $30 to $50 cheaper than the comparable Ninja Foodi. If budget is your only concern, the Instant Pot wins. If you want better results, the Ninja is worth the extra cash.

Product Reviews

1. Ninja Foodi 8-Quart 6-in-1 (FD401)

Rating: 4.5/5

The FD401 is the sweet spot in Ninja's lineup. It's the 8-quart model with the TenderCrisp technology—that's Ninja's fancy name for pressure cooking then air frying without swapping pots.

How it performed: I pressure cooked a whole chicken for 25 minutes, then air fried it for 10 minutes at 390°F. The skin was crackling crisp. The meat stayed juicy. That two-step process is the killer feature. No other budget multicooker does it this well.

Air frying: French fries came out golden and crunchy in 15 minutes. I didn't need to shake the basket as often as with the Instant Pot—maybe twice instead of every 5 minutes.

What I didn't like: The unit is tall. It takes up serious counter space (15.5 x 14 x 12.5 inches). The pressure cooker lid has a gasket that needs replacing every 12-18 months. That's a $15 recurring cost.

Verdict: Best all-around choice for most families. The crisping is superior and the lid design is smarter.

2. Instant Pot Duo Crisp 8-Quart (model 140-0034-01)

Rating: 4/5

Instant Pot's answer to the Foodi is the Duo Crisp. It does everything the Ninja does, but with a single lid and a lower price tag.

How it performed: The pressure cooking is flawless—it's an Instant Pot, after all. Stews, beans, and rice come out perfect every time. The air frying is where it falls short.

Air frying: The heating element sits closer to the food, so the top cooks faster than the bottom. I had to flip food constantly. Fries came out crispy on top but soggy on the bottom unless I shook the basket every 4 minutes. That's annoying.

The lid problem: The single lid weighs about 6 pounds. You have to flip it open and prop it back. When you're stirring food, the lid hangs over the counter and drips condensation. It's clunky.

What I didn't like: The air fryer basket is shallow. You can't fit a whole chicken in it—you have to cut the bird in half. Also, the lid's fan is loud. It's a whirring noise that gets on your nerves after 20 minutes.

Verdict: A good budget option if you pressure cook more than you air fry. Just know the air frying is a compromise.

3. Ninja Foodi 6-Quart (FD301)

Rating: 4.5/5

The little brother to the FD401. Same technology, smaller footprint.

Who it's for: Singles, couples, or small kitchens. It takes up less counter space (13.5 x 12.5 x 11 inches) and still fits a 4-pound chicken.

How it performed: Identical to the FD401 in testing. Same crispiness, same easy cleanup. The only difference is capacity. I could fit six drumsticks in the air fryer basket, but not eight.

What I didn't like: The smaller pot means you can't cook a large batch of stew for a crowd. And the price difference between the 6-quart and 8-quart is only $20, so you might as well go bigger unless space is tight.

Verdict: Perfect for apartments. Skip it if you have room for the 8-quart.

4. Instant Pot Duo Crisp 6-Quart (model 140-0033-01)

Rating: 3.5/5

The smaller Instant Pot Duo Crisp. It's the cheapest way to get pressure cooking and air frying in one machine.

How it performed: Pressure cooking is solid. Air frying is the same weak point as the 8-quart model—uneven crisping and a heavy lid.

The good: It's light (about 20 pounds) and takes up minimal counter space. It's also frequently on sale for under $100, which makes it an impulse buy.

What I didn't like: The 6-quart air fryer basket is tiny. You can only cook about 2 cups of fries at a time. For a family of four, you'll be cooking in batches.

Verdict: Only buy this if you're on a strict budget and air frying is a secondary feature.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the Instant Pot Duo Crisp replace both my pressure cooker and air fryer?

Yes, but with a caveat. The pressure cooking is as good as a standalone Instant Pot. The air frying is about 80% as good as a dedicated air fryer. If you air fry daily, keep your standalone unit. If you air fry twice a week, the Duo Crisp is enough.

Is the Ninja Foodi worth the extra money over the Instant Pot?

If you air fry often, yes. The Ninja's crisping is noticeably better and the lid design makes it easier to use. The $30-$50 difference pays for itself in better meals and less frustration.

Which model can cook a whole chicken?

Both 8-quart models can pressure cook a whole chicken. But only the Ninja's air fryer basket can fit a whole 4-5 pound bird for crisping. The Instant Pot's basket is too shallow—you'll need to spatchcock the chicken.

Are the accessories interchangeable?

No. Ninja and Instant Pot use different basket sizes and shapes. You can't swap parts between brands. Stick with one brand to avoid buying duplicate accessories.

Do these machines make good yogurt?

Both have a yogurt function. The Instant Pot's is more precise with temperature control. The Ninja's yogurt setting is basic—it just keeps a constant temperature without a customizable fermentation time.

Final Verdict

Buy the Ninja Foodi 8-Quart (FD401). It crisps better, cleans easier, and the two-lid system is just smarter design. The extra $40 over the Instant Pot is worth it. The only reason to pick the Instant Pot Duo Crisp is if you're tight on cash or you pressure cook 90% of the time and barely air fry. In that case, save the money and get the 6-quart Duo Crisp. But for most families, the Ninja Foodi is the one that'll earn its counter space.
