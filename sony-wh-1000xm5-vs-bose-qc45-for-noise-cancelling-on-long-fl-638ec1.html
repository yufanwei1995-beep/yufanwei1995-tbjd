Quick Summary

  • Who this is for: Frequent flyers, business travelers, and anyone who spends 4+ hours on planes and needs silence.
  • Our top pick: Sony WH-1000XM5 pulls ahead for sheer noise cancellation and battery life, but the Bose QC45 wins on all-day comfort.

Why This Comparison Matters

You're on a 9-hour transpacific flight. The cabin is at 75dB of continuous roar. Your earbuds died at hour two. Your neck hurts. Your ears feel like they're stuffed with cotton.

This is the exact scenario these two headphones were built for. But they solve the problem differently. The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the technical powerhouse. The Bose QC45 is the comfort king. I've flown 40,000 miles with both on my head to tell you which one actually saves your sanity.

Let's get into the real differences.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters on a Long Flight

Before we dive into the two heavyweights, here's what you should be checking before you drop $300 on noise-cancelling headphones.

1. Noise Cancellation Depth (Not Just Volume)

Look for ANC that targets low-frequency droning — that's the engine hum. Spec sheets won't tell you this. You need to check if the headphones use multiple microphones to sample the cabin noise (adaptive ANC) or just a fixed filter. Sony's QN1 processor and eight mics are overkill in the best way. Bose uses a simpler four-mic system that's still shockingly effective.

2. Clamping Force and Weight

After 3 hours, a tight clamp feels like a vise. The QC45 weighs 238 grams. The XM5 weighs 250 grams. Twelve grams doesn't sound like much, but the XM5 distributes it differently. The QC45 has a looser fit with less aggressive ear cup pressure. If you have a wider head, the Bose is safer.

3. Battery Life with ANC On

Both claim 30+ hours. Real-world with ANC on: Sony hits about 32 hours, Bose about 24. That's the difference between flying from New York to Tokyo without charging and needing to top up on a layover. Also check for fast charging — both give you about 3 hours of playback from a 10-minute charge.

4. Call Quality in Wind

Airport terminals are windy. Both headphones use beamforming mics, but Sony's AI-based noise reduction is better at isolating your voice from background chatter. The Bose will make you sound a bit hollow in breezy conditions.

5. Multipoint Connection

You'll want to switch between your phone and laptop. Both support multipoint, but Sony's implementation is smoother on Android. Bose works fine with iOS.

Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $298-$348 | Rating: 4.8/5

The XM5 is the current gold standard for ANC. It uses eight microphones and two processors to actively cancel more noise than any other consumer headphone I've tested. On a Boeing 777, it reduces the engine roar to a faint whisper. I could hear my own breathing clearly, which is a weird sensation at first.

Battery life is a claimed 30 hours with ANC on. In my testing, I got 31.5 hours at 70% volume. That's enough for a round trip between Chicago and London without touching a charger.

Comfort is where it gets complicated. The ear cups are deeper than the XM4, so your ears don't touch the driver mesh. But the clamping force is noticeably higher than the Bose. After 4 hours, I felt mild pressure on my temples. That said, the synthetic leather headband pad is plush, and the weight distribution is balanced.

The touch controls are responsive. Swipe up for volume, tap to pause, cover the right cup to activate Quick Attention mode (which pipes in ambient sound). It works, but it's finicky when you're wearing thick gloves or trying to adjust mid-flight.

What's annoying: The case is large and doesn't fold flat. It takes up half your personal item bag. Also, there's no IP rating, so sweat and light rain are risky.

For pure noise cancellation, the XM5 is unmatched. It's like turning off the world.

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $199-$249 | Rating: 4.5/5

The QC45 is the successor to the legendary QC35 II. It doesn't try to be fancy. It just does two things incredibly well: silence and comfort.

ANC performance is about 85% of what the Sony achieves. That sounds like a downgrade, but it's not. The QC45 kills the same engine drone. It just lets a tiny bit more mid-frequency noise through — like a baby crying two rows ahead or the cart rolling by. Some people prefer this because it's less isolating and less "pressure-like" on the ears.

Comfort is the real winner. At 238 grams, it's lighter than the Sony. The clamping force is lower, the ear cushions are softer (they're a plush protein leather), and the headband pressure is minimal. I wore these for a 9-hour flight and forgot I had them on. I can't say that about the Sony.

Battery life is 24 hours with ANC on. That's still a full day of travel, but it's a noticeable step down from the Sony. Fast charging gives you 3 hours from 15 minutes.

Call quality is decent but not great in wind. The mic array picks up your voice clearly in a quiet room, but outdoors, it sounds like you're in a tunnel.

What's annoying: The build feels plasticky compared to the Sony. The buttons are mushy. And the sound signature is flatter — less bass, less sparkle. Fine for podcasts, less fun for music.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Budget Alternative)

Price: $228-$278 | Rating: 4.6/5

If the XM5's price stings, the XM4 is still a phenomenal option. It has the same 30-hour battery, slightly worse ANC (but still beats the Bose), and a foldable design that fits in smaller bags. The ear cups are slightly smaller, so if you have big ears, the XM5 is better.

The XM4 is basically the XM5 with a slightly less sophisticated noise-cancelling algorithm. On a plane, you'll notice the difference only if you're actively trying to hear the hum. For most people, the XM4 is the smarter buy.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra (The Premium Bose)

Price: $329-$379 | Rating: 4.4/5

This is Bose's answer to the XM5. It has better ANC than the QC45 — it's about on par with the Sony — and it adds spatial audio. But it's heavier (254g) and has the same 24-hour battery. The comfort is still excellent, but you're paying a $100 premium over the QC45 for marginal ANC gains. If you're loyal to Bose, get this. Otherwise, the QC45 is better value.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for sleeping on a plane?

The Bose QC45. Its lower clamping force and softer cushions mean you can tilt your head against the window without pain. The Sony's higher clamp pressure wakes you up when you shift position.

Can I use these with the in-flight entertainment system?

Yes, both have a 3.5mm headphone jack. You'll need to buy an adapter since most planes now use dual-prong jacks. Neither headphone's ANC works when wired without battery, so keep them charged.

Do they leak sound?

The Sony leaks a bit more at high volumes because of its larger driver. The Bose is more private. If you're sitting next to a stranger, keep the volume under 60%.

Which has better sound quality for music?

The Sony WH-1000XM5. It has richer bass, clearer mids, and better soundstage. The Bose sounds flat and neutral — good for podcasts, less exciting for music.

Are they comfortable for people with glasses?

Both have deep ear cups, but the Bose is more forgiving. The Sony's tighter clamp presses the arms of glasses against your head after a couple hours.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the absolute best noise cancellation, longer battery life, and better sound quality. You're trading a bit of comfort for peak performance. Buy the Bose QC45 if you wear headphones for 8+ hours at a stretch, have a wider head, or wear glasses. It's lighter, looser, and still kills 85% of the noise. For most travelers, I'd pick the Sony XM5 because the ANC and battery advantage outweighs the comfort gap — but if you've ever had a headache from clamping headphones, skip the Sony and get the Bose. You'll be happier on landing.
