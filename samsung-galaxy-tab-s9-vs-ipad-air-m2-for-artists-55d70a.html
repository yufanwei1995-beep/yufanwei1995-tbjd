This is for artists, illustrators, and designers who want a tablet for drawing, painting, photo editing, or 3D modeling. You're stuck between Android (Tab S9) and iPadOS (iPad Air M2). I've used both for 3 months as my primary art machines. Here's what I found.

Buyer's Guide: What Artists Need in a Tablet

Before you buy, know what matters for art. Here's the checklist I used:

  • Stylus latency: The delay between your hand and the line on screen. Under 10ms is good. Under 5ms is great. The Apple Pencil Pro and S Pen both deliver under 4ms.
  • Pressure sensitivity: More levels mean finer control. 4096 is standard. 8192 is better. Both tablets support 4096+ levels.
  • App ecosystem: Procreate is iPad-only. Clip Studio Paint runs on both. Adobe Fresco works on both but is better on iPad. If you need Procreate, get the iPad. Period.
  • Screen quality: OLED gives true blacks and vibrant colors. LCD is fine but less punchy. Tab S9 has a 11-inch 2560x1600 OLED. iPad Air M2 has a 11-inch 2360x1640 LCD.
  • Battery life: You'll draw for hours. iPad Air M2 lasts 9-10 hours of heavy drawing. Tab S9 lasts 8-9 hours.
  • Storage: Art files eat space. iPad Air M2 starts at 128GB but has no expandable storage. Tab S9 starts at 128GB and has a microSD slot (up to 1TB).
  • Portability: Both are 11-inch tablets. Tab S9 is 498g. iPad Air M2 is 462g. Both are easy to carry.

Individual Product Reviews

1. iPad Air M2 (2024 Model) – Best for Most Artists

Price: $799 (128GB) – $1,199 (1TB) | Rating: 4.5/5

Best for: Artists who want Procreate, the best stylus experience, and a mature app ecosystem.

Pros: M2 chip is fast (8-core CPU, 10-core GPU). Apple Pencil Pro has 4ms latency, tilt, and barrel roll. Procreate is the best drawing app on any tablet. 2732x2048 resolution is sharp. USB-C works with external drives.

Cons: No expandable storage. LCD screen (not OLED). No headphone jack. Apple Pencil Pro costs $129 extra.

The iPad Air M2 with the Apple Pencil Pro is the best drawing tablet under $1,000. The M2 chip handles 50-layer Procreate files without lag. The screen is bright and color-accurate (P3 wide color). But the LCD means blacks look gray in dark rooms. If you draw in dim light, the Tab S9's OLED is better.

Procreate alone makes this the winner for most digital painters. Clip Studio Paint runs smoothly. Adobe Fresco gives you vector and raster brushes. The only real downside is the cost of the Pencil Pro. But the feel is worth it.

2. Samsung Galaxy Tab S9 – Best for Android Artists

Price: $799 (128GB) – $1,099 (512GB) | Rating: 4.3/5

Best for: Artists who want an included stylus, OLED screen, and expandable storage.

Pros: 11-inch 2560x1600 OLED (120Hz). S Pen is included and free (no extra $129). IP68 water-resistant. MicroSD slot up to 1TB. 8 Gen 2 chip is fast. USB-C 3.2.

Cons: No Procreate. Android drawing apps are weaker (Clip Studio Paint is good but not as polished as iPad). S Pen feels slightly less precise than Apple Pencil Pro. Fewer art apps overall.

The Tab S9's OLED screen is gorgeous. Blacks are truly black. Colors pop. The 120Hz refresh rate makes drawing feel smooth. The S Pen is included and works well. It has 4,096 levels of pressure and tilt. But the tip is a bit soft compared to the Pencil Pro. You'll feel more friction on the screen.

The biggest problem is the app ecosystem. Clip Studio Paint is the best drawing app here. But it's not as optimized as on iPad. Adobe Fresco works but has fewer brushes. If you can live without Procreate, the Tab S9 is a solid choice.

Price: $999 (256GB) – $1,299 (512GB) | Rating: 4.4/5

Best for: Artists who need a larger canvas (12.4-inch) and don't mind the extra weight.

Pros: 12.4-inch 2800x1752 OLED. Same S Pen included. Expandable storage. 8 Gen 2 chip. IP68.

Cons: Heavy (581g). Expensive. Still no Procreate. App gap remains.

The Tab S9+ is for artists who draw large illustrations or want a bigger canvas for photo editing. The 12.4-inch screen is amazing for multi-window workflows. You can have your reference image on one side and your drawing on the other. But at 581g, it's heavy for long drawing sessions. The extra screen real estate is great, but the iPad Air M2 is more portable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Procreate on the Galaxy Tab S9?

No. Procreate is only available on iPad. There are alternatives like Clip Studio Paint, Infinite Painter, and Sketchbook on Android. None match Procreate's polish and brush engine.

Which has better stylus latency?

Both are under 4ms. The Apple Pencil Pro feels slightly tighter with less jitter. The S Pen is close but has a softer tip that adds a bit of drag. In practice, both are good enough for professional work.

Is the OLED screen on the Tab S9 better for art?

Yes, for dark scenes and vibrant colors. OLED gives true blacks and infinite contrast. The iPad Air's LCD is fine but blacks look gray in dark rooms. The Tab S9's 120Hz refresh rate also makes scrolling and drawing smoother. But the iPad Air's 60Hz is still fine for most artists.

Which is better for photo editing?

The iPad Air M2. Adobe Lightroom and Photoshop are better optimized on iPad. The M2 chip handles raw files faster. The Tab S9 is good but app support is weaker.

Can I use both tablets for 3D modeling?

Yes. Both run Nomad Sculpt (iPad) and ZBrush (Android). The M2 chip has a slight edge in rendering speed. But the Tab S9's 120Hz screen makes sculpting feel smoother.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the iPad Air M2 with Apple Pencil Pro if you want the best drawing experience, need Procreate, and don't mind paying extra for the stylus. It's the most polished, reliable art tablet under $1,000. Buy the Samsung Galaxy Tab S9 only if you must have an OLED screen, want expandable storage, or can't justify the $129 Apple Pencil Pro cost. The Tab S9 is a good tablet. The iPad Air M2 is a great art tool. For most artists, the iPad wins.
