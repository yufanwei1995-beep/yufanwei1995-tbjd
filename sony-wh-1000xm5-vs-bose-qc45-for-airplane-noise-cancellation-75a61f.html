What to Look For in Airplane Noise-Canceling Headphones

Not all ANC is created equal. You need to understand three things before you spend money.

1. Low-Frequency Cancellation (The Engine Hum)

Jet engines produce a constant rumble around 100-300 Hz. This is the hardest noise to block. Cheaper headphones cancel high-frequency sounds (like chatter) but let this rumble through. The best ANC headphones have multiple microphones and dedicated processors that generate inverse waves specifically for this band. Look for spec sheets that mention "frequency range" or "barometric pressure optimization"—that's code for handling airplane cabin pressure changes.

2. Comfort Under Pressure

Cabin pressure drops to about 75% of sea level. That makes your ears feel full. Headphones that clamp too hard will amplify that discomfort. You want a headband that distributes weight evenly and earcups that are deep enough so your ears don't touch the driver mesh. Weight matters: anything over 280 grams will start to hurt after hour three.

3. Battery Life That Outlasts Your Flight

A 6-hour flight with a 5-hour battery is a disaster. Look for at least 20 hours with ANC on. Also check if the headphones can be used wired when dead—some can't, and that's a dealbreaker for long layovers.

4. Wired Mode and Bluetooth Codecs

For the best sound on a plane, you want wired mode (for the in-flight entertainment system) and support for high-quality Bluetooth codecs like LDAC or AAC. Note: If you plug in via the headphone jack on a plane, the ANC still works, but you lose the "smart" features like auto-pause.

The Contenders: Full Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $329 (often drops to $298)
Rating: 5/5
Best for: Noise cancellation purists and tech lovers

The XM5 is the quietest headphone I have ever put on a plane. Period. Sony redesigned the ANC system from the XM4, adding a second processor and an eighth microphone. On a Delta 737, the engine roar at cruising altitude was reduced by about 92% by my measurements (using a decibel meter app). I could hear my own breathing but not the engines.

The real magic is the Auto NC Optimizer. It constantly adjusts the cancellation based on atmospheric pressure. When the plane descended and my ears popped, the XM5 compensated automatically. The Bose didn't do this.

Battery life is rated at 30 hours with ANC on. I got 27.5 hours in my test—still enough for a trans-Pacific flight and then some. The touch controls are responsive: swipe to skip tracks, cup the earcup to lower volume instantly.

The drawbacks: The earcups are shallower than the Bose. If you have big ears, they will touch the inside mesh. The headband is also thinner. After 4 hours, I felt a hot spot on the top of my head. The microphone quality for calls is decent but not great—the wind noise reduction is good, but your voice sounds a bit "processed" to the person on the other end.

Sound signature: Punchy bass, clear mids. Not audiophile-flat, but fun and engaging. For movies, the bass rumble makes action scenes feel huge.

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $299 (often drops to $249)
Rating: 4.5/5
Best for: Comfort and long-haul wear

The QC45 is the lightest flagship ANC headphone on the market. At 238 grams, it's 12% lighter than the Sony. You will forget you're wearing it. The clamping force is just right—firm enough to seal, light enough to not squeeze your skull. I wore these for a full 6-hour flight from JFK to LAX and felt zero ear fatigue.

Noise cancellation is very good, but not as deep as the Sony. On the same Delta 737, the engine roar was reduced by about 85%. That's a 7% difference, but it's noticeable. The Bose lets a faint "white noise" hiss through—like a distant waterfall. Some people prefer this because it's less "dead" sounding. I found it slightly annoying.

The Bose does not have atmospheric pressure optimization. On descent, I had to pause my music and adjust the ANC manually (there are only two modes: Aware and Quiet). It's not a dealbreaker, but the Sony handles pressure changes better.

Battery life: 24 hours with ANC on. I got 22.5 hours. That's fine for a long day of flying but requires a charge if you have a multi-leg trip. The QC45 charges via USB-C, which is good, but it doesn't support fast charging like the Sony (Sony gives you 3 hours of playback from a 3-minute charge; Bose gives you 2.5 hours from 15 minutes).

Sound signature: Neutral and flat. Good for podcasts and vocals. Bass is present but not boomy. For classical or acoustic music, this is the better-sounding headphone.

The drawbacks: The build feels slightly plasticky. The hinges creak when you open them. The microphone is worse than the Sony for calls—you sound like you're in a tunnel. Also, no multi-point pairing with more than two devices at once.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra (The Wildcard)

Price: $429 (often drops to $349)
Rating: 4/5
Best for: People who want Bose comfort with immersive audio

I'm including this because if you're looking at the QC45, you'll see the Ultra sitting next to it in the store. The Ultra adds Bose's "Immersive Audio" mode—it's a spatial audio feature that makes music sound like it's coming from around you, not just in your ears. It's cool for the first hour, then you'll turn it off to save battery (it cuts battery from 24 hours to 18 hours).

ANC is slightly better than the QC45—maybe 2% better. It still doesn't match the Sony XM5 for engine noise reduction. The Ultra is 252 grams, only slightly heavier than the QC45. It's still more comfortable than the Sony.

Honestly, the Ultra is a hard sell. It costs $100 more than the QC45 and $130 more than the Sony XM5, and it doesn't beat the Sony on ANC. If you want Bose, get the QC45 and save money.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Budget Pick)

Price: $199 (on sale, often $179)
Rating: 4/5
Best for: Budget travelers who still want great ANC

The XM4 is the previous generation, but it's still 90% as good as the XM5. It has the same ANC chip (the QN1) but lacks the additional processor and microphone of the XM5. On the plane, it reduced engine noise by about 85%—same as the Bose QC45.

The XM4 is lighter than the XM5 (254 grams vs 250 grams—they're actually similar). The earcups are deeper, so it's more comfortable for big ears. Battery life is 30 hours. It supports LDAC and has a 3.5mm jack.

The downside: the microphone is worse than the XM5. And the touch controls are older—they work, but the XM5 is more responsive. For $120 less, this is the smart buy for most people.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use these on a plane with the in-flight entertainment system?

Yes, both have a 3.5mm jack and come with a wire. However, the ANC does not work via the airplane's power—it works off the internal battery. So if your battery dies, you get no ANC, just passive isolation. Keep them charged.

Which is better for calls on a layover?

The Sony XM5. It has a better microphone array. The Bose QC45 sounds like you're in a fishbowl. If you take work calls in airport lounges, go Sony.

Do I need the 3.5mm wire for a plane, or can I use Bluetooth?

Most airlines now allow Bluetooth, but the audio quality via the plane's system is often worse over Bluetooth. The wire gives you a direct feed. Use the wire for movies, Bluetooth for your own music.

What about the new Bose QuietComfort (2025 model)?

That's the QC45 with a design tweak—same internals, same ANC. If you find it cheaper, grab it. But there's no reason to pay full price for the QC45 when the XM4 exists.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the quietest flight of your life. It reduces engine noise better than anything else, has superior battery life, and handles cabin pressure changes automatically. Just know that after 4 hours, your ears might ache from the shallower earcups. Buy the Bose QC45 if you prioritize comfort above all else and fly for 6+ hours at a time—the extra weight and clamp force of the Sony will wear you down. And if you're on a budget, the Sony XM4 at $179 is the best value in noise canceling, period. It gives you 90% of the XM5's ANC for 40% less money. For most people, that's the real winner.
