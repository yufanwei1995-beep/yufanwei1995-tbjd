Buyer's Guide: What to Look for in a Small Kitchen Ice Maker

Small kitchens mean every inch counts. Here’s what matters most when picking a countertop ice maker for 2026:

Size & Footprint

Measure your counter before buying. Most models are 10-14 inches wide and 12-18 inches deep. Look for a width under 12 inches if space is really tight. The Euhomy IM-001 is only 10.2 inches wide.

Ice Type & Quality

Bullet ice (like from a hotel) is common and cheap to make. Nugget ice (like Sonic) is softer and chewier — better for drinks. The GE Opal 2.0 makes nugget ice. Most budget models make bullet ice.

Ice Production Speed

Look for a model that makes 9-12 cubes in 6-10 minutes. Faster is better for parties. The Igloo ICEB26SS makes 9 cubes in 7 minutes.

Noise Level

All countertop ice makers make noise — fans and compressors. Budget models are louder (45-55 dB). Premium models like the GE Opal 2.0 use better insulation and quieter compressors (around 40 dB).

Self-Cleaning Feature

A must for small kitchens. Without it, you have to manually descale every few weeks. The GE Opal 2.0 and Euhomy IM-001 both have self-cleaning cycles.

Basket Capacity

Most hold 1.5 to 3 pounds of ice. That’s enough for 4-6 glasses. If you entertain, get a model with a 2.5+ pound basket.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do countertop ice makers need a drain?

No. Most models have a water reservoir you refill manually. No plumbing needed. Just plug in and add water.

How often should I clean my ice maker?

Every 1-2 weeks, depending on use. Hard water makes mineral buildup worse. Models with self-cleaning make this much easier.

Can I leave ice in the basket overnight?

Yes, but it will melt and refreeze into a block. Most models have a keep-warm feature that prevents this — check your manual.

Which ice maker is quietest for a small kitchen?

The GE Opal 2.0 (38-42 dB) and Frigidaire EFIC117 (40 dB) are the quietest. Avoid the Igloo ICEB26SS if noise bothers you.

Do these use a lot of electricity?

No. Most use about 100-150 watts — similar to a small fan. They cost roughly $10-20 per year to run.
