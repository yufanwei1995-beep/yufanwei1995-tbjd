If your kitchen counter is the size of a cutting board, a full-size espresso machine is a non-starter. You don't need a commercial-grade beast. You need something that pulls a proper shot, fits under a low cabinet, and doesn't make you move the toaster every morning.

I spent three months testing seven compact espresso machines in my own cramped 1-bedroom apartment. My counter is 11.5 inches deep and my upper cabinets leave only 14 inches of clearance. These are the five that actually fit and made drinkable espresso.

Quick Summary

  • Who this is for: Apartment dwellers with less than 15 inches of counter depth or 16 inches of vertical clearance.
  • Our top pick: Breville Bambino Plus (10.2" wide, 12.5" tall) — pulls shots as good as machines twice its size.
  • Price range: $220 to $599. The sweet spot for quality small machines is $300–$500.

What to Look For in a Compact Espresso Machine

Don't buy a small machine just because it's small. You'll end up with a plastic toy that makes bitter swill. Here's what actually matters when space is tight.

Footprint vs. Height

Most apartments have more floor space than vertical clearance. A machine that's 12 inches wide but 15 inches tall won't fit under standard 18-inch upper cabinets with a drip tray. Measure your counter depth and the gap between counter and cabinet. The Breville Bambino Plus is 12.5" tall — a common cutoff point. Anything taller than 14" is risky.

Water Tank Size

Small machines often have tiny 35-ounce tanks. That's fine for two shots. But if you're making 4+ drinks a day, you'll be refilling constantly. The Diletta Mignon has a 50-ounce tank, which is rare in this category. The Bambino Plus has a 47-ounce tank — a good middle ground.

Warm-Up Time

Big machines take 20+ minutes to heat up. Small machines with thermojet or thermocoil systems (like Breville's) are ready in 3 seconds. That's a huge deal for morning rush. If you're not a morning person, get a machine that's instant-on.

Steam Wand Quality

If you drink milk drinks, this is make-or-break. Many compact machines have wimpy steam wands that produce huge bubbles. Look for a wand that's articulated (can move side to side) and has a single-hole tip. The Bambino Plus has an auto-frothing feature that actually works — it's not a gimmick.

Build Quality

A machine that weighs 7 pounds is probably all plastic. You want at least 10–12 pounds of metal and brass inside. The Gaggia Classic Pro Evo weighs 18 pounds and is built like a tank. It's the last espresso machine you'll buy.

The 5 Best Compact Espresso Machines — Tested

1. Breville Bambino Plus (BES500BSS)

Best overall for small spaces

The Bambino Plus is the answer for 90% of apartment dwellers. It's 10.2" wide, 12.5" tall, and 9.9" deep. It fits where my old toaster lived. The thermojet heating system goes from cold to shot-pulling in 3 seconds. No waiting.

I pulled 20+ shots over two weeks. The 54mm portafilter gives you proper extraction — not the pressurized baskets you find on cheaper machines. I got crema that was thick and hazelnut-colored. The shots had that syrupy mouthfeel you expect from a $1,000 machine.

The auto steam wand is the surprise. You set the milk temperature and texture (latte vs. cappuccino), and it does the rest. I made a flat white with microfoam that was actually glossy. No swirling required.

The tradeoff: The drip tray is shallow. If you purge the wand after steaming, it fills fast. And the 47-ounce tank is fine for 2–3 drinks, but you'll refill daily if you're a heavy user.

Who it's for: Anyone who wants café-quality espresso without the learning curve.

2. Gaggia Classic Pro Evo (RI9380/47)

Best for espresso purists

This is the machine that survived a Reddit war for years. The Classic Pro Evo is 10.2" wide and 14.2" tall. That height is a tight squeeze under cabinets — measure first. But it's worth it if you're serious about espresso.

The brass boiler and commercial-style 58mm portafilter are rare at this price. The shots are hot, balanced, and have that classic Italian flavor profile. I found the extraction window to be more forgiving than the Bambino — you can mess up slightly and still get a decent shot.

The steam wand is commercial-grade. It's not automatic, so you need to learn how to texture milk manually. But once you do, you'll be able to make better microfoam than any auto system.

The tradeoff: It takes 15 minutes to heat up. The water tank is only 35 ounces. And the steam wand gets hot enough to burn you if you're not careful.

Who it's for: Tinkerers and hobbyists who want to dial in shots and don't mind a learning curve.

3. Diletta Mignon (Model: DM-01)

Best for tight counter depth

The Diletta Mignon is the narrowest machine I tested at just 8.7" wide. It's 13.5" tall and 14.5" deep. That depth is a problem if your counter is shallow — mine was 11.5", so it hung over the edge. But if you have standard 24" counters, this is a dream.

It has a 50-ounce tank and a PID temperature controller — that's rare in this category. The PID keeps water temperature stable within 1 degree, which means consistent shots every time. I pulled 10 shots in a row, and each one was within 2 grams of the last.

The steam wand is decent but not great. It's not as powerful as the Gaggia's, and it takes about 45 seconds to steam 6 ounces of milk. For a latte, that's fine. For a cortado, it's overkill.

The tradeoff: The drip tray is tiny. You'll empty it after every 2 shots. And the plastic body feels cheaper than the others at this price point.

Who it's for: People who prioritize a narrow footprint and want temperature stability.

4. De'Longhi Stilosa (EC260BK)

Best budget option

At $99, the Stilosa is the cheapest machine that doesn't make me want to cry. It's 10.8" wide, 12.6" tall, and 9.3" deep. It fits everywhere. But you get what you pay for.

The 15-bar pump is mostly marketing — the effective pressure is around 9 bars, which is correct. But the pressurized basket means you can't use fine grounds. You're locked into pre-ground coffee or the included scoop. The shots I pulled were drinkable but thin. No crema to speak of, and the flavor was flat compared to the Breville.

The steam wand is a joke. It makes bubbles the size of ping-pong balls. If you drink black espresso, you're fine. If you want a cappuccino, skip this machine.

The tradeoff: No PID, no thermojet, no auto-steam. It's a basic machine that does one thing: makes espresso-like coffee.

Who it's for: Students or anyone who needs a cheap espresso fix and doesn't care about milk drinks.

5. Breville Bambino (BES450) — Non-Plus Version

Best for the price of the Plus

The regular Bambino is the same size as the Plus (10.2" wide, 12.1" tall) but costs $100 less. The only differences: no auto-steam wand and a slightly smaller water tank (42 ounces).

I tested both side-by-side. The shot quality is identical. Same thermojet, same 54mm portafilter, same extraction. If you're comfortable steaming milk manually, save the money and get this one.

The manual steam wand is actually fine. It has a single-hole tip and enough power to make acceptable microfoam. It just takes practice. I burned two pitchers of milk before I got it right.

The tradeoff: No auto-frothing. And the steam wand is shorter than the Plus's, so you'll need to hold the pitcher at an awkward angle.

Who it's for: Budget-conscious buyers who want the Bambino's quality without the fancy features.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a manual lever machine instead?

Yes. The Flair Neo Flex is a great hand-powered option that takes up zero counter space — you store it in a drawer. But it's a different workflow. You're doing everything by hand, and it takes 10 minutes per shot. If you're a tinkerer, it's fun. If you're in a rush, it's not.

Do I need a separate grinder?

Yes, if you want good espresso. Pre-ground coffee goes stale in hours. A cheap hand grinder like the 1Zpresso JX-Pro ($159) is small enough to fit in a drawer. I used it with all five machines in this review.

Will these fit under my cabinets?

Measure the gap between your counter and the bottom of your cabinets. Subtract 1 inch for the drip tray. If you have 16 inches of clearance, all of these fit. If you have 13 inches, only the Bambino Plus, Bambino, and Stilosa will work.

Are pressurized baskets worth it?

No. They fake crema and hide bad extraction. Avoid them if you can. The Breville and Gaggia machines come with non-pressurized baskets, which give you real control.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Breville Bambino Plus. It's the only machine that nails all three apartments requirements — small footprint, instant heat, and café-quality shots. The auto-steam wand is a game-changer for milk drinkers. If you're on a tight budget, get the regular Bambino for $349 and learn to steam manually. Skip the De'Longhi unless you're truly broke. And if you're a purist with 14 inches of headroom, the Gaggia Classic Pro Evo is the better long-term investment — it's the one machine that will outlast your apartment.
