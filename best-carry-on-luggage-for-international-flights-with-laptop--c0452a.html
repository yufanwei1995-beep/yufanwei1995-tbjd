What to Look For in an International Carry-On

Most US airlines allow 22x14x9 inches. International carriers are stricter. Budget airlines like Ryanair and EasyJet often only allow 55x40x20cm. You need a bag that is smaller than the limit, not exactly at it. Bag sizers are unforgiving.

1. The 3-4-5 Rule

Forget 22 inches. Look for a bag that is 21.5 inches or shorter in height. This gives you a buffer. A hard-shell bag that measures 22 inches is often a tight squeeze or a gate-check disaster on an A320. The bags below all stay under 21.7 inches.

2. Laptop Compartment Placement

You want a back panel or side panel laptop sleeve. If the sleeve is in the middle of the bag, it pushes the weight away from your back and makes the bag feel heavier. A suspended sleeve (one that doesn't touch the bottom) is key. If the bag drops, the laptop shouldn't absorb the impact.

3. Wheels and Handles

International travel means cobblestones and long train transfers. Look for spinner wheels that are at least 50mm in diameter. Smaller wheels vibrate and get stuck. The handle should have minimal wobble. A wobbly handle makes a 15lb bag feel like 30lbs.

4. Expandability is a Trap

Some bags have an expansion zipper. On an international flight, you will almost certainly be asked to use the sizer if you look like you've expanded the bag. If you expand it, it won't fit. Buy a bag that fits without expansion and use a compression packing cube instead.

5. Weight Matters

International airlines have a 7kg (15.4 lbs) carry-on limit in economy on many routes (especially in Asia and Europe). A bag that weighs 9 lbs empty leaves you only 6.4 lbs for your stuff. That's a jacket, a laptop, and a charger. Aim for a bag under 6 lbs.

6. Fabric vs. Hard Shell

For international travel, I recommend soft-sided. It's more flexible. You can stuff it into an overhead bin that's slightly too small. Hard shells crack and don't flex. They also have less external organization for passports and boarding passes.

7. The "Laptop-On-Bottom" Test

Turn the bag upside down. If you feel the laptop bumping, the sleeve is too shallow. The laptop sleeve should be deep enough that the computer is fully enclosed and secured by a strap. Many bags fail this test.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my 22-inch bag fit on a Ryanair flight?

No. Ryanair allows 55 x 40 x 20 cm (21.6 x 15.7 x 7.8 inches). You need a bag that is 7.8 inches deep. Most standard carry-ons are 9 inches deep. You'll need a smaller "personal item" size bag for Ryanair unless you pay for priority boarding.

How do I know if my laptop compartment is safe?

Check if the sleeve is padded on the bottom or if it's suspended (raised off the base of the bag). If the sleeve touches the bottom, the laptop will hit the floor when you set the bag down. The Travelpro and Briggs & Riley both pass this test.

Is a hardside or softside better for international travel?

Softside is better. It's more flexible. If the overhead bin is slightly smaller than expected, you can push the bag in. A hardside bag won't flex. It will just get stuck and you'll have to gate-check it.

Can I take a 17-inch laptop in these bags?

No. Almost all carry-on bags are designed for laptops up to 15.6 inches. A 17-inch gaming laptop (which is usually wider than 16 inches) won't fit in the sleeves on these bags. You'll need to carry it in a separate sleeve.

What is the actual weight limit for carry-on luggage?

It varies. Lufthansa and Air France allow 8kg (17.6 lbs). Most Asian carriers like Singapore Airlines allow 7kg (15.4 lbs). US carriers don't have a weight limit for carry-on bags, but they do have size limits. Always check your specific airline before packing.
