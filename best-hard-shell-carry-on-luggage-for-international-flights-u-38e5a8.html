International airlines are brutal with carry-on sizes. A bag that fits fine on Delta or American will get flagged at the gate in Frankfurt or London. The 62 linear inch standard for checked bags doesn't apply here. Most international carriers cap carry-ons at 45 linear inches (115 cm total). This review focuses on hard shell cases that stay under 60 linear inches—but honestly, you should be aiming for the 45-inch mark to avoid gate checks.

I tested five hard shell carry-ons over the past month on actual flights, not just in my hallway. I measured them, weighed them, jammed them into overhead bins on a Boeing 737 and an Airbus A320, and dragged them through cobblestone streets in Lisbon. Here’s what matters and what doesn’t.

What to Look For in a Hard Shell Carry-On

Before we get to the reviews, here are the non-negotiables you need to check before buying.

Size: The 55cm Rule

Most international airlines use the IATA recommendation of 55 x 35 x 25 cm (21.5 x 13.5 x 9.8 inches). But budget airlines like Ryanair and Wizz Air use 55 x 40 x 20 cm (21.5 x 15.5 x 7.8 inches). That 5 cm difference in depth is massive. If you buy a bag with 9 inches of depth, you'll be paying €70 at the gate to check it. Look for bags that are under 9 inches deep when compression zippers are closed.

Weight: The 7kg Problem

Hard shells are heavier than soft bags. A typical polycarbonate case weighs 7 to 9 pounds empty. Many Asian and European carriers have a 7kg (15.4 lbs) carry-on weight limit. If your bag weighs 8 lbs empty, you only have 7 lbs for your clothes. That’s a single pair of jeans and a laptop. Look for bags under 6.5 lbs if you plan to fly on weight-restricted routes.

Material Matters

Not all hard shells are equal. You want polycarbonate or polypropylene. These flex and pop back after impact. ABS plastic is cheaper but cracks under stress. A bag that cracks is a bag that fails. Check the material before you buy—if it says ABS only, keep looking.

Wheels and Handles

You need dual spinner wheels (4 wheels). These roll sideways down the aisle and let you walk upright. Not all spinners are smooth—cheap ones rattle and wobble. The handle needs to be a telescopic aluminum tube, not a flimsy plastic one. A wobbly handle makes a 45-minute layover a nightmare.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "60 linear inches" mean for carry-ons?

Linear inches is the sum of height, width, and depth. 60 linear inches is the checked bag limit for most airlines. Carry-ons are typically limited to 45 linear inches (115 cm). This article focuses on bags under 60 inches, but you should aim for 45 inches or less to avoid gate checks.

Why do budget airlines have smaller size limits?

Ryanair and EasyJet use smaller sizers to force you to check bags and pay fees. If your bag is 1 cm too deep, you’ll pay €60 to check it at the gate. Always check the airline’s specific size limit before you fly.

Is a hard shell or soft shell better for international travel?

Hard shells protect fragile items like electronics and bottles. Soft shells have more external pockets and can be squished into overhead bins. For international flights with strict sizers, a hard shell with a compression zipper is better—it keeps your items safe and maintains its shape.

Can I take a hard shell carry-on on a plane?

Yes, as long as it fits the airline’s size limit. The bags in this review all fit standard international overhead bins. Check the specific dimensions for your airline before you book.

If you fly international routes more than twice a year and want a bag that never fails you, buy the Briggs & Riley Baseline Essential. It’s expensive, but the lifetime warranty covers everything, including airline damage. Choose based on your airline, not your Instagram feed.
