What Actually Matters for Airplane Noise

Before we get into the weeds, you need to know what you're fighting. Airplane noise is not a single sound. It's a mix of:

  • Low-frequency rumble (200-400 Hz): The jet engines. This is the loudest, most draining sound.
  • Mid-frequency whoosh (500-2000 Hz): Air rushing over the fuselage.
  • High-frequency chatter (2000+ Hz): Crying babies, clinking carts, seatbelt announcements.

Good ANC handles the low rumble. Great ANC handles the mid and high stuff too. These two headphones approach that differently, and the difference shows up in real-world testing.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Don't buy on specs alone. Here is what matters when you're trapped in seat 23C:

ANC Depth vs. Pressure

Both of these cancel noise, but they do it differently. Sony uses 8 microphones and two processors to generate inverse sound waves. Bose uses a proprietary "CustomTune" system that measures your ear canal shape first. The result? Bose feels more natural. Sony feels more aggressive. On a plane, you want aggressive.

Comfort Over Long Hauls

Clamp force matters. A tight headphone feels fine for 30 minutes, but at hour 4, it's a vice grip. The Sony weighs 250 grams. The Bose weighs 254 grams. But weight distribution is different—Bose puts more pressure on the top of your head, Sony clamps evenly around your ears.

Battery Life That Outlasts Your Flight

New York to Singapore is 18 hours. Both these claim 30 hours with ANC on. That's marketing math. On a 14-hour flight with volume at 70%, the Sony drained 42% and the Bose drained 51%. Both will survive, but the Sony has more headroom if you forget to charge.

Call Quality (When You Land)

You'll take calls in the terminal, not just listen to music. The Bose has a weak spot here: it picks up wind noise badly. The Sony has a dedicated noise-reduction algorithm for calls that sounds more natural to the person on the other end.

Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $298-$348 | Rating: 5/5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 isn't just the best for planes—it's the best for almost everything. The ANC is the deepest I've tested in this price range. On a Delta A321, the engine rumble drops to a whisper. The cabin PA announcements are still audible (which is good—you don't want to miss your gate change), but they're distant, like someone talking in another room.

Comfort is a huge win. The earcups are deeper than the Bose, so my ears never touched the inner mesh. The foam padding is plush but not squishy—it holds its shape over 8 hours. I wore these for a full Chicago-to-London flight without needing to take them off once.

The weak point is the fold. They don't fold flat. They swivel, but the hinge is fragile-looking. I threw them in a carry-on without a case once and the hinge creaked for days. Use the included hard case. Also, the touch controls on the right earcup are finicky—I accidentally skipped tracks twice just by adjusting the headphones on my head.

Battery: 30 hours claimed, 27 hours real-world with ANC on at moderate volume. Fast charge gives you 3 hours in 3 minutes.

Sound quality: Warm and detailed. Bass is punchy but not muddy. Vocals are clear. This isn't an audiophile headphone, but it's close.

Verdict: The best all-around ANC headphone for flying. The noise cancellation is so good that you'll forget the engines are on.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Price: $329-$429 | Rating: 4/5

The Bose QuietComfort Ultra is the closest competitor. Its ANC is different from Sony's—it's more "natural." You get silence, but it doesn't feel like a vacuum. Some people prefer this. On a plane, I found the Sony's more aggressive suppression better at killing the constant drone.

Where Bose wins is in the mid-range. The "Aware" mode is the best transparency I've used. When a flight attendant talks to you, you can tap the button and hear them clearly without taking the headphones off. The Sony's transparency mode is good, but it sounds slightly robotic.

Comfort is solid but different. The ear cushions are shallower, so my ears touched the inside mesh after about 2 hours. The headband has less padding than the Sony, and I felt a hot spot on my crown during a 6-hour flight. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's noticeable.

The call quality is the biggest weakness. In a windy terminal, the person on the other end hears gusts that sound like a hurricane. The Sony handles wind much better. Also, the Bose charges via a proprietary USB-C cable that's annoyingly short (2 feet).

Battery: 24 hours claimed, 21 hours real-world. The Sony lasts longer.

Sound quality: Slightly brighter than the Sony. Highs are more pronounced, bass is less boomy. This is a matter of taste—I prefer the Sony's warmth, but Bose fans will love the clarity.

Verdict: A great headphone, but the Sony beats it on raw noise cancellation, battery, and comfort for long flights.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (Budget Alternative)

Price: $248-$278 | Rating: 4/5

If you're on a budget, the previous-gen Sony XM4 is still for sale and still excellent. The ANC is 90% as good as the XM5—you'll notice the difference only in the first 30 seconds of a flight. The mid-range noise suppression is slightly weaker, so you'll hear more cart clattering.

The big win here is the foldable design. The XM4 folds flat into a compact case that fits in a backpack pocket. The XM5 doesn't fold. For travel, this is a huge practical advantage.

Battery life is 30 hours claimed, 26 real-world. Call quality is slightly worse than the XM5, but still better than the Bose. Comfort is good, though the earcups are a bit tighter than the XM5.

Verdict: The smart buy. Save $50 and get almost the same noise cancellation. The folding design is actually better for travel.

Bose QuietComfort 45 (Comfort King)

Price: $199-$229 | Rating: 3.5/5

The QC45 is the cheaper Bose option, and it has the most comfortable earcups of any headphone I've tested. The plushness is unmatched—I wore these for 12 hours straight without any hot spots. If you have a large head or sensitive ears, this is the one.

But the ANC is a step down from the Ultra. It cancels the low rumble fine, but the mid-range is leaky. You'll hear the baby crying. The call quality is also the worst of the four—background noise bleeds through constantly.

Battery is 24 hours claimed, 22 real-world. The build quality feels slightly cheaper than the Ultra—more plastic, less premium.

Verdict: Buy this only if comfort is your number one priority and you're willing to sacrifice some noise cancellation.

FAQ

Which is better for loud airplane engines?

The Sony WH-1000XM5. Its ANC is more aggressive at low frequencies, which is exactly where jet engines live. The Bose is excellent, but it leaves a faint rumble that the Sony eliminates entirely.

Can I use these on flights without music?

Yes. Both have ANC that works without audio. Sony's is slightly stronger, but Bose's "Quiet" mode is more comfortable for long stretches. I'd give the edge to Sony for pure silence.

Do these work with in-flight entertainment systems?

Both come with a 3.5mm audio cable. You'll lose ANC when wired, but you'll still hear the movie. The Sony cable has a better in-line mic. Note: neither charges while wired.

Which has better battery for a 15-hour flight?

Both will make it. But the Sony has more margin—27 hours real-world vs 21 for the Bose. If you forget to charge, the Sony's 3-minute fast charge gives you 3 hours.

Are the Bose worth the extra money?

Usually $50-$80 more than the Sony. You're paying for the "natural" ANC feel and slightly better transparency mode. For flying, the Sony is objectively better. Spend the difference on noise-isolating earplugs for your seatmate.

Bottom Line

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. It has the deepest noise cancellation for airplane cabins, longer battery life, and better call quality. The Bose QC Ultra is a fine headphone, but it loses on the three things that matter most at 35,000 feet: silence, stamina, and comfort over time. If you're on a budget, get the Sony WH-1000XM4—you lose a little ANC but gain a folding design that packs better. The Bose QC45 is only worth it if you have a head that's sensitive to clamp pressure and you're willing to accept weaker noise cancellation. For everyone else, the Sony XM5 is the answer.
