Who This Is For (And the Problem We're Solving)

This is for anyone whose face looks like a glazed donut by 10 AM. You have oily skin, you hate the look of foundation, but you also want some coverage to minimize the look of large pores. You need a product that does three jobs at once: protects you from the sun, blurs your pores, and controls oil without turning into a cakey mess.

The problem? Most tinted sunscreens are greasy. They slide around, settle into your pores, and make you look shinier than a freshly waxed car. I spent 6 weeks testing the top contenders on the market. I wore each one for a full workday, checked shine at the 2-hour and 6-hour marks, and took close-up photos to see how they handled pore blurring.

What to Look For: A Quick Buyer's Guide

Before you buy, check the ingredient list and the label. Not all tinted sunscreens are created equal. Here is what matters for oily, porous skin.

1. The Base: Dimethicone is Your Friend

Look for dimethicone or silica in the first five ingredients. These are silicone-based powders that sit on top of the skin, filling in the dips of your pores and absorbing oil. They are the secret to that "blurred" look. If the first ingredient is a heavy oil like coconut or jojoba, put it back on the shelf.

2. SPF Level and Filters

You need SPF 30 or higher for daily wear. But check the filter type. Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are physical blockers—they sit on top of the skin and are less likely to clog pores. Chemical filters like avobenzone can feel greasier. For oily skin, I prefer a mineral-based formula or a hybrid that uses a lot of silica.

3. The Finish: Look for "Matte" or "Natural"

Do not buy anything labeled "Dewy" or "Glow." That is marketing speak for "oily." Look for the words "matte," "oil-free," or "non-comedogenic." A natural finish is okay if you plan to set it with powder, but a true matte finish is your best bet for a no-fuss routine.

4. Shade Range

Tinted sunscreens are notorious for looking like chalk on darker skin tones. Check the shade range before you buy. If a brand only offers 2 shades, you will likely look like a ghost. Brands like EltaMD and Supergoop! have expanded their ranges, but you still need to check the undertone (neutral, pink, or yellow).

The 5 Best Tinted Sunscreens for Oily Skin & Large Pores

I tested these on my own combination skin. I live in a humid climate, so if they survive here, they will survive your commute. Here is the honest breakdown.

1. EltaMD UV Clear Tinted Broad-Spectrum SPF 46

The Holy Grail for a Reason

EltaMD UV Clear has been the dermatologist favorite for years, and it lives up to the hype. This is a lightweight, oil-free lotion that uses 9% zinc oxide. The star ingredient is niacinamide, which helps reduce the appearance of pores over time and controls oil production.

I wore the tinted version (Shade 2) for a 10-hour day. At the 2-hour mark, my T-zone was barely shiny. By hour 6, I had a natural glow—not a grease slick. It did not settle into my large cheek pores. It actually looks like a skin tint, giving you a "your skin but better" finish.

The catch: The shade range is limited. It comes in 3 shades (Fair, Light, Medium), and the undertones lean neutral. If you have deep skin or a very warm undertone, this may not match. It is also a bit pricey for a small 1.7 oz bottle.

  • Finish: Natural matte
  • Pore blurring: 9/10
  • Shine control: 8/10 (starts to glow by hour 5)

2. Supergoop! Mattescreen SPF 40

The Silicone Powerhouse

This is a different beast. Supergoop! Mattescreen is a lavender-tinted balm that goes on completely invisible. It is packed with silica and micro-powders that give you an instant, photoshopped blur effect. It feels like a primer, not a sunscreen.

I tested this on a sweaty day. It did not move. It did not get greasy. My pores looked like they had vanished. The downside? It is a balm, so you need to warm it up in your fingers before applying. If you rub it directly on your face, it can pill. Also, the tint is very sheer—do not expect any coverage for redness or dark spots.

The catch: It is not a moisturizer. If you have any dry patches, this will cling to them and look flaky. You need to prep with a lightweight moisturizer first.

  • Finish: True matte
  • Pore blurring: 10/10 (best in test)
  • Shine control: 9/10

3. La Roche-Posay Anthelios Clear Skin Dry Touch SPF 60

The Heavy Duty Option

La Roche-Posay's Dry Touch formula is specifically designed for oily skin. It uses a patented technology that absorbs oil with perlite and silica. The tinted version is a new addition to the lineup, and it is impressive.

This has the highest SPF on this list (SPF 60), making it great for outdoor days. The texture is a thin liquid that dries down to a powder-like finish. It blurs pores well, though not as effectively as the Supergoop!. The tint is a bit more pigmented than the EltaMD, offering light-medium coverage.

The catch: The tint is very yellow-leaning. It looked a bit off on my neutral-cool skin. Also, the "Dry Touch" technology can feel a little tight or drying by the end of the day. You will need a good cleanser to get it off.

  • Finish: Matte
  • Pore blurring: 7/10
  • Shine control: 10/10 (best for oil)

4. CeraVe Hydrating Mineral Sunscreen SPF 30 Tinted

The Budget-Friendly Option

CeraVe's tinted mineral sunscreen is a drugstore hero. It uses zinc oxide and titanium dioxide for broad-spectrum protection. It also contains niacinamide and ceramides to support your skin barrier.

This is a thicker formula than the others. It feels more like a moisturizer. It blends out well, but it leaves a slightly dewy finish that you will need to set with powder if you are very oily. I found that it did not emphasize my pores, but it did not blur them either. It just evened out my skin tone.

The catch: The shade range is a joke. It comes in two shades: one that is too pink and one that is too orange. It is very hard to get a good match. Also, the finish is not matte enough for my taste.

  • Finish: Natural Dewy
  • Pore blurring: 5/10
  • Shine control: 4/10 (you will need powder)

5. Australian Gold Botanical Tinted Mineral SPF 50

The Outdoor Sports Pick

Australian Gold is a cult favorite for a reason. This is a 100% mineral, water-resistant formula that is sweat-proof. It has a unique iron oxide tint that adapts to your skin tone reasonably well, and it dries down to a very dry, matte finish.

This is the best option if you are going to be sweating or swimming. I wore it on a hike, and it did not budge. It also has a subtle tint that evens out redness. However, the finish is almost too dry. It can look chalky or cakey if you apply too much, and it tends to settle into fine lines.

The catch: The tint is limited to a "medium" shade that looks ashy on deeper skin tones. It also has a strong sunscreen smell that takes a few minutes to fade.

  • Finish: Matte (almost powdery)
  • Pore blurring: 8/10
  • Shine control: 10/10

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I wear powder over these sunscreens?

Yes. If you are extra oily, a light dusting of translucent powder over the EltaMD or La Roche-Posay will extend your shine-free wear by a few hours. Avoid powder over the Supergoop! Mattescreen—it can cause pilling.

Do I still need a moisturizer underneath?

For the Supergoop! and La Roche-Posay, yes—they are drying. For the EltaMD and CeraVe, you can skip it if your skin is only oily, not dehydrated.

Do these clog pores?

All five are labeled non-comedogenic. However, the CeraVe and Australian Gold are thicker and might feel heavy for some. If you are prone to milia, stick to the thinner liquids like EltaMD or Supergoop!.

The Bottom Line

If you want the best all-rounder that protects, blurs, and controls oil without feeling like a mask, buy the EltaMD UV Clear Tinted. It is the closest thing to a skincare product that doubles as a makeup primer. If you have severely oily skin and prioritize invisibility over coverage, buy the Supergoop! Mattescreen—it is the ultimate pore blurrer. Skip the CeraVe if you are very oily; it is too dewy. The La Roche-Posay is your go-to for a beach day, but the yellow tint is a dealbreaker for cooler undertones. The Australian Gold is a steal for outdoor sports, but it lacks shade range. Start with the EltaMD; it is the safest bet for the most people.
