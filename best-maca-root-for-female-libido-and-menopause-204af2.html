What to Look for When Buying Maca Root

Not all maca is created equal. Here's what separates the good from the useless.

1. Gelatinized vs. Raw vs. Extract

Raw maca powder is hard on digestion. Most people get bloated and gassy. Gelatinized maca has the starches removed, making it easier on your stomach while keeping the active compounds. Maca extracts (high-ratio powders) are concentrated but often lose beneficial compounds during processing. My advice: stick with gelatinized or raw if you have a strong stomach.

2. Color Matters — Black vs. Red vs. Yellow

This isn't marketing fluff. Black maca is best for libido and energy. Red maca is better for prostate health in men and hormone balance in women. Yellow maca is the common all-rounder. For female libido, look for black maca or a blend that's mostly black.

3. Source and Processing

Maca grows in the high Andes of Peru. Quality brands source from small farms and test for heavy metals and pesticides. If a brand doesn't show third-party testing (like a Certificate of Analysis on their site), walk away.

4. Dosage

Clinical studies use 1,500 to 3,000 mg per day for libido and menopause. Anything below 1,000 mg is likely too weak to notice a difference. Check the label for active ingredient amount, not capsule count.

5. Additives and Fillers

You want maca and nothing else. Some brands add caffeine, herbs, or proprietary blends that dilute the maca. Avoid anything with "proprietary blend" — you don't know how much maca you're actually getting.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does maca take to work for libido?

Most women notice a difference in 2 to 6 weeks. It's not a quick fix. Your body needs time to adjust to the adaptogenic compounds. If you don't feel anything after 8 weeks, either the dose is too low or maca isn't right for your body.

Can maca help with hot flashes?

Yes. Studies show maca can reduce hot flash frequency by up to 40-50% in some women. It works by balancing estrogen levels, not by adding hormones — which is why it's a popular natural alternative to HRT. But results vary. Some women get complete relief, others see minimal change.

Are there any side effects?

Mostly digestive issues with raw powder. Some women report acne or changes in menstrual flow. If you have a thyroid condition, check with your doctor — maca contains goitrogens that can interfere with thyroid function in high doses. Also, if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, skip it.

What's the difference between maca and estrogen therapy?

Maca doesn't contain hormones. It works by supporting your endocrine system and helping your body balance its own hormones. HRT replaces estrogen directly. Maca is gentler but less predictable. For severe symptoms, HRT might be necessary. For mild to moderate symptoms, maca is worth trying first.

Can I take maca with my antidepressants or blood pressure meds?

Generally yes, but talk to your doctor. Maca can interact with MAOIs and may affect blood pressure in some people. Always disclose supplements to your physician.
