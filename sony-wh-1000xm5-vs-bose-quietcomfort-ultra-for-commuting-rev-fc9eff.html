But they handle the chaos of a commute very differently. I tested both for three weeks on my daily subway and bus route, plus a few flights, to see which one actually survives the grind.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Commuting

Before you drop $400, understand what separates a good commuter headphone from a desk-only one. Here are the five things I check first.

1. Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) Quality

Commutes are full of droning noises: subway motors, bus engines, HVAC systems. Look for headphones that handle low-frequency rumble well. High-pitched chatter is easier to block; the low stuff is what separates the best from the rest.

2. Comfort for Long Wears

You'll have these on for 45 minutes to 2 hours straight. Clamping force matters. So does weight. Anything over 300 grams gets noticeable after an hour. Pay attention to ear pad depth too — shallow pads press on your cartilage and cause hotspots.

3. Call Quality in Wind

This is the hidden killer. Walking to the station on a windy day, the person on the other end hears everything except you. Check for wind-reduction tech and multiple microphones. I test calls on a busy street corner, not in a quiet room.

4. Battery Life with ANC On

You don't want to charge these daily. Look for at least 24 hours with ANC active. Also check if they have quick-charge — 10 minutes of charging should get you at least 2-3 hours of playback.

5. Multipoint Bluetooth

If you switch between a work phone and personal phone, or a laptop and phone, multipoint is non-negotiable. It lets you stay connected to two devices at once. Both headphones here have it, but they handle it differently.

Product Reviews: The Real-World Test

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $329-$348 | Rating: 4.8/5 | Best for: Noise reduction and battery life

The XM5s are the quietest headphones I've ever worn on a subway. The ANC is freakishly good at canceling the low-frequency drone of train motors and the rumble of wheels on tracks. It doesn't eliminate voices entirely — nothing does — but it pushes them so far back you can focus on a podcast at 30% volume.

Comfort is a big win. At 250 grams, they're lighter than the Bose pair. The ear cups are deep and plush, and the clamping force is gentle. I wore them for a 3-hour train ride without needing to adjust them. The headband distributes weight evenly, so no hot spots on top of my head.

Battery life is class-leading: 30 hours with ANC on, 40 with it off. In three weeks of daily commutes, I charged them twice. The quick-charge feature gives you 3 hours of playback in 3 minutes of charging. That's saved me more than once.

Call quality is where the XM5s stumble slightly. The microphones handle voices well indoors, but on a windy sidewalk, the person on the other end hears a lot of whooshing. It's not terrible, but it's not Bose-level. Sony's wind-reduction mode helps a bit, but you have to toggle it manually in the app.

The touch controls work well — swipe to skip tracks, tap to pause. But they're sensitive; adjust the headphones and you might accidentally pause your music. I did this a dozen times in three weeks.

Tradeoffs: No IP rating, so rain and heavy sweat are risky. The case is larger than the Bose one, taking up more backpack space. And the touch controls can be finicky.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Price: $329-$429 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: Call quality and immersive sound

The QC Ultra headphones are Bose's flagship, and they nail the basics. The ANC is excellent — slightly better than the XM5s at canceling mid-frequency noise like human chatter and road noise. On a bus, the constant hum of traffic disappears almost completely.

But here's the catch: the ANC doesn't have the sheer force of the Sony pair on low-frequency rumble. On a subway, you'll hear more of the motor's growl with the Bose. It's still quiet, but the XM5s are a step ahead in this specific area.

Comfort is subjective. The Bose pair weighs 254 grams, nearly identical to the Sony. But the ear cups are shallower. If you have larger ears, they'll press against the inside of the cup. I have average-sized ears and felt mild pressure after 90 minutes. The clamping force is slightly stronger than the Sony, which keeps them secure during quick head turns but can feel tight on wider heads.

Call quality is the Bose's superpower. The microphone array is superb at isolating your voice from background noise. I walked down a busy street with a delivery truck idling nearby, and the person on the other end couldn't tell I was outside. The wind reduction is also better — less whooshing than the Sony.

Battery life is a disappointment: 24 hours with ANC on. That's 6 hours less than the Sony. In my testing, I got through 4 days of commutes before needing a charge. The quick-charge gives you 2.5 hours in 15 minutes, which is slower than Sony's. If you forget to charge overnight, you're in trouble.

The "immersive audio" mode is Bose's spatial audio feature. It's fun for music but drains the battery faster and doesn't do much for podcasts. I turned it on once and never used it again.

Tradeoffs: Shorter battery life, shallower ear cups, and a higher price if you buy at launch MSRP. The case is smaller and more pocketable, which is nice.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Budget Alternative)

Price: $248-$298 | Rating: 4.3/5 | Best for: Budget buyers who want 90% of the XM5's performance

The XM4s are the previous generation, and they're still fantastic. The ANC is slightly weaker than the XM5s — you'll hear a bit more rumble on trains — but it's still better than most headphones on the market. The ear cups are a bit shallower than the XM5s, but the overall comfort is solid.

Battery life is 30 hours with ANC, matching the XM5s. Call quality is similar — decent indoors, windy outside. The big difference is the design: the XM4s fold flat, while the XM5s don't. That makes them easier to pack in a backpack.

They're often $100 cheaper than the XM5s. If your budget is tight, this is the smart buy. You lose a little noise cancellation and the sleeker look, but not much else.

Tradeoffs: Slightly worse ANC, older Bluetooth chip, and the microphones pick up more wind noise than the newer models.

Bose QuietComfort 45 (The Travel Workhorse)

Price: $199-$249 | Rating: 4.0/5 | Best for: Frequent flyers who need reliable ANC on a budget

The QC45s are Bose's mid-range option. The ANC is very good, though not as aggressive as the QC Ultra or the XM5s. It handles airplane hum well, which makes them great for flights. On a subway, you'll notice more background noise than the flagship pairs.

Call quality is decent but not great. Wind noise is noticeable on walks. The ear cups are the same shallow design as the QC Ultra, so comfort for larger ears is a concern.

Battery life is 24 hours, same as the QC Ultra. They charge via USB-C, which is good. The build feels slightly less premium than the Ultra, but they're lighter at 240 grams.

If you can find them under $200, they're a steal. At full price, the XM4s are a better value.

Tradeoffs: No multipoint Bluetooth (only one device at a time), weaker ANC than the flagships, and a dated design.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has better noise canceling for trains?

The Sony WH-1000XM5. It's stronger at canceling the low-frequency rumble of train motors and wheels. The Bose QC Ultra is better at voices and mid-frequency noise.

Are the Bose QC Ultra worth the extra money?

Only if you take a lot of calls during your commute. The call quality is noticeably better. If you mostly listen to music or podcasts, the Sony XM5 is the better value at a lower price.

Can I wear these in the rain?

Neither has an IP rating. Light drizzle is probably fine, but heavy rain or sweat can damage them. Keep them in your bag if it's pouring.

Which is more comfortable for glasses wearers?

The Sony XM5s. The deeper ear cups create less pressure on the arms of your glasses. The Bose QC Ultra's shallow cups press harder against your head, which can cause sore spots behind your ears.

Do these work with iPhones and Androids?

Yes, both work with iOS and Android via Bluetooth. However, the Sony app on Android gives you more control over the EQ. On iOS, the Bose app feels more polished.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if your commute is mostly trains, subways, or buses. The ANC is the best on the market for low-frequency noise, the battery lasts 6 hours longer, and the deeper ear cups are more comfortable for longer wear. The only real weakness is call quality in wind, which matters if you're on the phone while walking. If you spend more time talking than listening, or if you walk on busy streets and need your voice to come through clearly, get the Bose QuietComfort Ultra instead — just be ready to charge more often. For budget buyers, the Sony WH-1000XM4 is the smart pick; it's nearly as good as the XM5 for $100 less.
