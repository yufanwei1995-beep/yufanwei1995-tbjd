What to Look For in a Budget Swim Smartwatch

Before you buy anything, understand these four specs. They make or break the swimming experience.

Water Resistance Rating (ATM vs IP)

Ignore IP68 ratings. They mean dust and shallow water only. Look for 5ATM or higher. This means the watch survived 50 meters of static water pressure. That's fine for pool swimming. If you dive or do open water, get 10ATM.

Also check for ISO 22810 certification. It's the standard for dive watches and guarantees water resistance, not just marketing hype.

Heart Rate Sensor Quality

Cheap optical sensors struggle underwater. Water scatters light, and the sensor gets confused by arm movement and turbulence. Look for watches with multi-channel sensors or a history of accurate swim HR tracking. If a brand doesn't publish swim HR accuracy data, assume it's mediocre.

Swim Mode Features

A true swim mode does three things: counts laps, detects stroke type (freestyle, breaststroke, backstroke), and tracks SWOLF (swimming efficiency score). If the watch only has a generic "activity" mode, skip it.

Battery Life

Swim tracking uses GPS and heart rate simultaneously, which drains batteries fast. Look for at least 7 days of typical use, or about 5 hours of continuous swim tracking. You don't want to charge this thing every night.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I wear these watches in a hot tub or sauna?

No. 5ATM ratings are for cold or room-temperature water. Hot water expands the seals and lets moisture in. Take the watch off before you hit the sauna.

How accurate is optical heart rate monitoring underwater?

It's less accurate than a chest strap, but the gap is closing. The Garmin Swim 2 was within 3 bpm in my tests. Cheaper watches like the Fitbit Charge 6 can be off by 10-20 bpm during intense intervals.

What does SWOLF mean?

It's a swimming efficiency score. It adds your stroke count to your time per lap. Lower is better. A SWOLF of 40 is elite, 50 is good, 60+ needs work.

Do I need GPS for pool swimming?

No. Pool swim tracking uses the length of the pool and your turns to calculate distance. GPS is only useful for open water swims, where you're swimming in a straight line and need actual distance tracking.
