Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before you buy, you need to know what actually matters for swimming. Not all "waterproof" watches are created equal.

1. Water Resistance Rating (Don't Skip This)

Look for 5 ATM or 10 ATM ratings. This means the watch can withstand pressures equivalent to 50 or 100 meters of depth. Anything less than 5 ATM is for rain and hand-washing only. For swimming, you want 5 ATM minimum.

2. Heart Rate Accuracy in Water

Optical heart rate sensors struggle in water because water refracts light. A watch with a dual-sensor or a tighter-fitting strap will perform better. For this review, I measured accuracy against a Polar H10 chest strap. A variance of +/- 5 bpm is acceptable for a budget watch.

3. Dedicated Swim Mode

You need a watch that knows you are swimming. A dedicated swim mode automatically detects strokes (freestyle, breaststroke, backstroke) and counts laps. Watches without this mode just track generic "activity" time, which is useless for swim training.

4. GPS vs. Pool Lengths

In a pool, GPS is useless. The watch uses the pool length you set (e.g., 25m) and an accelerometer to count turns. For open water, you need built-in GPS to track distance. Some budget watches have this, many don't. Decide which you need before buying.

5. Battery Life

Swim tracking with heart rate drains battery fast. Look for at least 7 days of typical use and at least 5 hours of continuous tracking with GPS and heart rate on. You don't want to charge this daily.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I wear these watches in saltwater?

Yes, all of them are rated for swimming in pools and open water. However, you should rinse them with fresh water after saltwater or chlorinated pool use. The salt crystals can corrode the charging contacts over time.

Why is my heart rate so high/low in the water?

Optical sensors use light to measure blood flow. Water reflects light differently, and the pressure of the water can squeeze the watch against your wrist. Wear the watch snugly—one finger tight—but not so tight that it cuts off circulation.

Do I need a chest strap for accurate swim heart rate?

For interval training where you are spiking your heart rate quickly, yes. Wrist sensors lag by 5-10 seconds. For steady-state swimming, the wrist sensor is fine. The watches here all support Bluetooth chest straps if you decide to upgrade later.

What is SWOLF?

It's a metric that combines stroke count and time. Lower is better. It measures efficiency. If you are just starting, ignore it. If you are training, it's a great way to see if your form is getting better.
