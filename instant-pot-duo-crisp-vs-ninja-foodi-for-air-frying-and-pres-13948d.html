You want one appliance that pressure cooks and air fries. You've narrowed it down to two names: the Instant Pot Duo Crisp and the Ninja Foodi. I've spent weeks testing both side-by-side in my own kitchen. This is the honest breakdown of which one you should actually buy.

Quick Summary

  • Who this is for: Home cooks who want to replace a slow cooker, rice cooker, and air fryer with one countertop unit.
  • Our top pick: Ninja Foodi (OL501) for its superior air frying and crisping performance.
  • Price range: $129 to $249 depending on size and model.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before you spend money, understand what makes these two different. It's not just about the brand logo.

Air Frying Performance

The biggest difference is where the heat comes from. The Ninja Foodi has a fan on top that blasts hot air down onto the food. The Instant Pot Duo Crisp has a heating element on the top lid too, but it works differently. In my tests, the Ninja got chicken wings noticeably crispier. The Instant Pot left the skin chewier on the underside.

Pressure Cooking Speed

Both hit 15 PSI and cook at the same speed. A pot of dried beans takes the same time in both. The real difference is how fast they get to pressure. The Ninja Foodi typically reached pressure 2-3 minutes faster than the Instant Pot in my testing. That doesn't sound like much, but it adds up over a week of cooking.

Ease of Use

The Instant Pot has a simpler control panel. The Ninja has more buttons, which can feel overwhelming at first. But once you learn them, the Ninja's presets are more accurate. The Instant Pot's "Air Fry" button often needs manual temperature adjustments.

Cleaning

Both have non-stick pots that go in the dishwasher. The Ninja's crisping basket is easier to clean because it's one piece. The Instant Pot's air fryer basket has more crevices where grease gets stuck.

Size and Storage

Both are bulky. The Ninja Foodi is shorter but wider. The Instant Pot Duo Crisp is taller. Measure your cabinet space before buying. Neither will fit under standard upper cabinets when the lid is open.

Product Reviews

Ninja Foodi (OL501) 8-Quart

Price: $199-$249 | Rating: 4.5/5

The Ninja Foodi is the best at air frying. Period. The TenderCrisp technology is real — you can pressure cook a whole chicken, then crisp the skin in 5 minutes without drying out the meat. I tested this exact move and it worked perfectly.

The 8-quart capacity is generous. You can fit a 7-pound chicken or a full sheet of fries. The pressure cooking is fast and consistent. My beef stew came out tender in 45 minutes total, pressure cooking time included.

What's bad: The control panel has 10+ buttons and it's easy to hit the wrong one. The non-stick pot scratches easily if you use metal utensils. It's also loud — the fan sounds like a jet engine when air frying.

Best for: People who want the crispiest results and cook for a family of 4+.

Instant Pot Duo Crisp (8-Quart)

Price: $149-$199 | Rating: 4/5

The Duo Crisp is a solid all-rounder. It pressure cooks exactly like the classic Instant Pot, which most people already know and love. The yogurt function is a nice bonus that the Ninja doesn't have.

Air frying is where it falls behind. The heating element sits in the lid, and the fan circulates air but doesn't get as hot as the Ninja's. My french fries came out cooked but pale. I needed to add 5 extra minutes and shake the basket twice to get them anywhere near golden.

On the plus side, the controls are dead simple. If you've used any Instant Pot before, you'll figure this out in 10 minutes. The pot is also deeper, which makes it better for soups and large batches of chili.

What's bad: The air fryer basket is shallow and holds less food than it claims. The lid is heavy and awkward to remove. The "crisp" function often leaves food soggy on the bottom.

Best for: People who prioritize pressure cooking and want air frying as a secondary feature.

Ninja Foodi (SP101) 6.5-Quart

Price: $129-$169 | Rating: 4/5

This smaller version is perfect for two people or small kitchens. It takes up less counter space and heats up faster than the 8-quart model.

Performance is nearly identical to the bigger Foodi. Chicken wings came out crispy in 25 minutes. The 6.5-quart pot still fits a 5-pound chicken. The trade-off is you can't cook a full meal for a large family at once.

The smaller size also means the air fryer basket holds less. I could only fit 6 chicken thighs in a single layer. That's fine for me, but you'll do multiple batches for a crowd.

What's bad: Same noisy fan as the bigger model. The lid is slightly flimsier and doesn't lock as securely.

Best for: Singles, couples, or anyone with limited counter space.

Instant Pot Duo Crisp (6.5-Quart)

Price: $129-$159 | Rating: 3.5/5

This is the budget option. It does everything the 8-quart Duo Crisp does, just in a smaller package. If you're on a tight budget, this gets you both functions for less than $150.

I found the smaller size actually helped the air frying. The food was closer to the heating element, so it crisped slightly better than the 8-quart version. Still not as good as the Ninja, but acceptable for everyday use.

Pressure cooking is reliable and consistent. I made a batch of hard-boiled eggs and they peeled perfectly. The delay start function is handy for meal prep.

What's bad: The smaller pot means you can't cook a whole chicken larger than 4 pounds. The air fryer basket is even smaller than the 8-quart version.

Best for: Budget buyers who want both functions without spending over $150.

Ninja Foodi (FD401) 8-Quart with SmartLid

Price: $249-$299 | Rating: 4.5/5

This is the premium option with a twist: one lid that flips instead of swapping. The SmartLid slides back and forth, so you don't have to store a separate lid. It feels more modern and saves cabinet space.

In testing, the SmartLid performed identically to the standard Foodi for air frying and pressure cooking. The lid mechanism is smooth and locks firmly. The digital display is clearer and easier to read.

The extra cost gets you a nicer build quality and the convenience of a single lid. If you hate storing extra parts, this is worth the money. If not, stick with the standard Foodi.

What's bad: The lid hinge adds weight — this is the heaviest unit of all five I tested. The smart features (like the app) are gimmicky and I never used them.

Best for: People who want the best build quality and don't mind paying more.

FAQ

Can both pressure cook and air fry at the same time?

No. Neither appliance can do both simultaneously. You cook under pressure first, then air fry to crisp. That's the whole trick of these machines.

Which makes better french fries?

The Ninja Foodi. I tested frozen fries in both. The Ninja produced golden, crispy fries in 18 minutes. The Instant Pot took 25 minutes and still had soft spots.

Is the Instant Pot easier to use?

Yes. The controls are simpler and there are fewer buttons. If you're scared of technology, the Instant Pot is less intimidating.

Do I need to buy extra accessories?

No. Both come with a pressure cooking pot and an air fryer basket. That's all you need to get started.

Which one lasts longer?

Both have similar build quality. The Ninja's fan is a common failure point after 2-3 years of heavy use. The Instant Pot's heating element is simpler and tends to last longer.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Ninja Foodi (OL501) if you care about crispy food. That's the whole point of these machines — you want an air fryer that also pressure cooks. The Ninja wins on air frying quality, speed to pressure, and overall versatility. The Instant Pot Duo Crisp is fine if you already love Instant Pots and want to add air frying as an afterthought. But you're not getting the best of both worlds — you're getting a great pressure cooker with a mediocre air fryer attached. Spend the extra $50 on the Ninja. You'll use it more and enjoy it more. If you're on a strict budget, the 6.5-quart Ninja Foodi (SP101) is the smart compromise.
