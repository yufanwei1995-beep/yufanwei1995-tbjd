Eczema is miserable for babies and parents. The itching. The sleepless nights. The constant worry about what's touching their skin.

Your baby spends 12-16 hours a day on their mattress. If it's made with polyurethane foam, vinyl covers, or chemical flame retardants, it could be making things worse. Heat and sweat trigger eczema flares, and synthetic materials trap both.

Here's what actually matters when shopping for a crib mattress for eczema-prone skin, plus honest reviews of the five best options I've tested with real families.

What to Look for in a Non-Toxic Crib Mattress for Eczema

Skip the marketing fluff. Focus on these four things:

1. The Cover Material

This is the only thing touching your baby's skin. Look for organic cotton or a food-grade polyethylene (PE) cover. Both are safe, but they serve different purposes:

  • Organic cotton: Breathable and soft, but it absorbs moisture. If your baby sweats or spits up, the wet fabric sits against their skin.
  • Food-grade PE: Waterproof without vinyl or PVC. It's a thin, flexible plastic that blocks allergens, dust mites, and liquids completely. It's the best choice for severe eczema.

2. The Core Material

Most cheap mattresses use polyurethane foam. It's made from petroleum and off-gasses volatile organic compounds (VOCs). Not good for any baby, especially one with skin issues.

Better options:

  • Organic cotton (GOTS-certified): Natural, breathable, and fire-resistant without chemical additives.
  • Organic wool: Naturally flame-resistant and moisture-wicking. But wool is a common allergen. Skip it if your baby has a wool sensitivity.
  • Food-grade PE foam: Yes, it's still foam, but it's made from polyethylene instead of polyurethane. Much lower off-gassing.

3. Certifications

Don't trust labels that say "green" or "natural." Look for third-party certifications:

  • GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard): Confirms organic fibers
  • GREENGUARD Gold: Confirms low chemical emissions
  • MADE SAFE: Screens for toxic ingredients
  • CertiPUR-US: Confirms foam is free of heavy metals and formaldehyde

4. Firmness

All crib mattresses sold in the US must meet the federal firmness standard. Don't sacrifice safety for comfort. A firm mattress is non-negotiable for preventing SIDS.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use a mattress cover for eczema?

Yes, but make sure it's the right kind. Use a 100% organic cotton fitted sheet washed in fragrance-free detergent. Avoid vinyl mattress protectors — they trap heat and sweat. If your mattress has a food-grade PE cover already, you don't need an additional protector.

How often should I wash the sheets for eczema-prone skin?

Every 2-3 days, minimum. Dust mites and sweat build up fast. Wash in hot water (130°F or above) to kill dust mites. Use a gentle, fragrance-free detergent. Skip fabric softeners — they leave a residue that irritates skin.

Is organic cotton or food-grade PE better for eczema?

For severe eczema, food-grade PE is better. It's fully waterproof and blocks allergens completely. Organic cotton is breathable but absorbs moisture and needs more frequent washing. If your baby has mild eczema, either works.

Do I need a firm mattress for eczema?

Yes. Firmness is for SIDS prevention, not eczema. All crib mattresses sold in the US meet federal firmness standards. Don't add soft toppers — they increase suffocation risk and trap heat.

Can wool trigger eczema?

Yes, in some babies. Wool is a common allergen. If your baby has a wool sensitivity, avoid mattresses with wool cores or covers. The Avocado has a wool layer, so choose the Naturepedic or Newton instead.
