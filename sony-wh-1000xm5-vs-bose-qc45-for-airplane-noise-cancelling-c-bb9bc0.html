Quick Verdict: For Frequent Flyers, This Matters

If you live on planes, this is the comparison that matters. The Sony WH-1000XM5 and Bose QC45 are the two most popular premium noise-cancelling headphones for travel. I’ve spent 40+ hours wearing both on actual flights, in airport lounges, and on noisy buses. Here’s the short version:

  • Who this is for: Frequent flyers, road warriors, and anyone who needs silence in a metal tube at 35,000 feet.
  • Our top pick for airplane noise: Sony WH-1000XM5 — it cancels more engine roar and low-frequency drone than the Bose QC45.
  • Price range: Both typically sell between $250 and $350 depending on sales. The Bose often drops to $199 during deals, making it the value pick.

But it’s not a clean sweep. The Bose QC45 is lighter, more comfortable for small heads, and has a better microphone for phone calls. You need to pick your poison.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Airplane Noise

Before we dive into the two heavyweights, let’s talk about what you should look for. Not all noise cancelling is created equal.

1. ANC Type: Feedforward vs Hybrid

Hybrid ANC (which both of these have) uses mics inside and outside the ear cups. It adapts to your environment in real-time. This is non-negotiable for planes. The older feedforward systems just can't keep up with sudden pressure changes.

2. Low-Frequency Cancelling

Airplane engines produce a constant low-frequency rumble, usually between 100-300 Hz. The best ANC headphones attack this range aggressively. If a pair of headphones only cancels high-pitched sounds like voices, they'll fail on a plane. Look for specs or reviews that mention "low-frequency" performance.

3. Clamping Force and Weight

You'll wear these for 5+ hours. A headphone that weighs 300 grams might feel fine for 30 minutes but give you a headache by hour three. Clamping force matters too. Too tight, and your ears will ache. Too loose, and you'll lose the acoustic seal, which kills the bass response.

4. Battery Life with ANC On

Long-haul flights mean 10-14 hours of use. You need at least 20 hours of real-world battery life. Both of these deliver, but check if they have fast-charge features for layovers.

5. Multipoint Bluetooth

If you're juggling a laptop and a phone, you want multipoint. It lets you connect to two devices at once. The Sony has it. The Bose QC45 does not. That's a dealbreaker for some.

6. Carrying Case Size

This sounds silly, but a bulky case eats precious carry-on space. The Sony XM5 case is flat and slim. The Bose QC45 case is a hard-shell clamshell that's slightly deeper. Both fit in a backpack, but the Bose takes more room.

The Contenders: Sony WH-1000XM5 vs Bose QC45

Sony WH-1000XM5

Weight: 250g | Battery: 30 hours (ANC on) | Bluetooth: 5.2 with multipoint | Drivers: 30mm

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the current king of noise cancelling. The big change from the older XM4 is the design — Sony ditched the folding hinge and went with a slimmer, one-piece headband. This makes them feel more premium, but it also means they don't fold flat for storage.

How they perform on a plane: The ANC is genuinely a step above the Bose. On a 737 engine drone, the Sony reduces the rumble to a barely audible whisper. You can hear the cabin PA announcement, but it sounds like it's coming from another room. The low-frequency cancellation is aggressive, and it doesn't let up over time. There's no "ANC fatigue" where your ears feel pressure.

The 30mm drivers produce a warmer, more detailed sound. Bass is punchy without being muddy. Podcasts and audiobooks sound clear. The touch controls on the right ear cup are intuitive — swipe to skip, tap to pause. They work even with gloves on, which is handy on a cold plane.

The tradeoffs: The microphone quality is average. On a call, the other person will hear you fine, but they'll also hear your airplane cabin noise in the background. The ear cups are slightly shallower than the Bose. If you have large ears, they might touch the inside padding after a few hours. And they don't fold — so the case is long and flat, not compact.

Final rating: 4.8/5 — Best ANC, great battery, but call quality is mediocre.

Bose QuietComfort 45

Weight: 238g | Battery: 24 hours (ANC on) | Bluetooth: 5.1 (no multipoint) | Drivers: 40mm

The Bose QC45 is the lightest, most comfortable headphone in this class. At 238 grams, it's barely noticeable on your head. The clamping force is gentle — you can wear these for 10 hours and forget they're there.

How they perform on a plane: The ANC is very good, but it's a half-step behind the Sony. The Bose cancels the same engine rumble, but you'll hear a faint hum at the edges. It's not annoying — it's just there. The Bose excels at cancelling voices and mid-frequency sounds. If you're sitting next to a chatty person, the Bose will drown them out more effectively than the Sony.

The 40mm drivers deliver a more neutral, flat sound. It lacks the bass punch of the Sony, but it's more accurate for classical music and acoustic tracks. Vocals are crisp. The physical buttons (not touch) are easier to use blind — you can feel for them without fumbling.

The tradeoffs: The lack of multipoint is a big miss. You have to manually disconnect from your laptop to answer a call on your phone. The battery life is 24 hours, which is still enough for a transatlantic flight, but the Sony beats it by six hours. And the microphone is actually better than the Sony — the Bose QC45 has a clearer, more natural voice pickup.

Final rating: 4.5/5 — More comfortable, better calls, but less ANC power.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Weight: 254g | Battery: 24 hours (ANC on) | Bluetooth: 5.3 with multipoint | Drivers: 40mm

The Bose QC Ultra is the premium alternative if you want the Bose comfort but need better ANC. It's $429 retail, but it's been on sale for $329 recently. It adds spatial audio (which is gimmicky on a plane) but more importantly, it matches the Sony XM5 in noise cancelling.

How they perform on a plane: The ANC is noticeably better than the QC45. It's at parity with the Sony XM5 — the engine drone is gone. The key difference is the fit. The Ultra uses the same comfortable headband as the QC45, so you get that all-day comfort with top-tier noise cancelling.

The tradeoffs: It's heavy for a Bose at 254 grams. The spatial audio feature is useless on a plane — it makes music sound like it's in a giant empty room. And the price is hard to swallow when the Sony XM5 is usually $50 cheaper.

Final rating: 4.5/5 — The best of both worlds, but expensive.

Sony WH-1000XM4

Weight: 254g | Battery: 30 hours (ANC on) | Bluetooth: 5.0 with multipoint | Drivers: 40mm

This is the previous-gen Sony that still sells well because it folds flat. If you travel with a small bag, the XM4 is a smarter buy than the XM5. It has 95% of the same ANC performance — the difference is barely audible on a plane.

How they perform on a plane: The ANC is excellent. It's a hair behind the XM5 and QC Ultra, but ahead of the QC45. The folding hinge means the case is 40% smaller than the XM5 case, which is a huge win for carry-on space.

The tradeoffs: The plastic headband creaks. The ear cups are smaller than the XM5. And the touch controls are less responsive. But at $228 on sale, it's the best value here.

Final rating: 4.5/5 — Best value for frequent flyers.

Bose QuietComfort 35 II

Weight: 240g | Battery: 20 hours (ANC on) | Bluetooth: 4.1 (no multipoint) | Drivers: 40mm

This is the old workhorse. It's been replaced by the QC45, but you can still find it refurbished for $150. Honestly, unless you're on a tight budget, skip it. The ANC is a generation behind — it cancels a decent amount of engine noise but lets in more high-frequency chatter.

How they perform on a plane: It's passable. The ANC is about 70% as good as the QC45. You'll still hear the cabin buzz. The battery life is 20 hours, which is fine for most flights. But the Bluetooth 4.1 is ancient — it'll drop connections in crowded airports.

Final rating: 3.5/5 — Only if you're desperate and cheap.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has better noise cancelling on a plane?

The Sony WH-1000XM5 wins. It cancels more low-frequency engine roar. The Bose QC45 is close but lets in a faint hum. If you're on a noisy 737 vs a quiet A350, the difference is more noticeable.

Are the Bose QC45 more comfortable than the Sony XM5?

Yes. The QC45 is 12 grams lighter and has a gentler clamping force. The Sony XM5 has shallower ear cups, which can bother people with larger ears. If comfort is your top priority, choose the Bose.

Do I need the Bose QC Ultra instead?

Only if you want Bose comfort with Sony-level ANC. The QC Ultra costs more, but it's the best of both. If you can find it under $350, grab it.

Which is better for calls on a plane?

The Bose QC45. Its microphone picks up your voice clearly and filters out cabin noise better than the Sony XM5. The Sony sounds hollow and distant on calls.

Can I use these on a plane without the ANC on?

Yes, both have an "aware" or "transparency" mode. But on a plane, you'll want ANC on. The passive isolation alone isn't enough to block engine noise.

Bottom Line: Which One Should You Buy?

If you’re a frequent flyer who wants the quietest possible experience, buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. It has the best airplane noise cancelling, longer battery life, and multipoint Bluetooth. The mediocre microphone is the only real flaw, but if you're mostly listening, not talking, it doesn't matter.

If you fly occasionally, make lots of calls, or have sensitive ears, buy the Bose QC45 — especially if you catch it on sale under $250. It's lighter, more comfortable, and has a better mic. You'll give up a little noise cancelling, but you'll gain all-day wearability.

There's no wrong choice between these two. But if you're reading this and you're already on a plane with a crying baby two rows ahead, you'll wish you'd bought the Sony.
