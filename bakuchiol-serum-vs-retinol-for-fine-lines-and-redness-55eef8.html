Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before you buy anything, understand the core difference. Retinol is a vitamin A derivative that works by speeding up cell turnover. It's proven — decades of research back it up. But it's also a known irritant. Bakuchiol is a plant-derived compound (from the babchi plant) that targets the same anti-aging pathways through different mechanisms. The research on bakuchiol is newer but promising — one 2019 study in the British Journal of Dermatology found it comparable to retinol for photoaging with significantly less irritation.

Here's what to check before you buy:

  • Concentration: Retinol is effective at 0.1%–1%. Bakuchiol needs to be higher — 0.5%–2% — to see results. Anything less than 0.5% bakuchiol is basically a moisturizer with a fancy name.
  • Formulation matters more than the active: A 0.5% retinol in a well-buffered, soothing base beats a 1% retinol in a cheap base. Look for niacinamide, ceramides, or centella alongside the active.
  • Encapsulation: For retinol, encapsulated forms release slowly and reduce irritation. If you have redness, seek out encapsulated retinol or retinaldehyde (a step closer to retinoic acid but gentler).
  • Start low, go slow: Plan to use either product 2 nights a week for 2 weeks, then 3 nights, then every other night. If you skip this ramp-up, you will regret it.
  • Redness-specific ingredients: If redness is your main concern, pair either active with azelaic acid or centella in your routine. The active treats lines; the soothing ingredient treats the redness.

FAQ

Can I use bakuchiol and retinol together?

Yes, and it's actually a smart strategy. Use retinol at night and bakuchiol in the morning. Or alternate nights. The bakuchiol supports the retinol's effects while the soothing properties help counteract irritation. Naturium does this in one bottle, which is convenient.

How long until I see results?

Retinol shows visible fine line improvement in 4–8 weeks. Bakuchiol takes longer — usually 8–12 weeks. Neither is a quick fix. You need to commit for at least 3 months before judging.

Will bakuchiol cause a "purge" like retinol?

No. The "purge" (breakouts in the first few weeks) happens because retinol speeds up cell turnover, pushing out clogged pores. Bakuchiol works more gently and rarely causes purging. If you break out with bakuchiol, you're likely reacting to another ingredient in the formula.

Can I use either during pregnancy?

Retinol is not recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding. Bakuchiol is generally considered safer, but research is limited. Talk to your OB-GYN before using either.

Do I need sunscreen?

Yes. Both ingredients increase photosensitivity. If you skip SPF, you're undoing all your hard work and potentially making redness worse. Use SPF 30+ every single day.
