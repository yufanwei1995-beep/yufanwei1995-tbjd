You are on a crowded subway. Someone is watching TikTok without headphones. The brakes screech. You just want silence. Both the Sony WH-1000XM5 and the Bose QuietComfort 45 promise to deliver that. But they go about it very differently.

I spent two weeks wearing both on my daily commute—30 minutes of bus, 20 minutes of train, and a few flights. Here is the honest breakdown of which one you should actually buy.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look for in Commuting Headphones

Before diving into the reviews, you need to know what matters on a commute. Not all noise cancelling is the same. Here is the checklist I use.

Noise Cancellation Type

Look at whether the ANC targets low frequencies (engine hum, train rumble) or high frequencies (voices, brakes). For commuting, you need both, but low-frequency blocking is non-negotiable. Check if the headphone has adaptive ANC that changes with your environment.

Comfort Over 2+ Hours

Commutes get long. Clamping force matters. Too tight and your ears ache after 30 minutes. Too loose and the seal breaks, killing bass and ANC. Look for weight under 260 grams and plush ear pads that don't trap heat.

Battery Life and Charging Speed

You will forget to charge. A quick-charge feature is a lifesaver. Look for at least 24 hours of playback. If the headphone can give you 3 hours of playback from a 10-minute charge, that is a huge win. Also check if it charges via USB-C—no one carries micro-USB in 2026.

Call Quality in Wind and Noise

You will take calls on the street. Wind noise ruins everything. Look for microphones that are placed outside the ear cup and have some form of wind reduction technology. A headphone that sounds great for music but terrible for calls is a dealbreaker for commuters.

Portability and Build

Does it fold? The Sony XM5 does not fold flat. That is a real issue for cramming into a backpack. Check the carrying case size. Also look for a sturdy headband and swivel joints that won't snap after a year of abuse.

Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5 | $298-$348 | 5/5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the best noise cancelling headphone you can buy for a commute. Period. The new V1 processor and eight microphones combine to do something remarkable: it silences the world.

On a bus, the engine drone disappears completely. On a train, the track rumble is reduced to a whisper. The real magic is with voices. The XM5 targets mid-to-high frequencies better than the previous XM4, so a crying baby or loud talker is muffled to a distant murmur. It is not perfect, but it is 20% better than the Bose at this specific task.

Comfort is excellent. The ear cups are deeper and softer than the XM4, so your ears don't touch the driver. The clamp force is moderate—not too tight, not loose. At 250 grams, it is light enough for a three-hour flight without neck strain.

Battery life is rated at 30 hours with ANC on. In my testing, I got 28 hours with volume at 70%. The quick charge gives you 3 hours of playback from a 3-minute charge. That is faster than the Bose.

Call quality is a weak point. The XM5 uses bone conduction and multiple mics, but in a windy environment, the person on the other end will hear gusts. It is better than the XM4, but still not as clear as the Bose QC45 for calls.

The biggest downside is the shape. The XM5 does not fold. It comes in a hard case that is bulky. If you have a small backpack, this will take up a lot of room.

If you want the absolute best noise cancelling and don't mind a bigger case, buy the Sony. It is the king of silence.

Bose QuietComfort 45 | $278-$328 | 4.5/5

The Bose QC45 is the commuter's classic. It is lighter, more comfortable, and folds flat. The ANC is not as strong as the Sony, but it is close enough for most people.

On the same bus, the QC45 reduces engine noise almost as well as the Sony. The gap is noticeable only with voices and sudden high-pitched sounds. The Bose lets a bit more of the train's announcement through, which is actually useful if you are worried about missing your stop.

Comfort is where the Bose wins. At 238 grams, it is the lightest in this class. The ear pads are plush and the clamp force is very gentle. I wore them for four hours straight without any hot spots. If you have a large head or sensitive ears, this is the safer choice.

Battery life is 24 hours with ANC. That is six hours less than the Sony. The quick charge gives you 2.5 hours from a 15-minute charge, which is slower than the Sony's 3-minute trick. But 24 hours is still enough for a full week of commuting.

Call quality is the best in this comparison. The QC45 has a microphone system that handles wind exceptionally well. On a windy street, the person on the other end heard me clearly while the Sony picked up gusts. If you take a lot of calls during your commute, this is the one to get.

The folding design is a huge plus. The QC45 collapses into a small, rounded case that slips into any backpack pocket. This alone might be the reason to choose it over the Sony.

If you are a heavy phone caller or need something that packs small, the Bose QC45 is the better daily companion.

Sony WH-1000XM4 | $228-$278 | 4.5/5

The XM4 is the previous generation, but it is still a fantastic commuter headphone. It is cheaper than the XM5 and Bose, and it folds flat—which the XM5 does not.

In terms of ANC, the XM4 is about 85% as effective as the XM5. On a train, you will still get deep silence. The difference is only noticeable with high-pitched voices and sudden noises. For 60% of commuters, the XM4 is enough.

Battery life is 30 hours with ANC. Same as the XM5. The quick charge is the same 3-minute for 3 hours. Call quality is worse than both the XM5 and the Bose—wind noise is a real issue. But if you mostly listen to music, this is a non-issue.

The fit is tighter than the XM5. Some users report ear fatigue after 2 hours. The ear cups are shallower, so if you have big ears, they might touch the driver.

This is the budget pick if you want Sony's sound quality without the premium price.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra | $348-$398 | 4.5/5

The QC Ultra is Bose's premium option. It adds spatial audio and a slightly better ANC than the QC45. But for commuting, the extra $70 is hard to justify.

The ANC on the Ultra is marginally better than the QC45. It handles voices a bit better, but it is still not as good as the Sony XM5. The spatial audio is a gimmick for music—it makes everything sound like you are in a concert hall, which gets old after 10 minutes.

Comfort is identical to the QC45, which is excellent. Battery life is 24 hours with ANC. Call quality is the same as the QC45—which is to say, the best in the class.

The Ultra is only worth it if you want the spatial audio feature or if you find the QC45 on sale for the same price. Otherwise, save the money.

Anker Soundcore Space Q45 | $99-$129 | 4/5

This is the budget alternative. It costs a third of the Sony or Bose, and it gets you 80% of the way there. The ANC is decent for the price, handling engine rumble well but struggling with voices.

Battery life is a massive 50 hours with ANC off, 40 hours with ANC on. That beats everything else on this list. Call quality is average—fine for quiet rooms, poor for windy streets.

The build is plastic and creaks a bit when you stretch it. The ear cups are comfortable but shallow. It is heavy at 292 grams.

If you are on a tight budget and need something decent for a daily train ride, this is the best value. Just don't expect it to match the premium pairs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for blocking out airplane noise?

The Sony XM5. It has deeper low-frequency cancellation, which is what you need for the constant drone of jet engines. The Bose is good, but the Sony is noticeably quieter.

Are these headphones good for small heads or glasses?

Yes, but with caveats. The Bose QC45 is the most forgiving for glasses because of the light clamp force. The Sony XM5 is also fine, but if you wear thick frames, you might feel pressure after an hour.

Can I use these wired in case of dead battery?

Yes. Both the Sony and Bose have a 3.5mm jack that works passively. The Sony XM5 and XM4 sound fine wired. The Bose QC45 also works wired, but the sound is flatter without ANC on.

Which has better bass?

The Sony XM5 has more punch and sub-bass. The Bose QC45 has a more neutral, flat response. If you listen to hip-hop or EDM, the Sony is better. If you listen to podcasts or classical, the Bose is more accurate.

Do these work with multi-device pairing?

Yes. Both the Sony and Bose can connect to two devices at once. The Sony XM5 is slightly better at switching between devices automatically.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the best noise cancelling for your money. It is better at silencing the chaos of a commute, has longer battery life, and the faster quick-charge is a lifesaver. Just be ready to deal with the bulky case that doesn't fold. If you take lots of phone calls or need something that packs small, the Bose QC45 is the better pick. It is lighter, more comfortable for long wear, has superior call quality, and folds flat. For most commuters, the Sony is the winner—but the Bose is the one you will forget is on your head.
