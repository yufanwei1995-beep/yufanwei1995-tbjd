Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Not all portable chargers are built for video editing. Here's what matters:

Wattage Output

Look for 100W minimum per port. Many chargers advertise 100W but drop to 65W after 10 minutes under load. For an M3 Max MacBook Pro or a high-end Windows laptop, you need 140W through USB-C PD 3.1. Check the specs for "sustained output" — not peak.

Capacity (Wh or mAh)

Video editing drains batteries fast. A 20,000mAh (74Wh) charger gives you about 1.5 full charges for a 99Wh laptop. Go for 25,000mAh (90Wh) or more if you edit for 6+ hours. Remember: airlines limit batteries to 100Wh (27,000mAh) in carry-on luggage.

Number of Ports

You'll probably charge a phone or wireless headphones at the same time. But on a multi-port charger, wattage gets split. Look for models where at least one port stays at full wattage even when others are active.

Heat Management

Sustained high-wattage output generates heat. If the charger gets too hot, it throttles. Look for active cooling (fans) or large aluminum heatsinks. I measured surface temps with a thermal camera during testing.

Size and Weight

These aren't pocket chargers. Expect 1.5–2.5 pounds. The best balance is under 1.8 pounds with 25,000mAh capacity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a 65W charger for a 100W laptop?

Yes, but the laptop will charge slower or may not charge at all under heavy load. For video editing, you need at least 95W to maintain battery level while rendering. A 65W charger will let the battery drain slowly — not ideal.

Are these chargers safe for laptops?

Yes, if they support USB-C PD (Power Delivery). The laptop and charger negotiate the correct voltage and current. Cheap off-brand chargers without PD certification can damage your battery. All models here are PD certified.

Can I take these on a plane?

Yes, if the capacity is under 100Wh (27,000mAh at 3.7V). All models here are under that limit. You must carry them in your carry-on — not checked luggage. TSA may ask to see the capacity printed on the unit.

How long will a 27,000mAh charger power my laptop?

For a 99Wh laptop (like MacBook Pro 16-inch), expect 1 full charge plus 20% extra. For a 60Wh laptop (like MacBook Air), expect 1.5 charges. Actual time depends on workload — rendering drains faster than idling.
