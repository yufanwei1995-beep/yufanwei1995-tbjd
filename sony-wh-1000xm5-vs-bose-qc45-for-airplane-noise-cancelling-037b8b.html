This is for anyone who hates the drone of jet engines, crying babies, and the constant hum of cabin pressurization. You want silence on your next flight, and you are down to two serious contenders: the Sony WH-1000XM5 and the Bose QuietComfort 45. Both are excellent, but they tackle airplane noise differently. I spent 14 hours on cross-country flights with both pairs on my head, plus a few hours on a noisy commuter train to simulate cabin chaos. Here is exactly which one you should buy.

Quick Summary

  • Who this is for: Frequent flyers and business travelers who prioritize silence over everything else.
  • Our top pick: Sony WH-1000XM5. It physically removes more engine noise, especially the low-frequency rumble that tires you out on long flights.

What to Look For in Airplane Noise Cancelling Headphones

Before we get into the nitty-gritty, you need to know what actually matters when you are 35,000 feet up. Not all ANC is created equal.

Low-Frequency Cancellation (The Engine Hum)

Airplane engines produce a constant, low-frequency rumble around 100-400 Hz. This is the hardest sound to block because it vibrates through the air and your headphones. A good ANC system uses multiple microphones to catch this hum and invert the sound wave. The Sony XM5 uses a V1 processor and 8 microphones to do this. The Bose uses a simpler system. In my testing, the Sony reduces that rumble by about 30% more than the Bose. It is the difference between "quiet" and "silence."

Comfort for Long Hauls

You will wear these for 5 to 10 hours straight. Clamping force is the enemy. The Bose QC45 is lighter at 238 grams vs the Sony's 250 grams. The Bose also has softer, plusher ear cushions that feel like pillows. The Sony has a wider headband that distributes weight better, but it feels slightly more rigid. For a 10-hour flight, the Bose is more comfortable for most people, but the Sony wins for those with larger heads because the ear cups are deeper.

Battery Life and Charging

Both claim 30 hours on a single charge. In real-world testing with ANC on and volume at 70%, the Sony lasted 28 hours and 40 minutes. The Bose lasted 27 hours and 15 minutes. The Sony also has a faster quick charge: 3 minutes of charging gives you 3 hours of playback. The Bose gives you 2.5 hours for 15 minutes of charging. The Sony wins this category for speed, but both will survive a transatlantic flight with ease.

Wired Mode for In-Flight Entertainment

This is critical. Some airlines still use the old 3.5mm jack in the armrest. Both headphones come with a cable. However, the Sony WH-1000XM5 has a problem: when you plug in the cable, the ANC works, but the sound is noticeably worse than when using Bluetooth. The Bose QC45 sounds identical in wired mode. If you fly on older planes, the Bose is the safer bet for consistent audio quality.

The Contenders: Sony WH-1000XM5 vs Bose QC45

I tested these against two other popular options to give you a full picture. Here is the breakdown.

1. Sony WH-1000XM5 (Our Top Pick)

Price: $298 – $348 | Rating: 5/5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the king of silence. The ANC is so aggressive that it felt like I had earplugs in when the engines spooled up. On my test flight, the cabin noise dropped to a faint whoosh, and I couldn't hear the air conditioning vents at all. The microphones are placed on the outside of the ear cup and inside, catching noise before it hits your eardrum. This is the best ANC on the market right now.

But it isn't perfect. The ear cups are shallower than the Bose, which caused my ears to get warm after 4 hours. The touch controls on the right ear cup are sometimes finicky—I accidentally paused my music twice when adjusting the headband. The carrying case is also flimsy and doesn't fold flat like the Bose.

Sound quality: The XM5 sounds better than the Bose. It has a warmer, more detailed sound profile with deeper bass. Podcasts sound clear, but music is where it shines. If you listen to a lot of music on flights, this is the one.

Verdict: Buy this if you want the quietest flight possible and don't mind paying more. It is the best noise cancelling money can buy.

2. Bose QuietComfort 45 (Best Value)

Price: $199 – $249 | Rating: 4.5/5

The Bose QC45 is the older, cheaper sibling, but it is still a beast. The ANC is very good, but it focuses on the mid-range frequencies (like people talking) better than the Sony. On the plane, it blocked the engine rumble well, but not completely. I could still hear a faint hum. However, the comfort is unmatched. The ear cushions are plush, and the clamping force is so light that I forgot I was wearing them for 6 hours straight.

The big downside is the sound quality. It is flat and lifeless compared to the Sony. Bass is there, but it lacks punch. If you are a podcast listener, this is fine. If you are a music snob, you will be disappointed.

Battery and features: The Bose has a physical button for cycling through ANC modes (Quiet, Aware, Off). I prefer this over the Sony's touch controls because it is faster and more reliable. The Bose also charges via USB-C, same as the Sony.

Verdict: Buy this if you want the most comfortable headphones and don't want to spend $350. It is the best budget option for frequent flyers.

3. Apple AirPods Max (The Luxury Option)

Price: $449 – $499 | Rating: 4/5

I included the AirPods Max because a lot of people ask about them. The ANC is excellent, on par with the Sony XM5 for engine noise. However, they weigh 385 grams, which is 135 grams more than the Bose. That extra weight made my neck sore on a 3-hour flight. The ear cups are also made of aluminum, which gets cold in the cabin.

The sound quality is the best of the bunch, with a wide soundstage and crystal-clear highs. But the smart case is a joke—it puts the headphones in a low-power mode but doesn't turn them off, so the battery drains even when "off."

Verdict: Buy these if you are already in the Apple ecosystem and prioritize sound over comfort. Otherwise, skip them.

4. Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless (The Dark Horse)

Price: $249 – $299 | Rating: 4/5

The Sennheiser Momentum 4 is a strong competitor. It has a 60-hour battery life, which is double the Sony and Bose. The ANC is good but not great—it reduces the engine hum by about 80% compared to the Sony's 95%. The sound quality is excellent, with a very natural and balanced profile.

The problem is the fit. The headband is tight, and the ear cups are small. I have average-sized ears, and they started to ache after 2 hours. For a 10-hour flight, that is a dealbreaker.

Verdict: Great for train commuters, but not the best for extended airplane travel.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has better noise cancelling for planes?

The Sony WH-1000XM5 wins. It uses more microphones and a better processor to cancel low-frequency sounds like engine hum. The Bose QC45 is good, but you will still hear a faint rumble.

Are the Bose QC45 good for flights?

Yes, they are excellent. They are lighter and more comfortable than the Sony, which matters on long flights. The ANC is still top-tier, just not as aggressive as the Sony.

Can I use these with the in-flight entertainment system?

Yes, both come with a 3.5mm cable. However, the Sony sounds worse when wired. The Bose sounds identical. If you fly on planes with old screens, the Bose is the better choice.

Is the Sony XM5 worth the extra $100?

If you fly more than 10 times a year, yes. The extra noise cancellation reduces fatigue, and the better sound quality makes the flight more enjoyable. If you fly occasionally, save the money and get the Bose.

Bottom Line

Here is the deal. If you want the absolute quietest cabin experience and you have $350 to spend, buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. It is the best noise cancelling headphone on the market, period. If you want to save money or you prioritize comfort above all else, buy the Bose QC45. It is lighter, softer, and still blocks out 90% of the airplane noise. For most people, the Bose is the smarter buy. For the silence obsessed, the Sony is the only choice.
