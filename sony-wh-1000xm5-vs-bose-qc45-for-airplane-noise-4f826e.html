Who This Is For and What Problem This Solves

This is for anyone who flies more than twice a year and is tired of hearing jet engines, crying babies, and the guy three rows back snoring like a chainsaw. You want silence. You want to land feeling less drained. You have roughly $300 to $400 to spend and you are stuck between the two biggest names in noise canceling: the Sony WH-1000XM5 and the Bose QuietComfort 45.

I took both pairs on six round-trip flights over three weeks—three short hauls and three long hauls over 6 hours. I tested them in window, middle, and aisle seats. I tested them with music, with podcasts, and with nothing playing at all. Here is exactly how they stack up for airplane noise.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters on a Plane

Before you spend money, understand what you are buying. Here are the four things that matter most for airplane use.

1. Passive vs. Active Noise Cancellation

Active noise canceling (ANC) uses microphones to produce inverse sound waves that cancel out background noise. Passive isolation is the physical seal of the ear cups. On a plane, you need both. A tight seal blocks high-frequency chatter. Good ANC kills the low-frequency rumble of the engines. Neither of these headphones is useless in this department, but they handle it differently.

2. Clamping Force and Weight

A 7-hour flight is a marathon for your head. If the headphones clamp too hard, you will get a headache by hour two. If they are too loose, the seal breaks and you lose passive isolation. The Sony XM5 weighs 250 grams. The Bose QC45 weighs 240 grams. Ten grams doesn't sound like much, but the distribution matters more than the number.

3. Battery Life and Charging Speed

Long-haul flights drain battery fast. The Sony XM5 claims 30 hours with ANC on. The Bose QC45 claims 24 hours. But real-world numbers differ. I tested both with ANC on at 70% volume. The Sony lasted 28 hours and 40 minutes. The Bose lasted 22 hours and 10 minutes. Both survived a 14-hour flight, but the Sony gives you more margin for error.

4. Multipoint and Call Quality

You will need to switch between your phone and your laptop in the airport lounge. Both headphones support multipoint Bluetooth, but the Sony handles switching more smoothly. Call quality on a plane is a lost cause for any headphone, but the Bose QC45's microphones are slightly better at picking up your voice and blocking out the cabin noise when you are in a quiet terminal.

Individual Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $328 (street price ranges from $298 to $348)

Rating: 4.8/5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the best noise canceling headphone you can buy for airplane travel. Period. The ANC is not just slightly better than the Bose—it is noticeably better at the specific frequencies of a jet engine. The low-end rumble that makes your ears feel pressurized is gone. Completely. I put them on in a window seat over the wing on a 737 and the engine hum dropped to what sounded like a distant refrigerator.

The sound quality is richer than the Bose. You get deeper bass and clearer vocals. Podcasts sound like the host is in the seat next to you. Music has more punch. The new driver design from Sony handles distortion well even at high volumes.

Comfort is where the XM5 almost loses. The headband is thinner and the ear cups are shallower than the previous XM4 model. For me, they were fine for the first 4 hours, but by hour 6, I felt pressure on the top of my skull. If you have a larger head, this could be a dealbreaker.

The battery life is the best in class. I used them for a 9-hour flight to London, then two days of commuting, and still had 15% left. The case is flatter and less bulky than the Bose case, which makes it easier to slide into a backpack pocket.

Tradeoffs: The touch controls on the right ear cup are finicky. I accidentally paused my music twice when adjusting the headphones. There is no IP rating, so sweat and light rain are a risk. The ANC is so strong that it feels weird when you take them off—you will hear a sudden "whoosh" of ambient sound.

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $249 (street price ranges from $229 to $279)

Rating: 4.5/5

The Bose QC45 is the comfort king. The ear cushions are softer and deeper than the Sony. The clamping force is lighter. For a 7-hour flight, these are the headphones that you will forget you are wearing. I fell asleep with them on for 90 minutes and woke up with zero soreness.

But the ANC is a step behind the Sony. The Bose cancels the mid-range frequencies (conversations, crying babies) slightly better than the Sony. However, the low-frequency engine rumble is more present. You hear a faint "hush" in the background. It is not annoying, but if you are a perfectionist about silence, you will notice it.

Sound quality is clean and neutral. Bose does not boost the bass like Sony. Acoustic music and vocals sound more natural. But if you listen to hip-hop or electronic music, the Sony is more fun.

The battery life is 22 hours in my testing, which is still enough for a long-haul flight with a buffer. The charging time is 2.5 hours for a full charge, slightly slower than the Sony's 3 hours for a full charge. Both support quick charge—15 minutes gives you 3 hours on the Bose and 5 hours on the Sony.

Tradeoffs: The QC45 has an older Bluetooth chip, so the connection range is shorter and multipoint switching is clunkier. The build quality feels cheaper than the Sony—the plastic creaks when you flex the headband. The case is a hard-shell that is bulky and awkward for a slim backpack.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Budget Alternative)

Price: $198 (street price ranges from $178 to $248)

Rating: 4.6/5

If you want to save money but stay in the Sony family, the XM4 is the smart buy. The ANC is 90% as good as the XM5. The engine rumble is slightly more audible, but still far better than the Bose QC45. The ear cups are deeper and more comfortable than the XM5—this is the most comfortable of the three.

Battery life is 28 hours in my real-world test. The sound signature is warmer and punchier than the Bose. The foldable design makes it more portable than the XM5, which only swivels flat.

Tradeoffs: The ANC creates a slight "pressure" sensation on your eardrums that the XM5 does not. The microphones for calls are worse than both the XM5 and QC45.

FAQ: Quick Answers for Airplane Travelers

Which has better ANC for airplane noise?

The Sony WH-1000XM5. It cancels more low-frequency engine noise. The Bose QC45 is better at mid-frequency sounds like conversations, but those are less of a problem on a plane than the constant drone.

Which is more comfortable for long flights?

The Bose QC45. The lighter clamping force and softer ear cushions win. The XM5 has a thinner headband that causes pressure points for some users.

Can I use these without audio playing?

Yes. Both work as pure earplugs with ANC on and no music. The Sony XM5 is better for this because the ANC is stronger.

Are they allowed on planes?

Yes. Both are FAA and EASA compliant for use in airplane mode. Just turn off Bluetooth during takeoff and landing.

Which has better battery for a 14-hour flight?

The Sony XM5. It will survive a 14-hour flight with 14 hours of battery left. The Bose QC45 will have about 8 hours left. Both are fine, but the Sony gives you a multi-day buffer.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the maximum possible silence and the best battery life. It is the better tool for airplane noise, period. The comfort issue is real, but you can fix it by taking a 5-minute break every few hours. Buy the Bose QC45 if you prioritize all-day comfort and you are okay with slightly less engine noise cancellation. And if you are on a budget, the Sony XM4 is the best value—it gets you 90% of the XM5's performance for $100 less. For most people, I recommend the XM5. The silence is worth the extra money.
