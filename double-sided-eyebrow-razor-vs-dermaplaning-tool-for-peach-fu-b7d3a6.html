Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before you buy, understand the difference. A double-sided eyebrow razor (like the Tinkle razor) has a thin blade on a small plastic handle. It is precise but narrow. A dermaplaning tool has a wider blade with a safety guard. It covers more surface area and exfoliates dead skin along with the fuzz.

Here are the four things I check on every tool:

  • Blade width: Measure the blade edge. Eyebrow razors are about 0.5 inches. Dermaplaning tools are 1.5 to 2 inches. Wider is faster, but harder to navigate around your nose.
  • Safety guard: Look for a micro-comb or guard between the blade and your skin. This prevents you from pressing too hard and slicing yourself. The Schick and Tinkle have this. Cheaper knockoffs do not.
  • Handle grip: You will be holding this at a 45-degree angle. A slippery handle means you will press harder. Rubber grips win here.
  • Blade replacement: Some tools are disposable. Others have replaceable blades. For hygiene, you should change blades every 2-3 uses. If the tool costs $8, just buy a new one.
Pro tip: Do not use these on active acne or cold sores. You will spread bacteria and make it worse. I learned this the hard way.

FAQ

Will my peach fuzz grow back thicker?

No. This is a myth. Shaving removes the tapered tip of the hair, so it grows back with a blunt end. It feels thicker and looks darker for a few days, but it is the same hair. It will not change your hair follicle size.

How often should I dermaplane?

Every 2 to 3 weeks. Your skin turnover cycle is about 28 days. If you do it more often, you risk over-exfoliating and breaking your skin barrier. Your moisturizer will sting if you do it too much.

Can I use a dermaplaning tool on my upper lip?

Yes, but do it dry. Water and shaving cream make the skin slippery, and you lose control. Use short, downward strokes, and do not go over the same area twice.

What is the difference between a double-sided razor and a dermaplaning tool?

The blade angle and the handle. A double-sided razor (like Tinkle) has the blade at a 45-degree angle for precise shaping. A dermaplaning tool has the blade at a 30-degree angle for scraping. The dermaplaning tool also has a wider head.
