What to Look For in Multi-Cat Litter

Before you grab the cheapest bag, understand what actually matters when you have multiple cats. It's not just about odor — it's about how the litter holds up under heavy use.

Clumping strength: Weak clumps break apart when you scoop, leaving bits of waste in the box. That's a bacterial breeding ground. Look for litters with bentonite clay or plant-based binders that form rock-hard clumps within 30 seconds.

Odor control technology: Baking soda is cheap but ineffective for multi-cat. Activated carbon actually absorbs ammonia molecules. Enzyme-based formulas (like Boxiecat) break down the urine itself. Check the ingredient list for these specific odor fighters.

Dust levels: Low dust isn't just for your lungs — cats with sensitive respiratory systems can get sick from dusty litter. Tilt the bag in the store and look for visible dust clouds. If you can't test it, read reviews for "dust" mentions.

Tracking: Multi-cat means more traffic in and out of the box. Heavier granules track less, but fine clumping clay tracks everywhere. A litter mat helps, but choose a litter with larger particle sizes if tracking drives you nuts.

Price per use: Cheap litter often needs more frequent full changes. A $20 bag that lasts 6 weeks is cheaper than a $10 bag that needs replacing every 2 weeks. Calculate cost per month, not per bag.

2. Arm & Hammer Slide

Arm & Hammer's Slide formula was built for the "non-stick" promise — the clumps literally slide off the bottom of the box without scraping. That's a lifesaver if you have a cat that pees right against the corners.

The odor control is solid thanks to their baking soda infusion, but it's not on Boxiecat's level for heavy multi-cat use. We noticed ammonia peeking through around day 7 with three cats. Clumping is excellent though — hard, solid clumps that don't crumble.

The dust is moderate. Not terrible, but not the low-dust experience premium brands offer. The scent is a bit perfumey out of the bag, which some cats might reject. If your cat is picky about smells, do a slow transition.

  • Pros: Non-stick clumps actually work, affordable, widely available in stores.
  • Cons: Moderate dust, scent can be strong, odor control fades by week 2.

3. Fresh Step Simply Unscented Clumping Clay

If your cats have sensitive noses or you just hate perfumed litter, Fresh Step's Simply Unscented is a solid choice. It skips the fragrance and relies on activated charcoal to absorb odors.

The clumping is good but not great — clumps are firm but can break if you scoop aggressively. It's a lighter clay, so you'll go through it faster than heavier options. Odor control is decent for two cats but struggled in our three-cat test, needing a full change every 10 days instead of 14.

What wins here is price and availability. It's cheap, you can find it everywhere, and unscented means no chemical smell clashing with your home. For budget-conscious multi-cat homes, this is the value pick.

  • Pros: No artificial fragrance, affordable, activated charcoal works well.
  • Cons: Lighter clumps break easily, needs more frequent full changes with 3+ cats.

4. Tidy Cats Free & Clean Unscented

Tidy Cats is a household name, and the Free & Clean Unscented version has been a staple for years. It uses a blend of clumping clay and zeolite — a volcanic mineral that traps ammonia.

In practice, the zeolite helps, but the odor control is still a step behind Boxiecat. We got 8 solid days of no odor with three cats, which is respectable. Clumping is decent — not rock hard, but scoopable without too much crumbling.

The dust level surprised us — it's lower than Arm & Hammer but higher than Boxiecat. The real issue is tracking. The granules are small, so they end up everywhere. Pair it with a good litter mat or you'll be sweeping daily.

  • Pros: Reliable odor control, unscented, good price point.
  • Cons: High tracking, clumps not as solid as premium options.

5. Dr. Elsey's Ultra Unscented

Dr. Elsey's is the go-to for cat shelters and breeders because it's effective and affordable in bulk. The Ultra formula is 99.9% dust-free, which is impressive for a clay litter.

Clumping is excellent — instant and hard, like Boxiecat. Odor control is good for two cats but fades with three or more. The granules are fine and track like crazy. You'll find it in your bed, your couch, everywhere.

It's unscented, which is great for sensitive cats, but the lack of any fragrance means you rely entirely on the clay's absorption. If your cats are heavy urinators, you'll need to add baking soda to stretch it.

  • Pros: Nearly dust-free, hard clumps, budget-friendly for bulk buyers.
  • Cons: Heavy tracking, odor control weakens with 3+ cats, no scent masking.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I change litter with multiple cats?

With 2 cats, a full change every 2-3 weeks is standard. With 3+ cats, push that to every 10-14 days depending on the litter quality. Scoop daily — no exceptions. Your nose will tell you when it's time.

Is unscented litter better for cats?

Most cats prefer unscented. Their sense of smell is 14x stronger than humans, and heavy perfume can deter them from using the box. If you want odor control without fragrance, look for activated charcoal or enzyme formulas.

Why does my multi-cat litter smell like ammonia even after scooping?

Ammonia smell means urine is breaking down in the box. This happens when you don't use enough litter (add 3-4 inches), clumps are left behind, or the litter lacks ammonia-absorbing ingredients like zeolite or enzymes. Consider switching to a deeper layer or a stronger formula.
