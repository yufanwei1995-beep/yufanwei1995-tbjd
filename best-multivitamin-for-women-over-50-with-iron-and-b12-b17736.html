Buyer's Guide: What to Actually Look For

Don't just grab the cheapest bottle at CVS. Here's what separates a good multivitamin from a gimmick for women over 50.

1. Iron Form Matters — Check for Ferrous Bisglycinate

Most cheap multivitamins use ferrous sulfate. It's cheap but causes constipation and nausea — the #1 reason women stop taking their vitamins. Look for ferrous bisglycinate (chelated). It's gentler on the stomach and absorbs 2-3x better. The label will say "bisglycinate" or "glycinate chelate."

2. B12: Cyanocobalamin vs Methylcobalamin

Your body can't use cyanocobalamin directly — it has to convert it. About 10-20% of women over 50 lack the enzyme to do this efficiently. Methylcobalamin is the active form. It's ready to use immediately. If you have gut issues or take acid reducers (like omeprazole), you need methylcobalamin.

3. Dosage: More Isn't Better

For iron, you need 8 mg daily after menopause (you stop losing blood monthly, so requirements drop from 18 mg). For B12, aim for 50-100 mcg — that's plenty for absorption without mega-dosing. Anything claiming 5000% DV is marketing fluff.

4. Third-Party Testing

Look for USP, NSF, or ConsumerLab seals. These verify the pill actually contains what the label says. In 2026, many brands skip this. Don't trust "physician formulated" without verification.

5. Pill Size and Coating

You're 50. Swallowing horse pills is miserable. Look for caplets under 1 inch or capsules you can open and sprinkle on food. If the pill is coated, it prevents aftertaste and makes it easier to swallow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need iron after 50?

Yes, but less than before. You need 8 mg daily (down from 18 mg pre-menopause). If you eat red meat weekly, you might not need a supplement. But if you're vegetarian, have heavy periods (yes, some women still do in perimenopause), or feel tired, a low-dose iron is smart.

Can I take too much B12?

B12 is water-soluble. Your body pees out what it doesn't use. Doses up to 1000 mcg are safe. The only risk is if you have kidney issues — check with your doctor.

Why do I need methylcobalamin instead of cyanocobalamin?

Cyanocobalamin is synthetic and needs conversion in your liver. As you age, that conversion slows down. Methylcobalamin is already active — your cells use it immediately. If you take acid reflux meds or have pernicious anemia, you must use methyl.

Should I take my multivitamin with food?

Yes, especially if it contains iron. Food (especially vitamin C-rich food like oranges or peppers) helps iron absorb. Avoid taking it with coffee, tea, or dairy — the tannins and calcium block absorption.

What if I have a sensitive stomach?

Choose a formula with ferrous bisglycinate (like Pure Encapsulations or Ritual) and take it right after a meal. If you still get nausea, switch to a liquid iron supplement or take it every other day.
