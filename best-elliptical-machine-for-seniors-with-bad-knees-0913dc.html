What to Look for in an Elliptical for Bad Knees

Not all ellipticals are knee-friendly. Here's what matters most.

Stride Length and Path

Look for a stride length of 18-20 inches. Too short (under 16") and you'll feel choppy — which transfers shock to knees. Too long (over 22") can overextend the joint. A natural, oval path is best. Avoid machines with an exaggerated up-and-down motion.

Pedal Design and Spacing

Wide, textured pedals reduce foot slippage. Pedals spaced 8-10 inches apart (called Q-factor) let your hips and knees stay in a neutral line. Too wide (over 12 inches) strains hips. Too narrow (under 6 inches) forces knees inward.

Low Starting Resistance

The first resistance level must be very low — under 20 watts. Seniors with bad knees need to start slow. Magnetic resistance is quieter and smoother than friction or air resistance.

Step-Through Frame

This is non-negotiable for seniors. A step-through frame has a low crossbar (under 10 inches from the floor). You walk onto the pedals instead of swinging your leg over a high bar. This prevents falls and hip strain.

Adjustable Pedal Angle

Some machines let you tilt the pedals forward or backward. A slight forward tilt (5-10 degrees) reduces ankle and knee flexion — a big help for stiff joints.

Handlebar Options

Stationary handlebars with pulse grips are essential for seniors who can't use moving arms. Moving arms that sync with leg motion add core engagement but can be hard for those with shoulder issues.

FAQ: Ellipticals for Bad Knees

Is an elliptical bad for knees?

No — it's one of the best low-impact machines. The elliptical mimics walking without the heel-strike impact. But only if the stride is smooth and the pedals are properly spaced. A poorly designed elliptical can aggravate knees.

What stride length do I need?

18-20 inches is ideal for most seniors. Shorter strides cause choppy motion. Longer strides overextend the knee.

Should I get a front-drive or rear-drive elliptical?

Rear-drive (like the Schwinn 470) typically has a smoother, more natural stride. Front-drive machines (like the Sole E95) feel more upright. Try both if possible.

How much should I spend?

$800-$1,500 gets you a very good machine for home use. Over $2,000 gets commercial quality. Under $700 usually means a short stride and flimsy build — avoid.

Can I use an elliptical after knee replacement?

Yes — most surgeons approve ellipticals after 6-8 weeks. Start with zero resistance and short sessions (5-10 minutes). Always consult your doctor first.
