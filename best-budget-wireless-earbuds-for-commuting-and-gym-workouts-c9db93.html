What to Look For in Budget Earbuds

Before we get into the reviews, here's what actually matters when you're spending under $80. Skip these specs and you'll regret it.

IP Rating (Sweat & Rain Protection)

For gym use, you need at least IPX4. That means sweat-proof and light rain-proof. IPX5 is better for heavy sweating. IPX7 means you can dunk them in water, but that's rare at this price. Don't buy anything below IPX4 for workouts — you'll kill them in three months.

ANC vs. Passive Isolation

Active Noise Cancelling (ANC) uses mics to cancel engine rumble and gym chatter. At this price, ANC won't be perfect, but it helps a lot on a bus or train. If you're only using them at the gym, passive isolation (a tight seal) might be enough. But for commuting, get ANC if you can.

Battery Life (Real Numbers)

Look for 7+ hours in the buds themselves. The charging case should give you at least 20 more. I test with volume at 60% and ANC on. Those are the real-world numbers you'll get.

Comfort and Fit

Earbuds that fall out during bench presses are useless. Look for wingtips or ear fins for stability. Also check the weight — anything over 5.5 grams per bud will feel heavy after an hour.

Bluetooth Codecs

Most budget buds use AAC or SBC. That's fine for iPhone users. Android users should look for LDAC support if they care about sound quality. But honestly, for gym music and podcasts, AAC is fine.

2. JBL Vibe Buds True Wireless — Best for Bass Heads

Price: $39.99
Rating: 4/5

JBL's Vibe Buds are the cheapest on this list, and they sound shockingly good. The 8mm drivers deliver a punchy, warm sound that makes heavy metal and trap music sound huge.

They have no ANC, but passive isolation is solid. The seal is tight, and they block out gym chatter well. On a loud subway, you'll still hear the screech of brakes, but music covers most of it.

Battery life is 8 hours in the buds, 24 total with the case. That's decent, but the case itself is plasticky and feels cheap. The IPX4 rating means they'll survive sweat, but don't rinse them off.

What I didn't like: The microphone is bad. Calls on the street sound muffled to the other person. If you take work calls during your commute, skip these.

Best for: Gym-only use where bass matters more than call quality.

3. TOZO NC9 — Best Budget ANC

Price: $45.99
Rating: 4/5

TOZO is a brand that flies under the radar, but their NC9 earbuds are a sleeper hit. The ANC is surprisingly aggressive for the price. It's not as refined as the Soundcore, but it kills more noise than the JBLs by a mile.

The 12mm drivers are big for this class, and they produce a wide soundstage. Acoustic songs sound airy, not boxed in. They're not as punchy as the JBLs, but they're more accurate.

Battery life is 7 hours with ANC on, 32 total with the case. The case supports wireless charging, which is rare at this price. IPX6 means they can handle heavy sweat and even a rainstorm.

What I didn't like: The fit is hit-or-miss. The nozzles are short, and I had to swap to third-party tips to get a secure seal. Also, the touch controls are finicky — single taps sometimes register as double taps.

Best for: Commuters who want maximum noise cancelling for the least money.

4. Beats Flex — Best for iPhone Users (Wired Neckband)

Price: $49.99
Rating: 3.5/5

Hear me out. Neckbands are not dead. The Beats Flex are a different category, but for gym and commute, they have one massive advantage: they can't fall out. The wire connecting them means you can lose a bud mid-squat and it just dangles.

Sound quality is decent. They're warm and musical, but not detailed. The W1 chip makes pairing with iPhones instant, and you get seamless switching between your iPhone and Apple Watch.

Battery life is 12 hours, which is great for a neckband. They charge via USB-C and give you 1.5 hours of play from a 10-minute charge. IPX4 rating means sweat won't kill them.

What I didn't like: The wire can bounce around during runs, and the inline remote is placed awkwardly. Also, no ANC at all — you're relying on the seal.

Best for: People who lose earbuds constantly or hate the fit of in-ears.

5. Anker Soundcore P3i — Best Under $40

Price: $34.99
Rating: 3.5/5

If you're on a super tight budget, the P3i is the sweet spot. They have ANC (basic, but it works), a bass-heavy sound, and a 10-hour battery life in the buds. Total playback hits 34 hours with the case.

The build quality is surprisingly good for $35. The case feels solid, and the buds have a matte finish that doesn't show fingerprints. IPX5 means they handle sweat and rain fine.

Sound is V-shaped — big bass, sparkly treble. It's fun but not accurate. Mids get recessed, so vocals in rock songs can sound distant.

What I didn't like: The ANC creates a slight hiss in quiet rooms. And the app is clunky — it takes three taps to switch between ANC and transparency modes.

Best for: Students or anyone who needs a reliable pair for under $40.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use these for running?

Yes, but look for ear fins or wingtips. The Soundcore Liberty 4 NC and the Beats Flex are the most secure. The TOZO NC9 need aftermarket tips to stay put during sprints.

Do I really need ANC for the gym?

No. Gyms are noisy, but music at a reasonable volume covers most of it. ANC matters more for commuting. If you only go to the gym, save money and get the JBL Vibe Buds.

How long do these last before the battery degrades?

Most budget buds lose about 20% of their battery capacity after 12-18 months of daily use. That's normal for lithium cells. The Soundcore and TOZO have the best battery management, so they degrade slower.

Are cheap earbuds bad for calls?

Yes, mostly. The JBLs and P3i have poor mics. The Soundcore Liberty 4 NC has a decent mic for calls in quiet rooms. The Beats Flex has a good mic, too, because the mic sits closer to your mouth.

Can I wear these in the shower?

No. IPX4-6 ratings mean sweat and rain are fine, but steam and direct water spray will kill them. If you want shower earbuds, you need IPX7 or higher, which doesn't exist in this price range.

Bottom Line

Buy the Soundcore Liberty 4 NC. They're the only pair under $60 that does everything well: strong ANC for your commute, punchy bass for your workout, and a battery that lasts a full week of daily use. If you're on a strict budget, the JBL Vibe Buds are a fantastic gym-only pair, but skip them if you make calls. And if you have an iPhone and keep losing wireless buds, the Beats Flex neckband is the most practical choice you can make.
