Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Don't overthink it. Here are the four things that actually matter for running audio.

1. Fit and Stability

If it falls out, it's useless. Look for ear hooks, fins, or wingtips. Over-ear headphones need a snug headband that doesn't bounce. Test the fit by shaking your head vigorously — if it shifts, move on.

2. Water and Sweat Resistance

IPX4 is minimum for sweat. IPX5 or IPX7 is better for rain or heavy sweat. Over-ear headphones rarely have IP ratings — avoid them in downpours. Earbuds are safer here.

3. Battery Life

Earbuds should last at least 6 hours per charge. Over-ear headphones should hit 20 hours minimum. Anything less and you'll be charging after every long run.

4. Sound Quality and Ambient Mode

You need clear mids for podcast or audiobook runs. Bass is nice but not essential. Ambient mode (letting in outside noise) is critical for road safety — don't run with full noise cancellation on busy streets.

Pro Tip: Always test the ear tips for a seal. A poor seal ruins bass and lets in wind noise. Use the app's fit test if available.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run with over-ear headphones?

Yes, if they stay on. The Anker Q30 works well. But they'll get sweaty and offer no water resistance. Earbuds are better for wet conditions.

What is the best budget running earbud brand?

Anker (Soundcore) and JLab consistently deliver reliable, affordable options with good fit.

Do I need ANC for running?

No. Ambient mode is safer. ANC blocks traffic noise — dangerous on roads. Save ANC for the gym or indoor runs.

How much should I spend on running earbuds?

$40-$80 gets you solid performance. Spending over $150 is unnecessary for running unless you want advanced features like spatial audio.

Bottom Line Verdict
