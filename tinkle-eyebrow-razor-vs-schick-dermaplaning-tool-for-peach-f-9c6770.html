Buyer's Guide: What to Look For in a Dermaplaning Tool

Before we get into the head-to-head, you need to know what separates a good tool from a bad one. Here are the four things that matter most.

1. Blade Sharpness and Angle

A dull blade pulls at the hair instead of cutting it. That causes razor burn and ingrown hairs. The best tools use surgical-grade stainless steel with a 30 to 45-degree angle. The Tinkle uses a classic Japanese straight-edge design. The Schick uses a lighter, thinner blade that flexes more with your skin's curves.

2. Safety Guard or Exposed Blade

This is the biggest difference between the two. The Tinkle has a fully exposed blade on one side. The Schick has a micro-guard that sits between the blade and your skin. That guard prevents you from pressing too hard and scraping off the top layer of your epidermis. If you're a beginner, the guard is a lifesaver.

3. Handle Design and Grip

You're holding this tool at awkward angles over your cheekbones and jawline. A flimsy metal handle that slips will cause cuts. The Tinkle has a thin, flat metal handle that's hard to hold if your hands are even slightly damp. The Schick has a textured, ergonomic plastic handle that locks into your fingers.

4. Blade Replacement Cost

Both are disposable. But the Tinkle is so cheap that most people throw it away after one or two uses. The Schick is designed to last 3-5 uses (about a month if you dermaplane weekly). The replacement cost is higher, but you get more uses per blade.

Pro tip: Never use a dermaplaning tool on active acne, cold sores, or sunburned skin. You'll spread bacteria and make things worse. Wait until your skin is completely clear.

FAQ

Does dermaplaning make hair grow back thicker?

No. This is a myth. Shaving or dermaplaning cuts the hair at the surface, so the tip is blunt. It feels stubbly for a day or two, but it doesn't change the hair follicle. Your hair will grow back exactly the same as before.

How often should I dermaplane?

Once every 2-3 weeks is ideal. Your skin needs time to rebuild its protective barrier. Doing it weekly is fine for thick, oily skin. If you have dry or sensitive skin, stick to every 3-4 weeks.

Can I use these on my upper lip?

Yes, but be extra careful. The upper lip is a common area for razor burn. Use short, downward strokes and don't press hard. The Schick's guard makes this area much safer than the Tinkle.

Will dermaplaning break me out?

It can, if you have active acne. The blade drags bacteria across your skin. If your skin is clear, dermaplaning actually helps prevent breakouts by removing dead skin that clogs pores. Wait until any active pimples are fully healed.
