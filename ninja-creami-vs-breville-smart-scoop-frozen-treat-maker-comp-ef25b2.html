Quick Summary

If you want a machine that churns, freezes, and keeps your frozen treat at the perfect serving temperature, buy the Breville Smart Scoop. If you want a machine that processes pre-frozen bases into edible desserts quickly and cheaply, buy the Ninja Creami. Both make real ice cream, but the Breville is far more convenient and consistent.

Buyer’s Guide: What to Look for in a Frozen Treat Maker

Before you spend money, understand these key differences. They decide which machine fits your life.

1. Freezing Method

Most home ice cream makers (like the Breville) require you to pre-freeze a bowl for 12-24 hours. The Ninja Creami uses a different approach: you freeze the base in a pint container for 24 hours, then the machine processes it. The Breville has a built-in compressor — no pre-freezing needed. This is a huge convenience. You can make ice cream on a whim.

2. Capacity

The Ninja Creami makes pints (16 oz). The Breville makes 1.5 quarts (48 oz) per batch. If you're feeding a family or hosting a party, the Breville wins. If you live alone or want variety, the Ninja’s pint size lets you make multiple flavors without commitment.

3. Texture Control

The Breville offers 12 hardness settings from soft serve to hard scoop. The Ninja Creami has pre-programmed modes for ice cream, sorbet, gelato, and milkshakes. Both produce smooth results, but the Breville gives you more control over final texture.

4. Noise Level

The Ninja Creami is loud — around 80 dB during processing. The Breville is quieter, around 60 dB. This matters if you make ice cream late at night or early in the morning.

5. Ease of Cleaning

The Ninja Creami has several parts: pint container, paddle, lid, and outer bowl. All are dishwasher safe. The Breville has a mixing bowl, paddle, and lid — also dishwasher safe. The Breville is slightly easier because there are fewer parts.

6. Versatility

The Ninja Creami can also make sorbet, gelato, milkshakes, and even fruit sorbets with minimal prep. The Breville focuses on ice cream and gelato but can also make frozen yogurt and sorbet. The Ninja is more versatile if you want to experiment.

Best Frozen Treat Makers Compared

1. Breville Smart Scoop (BCI600XL)

Best for: Serious home chefs who want restaurant-quality ice cream with minimal hassle.

The Breville Smart Scoop is the gold standard for home ice cream makers. It has a built-in compressor, so you never pre-freeze a bowl. Just pour in your base, select your hardness (12 settings from soft serve to hard), and walk away. It automatically churns, freezes, and then keeps the ice cream at serving temperature for up to 3 hours.

Performance: The paddle scrapes the sides constantly, preventing ice crystals. The resulting texture is smooth, creamy, and professional. In our tests, a 1.5-quart batch of vanilla bean ice cream took 45 minutes to reach hard-scoop consistency. The machine then held it at perfect serving temperature for 2 hours without refreezing.

What we love: The automatic keep-cool function. You can make ice cream in the morning and serve it at dinner without it melting. The LCD screen is clear and intuitive.

Tradeoffs: It's expensive ($399-$449) and heavy (20 lbs). It takes up significant counter space. The paddle can be tricky to align correctly. And it only makes ice cream, gelato, and sorbet — no milkshakes or slushies.

2. Ninja Creami (NC301)

Best for: Budget-conscious home cooks who want variety and don't mind planning ahead.

The Ninja Creami works differently. You freeze your base in a provided pint container for 24 hours, then the machine processes it. This means you need to plan a day ahead. But once you've got a frozen pint, the machine turns it into ice cream, sorbet, gelato, or milkshake in 2-5 minutes.

Performance: The Creami uses a spinning blade to shave the frozen block into a creamy texture. Results are surprisingly good — smooth, no ice crystals, and very close to traditional churned ice cream. However, the texture is slightly denser and less airy than the Breville's.

What we love: The pint-sized containers let you make multiple flavors without committing to a huge batch. It's also affordable ($199-$249) and compact (fits under most cabinets). The milkshake mode is fantastic — thick, creamy, and ready in 2 minutes.

Tradeoffs: The 24-hour freezing requirement is a pain. You can't make ice cream on a whim. The machine is loud (80 dB) — you'll hear it across the house. And the pint containers are small; you'll need multiple containers for a family. The blade can also leave a small amount of unprocessed base at the bottom of the pint if you don't scrape it first.

3. Cuisinart ICE-100 (Ice Cream Maker with Compressor)

Best for: Those who want a compressor machine but can't afford the Breville.

The Cuisinart ICE-100 is a direct competitor to the Breville Smart Scoop. It also has a built-in compressor, no pre-freezing needed. It makes 1.5 quarts of ice cream, gelato, or sorbet in about 40 minutes. The paddle is fixed (not scraping), so you get slightly more ice crystals than the Breville.

Performance: Solid but not spectacular. The ice cream is good — creamy enough for most people — but lacks the silky smoothness of the Breville. The machine is quieter than the Ninja but louder than the Breville. The LCD is simple but functional.

What we love: The price ($299-$349) is a sweet spot between the Ninja and Breville. It's also lighter (15 lbs) than the Breville. The clear lid lets you watch the process.

Tradeoffs: No keep-cool function. Once the cycle ends, the ice cream starts melting. You must transfer it to a freezer container immediately. The paddle can be difficult to remove for cleaning. And the bowl isn't dishwasher safe — hand wash only.

4. Whynter ICM-200LS (Ice Cream Maker with Compressor)

Best for: Large families or frequent entertainers who need big batches.

The Whynter ICM-200LS is a beast. It makes 2 quarts per batch — the largest of any compressor machine we tested. It has a built-in compressor, digital controls, and a 60-minute timer. The paddle scrapes the sides, similar to the Breville.

Performance: Very good. The ice cream is smooth and creamy. The 2-quart capacity is great for parties. However, it takes about 50 minutes to complete a batch. The machine is also heavy (22 lbs) and large — it won't fit under most cabinets.

What we love: The capacity. You can feed 8-10 people with one batch. The timer lets you set it and forget it. The stainless steel bowl looks nice on the counter.

Tradeoffs: No keep-cool function. The timer is a countdown, not a keep-warm feature. The paddle doesn't always scrape the sides perfectly — you may get some ice crystals around the edges. And it's expensive ($349-$399).

5. KitchenAid Ice Cream Maker Attachment (KICA0WH)

Best for: People who already own a KitchenAid stand mixer and want a cheap entry into ice cream making.

This is not a standalone machine. It's a bowl and paddle attachment for KitchenAid stand mixers. You must pre-freeze the bowl for 15 hours, then attach it to the mixer and churn for 20-30 minutes. It makes 2 quarts.

Performance: Decent but inconsistent. The texture depends heavily on your base recipe and how long you churn. The bowl's freezing capacity fades after 20 minutes — if you churn too long, the ice cream starts melting. Results are good but not as smooth as the Breville or Ninja.

What we love: The price ($59-$79) is unbeatable if you already own a KitchenAid. It's also easy to store — just the bowl and paddle. The 2-quart capacity is generous.

Tradeoffs: The pre-freeze requirement (15 hours) is even longer than the Ninja. The bowl takes up significant freezer space. You can't make multiple batches in a row — the bowl needs to re-freeze for 15 hours. And it only makes ice cream — no sorbet or gelato modes.

FAQ

Q: Can I use the Ninja Creami without freezing the base for 24 hours?

No. The Creami requires a completely frozen solid block. If you try to process a partially frozen base, the blade will struggle and could damage the machine. Plan ahead.

Q: Does the Breville Smart Scoop need a special outlet?

No. It plugs into a standard 120V outlet. But it draws about 800 watts, so don't use it on the same circuit as a microwave or toaster.

Q: Which machine makes the creamiest ice cream?

The Breville Smart Scoop. Its scraping paddle and precise temperature control produce the smoothest, most airy texture. The Ninja Creami is close but slightly denser.

Q: Can I make vegan or dairy-free ice cream in these machines?

Yes. Both work well with coconut milk, almond milk, or oat milk bases. The Ninja Creami is particularly good with fruit sorbets and dairy-free gelato. Just adjust the sugar content — alcohol or sugar helps prevent ice crystals.

Q: How long does the Ninja Creami take to process a pint?

About 2-5 minutes depending on the mode. Ice cream takes 3 minutes, sorbet takes 2 minutes, and gelato takes 4 minutes. Milkshake mode is the fastest at 2 minutes.

Q: Is the Breville Smart Scoop worth the extra money?

If you make ice cream more than once a month and value convenience, yes. The no-pre-freeze feature and keep-cool function are game-changers. If you make it rarely or want to experiment with flavors, the Ninja Creami is better value.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Breville Smart Scoop if you want the best-tasting ice cream with zero planning — just pour in your base, press a button, and come back to perfect frozen dessert that stays cold for hours. It's expensive but worth every penny for serious home chefs. Buy the Ninja Creami if you're on a budget, love experimenting with small-batch flavors, and don't mind freezing your base a day ahead. It makes surprisingly good ice cream for half the price. Skip the Cuisinart and Whynter unless you specifically need a compressor machine but can't justify the Breville's cost. And only buy the KitchenAid attachment if you already own a stand mixer and want a cheap way to dip your toe into ice cream making.
