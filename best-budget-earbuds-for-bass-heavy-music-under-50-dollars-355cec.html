Budget Bass Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters

Before you click buy, understand these four specs. They make or break the bass experience.

1. Driver Size vs. Tuning

Bigger drivers (10mm+) often move more air, but a cheap 12mm driver can sound worse than a well-tuned 8mm. Look for dynamic drivers with a bass-boosted EQ out of the box. The Soundcore P20i uses an 8mm driver with a custom "BassUp" mode—it hits harder than many 10mm competitors because the tuning is aggressive.

2. Codec Support (AAC vs. aptX)

On Android, aptX helps with latency and detail. On iPhone, AAC is standard. At this price, you mostly get AAC and SBC. Don't obsess over codecs—focus on the physical tuning. A good SBC tune beats a bad aptX tune every time.

3. Fit and Seal

Bass needs a tight seal. If the earbud doesn't sit snug in your ear canal, all the bass leaks out. Look for multiple ear tip sizes included and a lightweight body (under 5g per bud). The JBL Vibe Beam has a bulky stem that can feel loose for small ears—try before you buy if possible.

4. Battery Life vs. Size

Bigger batteries mean bigger cases. Under $50, expect 6-9 hours per charge with a case that adds 20-30 more. If you need all-day battery, the Redmi Buds 5 deliver 10 hours but with a bulky case. If you want pocketable, the Soundcore P20i is smaller but gives 7.5 hours.

Pro tip: Don't buy anything with a "gaming mode" that sacrifices bass for latency. You'll hear less thump and feel the lag anyway.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to spend more than $50 for good bass?

No. The Soundcore P20i at $25 outperforms many $60 buds in bass depth. You're paying for ANC and app polish beyond $50, not raw thump.

What's the difference between "bass" and "sub-bass"?

Bass is the thump you feel (80-250Hz). Sub-bass is the deep rumble below 60Hz—the part that shakes your car mirrors. The P20i and Redmi Buds 5 do sub-bass well. The Tozo T6 only does upper bass.

Can I EQ more bass into any earbuds?

Yes, but boosting a weak driver causes distortion. If the physical driver can't handle it, you'll hear crackling. Start with a well-tuned bud first.

Are these good for phone calls?

No. All five have subpar mics. If calls are a priority, get the JBL Vibe Beam—it's the least terrible. Otherwise, use your phone's mic.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Soundcore by Anker P20i if you want the most bass per dollar—period. The BassUp mode, deep sub-bass, and tiny price make it the undisputed king under $50. But for pure, chest-thumping bass on a budget, the P20i is the only answer.
