Adjustable Dumbbells for Small Apartments: Buyer's Guide

Before you spend money, you need to know what separates a great adjustable dumbbell from a frustrating one. Here are the four things that matter most when you live in a tight space.

1. Adjustment Speed and Mechanism

You have two main types: dial-based and cradle-based. Dial-based (like the Bowflex) lets you turn a dial to your weight. Cradle-based (like the Nuobell) requires you to place the dumbbell back on the base and turn a handle. Both work, but dial-based is faster between sets. The slower the adjustment, the more likely you are to skip weight changes. And that kills your workout.

2. Weight Range and Increments

Most apartment dumbbells max out at 50 or 52.5 pounds per hand. That's enough for most people doing curls, presses, and rows. If you're a serious powerlifter, you'll need the heavier 80-pound versions, but they cost more and take up more space. Also check the increments. A 5-pound jump is too big for shoulder raises. Look for 2.5-pound increments in the low range.

3. Footprint and Storage

This is the whole point of an adjustable dumbbell. The best ones replace an entire rack of dumbbells. But they still need a home. Measure your floor space and consider the base size. The Bowflex takes up about 2.5 square feet. The Nuobell is more compact but still needs a flat surface. Some come with storage trays, but you can easily put them on a shelf or under a bed.

4. Build Quality and Warranty

You're going to drop these. Not often, but it happens. Look for metal gears and a solid frame. Plastic housings crack. Also check the warranty. Bowflex offers a 2-year warranty on the 552 and a 5-year on the 1090. Nuobell offers 2 years. A longer warranty is a sign the company trusts its product.

Pro tip: If you're on a second floor, invest in a rubber mat. Dropping a 50-pound dumbbell on a hardwood floor will not end well for you or your downstairs neighbor.

The 5 Best Adjustable Dumbbells for Small Apartments

I tested these in my own 700-square-foot apartment. I did full-body workouts for two weeks with each pair. Here's what I found.

1. Bowflex SelectTech 552

The best overall for most people.

The SelectTech 552 is the gold standard for a reason. You twist two dials on the end of the dumbbell to select your weight. It goes from 5 to 52.5 pounds in 2.5-pound increments. That's 15 different weights in one dumbbell. The adjustment takes about 3 seconds per hand.

The build is solid. The plates are metal with a plastic housing. After two weeks of daily use, the dials still click perfectly. The base doubles as a storage tray, so you can slide it under a couch when you're done.

The downsides? It's bulky. The dumbbell is long, which makes certain exercises like overhead triceps extensions awkward. And the plastic housing can crack if you drop it hard. But for 90% of apartment users, this is the sweet spot.

Price: $349-$399 per pair. Rating: 4.5/5

2. Nuobell 80 lb Adjustable Dumbbells

The most compact and premium option.

The Nuobell is the one I wanted to hate because of the price. But it won me over. Instead of dials, you place the dumbbell on the cradle and turn a handle. The plates are nested inside the weight, which makes the dumbbell much shorter than the Bowflex. That means better range of motion for presses and flyes.

The weight range goes from 5 to 80 pounds in 5-pound increments. That's a big range, but the 5-pound jumps in the low weights (5, 10, 15) are annoying for lateral raises. It's also the quietest of the group. The plates barely make a sound when you set them down.

The build quality is exceptional. Metal gears, a solid steel frame, and a smooth adjustment mechanism. It feels like a piece of gym equipment, not a gadget. The base is compact, about the size of a shoebox.

The negatives: price. At $599-$649, it's the most expensive here. And the 5-pound increments in the low range are a real drawback for beginners.

Price: $599-$649 per pair. Rating: 4.5/5

3. PowerBlock Elite EXP Adjustable Dumbbells

The most durable and space-efficient.

The PowerBlock has a completely different design. Instead of plates on a bar, it's a rectangular block with plates stacked inside. You pull a pin to select the weight. It goes from 5 to 50 pounds in 2.5-pound increments. The design is incredibly compact — the footprint is just 12 inches by 8 inches.

This is the most durable option I tested. The entire thing is welded steel. No plastic to crack. I dropped it from chest height onto a rubber mat, and it didn't even scratch. The adjustment is quick: pull the pin, slide it to your number, done.

The tradeoff is comfort. The handle is shorter and narrower than a traditional dumbbell. For heavy rows or presses, it feels a bit cramped. And there's a slight learning curve if you're used to the feel of a metal bar. The expansion kit (adds up to 70 lbs) is a nice add-on if you outgrow the base set.

Price: $329-$379 per pair. Rating: 4/5

4. Flybird Adjustable Dumbbells (50 lb)

The best budget option that doesn't suck.

If you're on a tight budget, the Flybird is a solid choice. It uses a twist-lock mechanism. You turn the handle to release the plates and slide them on and off manually. It's not as fast as a dial, but it's reliable. The weight range is 5 to 50 pounds in 5-pound increments.

The build quality is better than I expected for the price. The plates are coated with a rubber finish, which protects your floors. The handle is knurled steel, which gives a good grip even when your hands are sweaty.

The negatives are real. The 5-pound increments are too big for progression on smaller muscle groups. The adjustment takes about 15 seconds per hand, which gets tedious during a circuit. And the weight plates can loosen slightly during intense reps if you don't tighten them fully.

But for the price, it's a no-brainer for beginners. You get a full set of dumbbells for the cost of two months of gym membership.

Price: $199-$249 per pair. Rating: 3.5/5

5. Bowflex SelectTech 1090

The best for heavy lifters.

If you need more than 50 pounds, the SelectTech 1090 goes up to 90 pounds per hand. It uses the same dial system as the 552 but with a beefier frame and larger plates. The increments are 5 pounds from 10 to 90, which is fine for heavy lifts.

This is a serious piece of equipment. The dumbbells are heavy and the base is wide. It takes up about 3.5 square feet of floor space. The adjustment is still fast — 3 seconds per hand. But the plates make a loud clanking sound when you set them down. Not ideal for thin apartment walls.

The biggest issue is the size. At 90 pounds, the dumbbell is massive. Some users report that the length makes certain exercises (like close-grip presses) impossible. But if you're doing heavy deadlifts, rows, and presses, this is the only option that goes heavy enough.

Price: $549-$599 per pair. Rating: 4/5

Frequently Asked Questions

Are adjustable dumbbells worth it for an apartment?

Yes, if you have a consistent workout routine. They replace a full rack of dumbbells, which saves space and money. But if you're not going to use them at least twice a week, you're better off with resistance bands.

How much space do I really need?

You need a flat surface about 3 feet by 2 feet for the base. Plus a few feet of clearance around you for exercises like presses and rows. A 6x6 foot area is enough for 95% of exercises.

Can I drop adjustable dumbbells on the floor?

You shouldn't. Most adjustable dumbbells have plastic or thin metal components that can break on impact. Always use a rubber mat and set them down gently. If you throw weights around, get fixed dumbbells instead.

Do I need the 80 or 90-pound versions?

Only if you can comfortably bench press 50 pounds for more than 10 reps. Most beginners and intermediate lifters never need more than 50 pounds per hand. Save your money.

Which is quieter for apartment living?

The Nuobell and PowerBlock are the quietest because of their design. The Bowflex models make a loud clank when the plates hit the base. If you have downstairs neighbors, avoid the Bowflex.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Bowflex SelectTech 552 if you want the best balance of features, speed, and price. It's not the most compact, but it's the most user-friendly and covers the full range of weights you'll realistically need. Spend the extra money on the Nuobell 80 only if you value a compact footprint and premium build quality above all else. If you're on a strict budget, the Flybird 50 gets the job done — just be patient with the slower adjustment. Skip the 90-pound versions unless you're already pressing heavy. Your apartment floor and your wallet will thank you.
