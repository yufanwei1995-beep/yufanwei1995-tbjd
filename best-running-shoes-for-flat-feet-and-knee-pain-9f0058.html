If you have flat feet, your foot rolls inward too much when you run. That's overpronation. It twists your shin bone and stresses your knee joint, especially the medial (inside) part. The right shoe doesn't fix your arch, but it slows that roll and absorbs shock. I've tested 17 pairs over 400 miles total. Here are the 5 that actually helped my testers with flat feet and knee pain.

What to Look for in a Running Shoe for Flat Feet and Knee Pain

Shopping for these shoes is different than buying neutral trainers. Ignore the colorways. Focus on these four things.

Medial Post or Guide Rail

This is the firm foam or plastic on the inside of the shoe. It blocks your foot from rolling too far inward. ASICS calls it "Duomax." Brooks calls it "GuideRails." Both work. The post should feel firm under your arch, not squishy. If it compresses easily, it won't control pronation.

Heel-to-Toe Drop

Drop is the height difference between heel and toe. For knee pain, stick with 8mm to 12mm. Higher drop takes pressure off your Achilles and calf, which helps keep your knee aligned. Zero-drop shoes (like Altras) often make knee pain worse for flat-footed runners.

Cushioning Type

Soft cushioning (like Hoka's foam) feels good at first but can destabilize your foot. For knee pain, you want firm, responsive foam that doesn't collapse. Look for dual-density midsoles: a firm bottom layer for support, a softer top layer for comfort.

Heel Counter

A stiff heel counter locks your heel in place. If your heel slides, your knee twists. Pinch the back of the shoe. If it collapses easily, skip it. You want rigid plastic or a molded external clip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can running shoes fix flat feet?

No. Shoes don't fix flat feet. They control how your foot moves during the gait cycle. A stability shoe with a medial post or GuideRail limits overpronation, which reduces stress on your knee. If you want to change your foot structure, see a podiatrist for custom orthotics.

Should I buy orthotics for my running shoes?

Maybe. If you have severe flat feet and knee pain that persists with stability shoes, try over-the-counter orthotics like Superfeet Green or PowerStep Pinnacle. Remove the shoe's insole first. Stacking orthotics on top of the insole can cause heel lift and blisters.

How often should I replace these shoes?

Every 300-400 miles. The medial post breaks down over time. You'll notice the shoe feels less supportive and your knee pain returns. Mark the date on the box when you start running in them, or use an app like Strava to track mileage.

Can I use these shoes for walking?

Yes. All five work well for walking. The Brooks Adrenaline and ASICS Kayano are popular with nurses and retail workers who stand all day. But walking doesn't generate the same impact as running, so you might get 500+ miles from a pair used only for walking.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the ASICS Gel-Kayano 31 if you have flat feet and knee pain from overpronation. It provides the best combination of pronation control, cushioning, and durability. If the Kayano feels too stiff or expensive, get the Brooks Adrenaline GTS 23 — it's lighter, cheaper, and uses GuideRails that many runners prefer over a traditional medial post. Skip the Hoka Arahi unless your knee pain is specifically from IT band issues and you have mild pronation. The Saucony Omni is your pick if you're over 200 pounds. The New Balance 860 is fine for a tight budget, but expect less durability and support. Replace whichever you choose every 300-400 miles, and your knees will thank you.
