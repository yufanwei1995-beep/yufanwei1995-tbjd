What to Look for in a Small-Footprint Ice Maker

Don't buy on looks. Here are the specs that actually matter when counter space is precious.

Physical Dimensions

Measure your space first. Most "compact" makers are 12-14 inches deep and 9-12 inches wide. The GE Opal is the outlier at 16.5 inches deep and 11.5 inches wide — it's a chunky boy. The Frigidaire EFIC117 is slimmer at 12.2 inches wide and 12.6 inches deep. Know your numbers before you shop.

Ice Type: Nugget vs. Bullet

Nugget (Sonic-style) ice is soft and chewable. Bullet ice is hard, clear-ish cubes that look like little cylinders. Nugget machines are bigger, louder, and pricier. Bullet machines are cheaper, smaller, and faster. For small kitchens, bullet ice is usually the smarter trade-off — unless you specifically want chewable ice, then the Opal is worth the space.

Production Speed and Capacity

Look at two numbers: ice per day (usually 26-40 lbs) and first batch time (6-12 minutes). For a couple, 26 lbs/day is plenty. For a family or frequent host, aim for 35+ lbs/day. Also check the reservoir capacity — smaller machines hold 1.5-2 lbs, which means more refills and more melting between batches.

Noise Level

Small machines are loud. The compressor hums, and ice dropping into the basket clatters. The Frigidaire measures around 48 dB — like a quiet conversation. The Opal hits 55 dB — noticeably louder. If you're watching TV nearby, this matters.

Self-Cleaning Function

Ice makers get slimy mold if you don't clean them. A self-clean cycle (most have it) uses a button press and vinegar/water solution. Without it, you're manually scrubbing every two weeks. Don't skip this feature.

FAQ

How often do I need to clean a countertop ice maker?

Every 1-2 weeks. The self-clean cycle with a 50/50 vinegar and water solution takes 15 minutes. Skipping it leads to mold and slime in the water lines. Set a reminder on your phone.

Do these machines need a drain?

No. All the models here are countertop units with a water reservoir you fill manually. The melted ice drains back into the reservoir and gets used for the next batch. No plumbing required.

Why is my ice cloudy or melting fast?

Cloudy ice is normal — it's from rapid freezing, not poor quality. Melting fast usually means the ice basket is full and the machine is cycling warmer water. Dump the basket every few hours if you're not using it.

Can I use these in an RV or camper?

Yes. They run on standard 120V outlets and don't need a water line. The Euhomy and Frigidaire are popular RV picks because of their small footprint. Just note they vibrate a bit — set them on a non-slip mat.

Do nugget ice makers use more electricity?

Slightly. The Opal pulls about 200 watts during operation vs. 150 watts for bullet machines. Over a month of daily use, the difference is a couple of dollars. Not a deciding factor.

Bottom Line

If you have a normal small kitchen and want reliable ice without the footprint of a mini-fridge, get the Frigidaire EFIC117. It's compact, quiet, well-built, and under $130. If you're a nugget ice devotee and can spare 16.5 inches of depth, the GE Opal is worth every penny — just know it's a counter hog. For the absolute tightest spaces, the Euhomy fits where nothing else will, but you'll trade build quality and noise for that footprint. Measure your space, decide if you need chewable ice, and buy accordingly.
