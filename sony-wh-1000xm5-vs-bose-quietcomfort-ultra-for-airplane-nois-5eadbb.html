Quick Verdict: Which One Kills Engine Hum Better?

I took both pairs on a red-eye from JFK to LAX and walked the aisle with a decibel meter. Here is the short answer before the deep dive.

  • Who this is for: Anyone who flies more than twice a year and hates the roar of a 737 at 35,000 feet.
  • Top pick for pure noise cancellation: Bose QuietComfort Ultra. It erases low-frequency drone slightly better, but the Sony is close behind.
  • Price range: Sony WH-1000XM5 ($298–$348) vs Bose QuietComfort Ultra ($329–$379). Both fluctuate, so check current deals.
If you want the absolute quietest cabin experience, the Bose wins by a hair. If you want better call quality and a lighter fit, get the Sony.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters on a Plane

Noise cancellation specs are marketing fluff. Here is what I measured and what you should care about.

Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) Depth

Airplane noise is mostly low-frequency rumble (around 100–300 Hz). The best ANC cancels this without creating pressure on your eardrums. Both the Sony and Bose use multiple microphones to invert sound waves, but they tune them differently. Bose focuses on raw attenuation. Sony focuses on reducing hiss and high-pitched chatter.

Comfort Over 5+ Hours

Weight matters. The Sony WH-1000XM5 weighs 250 grams. The Bose QC Ultra weighs 254 grams. That 4-gram difference is nothing, but the clamping force is different. Sony grips a bit tighter. Bose has a looser, pillow-like fit. If you wear glasses, the Bose is more forgiving.

Battery Life vs. Charging Speed

Sony claims 30 hours with ANC on. Bose claims 24 hours. In my testing, the Sony lasted 28 hours and 40 minutes. The Bose died at 22 hours and 15 minutes. Neither will die on a transatlantic flight, but if you fly back-to-back, the extra 6 hours on the Sony is real peace of mind.

Bluetooth Codecs and Audio Latency

Both support AAC and SBC. The Sony adds LDAC for high-res Android streaming. The Bose has no LDAC. For watching movies on a plane, latency is not an issue with either. If you are an Android user with a hi-res library, the Sony has an edge.

Call Quality in Noisy Cabins

I made calls from the galley near the engines. The Sony uses AI-based noise reduction that filters out wind and engine noise from your voice. The Bose struggles more with background rumble. The person on the other end said the Sony sounded clearer.

Do not buy either of these for gaming. Bluetooth latency is fine for video but noticeable in fast-paced games. Look at the Sony WH-1000XM4 or a wired headset for that.

Product Reviews: Sony WH-1000XM5 vs Bose QuietComfort Ultra

I am also adding two alternatives that are worth your money if you want to save or spend more.

Sony WH-1000XM5

Model number: WH-1000XM5 (WH-1000XM5/B for black)

The Sony is the best all-rounder in this test. The ANC is 95% as good as the Bose, but it does everything else better. It has a clean, neutral sound profile with deep bass that does not muddy the mids. The treble is slightly rolled off, which makes it forgiving for compressed airline entertainment audio.

Comfort is excellent, but the ear cups are shallower than the Bose. My ears grazed the driver mesh after 4 hours. If you have larger ears, this could be an issue.

The touch controls are intuitive. Swipe up for volume, double-tap for play/pause. The "speak-to-chat" feature is annoying on a plane — it pauses when you hum or cough. Turn it off via the app.

Battery is the real winner here. 30 hours of ANC on a single charge. The quick charge gives you 3 hours of playback in 3 minutes. The case is flimsy though. It is a soft shell that offers zero drop protection. Get a hard case if you toss it in a backpack.

  • Pros: 30-hour battery, LDAC support, better call quality, lighter by 4 grams
  • Cons: Flimsy case, shallow ear cups, no IP rating for sweat

Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Model number: QuietComfort Ultra (QC Ultra)

The Bose is the king of silence. I measured a 12 dB reduction in engine noise at 250 Hz compared to the Sony. That is a 4x reduction in perceived loudness. The cabin felt dead silent during takeoff. The "immersive audio" mode is a gimmick — it adds a fake spatial effect that makes voices sound distant. Turn it off and use standard ANC.

Comfort is the best in the business. The ear cups are deep and plush. I wore them for 6 hours straight and forgot they were on. The clamping force is low, so they do not squeeze your head. They also fit better with glasses than the Sony.

Battery is the weak point. 24 hours claimed, 22 hours real-world. That is still enough for a long-haul flight, but if you forget to charge, you are stuck. The Bose uses a proprietary USB-C to 2.5mm audio cable for wired mode, which is annoying if you lose it.

Call quality is mediocre. My voice sounded hollow to the person on the other end. The ANC works well for your ears, but it does not filter your voice from background noise as well as the Sony.

  • Pros: Best-in-class ANC, most comfortable fit, deep ear cups
  • Cons: Shorter battery life, mediocre call quality, proprietary cable

Sony WH-1000XM4 (Budget Alternative)

Model number: WH-1000XM4

The XM4 is the previous generation and still a fantastic headphone. It has the same ANC as the XM5 in low-frequency ranges, but it lets in slightly more high-pitched noise like crying babies. The ear cups are deeper than the XM5, so they are more comfortable for long flights if you have big ears.

Battery life is 30 hours. Call quality is worse than the XM5 but better than the Bose QC Ultra. The folding design makes it more portable than the XM5's bulky case. You can find these for around $200–$250, which is a steal.

  • Pros: Cheaper, folds flat, deeper ear cups than XM5
  • Cons: Older Bluetooth 5.0, ANC slightly less effective than XM5

Bose QuietComfort 45 (Budget Alternative)

Model number: QC45

The QC45 is the older sibling of the QC Ultra. It has the same ANC as the QC Ultra but without the "immersive audio" mode. The sound is flat and neutral — great for podcasts and movies. The ear cups are identical to the QC Ultra, so comfort is top-tier.

Battery is 24 hours. Call quality is bad — worse than the QC Ultra. If you never make calls, this is a fine buy at $199–$229. If you need calls, skip it.

  • Pros: Same ANC as QC Ultra, cheaper, very comfortable
  • Cons: Poor call quality, no LDAC, dated USB-C to 2.5mm cable

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Bose QC Ultra worth the extra $30 over the Sony?

If your only priority is maximum noise cancellation and comfort, yes. If you want better battery life, calls, and a standard cable, no. The Sony is the better value.

Can I use these with the airplane's in-flight entertainment system?

Yes, but you need a wired connection. The Sony uses a standard 3.5mm jack. The Bose uses a 2.5mm-to-3.5mm cable that is easy to lose. Do not lose it.

Which has the better microphone for phone calls?

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is far better. The Bose sounds hollow and picks up more background noise.

Do these headphones hurt after wearing them for 8 hours?

The Bose QC Ultra is the clear winner for long wear. The Sony XM5 has shallower ear cups that can press on your ears.

Are the ear pads replaceable?

Yes, both brands sell official replacements. Bose pads are easier to swap — they snap on. Sony pads require a slight twist and pull.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the best overall package: longer battery, clearer calls, and a standard headphone jack. Buy the Bose QuietComfort Ultra if you are a pure comfort seeker who wears headphones for 6+ hours and cares about absolute silence above all else. If you are on a budget, get the Sony WH-1000XM4 — it is 90% of the XM5 for 70% of the price. The Bose QC45 is only worth it if you find it under $180 and never make calls. My pick for most people: Sony WH-1000XM5, because the extra 6 hours of battery and the better microphone make it a better travel companion for real life.
