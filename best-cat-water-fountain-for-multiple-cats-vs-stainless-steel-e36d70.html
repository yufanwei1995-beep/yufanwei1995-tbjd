Multiple cats means multiple problems. One cat won't drink. The other knocks over the bowl. And every morning, there's a film of slime and hair floating in the water. A decent fountain solves the freshness issue, but the real question is material: plastic vs. stainless steel. I've tested both for years, and here's the honest breakdown.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before I get into the specific models, you need to know what actually matters when you have more than one feline.

Capacity is King

A single cat drinks about 3.5–4.5 oz of water per 5 lbs of body weight daily. Two 10-lb cats need roughly 16 oz minimum, but that's just baseline. In summer, or if they eat dry food, they'll drink more. You want a reservoir that holds at least 80 oz for two cats. For three or more, bump that to 100 oz+. Otherwise, you're refilling every day, which defeats the purpose of a fountain.

Plastic vs. Stainless Steel — The Core Showdown

Plastic fountains are cheaper ($30–$50) and lighter. But here's the issue: plastic scratches easily, and those micro-scratches are bacteria magnets. Even if you wash it daily, you're fighting a losing battle. Also, some cats get "whisker fatigue" from the smell of plastic and refuse to drink.

Stainless steel doesn't scratch as easily, doesn't hold odors, and is dishwasher safe. The downside? It's heavier and usually costs $20–$30 more. But for multi-cat homes, the durability pays off in the long run — you won't need to replace it in 18 months because the surface is grooved and gross.

Filter System Matters

With multiple cats, you're dealing with more hair and debris. Look for a fountain with a multi-stage filter: foam to catch hair, activated carbon to remove odors, and ion-exchange resin to soften water. If the filter is just a single piece of foam, skip it. You'll be changing it constantly.

Noise Level

Most pumps run 40–50 dB, which is about the volume of a quiet library. But some cheap models buzz louder, especially when water levels drop. A loud fountain will scare your cats away — and they'll go back to drinking from the toilet. Look for pumps with adjustable flow or a "quiet" rating in the product specs.

Ease of Cleaning

You'll clean this thing every 2–3 days with multiple cats. If the fountain has weird crevices, angled corners, or a pump that disassembles into 5 tiny pieces, you'll hate it by day 10. Stainless steel models win here because they're usually one solid piece you can toss in the dishwasher. Plastic models require hand-washing with warm soapy water to avoid warping.

5. Stainless Steel Bowl (Static) — The Honest Alternative

Rating: 3.8/5

Let's be real: a simple stainless steel bowl is the cheapest "fountain alternative." A 2-pack of 32-oz bowls runs about $15. You put one on each floor, and you rotate them daily — one in use, one in the dishwasher.

The pros are obvious: zero plastic, zero filter costs, zero pump failures, zero electricity. Cats can't knock them over because they're heavy and low-profile. Some cats actually prefer still water because it doesn't splash their whiskers.

The con is that you're changing water twice a day with multiple cats. Stagnant water grows bacteria within 12 hours, even in steel. And you lose the behavioral benefit — many cats drink more from moving water because their instincts tell them running water is safer.

Who it's for: Minimalists, people who travel with their cats, or owners whose cats simply refuse to use a fountain.

Trade-off: No moving water means less drinking. If your cat is already a poor drinker, a bowl won't fix that.

FAQ

Q: Do cats actually drink more from a fountain?

Yes, most do. Studies show moving water increases consumption by 10–30% in cats who are hesitant drinkers. But it's not universal — some cats don't care. Watch your cat for a week before committing.

Q: How often do I really need to clean the fountain with multiple cats?

Every 2–3 days. The pump and basin will develop a biofilm that feels slimy if you skip this. Stainless steel is easier to wipe clean. Plastic needs a thorough scrub with dish soap.

Q: Can I use a fountain for kittens?

Yes, but choose a low-flow setting if available. The Catit Flower has a gentle stream that works well. Avoid the strong jet sprays on the PetSafe models until kittens are 6+ months.

Q: Are replacement filters expensive?

Expect $10–$15 per month per fountain. Some brands offer cheaper off-brand filter packs, but quality varies. Stick with OEM filters for reliability.

Q: Will the pump break if it runs dry?

Yes. Most pumps burn out if they run dry for more than 10 minutes. Get a fountain with a low-water indicator light (like the Drinkwell Platinum) or set a phone reminder to check levels daily.
