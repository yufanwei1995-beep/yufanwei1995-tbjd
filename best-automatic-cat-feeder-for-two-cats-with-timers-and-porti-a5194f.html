We tested five popular automatic feeders designed for multi-cat use, focusing on timer accuracy, portion control, and whether they can actually tell your cats apart.

What to Look For in a Two-Cat Feeder

Before you buy, understand this: most feeders can't tell your cats apart. If you have one chunky cat and one grazer, a single feeder with one bowl won't solve your problem. You need either two separate feeders or a single feeder with a microchip reader.

Key Features to Consider

  • Microchip or RFID Recognition — This is the only way to ensure Cat A doesn't eat Cat B's food. The feeder only opens for the specific microchip. If your cats aren't chipped, you can use the included RFID collar tags.
  • Portion Control Precision — Look for feeders that dispense in small increments (like 1/8 cup or 10 grams). Some cheap feeders only do 1/4 cup at a time, which is too much for a small meal.
  • Timer Flexibility — How many meals per day? Can you set different times for each cat? The best feeders allow up to 10 meals daily with 1-minute increments.
  • Number of Bowls — A single-bowl feeder with a partition is fine, but a true two-bowl system is better for wet food or for cats that hate each other. We tested both.
  • Battery Life & Backup — If the power goes out, you need battery backup. Check if it runs on AC power with battery backup (like the PETLIBRO) or just batteries alone.
  • Cleaning Ease — The hopper and bowl need to come apart. If it's not dishwasher-safe, you'll hate it in a month.
  • App Connectivity — Nice to have, not essential. It lets you check if food was dispensed, but it adds $50 to the price.
Pro tip: If your cats have different dietary needs, buy two separate single-cat feeders instead of one dual feeder. It's often cheaper and gives you independent control.

Product Reviews

1. PETLIBRO PLISTA (PL-AT005)

The PETLIBRO PLISTA is the most feature-complete feeder we tested. It has a camera, two-way audio, and a microchip reader. The portion control is excellent — you can adjust in 10-gram increments, which is rare. The app is intuitive and gives you a feeding log with photos.

The big selling point for two cats is the microchip reader. It works flawlessly with your cat's existing chip. Without a chip, you get two RFID collar tags. The only downside? It's a single-bowl feeder. If both cats are chipped, they'll eat from the same bowl, which defeats the purpose of separate portions. You'll need two of these, and that gets expensive.

Battery life is a claimed 180 days on 4 D batteries with daily use. In our testing, it lasted about 5 months. The camera has night vision and a 1080p feed, which is sharp enough to see who's eating. The app is responsive but has occasional lag when loading the live view.

The ugly: It's $250. And the hopper only holds 4L, which is fine for two cats but tight for a week-long vacation. The bowl is stainless steel, but the tray is plastic and can scratch.

2. Catit PIXI Smart Feeder

The Catit PIXI is the budget winner. At $80, it does 90% of what the PETLIBRO does, minus the camera. It has a microchip reader, portion control in 10-gram increments, and an app that works reliably. The design is sleek — it looks like a modern appliance, not a plastic box.

Setup took 5 minutes. The app walked me through connecting to Wi-Fi and scanning the microchips. The portion control is accurate to within 2 grams, which impressed me. The hopper holds 3.5L, which is decent.

The tradeoff: The app is basic. You can't see feeding history beyond the last 10 meals. The battery backup is AA batteries, but it only uses them for emergencies — it doesn't run on batteries alone. The bowl is wide but shallow, which is good for whisker fatigue.

For two cats, you'll need two PIXI feeders. At $80 each, that's $160 total, which is still less than one PETLIBRO. This is the smart budget move.

3. SureFeed Microchip Pet Feeder

This is a different beast. The SureFeed is a covered feeder with a lid that opens only for the chipped cat. It's perfect for wet food because the lid keeps flies away and food moist. For two cats, you can buy the Dual version — it's a single base with two separate compartments, each with its own lid and microchip reader.

The Dual version is $200 and is the only feeder we tested that genuinely handles two cats with separate portions in one unit. The portion control is manual — you fill the bowl, and the feeder just controls access. There's no timer or automatic dispensing. You have to refill it daily.

If your problem is that one cat steals the other's food, this is the answer. If your problem is that you're not home to feed them on time, this won't help. It requires daily refilling, which defeats the purpose of automation.

The good: It's built like a tank. The lid mechanism is quiet and reliable. It runs on 3 C batteries that last 6 months. The bad: No timer, no app, no portion control. It's a gatekeeper, not a dispenser.

4. PetSafe Healthy Pet Simply Feed

The PetSafe Simply Feed is the old reliable. It's been around for years, and it still works. It has a timer with up to 12 meals per day, portion control in 1/2-cup increments (yes, 1/2 cup — that's a lot), and a slide-lock lid. It's $120.

The portion control is the weakest part. The smallest increment is 1/2 cup, which is too much for most cats. You can adjust it by turning the dial to "partial" servings, but it's not precise. For context, a typical cat meal is 1/4 to 1/3 cup. This feeder can't do that accurately.

It has a "Slow Feed" option that dispenses over 15 minutes, which is good for gulpers. But for two cats, you're back to the same problem — one bowl, no identification. You'd need two units, and at $120 each, that's $240 for imprecise portioning.

Who should buy this: Someone with a single cat that eats a set amount and doesn't need microchip security. For two cats with different needs, skip it.

5. WOpet SmartFeeder (W3)

The WOpet W3 is a cheap alternative at $60. It has a timer, portion control in 1/8 cup increments, and a voice recorder to call your cats. The app is clunky but functional. It dispenses reliably in our 2-week test.

The portion control is 1/8 cup, which is better than the PetSafe but still not precise. The hopper holds 4L. It does NOT have a microchip reader, so there's no way to prevent one cat from eating both servings.

Battery life is poor — about 2 weeks on 3 D batteries. The plastic feels thin, and the bowl is small. It's fine for a single cat on a budget, but for two cats, you'll be frustrated by the lack of identification and the flimsy build.

The bottom line: It's a $60 feeder that works, but it's not suited for multi-cat households. If you're on a strict budget and your cats get along, you could buy two and set them in different rooms. But don't expect precision or durability.

FAQ

Can one feeder feed two cats?

Yes, but not with separate portions unless it's the SureFeed Dual. Most feeders have one bowl. If your cats eat the same food and same amount, a single feeder works fine. If not, buy two.

Do I need a microchip feeder?

Only if one cat is stealing the other's food. If they're polite eaters, skip the added cost. If you have a chunky cat and a skinny cat, the microchip feature is worth every penny.

How long does a feeder last on vacation?

It depends on the hopper size and how many meals per day. A 4L hopper with 2 meals a day for 2 cats lasts about 5 days. For a week-long trip, you'll need a backup plan or a bigger hopper.

Are these feeders safe for wet food?

No. Only the SureFeed is designed for wet food, and it doesn't dispense — it just opens a lid. For wet food, you need a feeder with a cooling compartment, which costs $300+. Most people use dry food in automatic feeders.

Bottom Line

If your cats have different dietary needs, buy two Catit PIXI feeders. At $80 each, it's the best balance of price, precision, and microchip security. If you have a single cat and want the best tech, the PETLIBRO PLISTA is worth the $250. If your problem is food theft, not scheduling, get the SureFeed Dual. Skip the PetSafe and WOpet — they're too imprecise for multi-cat homes.
