What to Look For in a Microwave-Safe Glass Container

Before the reviews, here’s the buyer’s guide. These are the four things that actually matter when reheating in a microwave.

1. Type of Glass: Borosilicate vs Tempered Soda-Lime

Borosilicate glass (like old-school Pyrex from Europe) handles rapid temperature swings better. It can go from freezer to microwave without shattering. Tempered soda-lime glass (what US Pyrex is now) is more resistant to impacts but is more prone to thermal shock — meaning if you put a cold glass dish straight into a hot microwave, it can crack. For microwave reheating specifically, borosilicate is safer. For oven or dishwasher use, tempered soda-lime is fine. Check the label. If it doesn’t say “borosilicate,” it’s almost certainly soda-lime.

2. Lid Material and Seal

Microwave reheating creates steam. If the lid is too tight, pressure builds up and the lid pops off or warps. If it’s too loose, sauce splatters everywhere. The ideal lid has a small vent hole or a snap-lock that you can leave slightly open. Also check if the lid is BPA-free plastic or silicone. Silicone lasts longer and doesn’t warp in the microwave. Plastic lids warp after about 50 uses — budget brands warp faster.

3. Shape and Depth

Shallow, wide containers reheat more evenly than deep, narrow ones. Microwaves penetrate food from the outside in. A 2-inch deep container of soup will have cold spots in the middle. A 1.5-inch deep container of rice reheats perfectly. For soups and stews, you want a wider bowl with a lower profile, not a tall soup mug.

4. Thermal Shock Resistance

This is the big one. You’re putting a cold container in the microwave. The glass heats up fast. If it’s not designed for that, it cracks. Look for containers rated for at least a 100°C (212°F) temperature difference. Most cheap glass containers are only rated for 80°C. That’s the difference between a container that lasts 5 years and one that shatters in month 3.

Pro tip: Never put a glass container straight from the freezer into a microwave. Even borosilicate glass can’t handle a 150°F temperature swing in 30 seconds. Let it sit at room temp for 5 minutes first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Pyrex actually microwave safe?

Yes, but with a caveat. Modern Pyrex in the US uses tempered soda-lime glass, which is microwave safe for reheating food that’s not too cold. The Pyrex Simply Store line (made in France) uses borosilicate glass, which is more resistant to thermal shock. The FridgeSmart line is technically microwave safe but cracked in my testing. Always check the specific line, not just the brand name.

Can I put a glass container straight from the freezer into the microwave?

No. Even borosilicate glass can crack if the temperature difference is too extreme. Let the container sit at room temperature for 5-10 minutes first. This allows the glass to slowly warm up before the microwave hits it with high heat.

How many times can I reuse the plastic lids?

Budget lids (Bayco, FridgeSmart) start warping after 30-50 microwave uses. Higher-end lids (Glasslock, Anchor Hocking) last 150-200 uses before the plastic degrades. If you notice the lid no longer seals tightly, replace it. A loose seal means steam escapes and your food heats unevenly.

What’s the difference between borosilicate and tempered soda-lime glass?

Borosilicate glass has a lower thermal expansion coefficient, meaning it expands less when heated. This makes it more resistant to cracking from rapid temperature changes. Tempered soda-lime glass is physically stronger (more impact-resistant) but cracks more easily under thermal stress. For microwave reheating, borosilicate is better. For oven baking, tempered soda-lime is fine.

Can I use these containers in the oven?

Most glass containers are oven safe up to 350-400°F. Check the bottom of the container for the temperature rating. The Glasslock set is rated to 400°F. The Pyrex Simply Store rounds are rated to 350°F. Do not put the lids in the oven — they’re plastic or silicone and will melt.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Pyrex Simply Store 3-Cup Round if you want the safest, most reliable microwave reheating experience. The borosilicate glass handles thermal shock better than anything else I tested, the heating is even, and the vented lid prevents splatters. It’s more expensive per container than the budget options, but it will last years without cracking or warping. If you’re on a budget, the Glasslock 18-Piece Set is the best value — just be careful about reheating very cold food at full power. Skip the Pyrex FridgeSmart entirely for microwave use. The thin glass is a liability. And if you mostly reheat rice, grains, or other dry food, the Anchor Hocking TrueSeal is your best bet for even heating. One final tip: always reheat at 50% power for the first 2 minutes, then finish at full power. It takes longer, but it prevents hot spots and reduces thermal stress on the glass. Your food and your containers will thank you.
