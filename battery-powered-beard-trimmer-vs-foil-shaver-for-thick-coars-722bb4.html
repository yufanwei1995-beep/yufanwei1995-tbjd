Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Coarse Hair

Before we dive into specific models, you need to understand the engineering. Thick hair isn't just "more" hair. It's denser, often curlier, and lies flatter against the skin. That changes everything.

Blade Quality and Material

Look for Japanese stainless steel (often listed as "HSS" or "DLC coated"). Cheap Chinese steel dulls in three weeks. The coating matters too – diamond-like carbon (DLC) reduces friction, which means less heat and less tugging on coarse strands.

Motor Power (The Real Spec)

Ignore the marketing about "linear motors." Look for the actual RPM or pivot speed. A trimmer for thick hair needs at least 6,000 RPM. A foil shaver needs at least 13,000 CPM (cuts per minute). Anything less will skip over the hair, not cut it.

Foil vs. Rotary vs. Blade

Foil shavers use oscillating blades under a thin metal mesh. They cut close but struggle with long hair. Rotary shavers (like Braun) use spinning heads – they handle contours better but often leave a 0.5mm shadow. Trimmers with a blade guard are for shaping, not close shaves.

The honest truth: If you want a truly smooth face, a foil shaver is the only way. If you want a styled beard or a 5 o'clock shadow look, you need a trimmer. You probably need both. But if you can only buy one for thick hair, read on.

Battery and Charging

Coarse hair drains batteries faster because the motor works harder. Look for a device with at least 50 minutes of cordless runtime. Better yet, find one that works while plugged in – most cheap trimmers refuse to operate on the cord, which is a dealbreaker when the battery dies mid-shave.

FAQ: Thick Hair Edition

Can I use a foil shaver on a full beard?

No. Foils clog instantly on hair longer than 1mm. If you have a beard, you need to trim it down to stubble first, then shave. That's why you need both tools.

Why does my trimmer pull hairs?

Two reasons: dull blades or a weak motor. If it's under 6,000 RPM, it doesn't have the torque to cut a thick hair cleanly – it rips it. Upgrade or replace the blade cartridge.

How often do I need to replace blades?

For coarse hair, replace trimmer blades every 6 months. Foil shaver heads every 18 months. If you notice more tugging or longer shave times, it's time to swap.

Is wet shaving better for thick hair?

For foil shavers, yes. The water softens the hair. But for trimmers, no – water rusts the blades. Keep them separate.
