Buyer's Guide: What to Look for After 50

Before we get into the reviews, you need to know what separates a good collagen from a waste of money. After 50, your body's natural collagen production drops by about 1% every year. You're not just topping off; you're trying to slow a leak. Here's what matters.

Type of Collagen

Most joint and skin products use Type I and Type III collagen. These are the building blocks for skin, hair, nails, and bones. Type II collagen is specifically for cartilage and joint tissue. If you have arthritis, you might want a product that includes Type II, but the science is weaker for it. For all-around benefits after 50, stick with a Type I and III blend.

Hydrolyzed vs. Undenatured

You want hydrolyzed collagen peptides. This means the collagen protein has been broken down into smaller peptides that your gut can absorb. Undenatured collagen (usually Type II) works differently—it's for immune modulation in joints, but it doesn't help skin. For skin and joints together, hydrolyzed is the way to go.

Dosage

Clinical studies show you need 10 to 15 grams per day for joint pain relief. For skin elasticity, studies show results at around 5 to 10 grams. Many cheap brands sell you 3-gram scoops and call it a day. That's not enough. Check the label. If you're not getting at least 10 grams per serving, you're paying for dust.

Sourcing and Purity

Bovine (cow) collagen is cheaper and has a neutral taste. Marine (fish) collagen has smaller peptides that may absorb better, but it smells fishy and costs more. Look for brands that disclose their sourcing and test for heavy metals. Marine collagen especially can be contaminated if not properly sourced.

Third-Party Testing

Supplements are not FDA-regulated. Look for a NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Choice seal. This means the product is tested for banned substances and heavy metals. It's non-negotiable at this age.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long until I see results?

Most clinical studies show 8 to 12 weeks for significant joint pain reduction. Skin elasticity improvements typically appear around week 8. Some people feel a difference in two weeks, but don't expect miracles overnight. Consistency matters more than brand.

Can I take collagen with my blood pressure meds?

Collagen peptides are generally safe with most medications. However, if you take blood thinners, check with your doctor first. Some collagen supplements have added Vitamin K, which can interfere with anticoagulants. Read the label.

What's better: powder or pills?

Powder, hands down. You need 10 grams per day. A typical pill contains 500 mg to 1 gram. You'd have to swallow 10 to 20 pills daily. That's ridiculous. Stick with powder.

Does collagen make you gain weight?

No. Collagen is a protein, and 10 grams is roughly 40 calories. It actually helps with satiety. It won't make you gain weight, but it's not a meal replacement either.

Can I take collagen with coffee?

Yes, but let the coffee cool slightly first. Extreme heat can denature the peptides. Most brands dissolve best in cold or lukewarm liquid. If you're adding it to hot coffee, stir well and drink it right away.
