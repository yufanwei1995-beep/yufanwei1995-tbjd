  • Who this is for: Photographers torn between a foldable phone and a traditional flagship. You want a great camera without sacrificing the big screen or the classic slab design.
  • Our top pick: iPhone 17 Pro Max for consistent, reliable photos in all conditions. Galaxy Z Fold 7 for versatility and the best telephoto zoom.

I’ve spent the last three weeks shooting with the Galaxy Z Fold 7, the Pixel 9 Pro Fold, and the iPhone 17 Pro Max. I took over 1,200 photos in daylight, low light, portraits, and zoom shots.

The big question: can a foldable phone’s camera match a traditional flagship in 2026? Short answer: yes, but with tradeoffs. Long answer: read on.

Buyer’s Guide: What to Look For When Choosing a Camera Phone

Sensor Size Matters More Than Megapixels

Bigger sensors capture more light. The iPhone 17 Pro Max uses a 1/1.28-inch primary sensor. The Galaxy Z Fold 7 uses a 1/1.5-inch sensor. That difference means the iPhone handles low light better without noise.

Telephoto Lens Type

Foldables often use a periscope zoom to fit into a thin body. Traditional flagships can use a longer optical zoom. The Galaxy Z Fold 7 has a 5x optical zoom. The iPhone 17 Pro Max has a 3x optical zoom. But the iPhone uses a larger aperture, so its 3x shots are brighter.

Computational Photography

Google and Apple lead here. The Pixel 9 Pro Fold’s Magic Eraser and Best Take are genuinely useful. Apple’s Photonic Engine gives natural colors. Samsung’s processing can oversharpen, but the Fold 7 is better than last year.

Form Factor vs. Camera Bump

Foldables have a thicker camera bump because the lens stack is compressed. The Galaxy Z Fold 7 has a 2.4mm bump. The iPhone 17 Pro Max has a 1.7mm bump. That matters if you use a case or set the phone flat on a table.

Video Quality

If you shoot video, the iPhone 17 Pro Max is unbeatable. Its stabilization, dynamic range, and ProRes mode are best-in-class. The Galaxy Z Fold 7 is good, but it still has occasional focus hunting in 4K 60fps.

Individual Product Reviews

1. Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7

Price: $1,899 | Rating: 4.2/5 | Best For: Power users who want a tablet and camera in one

The Galaxy Z Fold 7 is the most versatile foldable you can buy. Its 50MP main sensor takes sharp, detailed photos in good light. The 5x optical zoom is excellent—I shot a bird 30 feet away and could see individual feathers.

Low light is where it falls behind. The 1/1.5-inch sensor means you’ll see noise in dim indoor shots. The night mode helps, but it’s not as clean as the iPhone. The ultrawide camera is 12MP and decent, but not great for landscapes.

Video is good, not great. Stabilization works well in 1080p, but in 4K 60fps, the camera hunts for focus when moving quickly.

Pros: 5x optical zoom, bright daylight photos, huge 7.6-inch main screen for editing photos on the go

Cons: Low-light noise, camera bump makes it wobble on a table, expensive

2. Apple iPhone 17 Pro Max

Price: $1,499 | Rating: 4.8/5 | Best For: People who need reliable photos every time, especially in low light

The iPhone 17 Pro Max has the best camera system in any phone, period. Its 48MP main sensor uses pixel-binning to produce 12MP photos with incredible dynamic range. The Smart HDR 6 engine handles tricky backlit scenes perfectly.

The 3x telephoto is bright (f/2.4) and sharp. It’s not as long as the Fold 7’s 5x, but the photos are cleaner. The ultrawide is 48MP now, so you can crop into it for a pseudo-2x zoom.

Low light is a clear win for Apple. Night mode activates automatically and takes 2-3 seconds. The results are natural, not fake-looking. Video is best-in-class—ProRes Log lets you color grade in post.

Pros: Best low-light performance, excellent video stabilization, consistent color science

Cons: Only 3x optical zoom, no foldable big screen, heavy at 248 grams

3. Google Pixel 9 Pro Fold

Price: $1,099 | Rating: 4.0/5 | Best For: Photo enthusiasts who want AI editing tools and a thinner foldable

The Pixel 9 Pro Fold is the cheapest foldable here. Its 48MP main sensor is good, but not great. Photos look natural with Google’s Real Tone processing. Skin tones are accurate, and HDR is well-controlled.

The 5x periscope zoom is decent, but it’s not as sharp as the Galaxy Fold 7’s. The ultrawide is 12MP and average. Where the Pixel shines is software. Magic Editor can remove objects, and Best Take lets you swap faces in group shots.

Low light is okay, not amazing. Night Sight takes 4 seconds and produces usable shots, but there’s noticeable noise in shadows. Video is fine for social media, but not for professional work.

Pros: Best AI photo editing tools, thinnest foldable at 5.1mm unfolded, cheaper than rivals

Cons: Average ultrawide camera, slow night mode, no Pro video mode

4. OnePlus Open 2

Price: $1,299 | Rating: 3.9/5 | Best For: People who want fast charging and decent cameras at a mid-range foldable price

The OnePlus Open 2 is the dark horse. Its 48MP main sensor with Sony IMX989 is actually larger than the Galaxy Fold 7’s. Photos are sharp and vibrant, with a bit of saturation boost.

The 3x telephoto is okay, but not great. The 6x hybrid zoom is usable in good light. The ultrawide is 48MP and surprisingly good for landscapes.

Low light is mixed. The large sensor helps, but processing can blow out highlights. Video is decent in 4K 30fps, but 60fps has noticeable jitter. The big win is 67W charging—full charge in 42 minutes.

Pros: Large main sensor, fast 67W charging, lighter than Galaxy Fold 7 at 239 grams

Cons: Inconsistent processing, average zoom, no wireless charging

Frequently Asked Questions

Do foldable cameras break easily?

No. All four phones here have Gorilla Glass Victus 2 or better. The camera lenses are protected by sapphire crystal on the iPhone and Fold 7. Dropping is the bigger risk.

Which phone has the best zoom?

The Galaxy Z Fold 7. Its 5x optical zoom is sharper than the Pixel’s 5x and the iPhone’s 3x. At 10x digital zoom, the Fold 7 still looks good.

Can I use a foldable for professional photography?

Yes, if you shoot in RAW and edit in Lightroom. The Galaxy Z Fold 7 and iPhone 17 Pro Max both support ProRAW and ProRes. The Pixel 9 Pro Fold supports RAW but not ProRes video.

Is the iPhone 17 Pro Max worth the extra money over the Galaxy Z Fold 7?

If camera quality is your top priority, yes. The iPhone takes better photos in more situations. If you want the big screen and don’t shoot in low light often, the Fold 7 is fine.

Bottom Line Verdict

If your number one priority is taking great photos in any situation, buy the iPhone 17 Pro Max. It has the best low-light performance, most consistent processing, and best video of any phone in 2026. If you want a foldable and shoot mostly in good light, the Galaxy Z Fold 7 is the best foldable camera you can buy. Skip the Pixel 9 Pro Fold unless you really need those AI editing tools. The OnePlus Open 2 is a good value, but its camera isn’t consistent enough to recommend over the iPhone or Galaxy.
