What to Look for in a D3 + K2 Supplement

Not all D3+K2 products are created equal. Here's the breakdown of what actually matters.

1. The Form of K2: MK-7 is the Only Choice

Vitamin K2 comes in two forms: MK-4 and MK-7. MK-4 is synthetic, has a half-life of about 1 hour, and you'd need to take it three times daily to get benefits. MK-7 is derived from natto (fermented soybeans) and stays active in your blood for 24+ hours. Only buy MK-7. If the label just says "Vitamin K2" without specifying, put it back on the shelf.

2. Dosage Ratio: 1000 IU D3 to 10 mcg K2 is Bare Minimum

The research suggests you need about 10 mcg of MK-7 for every 1000 IU of D3 you take. If you're taking 5000 IU of D3, you want at least 50-100 mcg of K2. Many cheap brands put a token 10 mcg of K2 with 5000 IU of D3. That's not enough to redirect calcium properly.

3. Fat Is Your Friend

Both D3 and K2 are fat-soluble vitamins. If the softgel doesn't contain a carrier oil (olive oil, MCT, coconut oil), your body absorbs almost nothing. Look for "softgels with oil" or "lipid-based delivery" on the label.

4. Third-Party Testing

Supplements are unregulated. Look for NSF Certified, USP Verified, or at minimum, a "Third-Party Tested" seal. I rejected three products in my testing because they claimed "lab tested" but had no verifiable certificate of analysis.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I take D3 and K2 together or separately?

Together. They work synergistically. D3 pulls calcium into your bloodstream, and K2 directs it to your bones. Taking them at the same time ensures they're both active in your system simultaneously. Take them with your largest meal of the day since fat improves absorption.

Can I get enough K2 from food?

Unlikely. Natto (fermented soybeans) is the richest source of MK-7, but most Westerners find it unpalatable. Cheese and egg yolks contain K2, but in tiny amounts. You'd need to eat a pound of cheddar cheese to get 45 mcg of K2. Just take the supplement.

Is 5000 IU of D3 safe?

For most adults, yes. The upper tolerable limit is 4000 IU per day from supplements, but many experts argue 5000 IU is fine, especially if you're deficient. The only risk is hypercalcemia (too much calcium in the blood), which the K2 actually helps prevent. If you're worried, get a blood test after 3 months.

Will this fix my bone pain or frequent colds?

Maybe. If you're deficient in D3, supplementation will improve immune function and bone density over 6-12 months. But it's not a magic pill. If you've had bone pain for months, see a doctor. If you get sick constantly, you might also be low on zinc or magnesium.
