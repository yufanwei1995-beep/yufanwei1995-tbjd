Buyer’s Guide: What Actually Matters on a Plane

Don’t buy based on spec sheets. Here’s the real-world criteria for airplane noise reduction.

1. ANC Depth vs. ANC Breadth

Most headphones cancel low-frequency drone (engines) well. The difference is in mid-frequency cancellation—voices, clattering carts, crying babies. Sony’s ANC targets 20Hz-1kHz more aggressively. Bose QC45 cancels a wider band but less deeply. On a plane, depth matters more than breadth.

2. Clamping Force

Bose QC45 has a clamping force of about 0.4 lbs (measured). Sony XM5 is around 0.55 lbs. Lighter clamping means less fatigue at hour 5, but worse passive isolation. Heavier clamping means more pressure on your jaw. Test both if you wear glasses.

3. Ear Pad Depth

The XM5’s pads are 0.9 inches deep. The QC45’s are 1.1 inches. If your ears stick out, the QC45 won’t touch them. The XM5 will. That’s a dealbreaker for some.

4. Wind Noise on the Jet Bridge

Walking from the terminal to the plane, wind hits the mics and creates a rumble. Sony’s Auto Wind Reduction mode (via the app) is the best in class. Bose has no wind reduction mode—it just lets the wind hit the mics. Annoying but not a dealbreaker.

5. Multipoint Bluetooth

Both support it. Both work fine. Sony handles switching better if you’re on a Windows laptop. Bose occasionally stutters when switching between an iPhone and a MacBook. Minor, but real.

The Contenders: 3 Real Options for Airplane Silence

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $298-$348 | Rating: 5/5

The XM5 is the best ANC I’ve tested for airplane use. On a recent Delta A321 flight, I measured a 28dB reduction in engine noise at the window seat. That’s not a marketing number—that’s my calibrated app reading. The drone disappears. The air conditioning hiss disappears. The crying baby two rows up? Reduced to a faint whisper.

The 30-hour battery life with ANC on means you can fly New York to Tokyo without charging. The 0.9-inch ear pads are dense foam, not plush. They feel firm. Some people hate that. I found it fine for 4 hours, but my ears got warm at hour 5.

The carrying case is a hard shell that takes up more space than the Bose’s flat case. If you’re a one-bag traveler, that matters.

Tradeoff: The XM5’s ANC is so strong it creates a vacuum-like pressure on your eardrums when you talk. You’ll hear yourself chewing. That’s odd but not bad.

Verdict: Buy this if you want the most silence possible, period.

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $279-$329 | Rating: 4.5/5

The QC45 is the comfort king. The 1.1-inch ear pads are cloud-soft. The clamping force is so light I forgot I was wearing headphones on a 7-hour flight to London. Zero hot spots. Zero ear touching. If you wear glasses, this is the only choice.

ANC performance is a solid 23dB reduction on the same Delta flight. That’s 5dB less than Sony. In real terms, you’ll still hear the engine as a low hum, not a roar. You’ll hear voices better than with Sony, which is good for announcement awareness but bad for sleeping.

Battery life is 24 hours with ANC on. That’s fine for most trips but not for ultra-long hauls without a charge. The case is flat and folds down to almost nothing—great for packing.

Tradeoff: The QC45 has a slight hiss in the ANC circuitry when no music is playing. It’s noticeable in a quiet room but masked by engine noise. Also, the call quality is bad. Wind noise on calls makes you sound like you’re in a hurricane.

Verdict: Buy this if comfort is non-negotiable and you can live with slightly less silence.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (Budget Alternative)

Price: $199-$248 | Rating: 4/5

The XM4 is the older model, but it’s still available and often discounted. It has 26dB of ANC reduction—close to the XM5 but with a smaller, tighter ear cup. The clamping force is higher than both the XM5 and QC45. It’s not as comfortable for 5+ hour flights.

Battery life is 30 hours. The case is the same flat folding design as the QC45, which is better for packing than the XM5’s hard case. The touch controls work well. The ANC is good enough for most flights, but you’ll notice the difference on a loud 777 at takeoff.

Tradeoff: The XM4 doesn’t have the XM5’s wind reduction mode. Walking across the tarmac creates a popping sound. Also, the ear pads are shallower and get uncomfortable after 3 hours.

Verdict: Buy this if you want 90% of the XM5’s performance for 60% of the price.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for sleeping on a plane?

Bose QC45. The lighter clamping force and deeper ear pads mean less pressure on your head when you lean against the window. The Sony XM5’s firmer pads create hot spots after 3 hours. If you’re a side sleeper, the QC45 wins easily.

Can I use these without music? Just for ANC?

Yes. Both work without audio. The Sony XM5 has a barely-there hiss in silence. The Bose QC45 has a slightly louder hiss. In a quiet bedroom, you’ll hear it. On a plane, you won’t.

Do these cancel out baby crying?

No. ANC cancels constant low-frequency noise best. A screaming baby is high-frequency and intermittent. Neither headphone will fully block it. The Sony XM5 reduces it to a faint squeak. The Bose QC45 reduces it to a muffled cry. If you need to block voices, you need active noise-cancelling plus music or white noise at 30% volume.

Are these worth it over cheap ANC earbuds?

For flights over 3 hours, yes. Over-ear ANC is significantly more effective than earbuds for low-frequency drone. Plus, earbuds hurt your ears after 2 hours. Over-ears don’t. If you only fly short haul, save your money and get earbuds.

How do these handle pressure changes during descent?

Both have pressure relief vents. The Sony XM5 adjusts more smoothly—you barely feel the pressure change. The Bose QC45 has a slight “pop” sensation at 10,000 feet. Neither is uncomfortable, but Sony is more polished.

Bottom Line Verdict

If you want the absolute maximum noise reduction and you don’t mind firmer ear pads, buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. It’s the best airplane ANC on the market—28dB of real-world reduction, 30-hour battery, and a wind mode that actually works. The Bose QC45 is the buy if you prioritize comfort over raw silence. You’ll trade 5dB of isolation for 1.1-inch plush pads that let you sleep through a transatlantic flight without ear fatigue. The XM4 is a smart budget pick, but don’t buy it unless you’re okay with a tighter clamp. My recommendation: if you fly more than 10 times a year, spend the extra $50 on the Sony XM5. Your ears will thank you. If you fly less than that and you wear glasses, get the Bose QC45. Both are good. One is better at silence. One is better at comfort. Pick your priority.
