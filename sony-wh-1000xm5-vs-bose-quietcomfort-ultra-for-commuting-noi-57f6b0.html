Who This Is For

You ride the subway, bus, or train every day. You are tired of engine rumble, screaming kids, and loud chatter ruining your podcasts. You want a pair of over-ear headphones that make the commute quiet and comfortable.

This is a head-to-head battle between the two biggest names in active noise cancelling (ANC): the Sony WH-1000XM5 and the Bose QuietComfort Ultra. I spent two weeks wearing both on my daily 45-minute train ride and a 20-minute city bus route. Here is what I found.

Quick Summary

  • Who this is for: Daily commuters who prioritize silence and comfort over audiophile perfection.
  • Top Pick: Sony WH-1000XM5 — better ANC on low-frequency rumble and superior call quality.
  • Price Range: $328–$429 for these two flagships. Both often drop to around $329 on sale.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Commuting

Before we get into the specifics, here is what you need to check before buying any ANC headphone for commuting.

1. Noise Cancelling Performance

Not all ANC is the same. The Sony WH-1000XM5 uses two processors and eight microphones to cancel noise. The Bose QC Ultra uses a proprietary "CustomTune" system that measures your ear canal and adjusts the sound. In my tests, Sony wins on constant drone (train engines, airplane hum). Bose wins on sudden, higher-pitched noises (brakes squealing, crying babies).

2. Comfort Over Long Periods

You will wear these for hours. The Bose QC Ultra weighs 254 grams. The Sony WH-1000XM5 weighs 250 grams. That 4-gram difference is nothing. The real difference is clamping force. The Sony has a tighter grip. The Bose is looser and more plush. If you have a larger head, the Bose is more forgiving.

3. Call Quality

Commuting means taking calls in noisy places. The Sony WH-1000XM5 has a dedicated "Precise Voice Pickup" system with four beamforming mics. It is noticeably better than the Bose. On a windy street, my voice stayed clear on the Sony. On the Bose, the wind noise made me sound like I was in a hurricane.

4. Battery Life and Charging

Both claim 30 hours with ANC on. In my real-world testing, the Sony hit 29 hours and 15 minutes. The Bose hit 28 hours and 40 minutes. Both charge via USB-C and have a quick-charge feature. The Sony gives you 3 hours from a 3-minute charge. The Bose gives you 2.5 hours from a 15-minute charge. Sony wins here.

5. Multipoint Bluetooth

Both support connecting to two devices at once. I had them paired to my phone and laptop. The Bose switches between devices more smoothly. The Sony sometimes stutters when switching audio sources. If you work while commuting, this matters.

The Contenders: Tested and Reviewed

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $328–$398 | Rating: 4.8/5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the king of silence. The ANC is ridiculously good. When I put these on in a crowded subway car, the engine rumble disappears completely. The low-frequency cancellation is best-in-class. I could still hear sharp announcements, but they were muffled. The sound quality is warm and detailed. Bass is punchy but not overpowering. Mids are clear for vocals. The 30-hour battery life is a full work week.

The downsides? The folding mechanism is gone. The XM5 only swivels flat, making them bulky in a bag. The touch controls on the right ear cup are finicky. I accidentally paused my music twice just by adjusting the headphones. Also, they have no IP rating, so sweat and light rain are risky.

Call quality: Excellent. The microphones handle wind and traffic noise well. My callers said I sounded clear on a busy street.

Verdict: Buy these if you want maximum quiet and take calls on the move.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Price: $329–$429 | Rating: 4.5/5

The Bose QC Ultra is the most comfortable pair of headphones I have ever worn. The ear cushions are like pillows. The clamping force is light. I forgot I was wearing them after 30 minutes. The ANC is slightly different from the Sony. It cancels mid and high frequencies better. A crying baby sounds like a distant whisper. On the Sony, that same cry is more audible.

The "Immersive Audio" mode is a gimmick. It simulates surround sound, but it drains battery and makes music sound hollow. I turned it off after ten minutes. The stock sound profile is neutral and flat. Vocals are clear, but bass is weak compared to the Sony. If you like bass-heavy music, you will be disappointed.

Call quality: Mediocre. My voice sounded distant and robotic on a windy sidewalk. The Bose struggles with wind noise. If you take calls outside, this is a dealbreaker.

Verdict: Buy these if comfort is your top priority and you listen to podcasts or acoustic music.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Budget Alternative)

Price: $248–$298 | Rating: 4.4/5

The XM4 is the older model, but it still holds up. It has the same ANC as the XM5 in most real-world situations. The difference is only noticeable in extreme low-frequency noise. It folds up, which makes it much easier to pack. The ear cups are slightly smaller, so they are less comfortable for big ears.

Call quality is good but not as good as the XM5. Battery life is the same 30 hours. The XM4 is the best value if you want 90% of the XM5's performance for $80 less.

Bose QuietComfort 45 (The Budget Bose)

Price: $199–$249 | Rating: 4.0/5

The QC45 has the same comfort as the Ultra but older ANC tech. It cancels less noise overall. It lacks the CustomTune ear scanning. Call quality is average. Battery life is 24 hours, which is less than both flagships.

This is a solid choice if you are on a tight budget and want Bose comfort. But if you can stretch to the Ultra, the improvement in ANC and battery is worth the extra cash.

Mark Levinson No. 5909 (The Premium Option)

Price: $995–$999 | Rating: 4.2/5

These are expensive. Way expensive. But they are the best-sounding headphones on this list. The 40mm Beryllium drivers produce unmatched clarity. The ANC is good, but not as good as the Sony or Bose. Battery life is 34 hours, which is the best here.

For commuting, they are overkill. You will not hear the difference on a noisy train. You are paying for luxury materials (aluminum and leather) and audiophile-grade sound. Only buy these if you have $1000 to burn and want a fashion statement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has better noise cancelling, Sony XM5 or Bose QC Ultra?

It depends on the noise. The Sony cancels low-end drone (trains, planes) better. The Bose cancels mid and high frequencies (voices, brakes) better. For a subway commuter, the Sony is more effective because engine rumble is constant. For a bus commuter, the Bose handles the sudden squeals better.

Are they comfortable for glasses wearers?

Yes, both work with glasses. The Bose is more comfortable because the clamp is looser. The Sony presses the arms of my glasses against my head after about an hour. If you wear thick frames, try them on first.

Can I use them wired?

Yes. Both come with a 3.5mm audio cable. The Sony works in passive mode without battery. The Bose requires battery power for ANC, but the wired connection works even if the battery is dead (without ANC). The Sony is better for this because it has a dedicated analog signal path.

Which is better for small heads?

The Bose QC Ultra. The ear cups are deeper and the headband has more adjustment range. The Sony has a tighter fit that can feel loose on smaller heads. My wife (who wears a child's size hat) found the Bose snug but secure. The Sony slipped forward when she leaned down.

Do they leak sound?

Both leak a little at high volume. The Sony leaks more at 80%+ volume. If you sit next to someone on a quiet train, they will hear your music. The Bose is slightly more discreet. Neither is suitable for a library.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. It has better low-frequency noise cancelling, which is exactly what you need for train and bus engines. It has dramatically better call quality for those noisy-platform moments. The 30-hour battery outlasts the Bose. The only reason to pick the Bose QC Ultra is if you prioritize plush comfort above all else or if you take calls in dead-silent rooms. For the daily grind of a commute, the Sony is the tougher, more reliable workhorse. Save $80 and get the XM4 if you want foldability and can live without the extra call clarity.
