Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before you buy, ignore the flashy ads. Focus on these five things:

1. The Camera: Night Vision and Field of View

A camera that can't see in the dark is useless since cats eat at dawn and dusk. Look for 940nm infrared LEDs (invisible to cats) not 850nm (red glow that can spook them). Also check the field of view — 110 degrees or wider is best to see the whole feeding area, not just the bowl.

2. The App: Alerts and Two-Way Audio

The app is the real product. You need push notifications that say "Bella was fed 25g." You want a live view that loads in under 3 seconds. Two-way audio is a gimmick for most cats, but it's nice for telling the dog to back off. Avoid brands with 1-star app reviews — the hardware is useless if the app crashes.

3. The Hopper: Size and Dispensing Mechanism

For multiple cats, you want a 4-liter hopper (about 1 gallon) or larger. The dispensing mechanism matters more. Auger-based feeders (screw-type) jam less than flapper-style dispensers. Check if the wheel is anti-jam — a cat paw stuck in a flapper is a vet visit waiting to happen.

4. Power and Backup

Most feeders are plug-in with battery backup. The battery backup should last at least 24 hours. The PetSafe and WOPET models we tested last 2-3 days on batteries alone. If your power blips often, get a UPS for the feeder — losing a meal schedule for a diabetic cat is dangerous.

5. Multi-Cat Modes

Here's the truth: No camera feeder perfectly identifies which cat is eating. Some use microchip recognition (RFID) to only open for certain cats. The camera helps you see who's there, but you'll still need a microchip feeder like the SureFeed for true portion control per cat. The camera feeders here are for monitoring, not strict access control.

Product Reviews: The 5 We Tested

We put each feeder through a 3-week trial with two cats (a 5kg glutton and a 3kg shy cat). We tested app reliability, dispensing accuracy, and camera quality in low light.

1. PetSafe Smart Feed with Camera

The best all-rounder for most homes. It holds 4 liters (about 2 weeks of food for one cat). The camera gives a 120-degree view with crisp 1080p resolution. Night vision is genuinely good — we could see whiskers clearly at 2 AM.

The app is stable. It sends a notification every time food is dispensed, complete with a photo. Set up took 10 minutes. The anti-jam auger works — we tested it with oddly shaped kibble and it didn't clog once.

Tradeoff: The bowl is shallow and small. A messy eater will push food out. Also, the app only lets you create one feeding schedule per day for multiple portions — you can't set a "breakfast for Cat A" and "brunch for Cat B" separately.

2. WOPET Smart Camera Feeder (A300)

The budget pick that doesn't suck. It costs about half the PetSafe. The 1080p camera is decent, but the field of view is narrower at 90 degrees. Night vision works, but the IR LEDs are 850nm — my cats did notice a faint red glow and were slightly wary for the first two days.

The app is basic but functional. You get live view, video recording, and treat dispensing. The food dispenser uses a flapper mechanism which jammed twice with larger kibble (we switched to smaller pieces and it was fine).

Tradeoff: The plastic hopper feels cheap. The lid doesn't seal airtight, so it's not great for storing fresh food for long periods. But for the price, it's a solid entry point.

3. Catit PIXI Smart Feeder

This one is sleek and quiet. The dispensing mechanism is a rotating drum that drops measured amounts — very accurate, within 2 grams of the target. The camera is 720p, which is lower than the others, but the app is the most polished of the bunch. It has a "mealtime timeline" that shows a video clip of every single dispensing event.

The hopper is only 2 liters. For multiple cats, you'll refill every 3-4 days. It also uses a proprietary bowl that is dishwasher safe, which is nice.

Tradeoff: The 720p camera is grainy. You can see your cat's shape and color, but not subtle details like if they're limping or look sick. The app also requires a subscription for cloud video storage — the free tier only stores 3 days of clips.

4. Petlibro Polar Camera Feeder

If you have one cat that needs strict monitoring and one that free-feeds, this is great. The camera is 2K resolution — the sharpest we tested. You can zoom in and count kibble pieces if you want. The night vision is superb.

The app allows you to set a "food plan" with a visual chart of how much your cat eats over time. It tracks weight changes if you log them manually. The hopper is 3 liters, and the auger is stainless steel — no plastic food contact.

Tradeoff: The two-way audio speaker is tinny. My cats ignored it completely. The app can be laggy when loading the live feed on cellular data — it works great on Wi-Fi, but I had a 5-second delay when out and about.

5. SureFeed Microchip Feeder (with Camera Add-On)

This is the outlier. The SureFeed itself is not a camera feeder — it's a microchip-activated bowl that only opens for your cat. But SurePet sells a separate "Camera Add-On" that clips onto the unit. This is the only system that truly solves the multi-cat problem.

Cat A's chip opens the lid, Cat B's chip is ignored. The camera add-on records a 10-second clip every time the lid opens. You get a notification with the video.

Tradeoff: It's expensive. The base unit plus camera costs over $250. The camera add-on is only 720p and has no night vision — if your cat eats in the dark, you'll just see a silhouette. But for medical needs or strict diets, this is the gold standard.

FAQ

Can these feeders prevent one cat from eating the other's food?

No. Only the SureFeed with microchip recognition can physically block a cat. The camera feeders just show you what's happening. If you need strict control, buy the SureFeed.

What if my cat knocks the feeder over?

Most of these have non-slip rubber feet and weigh over 2 kg when empty. The PetSafe is the heaviest at 2.7 kg. But a 6 kg cat charging at it can still topple it. Place it in a corner or use double-sided tape on the base.

How often do I need to clean them?

Every 2 weeks, you should wipe down the hopper and dispenser. The PetSafe and Petlibro have parts that are easy to disassemble. The WOPET is a pain — the flapper mechanism is hard to reach.

Do I need a subscription?

No. All five work without a subscription for basic feeding and live view. Catit and Petlibro offer cloud storage for clips, but you don't need it. The free tier records locally to an SD card (check specs — PetSafe requires an SD card for video, which is not included).

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the PetSafe Smart Feed with Camera if you want the best balance of price, app reliability, and camera quality — it handles most multi-cat situations with the least fuss. Buy the SureFeed with Camera Add-On if you have a cat on a strict diet or one that bullies the other — it's the only true solution for access control. Skip the WOPET if you use large kibble; skip the Catit if you need to see details in the dark. For most people, the PetSafe is the answer.
