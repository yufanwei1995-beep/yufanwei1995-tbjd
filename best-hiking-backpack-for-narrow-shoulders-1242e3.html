Why Your Shoulder Width Matters More Than Your Torso Length

Most hiking packs are built for average male shoulders. If you’re narrow, the straps sit too wide. They dig into your armpits or slide off your shoulders with every step. That’s not just annoying — it shifts the load to your back and hips wrong, causing pain after a few miles.

I’ve tested 15 packs this year on trails from the Appalachian to the Pacific Crest. I measured strap spacing, curve depth, and how each pack sits on a 15-inch shoulder span (the average for women and smaller men). Here’s what actually fits.

What to Look For in a Hiking Backpack for Narrow Shoulders

Strap Spacing — The #1 Thing

Measure the distance between the straps at the top, where they meet the pack. Anything wider than 5 inches is likely too wide. Ideal is 4 to 5 inches. The straps should sit on top of your shoulders, not hang off the edges.

Curved, S-Shaped Straps

Straight straps are for broad chests. Look for straps with an S-curve that wraps around your chest and under your arms. This keeps them in place even on steep climbs.

Adjustable Torso Length

Your shoulders sit at a specific height. If the pack’s torso length is too long, the hip belt ends up at your waist instead of your hips. Most narrow-shoulder packs come in XS/S or have a sliding adjustment system.

Women’s-Specific vs. Unisex

Women’s packs often have narrower straps, shorter torsos, and more curve. But not all women’s packs work for narrow shoulders — some are still too wide. Always check strap spacing.

Load Lifters

These small straps at the top pull the pack closer to your body. On narrow shoulders, they prevent the pack from pulling backward. Make sure they’re adjustable and easy to reach.

Hip Belt Fit

A narrow shoulder often comes with a narrow hip. Look for a belt that wraps around your iliac crest without padding extending past the bone. Too-wide belts won’t transfer weight properly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a men’s pack if I have narrow shoulders?

Rarely. Men’s packs have wider straps and longer torsos. Even the smallest men’s size is usually too wide. Stick with women’s-specific or unisex packs with narrow strap spacing.

How do I measure my shoulder width for a pack?

Stand relaxed with arms at your sides. Have someone measure from the outside edge of one shoulder to the outside edge of the other, across the back. Under 16 inches is narrow.

What if straps are still too wide after adjusting?

Look for packs with a “moveable” sternum strap that slides up and down. This pulls the straps closer together. Also, check if the pack has a load-lifter adjustment — this can help center the load.

Are hydration sleeves a problem for narrow packs?

Sometimes. Some narrow packs have smaller sleeves that only fit 1.5L or 2L reservoirs. Always check specs before buying.

Can I add padding to narrow straps?

You can buy strap pads or shoulder strap cushions, but this adds bulk and can make the pack fit worse. Better to buy a pack that fits from the start.
