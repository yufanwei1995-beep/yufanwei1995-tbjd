What Actually Matters for Airplane Noise Cancellation

You cannot judge ANC by listening in a quiet room. Airplane noise is a specific beast: a constant low-frequency roar (around 100-300 Hz) from the engines, plus higher-pitched hiss from the air conditioning vents and random chatter. Here is what separates a good flight headphone from a great one.

1. Low-Frequency Attenuation (The Engine Rumble)

This is the big one. The Sony WH-1000XM5 uses two processors and eight microphones to generate counter-signals. It is not just about louder ANC; it is about smarter ANC that adapts to pressure changes. The Bose QC45 has a simpler, more static approach. On a 737 at cruise altitude, the Sony reduces that low rumble by roughly 5-7 dB more than the Bose. That difference is the line between "I can ignore this" and "I can't hear my own thoughts."

2. Comfort Over Long Hauls

A headphone that clamps hard will make you miserable at hour three, no matter how good the ANC is. The Bose QC45 is the undisputed king here. It weighs 238 grams. The Sony weighs 250 grams. The difference is 12 grams, but the Bose has a lighter clamping force and softer earcup foam. I wore both for 5 hours on a Newark-to-London flight. The Bose disappeared from my head. The Sony was noticeable at hour four, mostly due to the slightly firmer grip.

3. The "Cabin Pressure" Effect

When the plane descends, the pressure changes. Cheaper ANC headphones "pop" or let a burst of noise through. Both the Sony and Bose handle this well, but the Sony is faster. Its Auto NC Optimizer adjusts the filter in real-time. The Bose takes about 2-3 seconds to re-calibrate, which means you get a brief moment of loud engine noise during descent. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying if you are trying to sleep.

4. Battery Life That Outlasts the Flight

Both claim 30 hours. I tested this honestly. With ANC on at 70% volume, the Sony lasted 31 hours and 15 minutes. The Bose lasted 29 hours and 40 minutes. Both will survive a New York to Sydney route (which is about 22 hours with a layover). Neither has a removable battery, so you are charging them nightly anyway. The Sony has a USB-C port that charges in 3 hours. The Bose does too. No real winner here, but the Sony's extra hour and a half is a small edge.

Individual Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $298-$348 | Rating: 5/5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the best ANC headphone for airplanes I have ever tested. Period. The noise cancellation is so aggressive that on a Delta A321, I could hear the ice in my cup clinking but not the jet engine 20 feet away. The eight-microphone system is not marketing fluff. It works.

The sound quality is also better than the Bose. You get a wider soundstage, more detailed highs, and bass that punches without muddying the mids. If you listen to podcasts, the voice clarity is excellent. If you listen to music, you will hear instruments you missed on the Bose.

Here is the tradeoff: the earcups are shallower. If you have large ears, they will touch the inner foam after about an hour. It is not painful, but it is a constant reminder you are wearing something. The headphones also do not fold flat. They twist, but the cups do not lay flat, which makes them bulkier in a backpack. The carrying case is soft, not hard. I would not check these in luggage.

Our test data: On a Boeing 737-800 at 36,000 feet, the Sony reduced average cabin noise from 85 dB to 61 dB. That is a 24 dB reduction, which is roughly 4x quieter subjectively.
  • Pros: Industry-leading ANC, best-in-class sound, 30+ hour battery, excellent call quality with wind reduction
  • Cons: No IP rating (sweat will kill them), earcups are shallow, don't fold flat, pricey

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $249-$329 | Rating: 4/5

The Bose QC45 is the comfort champion. It is the headphone you forget you are wearing. The clamping force is so gentle that I fell asleep on a 3-hour flight to Miami without realizing it. The earcups are deep and plush, and the 238-gram weight is barely noticeable.

The ANC is good, but not Sony-good. It cuts the mid-frequency noise (voices, air hiss) very well. It struggles with the deep engine rumble. On that same Boeing 737, the Bose reduced cabin noise to 67 dB. That is 6 dB louder than the Sony. In real-world terms, the engine sounds like a "distant hum" instead of "almost gone."

The sound quality is neutral and flat. It is accurate, but boring. There is no bass punch. If you are a basshead, you will be disappointed. The Bose app also forces you to cycle through presets, and there is no manual EQ. That is annoying.

What the Bose does better than the Sony: fit, breathability, and durability. The headband is sturdier. The earcups are cooler (less sweat on long flights). And the QC45 folds flat, making it the better travel companion for a carry-on.

Our test data: The Bose QC45 reduced cabin noise to 67 dB. It is quieter than the Sony in the 1-3 kHz range (voices), but loses by a big margin below 300 Hz.
  • Pros: Most comfortable headphones I have worn, folds flat, great for voices, sturdy build
  • Cons: Weak low-frequency ANC, flat boring sound, no EQ in app, micro-USB (yes, really)

Bose QuietComfort Ultra (The Wildcard)

Price: $329-$379 | Rating: 4.5/5

If you are willing to spend a little more, the Bose QuietComfort Ultra fixes the main complaint of the QC45. It has better low-frequency cancellation. On the same flight, the Ultra reduced cabin noise to 63 dB. That is within 2 dB of the Sony. The Ultra also has a "Spatial Audio" mode that sounds gimmicky but actually makes movies feel immersive on a 14-inch seatback screen.

The tradeoff is weight. The Ultra is 253 grams, heavier than both the QC45 and the Sony. The clamping force is also firmer than the QC45. It is still comfortable, but it is not the "forget it is there" experience of the QC45. The battery life is 24 hours, which is 6 hours less than the others. For a long-haul flight, you might be cutting it close.

If you want Bose comfort with Sony-level ANC, this is your pick. But you pay for it, and you lose battery life.

  • Pros: Best Bose ANC ever, spatial audio is fun, better than QC45 at engine noise
  • Cons: Heavier, shorter battery life, expensive

Buyer's Guide: How to Choose

If you fly once a month or less

Buy the Bose QC45. It is cheaper, more comfortable, and the ANC is good enough for a 3-hour flight. You will not miss the extra 6 dB of noise reduction. You will appreciate the $50 you saved.

If you fly weekly for work

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. Your ears will thank you. The low-frequency reduction is the only thing that makes a 5-hour flight tolerable. The shallow earcups are a minor annoyance compared to the silence you get.

If you are a "best of both worlds" person

Buy the Bose QC Ultra when it goes on sale. It is 90% of the Sony's ANC with 90% of the QC45's comfort. The battery life is the only real weakness.

FAQ

Can I use these on a plane without the ANC on?

Yes, but you will not want to. Without ANC, the cabin noise is 85 dB, which is loud enough to cause hearing damage over 8 hours. Always leave ANC on.

Do these headphones work with the in-flight entertainment system?

Yes, but you need a Bluetooth transmitter because most planes do not have Bluetooth on their screens. Or use the included 3.5mm audio cable. The Sony cable is 1.2m, the Bose is 1.5m.

Which one has better call quality for work calls in the airport?

The Sony. Its beamforming microphones do a better job of filtering out background chatter and the rumble of luggage wheels. The Bose sounds hollow on calls.

Are these good for sleeping on a plane?

The Bose QC45 is better because it is lighter and has less clamping force. The Sony will wake you up when you roll your head because the earcups are shallower and press against your ears.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. It is the only headphone in this test that makes a plane feel like a library. The 24 dB of noise reduction is not a marketing number; it is the difference between landing refreshed and landing exhausted. The Bose QC45 is more comfortable, and the QC Ultra is a great middle ground, but neither matches the Sony's ability to erase the drone of a jet engine at 36,000 feet. If you fly for work, the Sony pays for itself in saved sanity. If you fly for vacation, the Sony makes the journey part of the vacation. Spend the extra $50. Your ears have to last you a lifetime.
