Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters on a Plane

Before I break down the two contenders, you need to know what specs matter for airplane noise. Not all ANC is created equal.

Passive vs. Active Cancellation

On a plane, you deal with two types of noise. Low-frequency rumble (engines) and mid/high-frequency chatter (people, carts, crying babies). Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) crushes the low rumble. The ear cup seal handles the high stuff. Sony uses 8 microphones and two processors. Bose uses 4 mics and a simpler digital chip. More mics doesn't always mean better—it's about the tuning.

Comfort Over Long Haul

You will wear these for 6-10 hours straight. Clamping force and weight matter more than any spec sheet. The Bose QC45 weighs 238 grams. The Sony XM5 weighs 250 grams. That 12-gram difference is nothing. The clamp pressure is everything. Bose has a famously light touch. Sony grips a bit tighter for a better seal.

Battery Life and Charging

Both claim 30+ hours with ANC on. In my testing, both delivered close to that. The real difference is the cable. Sony uses USB-C (standard). Bose also uses USB-C. Both charge fast—10 minutes gives you about 3 hours. Not a differentiator.

Call Quality on the Tarmac

If you take calls at the gate or in the airport lounge, mic quality matters. Sony's beamforming mics are better at isolating your voice from background noise. Bose sounds more hollow on calls in windy conditions. I'll detail this below.

Foldability and Travel Case

This is a big one. The Bose QC45 folds flat and fits in a thin hard case. The Sony XM5 does not fold—the hinges are gone. It swivels flat but stays in a bulky case. If you travel with a backpack, the Bose is significantly easier to pack.

Sony WH-1000XM5 Review

Price: $298-$348 | Rating: 4.8/5 | Best for: Maximum noise cancellation

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the quietest consumer headphone I have ever worn on a plane. Period. I flew from New York to London (7 hours) with these on. When the engines spooled up for takeoff, the world just... stopped. The roar became a faint hum. It's not silence, but it's closer than anything else under $400.

The ANC uses two dedicated processors (the V1 and QN1). It adapts in real-time to altitude pressure changes. I noticed it working—the ANC seemed to "breathe" as we climbed. The noise floor is black. No hiss, no pressure buildup in the ears.

The tradeoff? The fit. The XM5 has a wider headband and shallower ear cups. For my average-sized head, it felt secure but slightly less plush than Bose. After 4 hours, I felt the pressure on the top of my head. Not painful, just noticeable.

Sound quality is warmer and bass-heavy compared to Bose. Music sounds more fun. But the ANC is the star. If you want to sleep on a plane, this is the one.

Call quality: Best in class. Four beamforming mics with wind reduction. I took a call in a busy terminal and the person on the other end couldn't hear the PA announcements. Bose can't do this.

Pros & Cons

  • Pros: Industry-leading ANC, 30-hr battery, excellent call mics, USB-C fast charge
  • Cons: Does not fold, heavy clamp after 5 hours, expensive, no IP rating

Bose QuietComfort 45 Review

Price: $249-$329 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: All-day comfort and easy travel

The Bose QC45 is the elder statesman. It doesn't have the fancy processors or the adaptive mics. What it has is the most comfortable ear cup design ever made. The plush synthetic leather and low clamp force mean you forget you're wearing them. On a 7-hour flight, I wore these the entire time without a single hotspot.

But how does the ANC compare? I flew the same route (NYC to London) with the QC45. The engine rumble is reduced by about 80% of what the Sony does. It's still quiet—you can hear a movie clearly at 50% volume—but the low-frequency drone is more present. The Sony reduces that drone to near-zero. The Bose reduces it to a distant hum.

Where Bose wins: The foldability. This is the biggest practical difference. The QC45 folds into a flat case that fits in a backpack pocket. The Sony requires a dedicated (large) hard case. If you travel light, this is a dealbreaker.

Sound quality is neutral and flat. Vocals are clear, but bass is anemic compared to Sony. For podcasts and audiobooks, it's perfect. For bass-heavy music, it's disappointing.

Call quality: Average. Mics pick up background noise. In a quiet room, you sound fine. Near a jet engine or in wind, the person on the other end struggles to hear you. This is the QC45's weakest spot.

Pros & Cons

  • Pros: Most comfortable headphones under $400, folds flat, lightweight, simple controls
  • Cons: Weaker ANC than Sony, mediocre call mics, bass-light sound, no adaptive features

Bonus: Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Budget Alternative)

Price: $228-$278 | Rating: 4.4/5 | Best for: Foldable Sony fans on a budget

You should also consider the older Sony XM4. It folds, has 30-hour battery, and 90% of the XM5's ANC. The ANC is slightly less refined—more pressure in the ears—but it's a huge discount. If you want Sony's sound signature but need the foldable case, this is the sweet spot. The only reason to skip it is if you plan to fly more than 10 times a year; the XM5's ANC is noticeably better on long hauls.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has better noise cancellation for airplanes?

The Sony WH-1000XM5. It reduces low-frequency engine drone significantly better than the Bose QC45. In blind tests on the same flight, the Sony felt noticeably quieter. The Bose is good; the Sony is exceptional.

Are the Bose QC45 comfortable for 10-hour flights?

Yes. This is their biggest advantage. The low clamp force and plush earcups make them the most comfortable headphones for extended wear. The Sony XM5 starts to feel tight around the 4-hour mark.

Can I use these with the airplane entertainment system?

Yes, both come with a 3.5mm audio cable. Note: The Sony XM5's ANC does not work in wired mode unless the battery is on. The Bose QC45 also requires battery power for ANC. If the battery dies and you're wired, you get passive noise isolation only (no active cancellation).

Do these have a transparency mode for hearing announcements?

Yes. Sony's "Ambient Sound" mode is more natural. Bose's "Aware" mode is okay but sounds a bit processed. For talking to flight attendants, Sony is better.

Which is better for taking calls at the airport?

The Sony XM5. Its beamforming mics with wind reduction are far superior. The Bose QC45 mics pick up too much ambient noise; callers will hear terminal chatter.

Bottom Line Verdict

If you fly long-haul more than once a quarter and want silence, buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. It has the best ANC on the market, excellent call quality, and a warm sound that makes movies and music immersive. The non-folding design is annoying, but the performance justifies the bulky case. If you fly for work and need to pack light, or if you have a sensitive head that hurts with clamping force, buy the Bose QC45. It's more comfortable, folds flat, and the ANC is still good enough to make a flight peaceful—it just won't erase the engine like the Sony. Both are excellent. The Sony is the superior tool for the specific job of killing airplane noise. The Bose is the better travel companion for everything else.
