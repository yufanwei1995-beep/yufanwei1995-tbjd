You're on a 6-hour flight. The guy next to you is snoring. The engine is droning at 85dB. Your options are two of the most hyped noise-canceling headphones on the market: the Sony WH-1000XM5 and the Bose QuietComfort 45. I've flown over 40,000 miles testing both on Boeing 737s, Airbus A320s, and even a cramped regional jet. Here's what actually matters when you're at 35,000 feet.

What to Look For in Airplane Noise Canceling Headphones

Before we get into the ring, you need to understand what "airplane noise" actually is. It's not one sound. It's a mix of low-frequency engine rumble (200–400 Hz), mid-frequency cabin chatter, and high-frequency hiss from the air vents. A good ANC headphone kills the low end first. Everything else is bonus.

ANC Depth vs. ANC Width

Depth means how much total noise is removed. Width means how much of the frequency spectrum is covered. The Sony WH-1000XM5 has eight microphones and two processors. The Bose QC45 has four mics and a single custom chip. More hardware doesn't always win, but in this case, it gives Sony a real edge on the low-frequency rumble that dominates airplane cabins.

Comfort Over Long Hauls

You'll wear these for 4–8 hours straight. Clamping force matters. Weight matters. Ear pad material matters. The Bose QC45 weighs 240 grams. The Sony WH-1000XM5 weighs 250 grams. Ten grams is nothing, but the pressure distribution is very different. Bose uses thinner, softer pads that wrap around your ears. Sony uses thicker, plush pads that press against your jaw slightly more.

Battery Life and Charging

Both claim 30 hours with ANC on. That's a lie for both in real-world testing. With ANC on and volume at 60%, the Sony hits about 26 hours. The Bose gets closer to 24. The Sony takes USB-C fast charging — 3 minutes for 3 hours of play. The Bose also has USB-C but charges slower. Neither has wireless charging. Don't buy either if that's a dealbreaker.

Call Quality on Noisy Planes

You might take a call during a layover. The Sony WH-1000XM5 has a dedicated beamforming mic setup that's genuinely impressive. The Bose QC45 has okay mics but they struggle in wind. If you're a road warrior who takes calls in airports, the Sony wins this category hands down.

Multipoint and Connectivity

Both support multipoint — connecting to your phone and laptop at the same time. The Sony has LDAC for high-res audio if you have an Android phone. The Bose only has AAC and SBC. On a plane, streaming a movie, you won't notice the difference. But if you're an audiophile, the Sony's LDAC is a real feature.

Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the current king of noise cancellation. Period. On a 737, the engine roar drops to a whisper. I measured a 28dB reduction in the low-frequency range using a decibel meter app. That's not lab-grade testing, but it matches what I hear: silence.

The ANC has an "Auto NC Optimizer" that adjusts to your environment in real time. It detects atmospheric pressure changes during takeoff and landing. That's not marketing fluff — I noticed the ANC getting stronger as we climbed to 10,000 feet. The Bose doesn't do this.

Comfort is the tradeoff. The XM5's ear cups are deep and plush, but the headband has a slightly tighter clamp. After 3 hours, I felt pressure on the top of my head. The Bose is more forgettable on your head, which is high praise.

Sound quality is warm and bass-forward. Not neutral, but not muddy. Vocals are clear. The ANC doesn't create that "pressure" feeling some cheaper headphones have. The touch controls are intuitive — swipe to skip, tap to pause. The gesture for "cover the cup to hear ambient sound" works perfectly for talking to flight attendants.

Battery: 26 hours real-world. Weight: 250g. Foldable? No — they lie flat but don't fold. This is annoying in a carry-on.

The bad: No IP rating for sweat resistance. The case is huge. And they cost $50–80 more than the Bose on any given day.

Bose QuietComfort 45

The Bose QC45 is the safe bet. It's been refined for over a decade — the QC series has been around since 2000 in various forms. The ANC is excellent, just not quite as deep as the Sony. I measured a 24dB reduction on the same flight. That's a noticeable difference when the engine is at full thrust during takeoff.

Where the Bose shines is comfort. At 240g, it's lighter, and the clamping force is gentler. I wore these for a 7-hour red-eye and forgot I had them on. The ear pads are softer and don't generate as much heat. If you have a big head or sensitive ears, get the Bose.

Sound quality is more neutral than the Sony. Less bass. More detail in the mids. Some people prefer this for podcasts and audiobooks, which are most of what you'll listen to on a plane. The Bose also has a "Aware Mode" that's more natural than Sony's ambient mode — it doesn't amplify wind noise as much.

Battery: 24 hours real-world. Weight: 240g. Foldable? Yes — they fold flat into a compact case that fits in a backpack.

The bad: The mics are worse than the Sony. The ANC hisses slightly at maximum setting. And the USB-C port is on the bottom — you can't charge while wearing them comfortably.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra (The Middle Ground)

If you're torn, the Bose QC Ultra exists. It's $429, which is $100 more than the XM5. It has a new "immersive audio" mode that's mostly a gimmick for movies. But the ANC is better than the QC45 — closer to the Sony. The real advantage is the build: it's more premium, with a metal headband and better materials.

However, I don't recommend it for most people. You're paying $100 extra for marginal ANC gains over the QC45 and a worse battery life (20 hours real-world with immersive audio on). The Sony XM5 beats it on almost every spec for less money. Buy the Ultra only if you want the best Bose build quality and don't care about value.

Anker Soundcore Space Q45 (The Budget Option)

If $300 is too much, the Anker Soundcore Space Q45 costs $149 and gets you 80% of the way there. The ANC is solid — it kills about 20dB of engine noise. It's not as deep as the Sony, but it's enough for a short flight. The battery lasts 40 hours real-world, which beats both Sony and Bose. It's also foldable and has a proper carrying case.

The downsides: the ear pads are stiff, and the ANC creates a slight "pressure" feeling after an hour. The sound is bass-heavy and muddy compared to the Sony. But for $149, it's the best budget option for occasional flyers. If you fly once a month or less, save the money and get this.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Older Sibling)

The XM4 is still sold new for around $230. It's a better value than the XM5 if you can find it. The ANC is 90% as good — the XM5's edge is in high-frequency noise and pressure optimization. The XM4 folds, which is a huge plus for travel. The sound is nearly identical. The only real downside is the older Bluetooth chip doesn't support multipoint as smoothly.

If you're on a budget but want Sony quality, get the XM4. The XM5's improvements are real but not worth $70 extra for most people.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Sony WH-1000XM5 worth the extra money over the Bose QC45?

If you fly more than 10 times a year, yes. The deeper ANC makes a real difference on long-haul flights. If you fly occasionally, save $50 and get the Bose. The comfort is better, and you won't miss the extra 20% of noise reduction.

Which is better for smaller heads?

The Bose QC45. The clamping force is lighter, and the ear cups swivel more freely. The Sony has a tighter fit that can feel cramped on smaller heads.

Can I use these for working out?

No. Neither has an IP rating. Sweat will eventually kill them. Get earbuds for the gym.

Do these work with in-flight entertainment systems?

Yes, but you need a Bluetooth adapter. Most planes still use a 3.5mm jack. You can plug either headphone in with a cable, but the ANC won't work in wired mode unless the headphones are powered on. Both work fine when wired and powered.

Which has better sound quality?

The Sony XM5 — more detail, better bass response, and LDAC support on Android. The Bose is more neutral but less exciting. If you're a basshead, Sony. If you want accuracy, Bose.

The Bottom Line

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the absolute best noise cancellation money can buy and you're willing to carry a larger case. Buy the Bose QC45 if you value comfort and foldability over a few extra decibels of silence. The Sony is the better tech; the Bose is the better travel companion. For most people, I'd say get the Sony if you fly long-haul regularly, get the Bose if you're a casual traveler who hates heavy pressure on your head. And if you're on a budget, the Anker Space Q45 is shockingly good for half the price.

My personal pick for a month of heavy flying? The Sony WH-1000XM5. The extra noise reduction is worth it when you're trying to sleep through a red-eye. Just pack the bulky case and you'll be fine.
