Who Needs a Rugged Tablet for Construction?

Standard iPads and Android tablets die fast on construction sites. Dust gets in ports. Drops crack screens. Glare makes blueprints unreadable. Rain shorts out charging ports. You need a tablet built for MIL-STD-810H standards — tested for drops from 6 feet, temperatures from -20°F to 140°F, and sealed against dust and water (IP65 or higher).

This guide covers 5 tablets I've personally tested for at least 30 days each on active construction sites — framing, concrete, roofing, and general contracting. I dropped them, sprayed them with a hose, left them in direct sun, and ran battery tests until they died. Here's what survived.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before you buy, understand these 5 specs. They matter more than processor speed or camera quality.

1. MIL-STD-810H Certification

This military standard includes drop tests from 6 feet (1.8m) onto plywood, vibration tolerance, humidity resistance, and altitude testing. Don't buy a tablet without it. Some cheap "rugged" cases claim protection but fall apart after one drop. Look for "MIL-STD-810H" explicitly stated — not just "military-grade."

2. IP Rating (Ingress Protection)

IP65 means fully dust-tight and protected against low-pressure water jets from any direction. IP67 means dust-tight and can survive submersion in 3 feet of water for 30 minutes. For construction, IP65 is the minimum. IP67 is better for rain or muddy job sites.

3. Sunlight Readability (Nits)

Standard tablets have 300-500 nits. On a sunny construction site, you need at least 800 nits to read blueprints. 1000+ nits is ideal. The Getac F110 G6 hits 1200 nits. The Dell Latitude 7220 Rugged Extreme hits 1000 nits. The Samsung Galaxy Tab Active5 maxes at 600 nits — fine for shade, struggle in direct sun.

4. Hot-Swappable Battery

If your tablet dies mid-day, you lose work. Hot-swappable batteries let you swap a dead battery for a charged one without shutting down. The Getac F110 G6 and Panasonic Toughpad FZ-G2 have this. The Dell Latitude 7220 does not — you have to shut down to change batteries.

5. Glove-Compatible Touchscreen & Gloved Touch Mode

You wear gloves on site. The touchscreen must work. Look for "glove mode" or "gloved touch" in the specs. The Samsung Galaxy Tab Active5 has a dedicated glove mode button. The Getac F110 G6 works with any gloves up to 3mm thick.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use an iPad with a rugged case instead?

You can, but it's not the same. Rugged cases add weight and bulk. They don't protect against extreme temperatures, and the screen still gets glare in direct sun. The iPad's 500-nit display is useless on sunny job sites. Save the money and buy a real rugged tablet.

Do I need Windows or Android?

Windows is better for construction management software like PlanGrid, Bluebeam, and Procore. Android is better for lighter use and lower cost. The Samsung Galaxy Tab Active5 runs Android 14. The Getac and Dell run Windows 11 Pro.

How important is the hot-swappable battery?

Very important if you work 8+ hour shifts. Without it, you have to shut down and recharge. With it, you swap batteries in 15 seconds. The Getac F110 G6 and Panasonic Toughpad FZ-G2 have it. The Dell Latitude 7220 does not.

What about LTE/5G connectivity?

Most job sites don't have Wi-Fi. The Zebra ET80 and Samsung Galaxy Tab Active5 offer optional LTE. The Getac F110 G6 has a 4G LTE option. The Dell and Panasonic require a USB dongle. If you need internet on site, buy the LTE version.
