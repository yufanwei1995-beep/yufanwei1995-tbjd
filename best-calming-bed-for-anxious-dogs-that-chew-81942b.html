What to Look for in a Calming Bed for a Chewer

Most "calming" beds are fluffy donuts. A chewer sees those as a stuffed toy. Here's what actually matters when your dog's anxiety comes out through its jaws.

1. Fabric: Ripstop vs. Flannel

If your dog chews, skip the faux fur and fleece. You need ballistic nylon or ripstop canvas. These are the same materials used in padded envelopes and military gear. They resist punctures. A standard "calming fur" bed will be in shreds by Tuesday.

2. The "Chew Proof" Warranty

This is your safety net. Brands like K9 Ballistics and PetFusion offer a chew-proof warranty on their tough beds. If your dog destroys it, they replace it. That's not marketing; that's them betting on their own product. If a bed doesn't have this warranty, it isn't chew-proof.

3. The Calming Shape

The "donut" or "cuddler" shape works because it provides a headrest and a sense of being held. But you can get that shape in tough fabric. Look for a bolster that's firm, not floppy. A floppy bolster is a chew target. A firm one is a pillow.

4. Washability

Anxious dogs drool, shed, and have accidents. If the bed isn't machine washable, you'll be scrubbing by hand. Look for a removable, zip-off cover. For the truly tough beds, you might have to hose them down instead. That's a tradeoff.

5. The Base

If the bed is on the floor, a chewer can dig at the seams. An elevated bed removes that leverage. It also keeps them cool, which helps with anxiety. A flat, low-profile bed is easier to flip over and attack.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I just use a crate mat instead?

Yes. If your dog is in a crate, a simple raised cot or a tough mat often works better than a plush bed. It gives them less to grab onto. We use the K9 Ballistics Orthopedic in the crate, but many owners use a simple rubber stall mat for horses.

Q: My dog chews the bed because of separation anxiety. Will a bed fix that?

No. A bed is a tool, not a cure. If the chewing is triggered by you leaving, you need to address the anxiety with training or a vet. The bed just survives the behavior while you work on it.

Q: How do I get my dog to stop chewing the bed?

Exercise first. A tired dog is less anxious. Then, make the bed "unattractive" by spraying it with a bitter apple spray. But don't rely on that—it wears off. The best trick is to catch them in the act and redirect to a bully stick.

Q: Is the memory foam safe if they eat it?

No. If your dog is a deep chewer that swallows pieces, you need to avoid foam entirely. Go with the elevated bed. Swallowing foam can cause a blockage. If you see them swallowing chunks, call your vet.
