Garmin vs Coros for Ultra Marathon Training: A Real Comparison

If you're clocking 15-hour weeks and planning 100-mile races, your watch is your lifeline. It tracks your effort, navigates you home in the dark, and tells you when to back off. Garmin and Coros are the two brands that dominate this space, but they approach ultra training differently.

This guide is for runners who want to stop second-guessing and just pick the right tool. I've spent years testing both ecosystems on trails, in rain, and across 24-hour races. Here's what matters and what doesn't.

Quick Summary

  • Who this is for: Trail runners and ultramarathoners who need reliable GPS, long battery life, and honest recovery data.
  • Our top pick: Coros Vertix 2S for most ultra runners — it wins on battery life and value.
  • Price range: $350 (Coros PACE 3) to $900 (Garmin Fenix 8 AMOLED).

What to Look For in an Ultra Running Watch

Before we get into specific models, understand the five things that actually matter for ultra training.

Battery Life in GPS Mode

This is the non-negotiable. A 100-miler takes most people 20 to 30 hours. Your watch must last that long with GPS on, plus have juice left for emergencies. Look for a minimum of 24 hours in full GPS mode. Coros leads here, but Garmin has closed the gap with newer models.

GPS Accuracy in Difficult Terrain

Tree cover and canyon walls scramble signals. You want a watch with multi-band GPS (also called dual-frequency). It uses more satellite frequencies to lock onto your position. This is critical for pacing in the mountains, where a wrong turn costs you an hour.

Navigation Features

Ultra courses often have poor markings. You need breadcrumb navigation, the ability to load a GPX file, and a map that shows you which way to go when the trail splits. Both brands do this, but Garmin's maps are more detailed; Coros is simpler but easier to read in bright sun.

Recovery and Training Load

After a 30-mile run, you need to know when to run hard again. Garmin uses Training Readiness and Body Battery. Coros uses Training Load and Recovery Time. Both are useful, but they calculate differently. Garmin's metrics feel more aggressive; Coros is more conservative.

Ease of Use

You'll be fiddling with buttons in the dark while exhausted. A simple menu system is worth more than a fancy feature you can't find. Coros has a cleaner interface. Garmin has more features but a steeper learning curve.

Product Reviews

1. Garmin Fenix 8 (AMOLED and Solar)

The Fenix 8 is Garmin's flagship for a reason. It's a rugged, feature-packed beast that does everything except cook your dinner.

The AMOLED version is gorgeous, but it chews battery. The Solar version is the one to buy for ultras — you get 48 hours in GPS mode with solar charging. That's enough for a 200-mile race if you're fast.

Navigation is the best in the business. TopoActive maps come preloaded, and the touchscreen makes zooming and panning intuitive. The new speaker and microphone let you make calls from your wrist, which is handy mid-race.

Downsides? It's heavy (80g) and expensive. The recovery metrics can be overwhelming with all the training status, HRV, and sleep scores. But if you want the most complete tool, this is it.

2. Coros Vertix 2S

The Vertix 2S is Coros's answer to the Fenix. It's lighter (65g), has a longer battery life (70 hours in full GPS mode), and costs less than the Fenix 8 Solar.

Battery life is the headline. You can run a 100-miler, leave the watch on your wrist for a week, and still have power. The new ECG app and improved heart rate sensor are nice additions, but battery is the killer feature.

The navigation is solid but basic. You get breadcrumb trails and elevation profiles, but no detailed topo maps. It's enough for following a route, but you'll want a phone for exploring.

The training load feature is simpler than Garmin's. It gives you a daily load number and a base fitness score. It's less overwhelming, but you lose some granularity.

3. Garmin Forerunner 965

If you want the Fenix's smarts in a lighter, cheaper package, the 965 is your pick. It's 47g, has a bright AMOLED screen, and includes full maps.

Battery life is 23 hours with GPS, which is tight for a 100-miler. You'll need to charge it mid-race or use the battery-saver mode that drops to 1-second recording intervals.

The training metrics are identical to the Fenix 8. You get Training Readiness, HRV status, and daily suggested workouts. These are excellent for periodizing your training.

The main tradeoff is durability. It's plastic, not titanium. It's fine for trails, but it won't survive a rock slide like the Fenix.

4. Coros PACE 3

At $349, the PACE 3 is the best value in this list. It's a lightweight (30g) running watch that does 90% of what the Vertix does.

Battery life is 38 hours in GPS mode — plenty for a 100-miler if you're under 24 hours. It has dual-frequency GPS, a barometer, and a surprisingly good wrist heart rate sensor.

The biggest omission is maps. You get breadcrumb navigation, not full topo maps. For well-marked courses, this is fine. For true mountain ultras, you'll want a phone backup.

It also lacks the training load sophistication of the Vertix. But for a runner who just wants a reliable, light watch that tracks distance and time, this is unbeatable.

5. Garmin Enduro 3

The Enduro 3 is a niche tool for ultra runners who don't need a touchscreen or maps. It's built for maximum battery life — 120 hours in GPS mode with solar.

It strips out the AMOLED screen and most of the smart features. You get a black-and-white display, but you get insane battery life and a 57g weight.

The physical buttons are easy to use with gloves or wet hands. The torchlight is a game-changer for night runs — it's bright enough to light the trail in front of you.

The downside? No maps. You get breadcrumbs only. And the price is high for such a minimalist feature set. But if battery life is your #1 priority, this is the winner.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which brand has better GPS accuracy?

Both are excellent with multi-band GPS. In my tests, Garmin is slightly more accurate in heavy tree cover, but Coros is more consistent on open trails. The difference is under 1% — not enough to matter for pacing.

Can I use Garmin or Coros for daily smartwatch features?

Yes, but neither is a great smartwatch. Notifications work, and you can control music. But don't expect app store support or contactless payments on Coros. Garmin has Garmin Pay, but it's limited to certain banks.

How do the training apps compare?

Garmin Connect is more detailed but cluttered. The Coros app is cleaner and easier to read. Both offer free cloud storage for your data. Garmin's training plans are more robust; Coros's are simpler but effective.

Which watch is more durable?

Garmin Fenix and Enduro are built like tanks with titanium bezels. Coros Vertix is also rugged but uses a polymer case. Both are water-resistant to 100m. For everyday trail running, both will last years.

Do I need a chest strap for heart rate?

For daily training, wrist HR is fine on both. For intervals and races, a chest strap is more accurate. Both brands support ANT+ and Bluetooth straps. Coros's wrist sensor is slightly better for steady-state runs; Garmin's is better for HIIT.

Bottom Line Verdict

For most ultra runners, the Coros Vertix 2S is the smart buy. It gives you 70 hours of GPS battery, a comfortable 65g weight, and all the training metrics you need — at $200 less than the Fenix 8. The only reason to pick Garmin is if you need detailed maps on your wrist or you want the most advanced recovery algorithms. If that's you, the Garmin Fenix 8 Solar is worth the premium. For budget runners, the Coros PACE 3 is unbeatable. Whichever you choose, you're getting a tool that will carry you through 100 miles and beyond.
