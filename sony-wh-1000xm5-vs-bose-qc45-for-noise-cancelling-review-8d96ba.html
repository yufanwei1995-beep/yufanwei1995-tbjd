Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters in ANC Headphones

Before diving into the two heavyweights, understand the four pillars of a good noise cancelling headphone. If you get these wrong, you waste your money.

1. Noise Cancelling Depth vs. Breadth

ANC is not just about "how quiet." It is about which frequencies get killed. Rumbling airplane engines are easy to cancel. Human voices, crying babies, and keyboard clacks are hard. A good ANC chip handles both. Sony uses two processors and eight microphones. Bose uses a simpler system with four mics. The result is different, as you will see below.

2. Comfort Over Long Sessions

A headphone that sounds amazing but hurts after 40 minutes is useless. Look at clamping force and ear pad depth. The Sony XM5 has a wider headband that distributes weight better but changes the pivot angle. The Bose QC45 uses a lighter frame with deeper ear cups. Test them for at least an hour before buying.

3. Call Quality and Microphone Performance

Most people buy ANC headphones for calls in noisy places. The mic array matters more than the driver size here. Sony's beamforming mics are aggressive at isolating your voice. Bose has a simpler array that sounds more natural but picks up more background noise. Which do you prefer? You will know after the first phone call.

4. Battery Life and Charging Speed

Both claim 24-30 hours. Real world testing shows the Sony lasts longer with ANC on (around 32 hours at 70% volume) versus the Bose (around 24 hours). But the Bose charges faster via USB-C: a 15-minute charge gives 3 hours. Sony gives 5 hours in 10 minutes. Both have quick charging, but the math works differently.

Sony WH-1000XM5 Review

Price: $298-$348 | Rating: 4.8/5 | Best for: Frequent travelers and audiophiles

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the current king of noise cancelling. It uses a V1 processor paired with the QN1 chip. That combination creates a "negative sound wave" that is more precise than anything I have tested. On a Boeing 737, the engine drone drops to a faint whisper. On the subway, the screech of rails is completely gone. It is spooky how quiet things get.

Sound quality is equally impressive. The 30mm carbon fiber drivers deliver tight bass and crisp highs. The soundstage is wider than the XM4, meaning you can pick out individual instruments. For hip-hop, the bass hits hard without muddying vocals. For classical, the strings have a natural texture. The only complaint? They sound "processed" compared to wired audiophile cans. But for wireless, they are top-tier.

Comfort is a mixed bag. The new "faux leather" pads are plush, but the headband is less adjustable than the XM4. If you have an oval-shaped head, the clamp feels uneven after 2 hours. The hinge design also means they cannot fold flat. They take up more space in a backpack.

Call quality is the weakest link. The eight mics are great at blocking wind, but your voice sounds slightly compressed. Callers say I sound like I am in a tunnel. The Bose is more natural here.

The bad: No IP rating for sweat resistance. The touch controls are finicky with gloves. The case is huge.

Bose QC45 Review

Price: $179-$249 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: All-day wear and budget buyers

The Bose QuietComfort 45 is the safe choice. It does not have the surgical precision of the Sony, but it kills noise in a broader, more natural way. The ANC here is "ambient aware" — it removes constant drone but lets sudden sounds (like a car horn) through slightly. That is actually safer for city walking. On a plane, the engine noise is reduced by about 90% compared to the Sony's 95%. The difference is noticeable but not deal-breaking.

Sound is neutral and flat. That is a compliment. Where Sony boosts bass, Bose keeps it honest. Acoustic guitars sound live. Podcasts and audiobooks are incredibly clear. If you are not a bass head, you will prefer this tuning.

Comfort is where the QC45 destroys the Sony. The ear cups are deeper and the clamping force is lighter. I wore them for 6 hours on a cross-country flight and forgot they were on. The headband has better pivot adjustment for different head shapes. This is the headphone to buy if you work 10-hour days.

Call quality is good but not great. Voices sound natural, but background noise (like a coffee shop) leaks in more than on the Sony. The mics are older tech.

The bad: The app is basic. No EQ adjustment on iOS (you get presets only). The plastic build feels cheaper than the Sony. The case is also bulky.

Alternatives Worth Considering

Not sold on either? Here are three more options that fill the gaps.

1. Sony WH-1000XM4

Price: $228-$278 | Rating: 4.6/5 | Best for: Budget Sony fans

The XM4 is the previous model. It folds flat, has a lighter headband, and the ANC is 90% as good as the XM5. The bass is slightly boomier. If you want Sony quality but hate the XM5's non-folding design, buy this. It is often $70 cheaper.

Pros: Folds flat, better headband adjustability, cheaper

Cons: Older Bluetooth chip, worse call quality

2. Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Price: $329-$379 | Rating: 4.7/5 | Best for: Immersive audio

The QC Ultra is the premium Bose. It adds spatial audio and a customizable ANC slider. The ANC is on par with the Sony XM5. But it costs more than both. Buy this if you want Bose comfort with Sony-level noise killing.

Pros: Best-in-class comfort, immersive sound, excellent ANC

Cons: Expensive, battery life drops to 24hrs with spatial audio

3. Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless

Price: $249-$299 | Rating: 4.4/5 | Best for: Music purists

The Momentum 4 has a 60-hour battery life (double the Sony) and the best sound quality of the group. The ANC is good but not class-leading. The build is premium with real leather. If you care more about audio fidelity than silence, this is your pick.

Pros: 60hr battery, superb sound, premium materials

Cons: Bulky design, ANC weaker than Sony/Bose

FAQ

Which has better noise cancelling, Sony or Bose?

Sony WH-1000XM5 blocks more noise, especially high-frequency sounds like voices and keyboard clicks. Bose QC45 is better at reducing pressure on your ears, which some people prefer.

Are the Sony XM5 worth the extra money?

Only if you fly weekly or work in a loud open office. The extra $100 buys you 8 more hours of battery and noticeably quieter flights. If you wear headphones all day at a desk, the Bose is the better value.

Do the Bose QC45 have an EQ in the app?

No. The QC45 app only has presets (Bass Boost, Treble Boost, etc.) with no custom EQ slider. The Sony app has a 5-band EQ. This matters if you like tweaking sound.

Can I use these for workouts?

Neither has an IP rating for sweat resistance. The Sony XM5 is more fragile due to the non-folding hinge. Do not run with these. Get the Beats Studio Pro or Jabra Elite 45h instead.

Which is better for phone calls?

Bose QC45 sounds more natural to callers. Sony XM5 blocks more background noise but makes your voice sound like a robot. It depends on where you take calls.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you fly more than once a month, hate hearing office chatter, and want the absolute best noise cancelling money can buy. You sacrifice foldability and some comfort, but the silence is worth it. Buy the Bose QC45 if you wear headphones all day, want a lighter feel, and want to save $100. The QC45 is the smarter purchase for most people because comfort wins over marginal ANC gains. If you have a big budget and want the best of both worlds, get the Bose QuietComfort Ultra. But for the money, the QC45 is the value king.
