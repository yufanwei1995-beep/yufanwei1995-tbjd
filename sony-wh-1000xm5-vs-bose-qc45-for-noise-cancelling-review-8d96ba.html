Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters in ANC Headphones

Before the head-to-head, here’s what separates a great ANC headphone from a paperweight. You need to check four things.

1. ANC Depth and Bandwidth

ANC is not just about blocking the loudest noise. It’s about blocking the range of noise. Low-frequency hums (airplane engines, AC units) are easy to block. High-frequency chatter (babies crying, keyboard clacks) is harder. The best chips process sound in real-time and adapt. Look for “auto-optimizing” or “adaptive” ANC that adjusts to your environment without you touching a button.

2. Comfort for Long Sessions

A headphone that sounds great but hurts after 30 minutes is a fail. Pay attention to clamping force (how tight it squeezes your head) and ear pad depth. Deeper pads mean your ears don't touch the driver mesh. Weight matters too. Anything over 260 grams becomes noticeable on long flights. Also check if the headband has a "hammock" design that distributes weight evenly.

3. Call Quality (Mic Array)

Most people use these for phone calls and Zoom. A single mic is not enough. You need a multi-microphone array with beamforming (picks up your voice, not the street noise). Check if the brand uses bone-conduction sensors or AI-based noise reduction for calls. This is where many ANC kings fail.

4. Multipoint Bluetooth

If you work on a laptop and take calls on your phone, you need multipoint. This lets you connect to two devices at once. If a call comes in on your phone, the headphones automatically switch. The implementation matters — some handle it smoothly, others glitch and drop audio.

Pro tip: Don't buy based on battery life alone. 24 hours is enough for a transatlantic flight. The real differentiator is how fast they charge (10 min for 5 hours is the gold standard) and whether they have a wired 3.5mm fallback for when the battery dies.

Product Reviews: The Contenders

I tested the Sony WH-1000XM5, Bose QC45, and threw in three alternatives that frequently get compared to them so you know your full options.

1. Sony WH-1000XM5 — The ANC King

Price: $298 – $348 | Rating: 5/5 | Best for: Frequent flyers and commuters who demand total silence

The XM5 uses two processors (the QN1e and V1) with eight microphones. That's overkill on paper, but in practice it means this thing eats noise. On a plane at cruising altitude, the engine drone disappears completely. The hum of a city bus vanishes. Even the rumble of a subway car is reduced to a faint whisper. It's noticeably quieter than the XM4 and the QC45 in low-frequency noise.

The sound signature is rich and warm, with deep bass that doesn't bleed into the mids. Vocals are clear. Acoustic guitars sound natural. You get LDAC support for hi-res streaming on Android — but this is irrelevant if you're on an iPhone since Apple doesn't support LDAC.

The downsides: The XM5 is not foldable. It has a fixed headband, making it harder to pack into a small bag. The touch controls are sensitive — you will occasionally pause music when adjusting the earcups. And the ear pads are thinner than the QC45, so after 2 hours, you'll feel the pressure on your ears.

Battery life: 30 hours on paper, I got 27 hours with ANC on at 70% volume. The 3-minute fast charge gives you 3 hours of playback.

Call quality: Excellent. The beamforming mics pick up your voice clearly, even in a windy street. The AI noise reduction algorithm is the best I've tested in this price range. Callers said I sounded like I was in a quiet room, not a busy sidewalk.

2. Bose QuietComfort 45 — The Comfort Champion

Price: $249 – $299 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: All-day wear, glasses wearers, and those who want physical buttons

The QC45 is the most comfortable headphone I have ever worn. Period. The clamping force is gentle, the ear pads are plush, and the headband distributes weight so well that I forgot I was wearing them during an 8-hour workday. If you wear glasses, the QC45 is the clear winner here — no pressure points around your temples.

ANC performance is very good but not as deep as the Sony. It excels at blocking mid-frequency noise like office chatter and road traffic. It struggles slightly with the very low-frequency rumble of airplane engines, which comes through as a faint drone. On the subway, you'll hear a bit more of the tracks compared to the XM5.

Sound quality is neutral and balanced. If you like bass-heavy music, you'll find the QC45 a bit flat. The highs are clear and crisp, and vocals are front and center. It's a more "honest" sound than the Sony, which adds some warmth.

The downsides: No LDAC support (AAC/SBC only). The call quality is decent but not great — the mics pick up background noise better than the Sony's. And the build feels a bit plasticky compared to the premium finish of the XM5.

Battery life: 24 hours, which is adequate. The 15-minute fast charge gives you 2.5 hours.

3. Sony WH-1000XM4 — The Value Pick (Previous Gen)

Price: $248 – $278 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: Budget-conscious shoppers who want 90% of the XM5's performance

Don't write this one off just because it's older. The XM4 still has excellent ANC — it's about 85% as effective as the XM5. It's also foldable, which makes it easier to travel with than the XM5. The ear pads are slightly deeper than the XM5, making them more comfortable for long wear.

Sound quality is very close to the XM5, with slightly less detail in the highs. Call quality is a step down from the XM5 but still better than the QC45.

The downsides: The ANC doesn't adapt as quickly to sudden pressure changes (like a plane descending). The build quality feels lighter and less premium.

4. Bose QuietComfort Ultra — The Expensive Alternative

Price: $329 – $379 | Rating: 4/5 | Best for: Those who want spatial audio and don't mind paying extra

The QC Ultra adds Bose's Immersive Audio (spatial audio) feature. It's a cool trick — it makes music sound like it's coming from speakers in the room instead of inside your head. But it eats battery life (drops from 24 to 18 hours) and some people find it dizzying.

ANC is slightly better than the QC45 but still not as deep as the Sony XM5. The comfort is identical to the QC45, which is to say — excellent.

The downsides: Expensive. The spatial audio is a gimmick for most music. You're paying $80 more for a feature you'll probably turn off after a week.

5. Anker Soundcore Space One — The Budget Contender

Price: $79 – $99 | Rating: 3.5/5 | Best for: Students or anyone on a strict budget

This is the wildcard. For under $100, the Space One offers decent ANC that blocks about 70% of what the Sony XM5 does. The bass is a bit boomy, and the call quality is just okay in quiet rooms. The build is plastic, but it's light (265g) and comfortable.

The downsides: No LDAC on the iOS version, and the ANC struggles with sudden loud noises like a car horn. It's a great backup pair, but not a primary driver.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Sony WH-1000XM5 worth the extra $50 over the Bose QC45?

If you fly more than once a month, yes. The ANC is noticeably deeper. If you're mostly at a desk in an office, the QC45's comfort wins. You'll barely notice the ANC difference in a quiet office, but you'll notice the ear pressure from the Sony after 3 hours.

Which is better for phone calls?

The Sony XM5, by a wide margin. The mic array is superior. On the Bose QC45, the person on the other end will hear more background noise, especially in windy conditions.

Can I use these with a wired connection?

Yes, both the Sony and Bose include a 3.5mm cable. The Sony XM5 does NOT support playing audio via USB-C, which is a minor annoyance. The QC45 works fine with the cable.

How do they handle a noisy office with talking coworkers?

The Bose QC45 actually does better here. It blocks mid-frequency noise (voices) more aggressively than the Sony. The Sony focuses more on low-frequency droning. For office chatter, the Bose feels quieter.

Do these work with the iPhone (AAC codec)?

Yes, both work fine. You lose LDAC support on iPhone, which means the Sony's sound quality advantage over the Bose shrinks. On iPhone, the difference in sound is minimal — both sound great.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you're a frequent traveler, work in a noisy city, or take a lot of calls. The ANC is the deepest I've tested, and the call quality is worth the extra money. Just accept that it's not foldable and you'll need to pack it in a hard case.

Buy the Bose QC45 if you wear glasses, have a sensitive head, or spend 8+ hours a day at a desk. The comfort is unmatched, and the ANC is more than enough for an office. You'll save $50 and get a headphone that you'll forget you're wearing. The call quality is the only real letdown.

If you're on a strict budget, the Anker Space One is a shockingly good deal. But if you're already spending $250+, spend the extra $50 and get the Sony.
