Most automatic feeders are built for dry kibble only. Wet food is messy, spoils fast, and jams the auger mechanisms. But if you have a cat on a urinary health diet or one that refuses kibble, you need a machine that can handle the sticky stuff.

I tested five feeders over three weeks with two cats — one grazer, one inhaler. I measured portion accuracy with a kitchen scale, timed how long wet food stayed at a safe temperature, and timed how long each unit took to fully disassemble and clean. Here's what matters and what's worth your money.

What to Look for in a Multi-Pet Wet Food Feeder

Before you buy, understand the three big problems these machines try to solve.

1. Ice Packs vs. Refrigeration

Wet food left out for more than 4 hours becomes a bacteria farm. Most feeders use removable ice packs that sit under the food tray. They work — for about 6-8 hours. If you need a full day of coverage, you need a feeder with an actual cooling compartment (like the PetSafe Smart Feed Duo) or you must accept that wet food meals need to be scheduled within a few hours of each other.

2. Portion Control Per Cat

With multiple cats, you can't just have one bowl. You need either two separate bowls with separate schedules, or a feeder that uses a microchip to only open for a specific cat. The chip readers work well for dry food, but they're rare on wet food feeders because the food needs to stay fresh in an open tray.

Honest tradeoff: No wet food feeder on the market has a reliable per-cat microchip lock. The best you can do is two separate bowls with separate scheduled times, and hope your cats don't swap bowls.

3. Cleaning Difficulty

Wet food residue is greasy and sticky. If the feeder has a dozen small crevices, you'll stop cleaning it after a week, and then you're feeding your cats bacteria. Look for:

  • Dishwasher-safe trays (top rack, no heating cycle)
  • No internal augers or complex dispensing wheels
  • Wide, flat surfaces that wipe down in under 2 minutes

WOPET SmartFeeder (Model: L9002)

Best budget option for two cats on dry food. This feeder has two bowls side-by-side, each with its own lid and schedule. It's not designed for wet food — there's no cooling and the bowls are shallow — but it's the cheapest way to manage two cats' portions.

Setup takes 10 minutes. The digital timer is on the unit itself, no app needed. You can set each bowl to open up to 4 times per day with portions from 1/8 to 2 cups.

Downsides: No ice pack included. The plastic feels cheap and scratches easily. The lids don't seal tightly, so smells escape and cats will sit by the feeder crying. If you want wet food, skip this one.

SureFeed Microchip Pet Feeder

Best for cats on different diets — but dry food only. This is the gold standard for multi-cat households where one cat eats prescription food. Each cat wears a microchip or RFID collar tag, and the feeder only opens for that specific cat. It completely stops food stealing.

It has a sealed lid that keeps dry kibble fresh and blocks flies. The portion is controlled by the bowl size, not a dispenser, so you fill it manually. That's actually fine for wet food — you can put a portion of wet food in the bowl, and the lid stays closed until the right cat arrives.

The catch: No scheduled dispensing. You have to be home to refill it. And there's no cooling, so wet food left in the bowl for hours will spoil. It's a solution for portion control, not for convenience.

Downsides: No wet food capability at all. The dispenser is a rotating wheel that crushes soft kibble and completely jams on wet food. It's also a single bowl, so you'd need two units for two cats. FAQ

Can any automatic feeder keep wet food fresh all day?

No. The PetSafe Smart Feed Duo comes closest with 8 hours of cooling via ice packs. After that, the food hits room temperature. If you work 12-hour shifts, you need to schedule meals within an 8-hour window and have a backup plan for the evening meal.

Will my cats fight over the bowls?

With the PetSafe Duo, each bowl opens on its own schedule, so if you set them at different times, cats can eat separately. The SureFeed is better if you need strict per-cat control, but you sacrifice the automatic dispensing.

How often do I need to clean a wet food feeder?

Every day. The bowl and lid need a rinse with hot soapy water. The ice pack base needs a wipe-down every 3 days. If you skip cleaning, you'll get biofilm and a smell that won't come out.

Is the app worth the extra cost?

Only if you need to adjust portions remotely. The PetSafe app lets you change meal times and portions from your phone. The WOPET has no app, but the on-unit timer works fine if you don't travel.

Bottom Line

If you need wet food for multiple cats, buy the PetSafe Smart Feed Duo. It's the only feeder that combines dual bowls, separate schedules, and actual cooling. The $200 price hurts, but it's the only one that does the job without spoiling food. If your cats are strictly dry food eaters and you want per-cat control, get the SureFeed Microchip Feeder and accept that you'll refill it manually. Skip the WOPET and Catit PIXI for wet food — they'll frustrate you within a week.
