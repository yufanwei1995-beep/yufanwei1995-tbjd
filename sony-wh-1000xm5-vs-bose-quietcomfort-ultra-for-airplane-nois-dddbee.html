Who this is for: Frequent flyers, business travelers, and anyone who wants silence in a metal tube at 35,000 feet. If you hate engine drone and crying babies, this is your guide.

Our top pick: Sony WH-1000XM5 — it has the edge on raw noise cancellation and battery life, but the Bose QC Ultra wins on comfort for those with wider heads.

Price range: $299–$429 depending on sales. Both are premium, but you can often find them under $350 if you wait.

Why Airplane Noise Cancelling Is Different

I've tested over 40 pairs of noise-cancelling headphones in the last decade. Airplane cabins are the ultimate torture test. The low-frequency rumble of jet engines is constant, and the cabin pressure changes how your ears feel. Most headphones that sound great at home fall apart here.

You need two things: deep ANC that kills that 100Hz–300Hz drone, and a comfortable clamp force that won't give you a headache on a 10-hour flight to Tokyo. The Sony WH-1000XM5 and Bose QuietComfort Ultra are the two best options. But they approach the problem differently.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before we dive into the reviews, here's what actually matters for airplane use. Skip the marketing hype and look at these four specs:

1. ANC Depth and Bandwidth

Look for headphones with multiple microphones (8+ is good) and adaptive noise cancelling. The best ones use a processor that adjusts in real-time. Sonys use an "Auto NC Optimizer" that reads your ear shape and altitude pressure. That's real tech, not buzzwords.

2. Clamp Force and Earcup Depth

Heavy clamp = headaches. Shallow earcups = your ears touch the driver. The Bose QC Ultra has a lighter clamp (around 5.5N) versus the Sony's 6.2N. If you wear glasses, this matters a lot. Test both if you can.

3. Battery Life With ANC On

Flight times can exceed 14 hours on routes like Singapore to New York. You want at least 24 hours of real-world battery. Both of these deliver, but the Sony wins by a significant margin. Also check if they have fast charging — a 10-minute charge that gives you 5 hours is a lifesaver during tight layovers.

4. Wired Mode for In-Flight Entertainment

Most airline screens still use a 3.5mm jack. Both of these come with a cable, but only the Bose works fully in wired mode. The Sony's ANC stays on but the sound quality drops slightly. Do not buy either without checking this.

Individual Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $348 (often drops to $298 on sale)

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the noise-cancelling king. I flew from London to Los Angeles with these on, and the engine roar was reduced to a faint whisper. The ANC uses four microphones on each earcup and a dedicated processor that adapts to the cabin pressure changes during descent. It's eerie how quiet it gets.

Battery life is the headline: 30 hours with ANC on. That's enough for two long-haul flights plus a layover. The 10-minute quick charge gives you 5 hours of playback, which got me through a missed connection without stress.

Comfort is good but not perfect. The clamp is a bit firm, and if you have a wider head, you'll feel it after hour three. The earcups are deep enough for most ears, but the synthetic leather doesn't breathe as well as the Bose's. My ears got warm on an 11-hour flight to Doha.

The touch controls on the right earcup are intuitive, and the "speak to chat" feature — which pauses music when you talk — works surprisingly well in a noisy cabin. Just cup the earcup to hear the flight attendant without removing them.

Downsides: The carrying case is cheap plastic. No IP rating, so sweat from rushing through terminals is a risk. And the sound signature is a bit bass-heavy for audiophiles, though it's fine for movies.

"The Sony's ANC is so good that I missed my gate change announcement. That's both a compliment and a warning."

Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Price: $429 (often drops to $379)

The Bose QuietComfort Ultra is the comfort champion. I wore these for 14 hours straight on a Sydney to San Francisco flight, and my ears felt fresh. The clamp force is noticeably lighter than the Sony, and the earcups are plush with a softer foam. If you have a sensitive head or wear glasses, these are the safer bet.

ANC performance is 95% as good as the Sony. The difference is in the low-end. The Bose lets a tiny bit more engine rumble through, but it's barely noticeable unless you're A/B testing. What the Bose does better is handle sudden noises — like a baby crying or a cart clattering. It reacts faster to those mid-frequency spikes.

The "immersive audio" mode is a gimmick for planes. It makes music sound like it's coming from speakers in the room, but it also weakens the ANC. Leave it off. Stick to the regular ANC mode.

Battery life is 24 hours with ANC on, which is solid but six hours short of the Sony. The wired mode works properly — you can plug into the seat-back screen and the ANC still runs. That's a real advantage if you fly on older planes with finicky jacks.

Downsides: The price is $80 higher than the Sony for slightly worse ANC and battery. The touch controls are less responsive than the Sony's — I accidentally skipped songs twice when adjusting the headband.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (Budget Alternative)

Price: $248 (older model, still sold new)

If you want to save money but still get excellent ANC, the XM4 is the buy. The noise cancelling is about 90% of the XM5, and it handles the low-frequency jet drone well. The foldable design is actually better for travel than the XM5's bulky case.

Battery is 30 hours, same as the XM5. The clamp is a touch lighter than the XM5, making it more comfortable for longer flights. You lose the "speak to chat" feature's accuracy and the better call quality of the XM5, but for pure airplane noise cancelling, you won't feel shortchanged.

The only real downside is the older Bluetooth 5.0 chip, which means you can't connect to two devices at once as smoothly. On a plane, that's irrelevant.

Bose QuietComfort Headphones (Budget Alternative)

Price: $249 (often on sale for $199)

This is the non-Ultra version, and it's a value trap. It has the same comfortable fit as the Ultra but with older ANC tech. The noise cancelling is noticeably weaker on low frequencies — you'll hear more engine hum than either the Sony or the Ultra. I'd only buy these if you prioritize comfort over everything and find the Ultra too expensive.

Battery is 24 hours, and the wired mode works well. But the sound quality is flat and lifeless compared to the competition. For $50 more, the XM4 is a better deal.

Which One Should You Buy?

For most flyers, the Sony WH-1000XM5 is the right call. It has the best ANC, the longest battery, and you can find it for under $300 on sale. The only reason to pick the Bose is if you have a wide head or wear glasses — the lighter clamp makes a real difference on 10+ hour flights.

If you're on a budget, the XM4 is the smart move. You lose a little ANC power but keep the long battery life and save $100. Skip the base Bose QuietComfort — it's not worth the money when the XM4 costs the same.

One last tip: test the clamp force before you buy. Head over to a Best Buy or an electronics store and wear each pair for 15 minutes. Headaches from tight headphones will ruin your trip faster than any engine noise.
