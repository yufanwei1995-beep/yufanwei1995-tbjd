What to Look for When Buying a Portable Power Station for Camping

You don't need an engineering degree to pick the right one. Focus on these five things.

1. Capacity (Wh) vs. Your Actual Needs

Capacity is measured in watt-hours (Wh). A 300Wh station charges a phone 20 times but won't run a mini-fridge overnight. For a weekend camping trip with a cooler, lights, and device charging, aim for 500Wh-1000Wh. For a week off-grid with a fridge and CPAP, go 1500Wh+.

Bigger isn't always better. A 2000Wh station weighs 50+ pounds. You'll hate carrying it from your car to the campsite. Be honest about what you'll actually plug in.

2. Solar Input (W) – How Fast It Charges in Sun

This is the number that matters when paired with solar panels. Look for maximum solar input in watts. A station with 200W max input will charge faster than one with 100W, assuming you have enough panel wattage.

Pro tip: Don't buy a station that maxes out below 100W solar input. It'll take all day to charge from empty. 200W+ input is the sweet spot for practical camping use.

3. Output Ports – What You'll Actually Plug In

Check the AC outlets. Most stations have a pure sine wave inverter (good for sensitive electronics). Count USB-A, USB-C (especially 100W PD for fast laptop charging), and 12V car ports. If you run a CPAP, verify it has a 12V output – it's more efficient than using the AC inverter.

4. Weight and Size – You Carry This

Anything over 30 pounds is a car-camping-only station. If you hike to a site, keep it under 15 pounds. The tradeoff: lighter stations have less capacity. Know your camping style.

5. Battery Chemistry – LiFePO4 vs. Lithium-Ion

LiFePO4 (Lithium Iron Phosphate) lasts 3,000-4,000 charge cycles vs. 500 for standard lithium-ion. It's safer (won't catch fire if punctured) and handles heat better. It's also heavier. For camping, LiFePO4 is worth the extra weight if you plan to use the station for years. Most premium 2026 models use it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run a CPAP machine on a power station all night?

Yes, but check the wattage. Most CPAPs draw 30-60W with heated humidifier off. A 500Wh station runs it for 8-10 hours. With humidifier on, draw jumps to 80-120W. You'll need at least 1000Wh for a full night. Always use the 12V car port – it's more efficient than AC.

How many solar panels do I need?

One 200W panel charges a 1000Wh station in 5-6 hours of good sun. Two 200W panels (400W total) cut that to 2.5-3 hours. For a 2000Wh station, you need at least 400W of panels for a full day charge. Don't buy more panels than your station's max solar input can handle.

Can I leave a power station plugged into solar panels all the time?

Yes. All modern stations have built-in charge controllers that stop charging when the battery is full. Leaving them connected is fine. But disconnect if storing for months – even trickle charging slowly degrades the battery over years.

Which battery type lasts longer?

LiFePO4 (Lithium Iron Phosphate) lasts 3,000-4,000 charge cycles. Standard lithium-ion lasts 500-1,000 cycles. For camping, LiFePO4 is better if you use the station weekly. If you camp twice a year, standard lithium-ion is fine and cheaper.

Do I need pure sine wave output?

Yes, for sensitive electronics like laptops, CPAPs, and camera batteries. Modified sine wave can damage them over time. All stations in this review use pure sine wave inverters. Don't buy one that doesn't.
