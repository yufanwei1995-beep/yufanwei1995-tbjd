You've got a dog that won't settle, eats dinner in 30 seconds flat, or destroys shoes when you're on Zoom calls. You've seen the ads for lick mats and snuffle mats. Both promise to "keep them busy." But they work in completely different ways, and picking the wrong one wastes money and leaves your pup frustrated.

I've tested both with my own border collie mix (who has the attention span of a gnat) and a foster greyhound (who is basically a couch potato). Here's the honest breakdown of what each tool does, which one you need, and the exact products worth your cash.

Lick Mat vs Snuffle Mat: The Core Difference

A lick mat is a flat silicone or rubber pad with grooves and ridges. You smear peanut butter, yogurt, or wet food on it, then let your dog lick. The repetitive licking action releases calming endorphins. It's passive — your dog stays in one spot.

A snuffle mat is a fabric mat with fleece strips sewn into a base. You hide kibble or treats deep in the strips. Your dog uses their nose and paws to sniff and dig out the food. It's active — they move their head, use their brain, and burn physical energy.

So the short answer: Lick mats are for calming. Snuffle mats are for tiring out. If you have an anxious dog that paces and whines, get a lick mat. If you have a dog with endless physical energy, get a snuffle mat. If you have a lab, get both.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Material and Safety

For lick mats, you want food-grade silicone. Not rubber — silicone won't hold odors and is easier to clean. The Lickimat brand uses medical-grade silicone, which is the gold standard. Avoid cheap PVC mats that smell like a shower curtain; your dog will be licking this for 20 minutes straight.

For snuffle mats, fleece is non-toxic and soft. But check the base. A non-slip backing (like TPR dots) keeps the mat from sliding across the floor while your dog digs. You don't want a mat that slides into the wall every time they use it.

Cleaning

Lick mats are easy — rinse under hot water or toss in the dishwasher. Snuffle mats are a pain. You have to shake out crumbs, then hand wash or machine wash on gentle. If your dog drools a lot, the fleece gets slimy. Look for mats with a mesh base that allows water to drain when washing.

Size and Durability

For lick mats, small (6-8 inches) works for dogs under 25 lbs. Large (10-12 inches) for bigger dogs. A flimsy mat will bend and warp after a few washes. The good ones stay flat.

For snuffle mats, bigger is better for large dogs. A 12x12 inch mat is fine for a chihuahua, but a 16x16 inch mat suits a golden retriever. Check the stitching — if the fleece strips are sewn tightly, they won't pull out. Loose stitching means you'll be picking fleece out of your dog's poop in a week.

FAQ

Can I use both a lick mat and a snuffle mat in the same day?

Yes, and you should. Use the snuffle mat for breakfast to burn energy, then the lick mat after a walk or before bed to calm down. They serve different purposes and complement each other.

How do I clean a snuffle mat without ruining it?

Shake out loose kibble, then soak in warm soapy water for 10 minutes. Rinse thoroughly and hang to dry. Machine washing on gentle is fine, but skip the dryer — heat melts the non-slip backing.

Can I leave my dog unsupervised with a lick mat?

Only if your dog isn't a chewer. Lick mats are made of silicone, which can be torn and swallowed. Supervise the first few uses, and if you see bite marks, take it away.

Which one is better for separation anxiety?

Lick mat, hands down. The repetitive licking releases serotonin and reduces cortisol (the stress hormone). Give it to your dog 5 minutes before you leave the house. The snuffle mat is too stimulating if they're already panicking.

Bottom Line

Buy the Lickimat Wobble if your dog needs to calm down, has separation anxiety, or eats too fast. Buy the AWOOF Snuffle Mat if your dog has endless energy and needs to burn it off through foraging. If you can only afford one, get the AWOOF — it serves more purposes (slow eating, energy burning, boredom relief) and works for more dogs. But the honest truth is that a truly busy dog needs both, so budget for the pair.
