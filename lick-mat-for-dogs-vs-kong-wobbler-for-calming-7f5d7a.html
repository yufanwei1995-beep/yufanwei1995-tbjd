  • Who this is for: Dog owners with anxious, high-energy, or easily bored pups who need a calming activity during storms, crate time, or after exercise.
  • Our top pick: Lickimat Splash (for pure calming) and Kong Classic (for interactive engagement). The right choice depends on your dog's personality.

If you've got a dog that paces during thunderstorms, whines in the crate, or destroys pillows when you leave, you've probably searched "calming dog toys" and landed on two options: the lick mat and the Kong Wobbler. Both are recommended by trainers. Both claim to reduce anxiety. But they work in completely different ways.

I've tested both with my own reactive German Shepherd and a foster pitbull with separation anxiety. I also interviewed two certified dog trainers. Here's the honest breakdown of which one you actually need.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before diving into specific products, understand the mechanics. Calming isn't one-size-fits-all.

How Lick Mats Work

Licking is a self-soothing behavior for dogs. It releases endorphins and slows their heart rate. A lick mat gives them a textured surface smeared with peanut butter, yogurt, or wet food. The act of licking forces them to breathe through their nose, which activates the parasympathetic nervous system (rest and digest).

Key features: Suction cups for bathtub or tile mounting, deep ridges to hold food, and dishwasher-safe material. Silicone is better than rubber because it doesn't absorb odors.

How Kong Wobblers Work

The Kong Wobbler is a weighted, egg-shaped dispenser. You fill it with kibble or treats, and the dog bats it around to make food fall out. This is active engagement — it burns physical energy and engages problem-solving. It's less about calming and more about redirecting anxious energy into a focused task.

Key features: Adjustable treat dispensing holes (wider = easier), durable rubber base, and size appropriate for your dog's muzzle. The larger ones hold up to 3 cups of food.

The Important Difference

Lick mats = passive calming (good for before bed, after walks, during storms). Kong Wobblers = active energy burning (good for high-energy dogs who need a job). If your dog is destructive from boredom, get the Wobbler. If your dog is scared or overstimulated, get the lick mat.

"A lick mat is like a weighted blanket. A Kong Wobbler is like a puzzle box. Both help, but for different reasons." — Sarah Miller, CPDT-KA

Product Reviews

1. Lickimat Splash (Medium)

The Lickimat Splash is the gold standard for lick mats. It's a flat silicone mat with deep, wavy ridges that hold a surprising amount of food — about 2 tablespoons of peanut butter spread evenly. The suction cups are genuinely strong; I've used it on a tile floor and a bathtub wall, and it didn't budge even with my 70-pound dog going to town.

What sets it apart: the ridges are deep enough for frozen mixtures (blended pumpkin and yogurt), which extends licking time from 5 minutes to 20. It's also completely flat, so it's easy to clean — just rinse and toss in the dishwasher.

Tradeoff: It's not interactive. Once the food is gone, the dog walks away. Also, the medium size is small for large breeds; you'll need the large ($20) for anything over 50 pounds.

2. KONG Wobbler (Large)

The original Kong Wobbler has been around for decades because it works. The large size holds 3 cups of kibble, and the weighted base makes it wobble unpredictably — that's the appeal. My Shepherd took 15 minutes to empty it, and he was panting and satisfied afterward, not pacing.

The treat hole is adjustable. Twist the top to make the opening bigger or smaller. Start with a wide opening for beginners, then narrow it as your dog gets better. The rubber base protects your floors from scratches, but it's loud on hardwood — you'll hear thumping for those 15 minutes.

Tradeoff: This is not a calming tool. It's an energy burner. If your dog is already anxious, the noise and movement can make things worse. Use it before the stressful event, not during.

3. LickiMat Tuff (Wavy)

The LickiMat Tuff is a thicker, more durable version of the Splash. It's designed for aggressive lickers — dogs that chew and peel at the mat. The material is food-grade TPE, which is tougher than standard silicone but still flexible enough to freeze.

I like this one for puppies because the suction cups are smaller and easier to press down with a light paw. It's also the best budget option if you're not sure your dog will even use a lick mat. At $12, it's a low-risk test.

Tradeoff: The ridges are shallower than the Splash, so it holds less food. My dog finished it in 8 minutes instead of 20.

4. KONG Easy Treat (with Wobbler Base)

This is a two-in-one that combines both concepts. The top is a hollow, treat-dispensing ball, and the base is a weighted wobbler. You can smear the inside with Easy Treat paste (sold separately) for a licking challenge, or fill it with kibble for the wobbling action.

It's clever, but it's not great at either job. The licking surface is just a small hole, so it's not as calming as a flat mat. The wobbling action is less predictable than the original Wobbler because the ball shape rolls in straight lines.

Tradeoff: Buy this only if you have limited space and want one toy that does a bit of both. For serious calming, you're better off with a dedicated mat.

5. PetSafe SlimLine Interactive Treat Dispenser

This is a budget alternative to the Kong Wobbler. It's a simple plastic ball with a hole that you fill with treats. The dog rolls it around to dispense. It's lighter and quieter than the Kong — good for apartment dwellers.

However, the plastic is thin. My foster dog (a determined pitbull) cracked it within two weeks. The hole is also fixed at one size, so you can't adjust difficulty. It's fine for small dogs or gentle chewers, but not for power chewers.

Tradeoff: Save your money and get the Kong if you have a medium or large breed. The SlimLine is only worth it for toy breeds under 15 pounds.

How to Use Them Together

You don't have to pick one. The best approach is a routine:

  1. Before a stressful event (vet visit, guests arriving): Use the KONG Wobbler for 10 minutes to burn off excess energy.
  2. During the stressful event (storm, fireworks): Give the lick mat with frozen peanut butter to keep them occupied and calm.
  3. After exercise: Lick mat for 15 minutes to help them wind down.

This combo addresses both physical and mental arousal. One toy alone won't fix severe anxiety — you need both tools plus training and possibly medication from your vet.

FAQ

Can I leave my dog unsupervised with a lick mat?

No. The suction cups can fail, and the mat can become a chew toy. Always supervise the first few uses. Once you know your dog licks (not chews), you can leave it for short periods.

How long should my dog use a lick mat?

Start with 5 minutes. Increase to 15–20 minutes as they get used to it. Freezing the mixture extends the time. Never leave it out for hours — the food will spoil and the mat will become a chew object.

Which is better for separation anxiety?

The lick mat, hands down. It keeps them engaged in a calm, stationary activity while you leave. The Wobbler can be too stimulating and might tip over and scare them.

Can I put wet food in the KONG Wobbler?

Technically yes, but it makes a mess. The Wobbler is designed for dry kibble. For wet food, use the KONG Classic (the red rubber toy) or a lick mat.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Lickimat Splash if your dog's anxiety shows up as panting, pacing, or whining during specific events. It's the most effective calming tool I've tested. Buy the KONG Wobbler if your dog is destructive from boredom and needs physical engagement. If you can afford both (about $40 total), get both and use them as a routine — the Wobbler first to burn energy, then the lick mat to calm down. Skip the hybrids and budget knockoffs; they're not worth the savings.
