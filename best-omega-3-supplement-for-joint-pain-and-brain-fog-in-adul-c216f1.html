What to Look for in an Omega-3 Supplement (Buyer's Guide)

Before we get to specific products, you need to understand four things. These four factors separate the effective supplements from the expensive placebos.

1. EPA and DHA Dosage — The Actual Numbers

Most bottles say "1000mg Fish Oil" in big letters. That's the total oil weight, not the active ingredients. You need to look at the "Supplement Facts" panel for EPA and DHA specifically. For joint pain and brain fog, clinical research suggests you need at least 1000mg of combined EPA + DHA per day. Some studies use 2000mg+ for inflammation.

If a capsule has 1000mg of fish oil but only 300mg of EPA/DHA, you'd need to take three or four capsules daily. That gets expensive and increases burp risk.

2. Triglyceride Form vs. Ethyl Ester

Omega-3s come in two main molecular forms. Triglyceride (TG) form is how fish naturally store the oil. It's absorbed 50-70% better than ethyl ester (EE) form. EE form is cheaper to manufacture but your body has to convert it first, which means less gets into your bloodstream.

Look for "triglyceride form" or "re-esterified triglyceride" on the label. It's worth the extra cost.

3. Oxidation Testing (Freshness)

Fish oil goes rancid. Rancid oil is not only useless — it's harmful. It creates free radicals that cause more inflammation. Professional bodies like the International Fish Oil Standards (IFOS) program test for oxidation levels.

If a product doesn't mention third-party testing, move on. You want a product with a Certificate of Analysis (COA) that shows low TOTOX (total oxidation) values.

4. Enteric Coating or Lemon Flavoring

Fish burps are real. They happen when the oil digests too quickly and releases fishy-smelling gases in your stomach. Enteric-coated capsules dissolve in your intestines, not your stomach. This eliminates burps almost entirely. Some brands add lemon oil to mask the taste, which helps but isn't as effective as enteric coating.

If you've tried fish oil before and hated the burps, prioritize this feature.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long until I notice a difference?

Don't expect overnight results. Omega-3s take time to build up in your tissues. Most clinical studies show benefits appearing after 8-12 weeks of consistent use. In my testing, I started noticing reduced morning stiffness around week three, but the full brain fog-clearing effect took about six weeks.

Can I take omega-3 with my blood pressure medication?

Omega-3s can slightly lower blood pressure and thin the blood. If you're on blood thinners like warfarin or high-dose blood pressure medication, talk to your doctor first. The interaction is usually mild at standard doses, but it's not worth the risk without medical advice.

Is krill oil better than fish oil?

Krill oil has a different molecular structure that some people absorb better. It also contains astaxanthin, a powerful antioxidant. However, krill oil is much more expensive and typically provides lower EPA/DHA per serving. Unless you have severe digestive issues with fish oil, standard fish oil is more cost-effective.

What about algae-based omega-3s?

Algae oil is an excellent option for vegetarians and vegans. It provides DHA primarily, with less EPA. For joint pain specifically, you need both EPA and DHA. Algae-based products are also more expensive. If you're not vegetarian, fish oil is better value.
