If you have multiple cats, you know the drill. The fast eater finishes her bowl, then pushes the slow eater aside and inhales his portion. The chunky one gets chunkier, and the timid one loses weight. You end up with one cat on a diet and another with a sensitive stomach, and you're playing referee at every meal.

This review is for anyone tired of that circus. A microchip feeder solves it by only opening for the specific cat whose microchip or RFID tag is registered to it. No chip, no food. This isn't about fancy tech for the sake of it—it's about portion control, reducing stress, and avoiding a $500 vet bill for a cat with pancreatitis from eating too much, too fast.

What to Look For in a Microchip Cat Feeder

Before you buy, understand that not all "microchip" feeders are equal. Some use the actual chip implanted in your cat. Others use a plastic tag hanging from the collar. Both work, but they have different failure points.

1. Chip or Tag? (The Big Question)

The implanted microchip is permanent. If your cat is chipped (most are), you don't need to remember to clip a tag on. But the sensor range is shorter and the door may not react as fast. Collar tags are easier to read from a distance, but they get lost, and some cats hate wearing collars. The best feeders support both—look for that.

2. Door Speed and Motor Strength

This is the #1 complaint with cheap feeders. A slow door means a fast cat can jam a paw in and pry it open. The motor needs to be strong enough to push back. Test this: if the door hesitates or makes grinding noises, the motor is weak. Look for a door that opens vertically (slides up) rather than a flap that flips, as vertical doors are harder to force open.

3. Number of Cats It Supports

Most feeders support unlimited cats, but that's a lie. The internal memory holds a certain number of chip IDs. If you have 4+ cats, check the spec sheet. The SureFeed supports up to 32 cats, which is overkill. The PetLibro supports 10. For most homes, 3-5 is fine.

4. Wet Food Support

If you feed wet food, you need a feeder with a stainless steel bowl and a sealed lid. Plastic bowls harbor bacteria. The lid must close tight to keep the food from drying out. Also, check if the bowl is removable and dishwasher safe. If it's not, you'll be scrubbing it by hand every day, and you'll hate it.

5. Backup Power

A power outage on a Sunday night is the worst time to find out your feeder is dead. The best models have a battery backup (AA batteries) that kicks in automatically. Some have a "fail-open" mode where the door stays open if the battery dies, so your cat isn't locked out from food. That's a good feature for safety, but it means the wrong cat can eat. Consider which risk is worse for your situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will this work if my cat doesn't have a microchip?

Yes. Every feeder on this list comes with a white RFID collar tag. You register that tag to the feeder. If your cat already has an implanted chip, you can use that instead—no need to buy anything extra.

What if my cat has a microchip but it's not ISO standard?

Most US and European chips are ISO 11784/11785. The SureFeed and PetLibro both support this standard. If your cat was chipped in a different country, check the chip frequency. Older chips in the US used a 125kHz frequency, which is NOT compatible. Call your vet if you're unsure.

Can I use it for wet food?

Yes, but only the SureFeed models have a bowl that seals well enough to keep wet food fresh. The PetLibro models will dry out the food within 4-5 hours. If you feed wet food twice a day, the SureFeed is your only good option.

What happens during a power outage?

All models have battery backup. The SureFeed uses 4 C batteries, which last up to a year. The PetLibro uses 4 AA batteries, which last about 3 months. If the batteries are dead AND the power is out, the door stays closed. That's why you should always keep fresh batteries in them.

Will the wrong cat get in?

Only if they're big and determined enough to force the door. The SureFeed's vertical door is the hardest to force. The PetLibro flap door is easier to push open with a paw. No feeder is 100% foolproof against a 15-pound cat who's determined to eat everything.

The Bottom Line

Buy the SureFeed Microchip Pet Feeder Connect if you can afford it. It's the most reliable, the door is the strongest, and the app data helps you monitor each cat's eating habits—which is the whole point of this purchase. If you're on a budget, get the SureFeed Original without the app. It's the same hardware for half the price. Skip the PetSafe Smart Feed unless you have 4+ cats and need the huge capacity—the collar tag requirement is too fragile. The PetLibro models are decent, but the plastic bowl and slower door make them a compromise. Spend the extra $30 and get the SureFeed. Your cats' health is worth it.
