Air Fryer vs Convection Oven: What's the Real Difference?

Both use a fan to circulate hot air. That's the shared DNA. But the air fryer is a tiny, powerful tornado. A convection oven is a gentle breeze in a big box.

An air fryer's heating element sits right next to a high-speed fan. It blasts food with hot air at high velocity. This strips moisture from the surface fast, creating that golden crust. A convection oven's fan moves air around a large cavity, but it's less intense. The air has more space to dissipate, so it hits the food slower.

For fries specifically, that difference matters. Here's what I found in my kitchen tests.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Capacity

Air fryers range from 3 to 10 quarts. A 5.8-quart model fits about 2 pounds of fries – enough for two hungry people. Convection ovens hold far more. A standard 30-inch oven fits a full sheet pan of fries for a family of six. If you cook for more than four people regularly, the oven wins on volume.

Airflow Power

Check the wattage. Air fryers typically run 1,200–1,700 watts. Higher wattage means faster preheat and better crisp. For convection ovens, look for "True Convection" with a third heating element near the fan. It's better than "Convection Bake," which uses only the bottom element.

Temperature Range

Most air fryers go up to 400°F. That's enough for fries. Convection ovens go higher, which is useful for roasting vegetables or baking bread. But for fries, 400°F is the sweet spot.

Ease of Cleaning

Air fryer baskets have non-stick coatings. Most are dishwasher safe. Convection ovens require manual scrubbing of racks and walls. If you hate cleaning, the air fryer is the better choice.

Noise

Air fryers are loud. Like a hairdryer on high. My Cosori measured 58 decibels. Convection ovens are quieter – around 45 decibels. If you watch TV while cooking, this matters.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you make crispy fries without oil in a convection oven?

Yes, but it takes longer. Use a wire rack on a baking sheet. This allows air to circulate around the fries. Bake at 425°F for 25–30 minutes, flipping halfway. They'll be crisp but not as crunchy as air fryer fries.

Is an air fryer just a small convection oven?

Basically, yes. But the smaller size means the fan is proportionally more powerful. That's why it crisps faster and better. The compact space concentrates the heat.

What type of potatoes make the best fries?

Russet or Yukon Gold. They have high starch and low moisture. That combination gives you a fluffy interior and a crispy exterior. Avoid waxy potatoes like red or new potatoes.

Do I need to soak fries before air frying?

For homemade fries, yes. Soak cut potatoes in cold water for 30 minutes. This removes excess starch. Dry them completely before cooking. It prevents sticking and improves crispiness.

Which uses less electricity?

Air fryers use less. A typical air fryer draws 1,500 watts. A full-size convection oven draws 3,000–5,000 watts. Even the Breville uses 1,800 watts. For a single batch of fries, the air fryer is cheaper to run.

Can I reheat fries in an air fryer?

Yes, and it's amazing. Reheat leftover fries at 350°F for 3–4 minutes. They come back to life – crisp and hot. A microwave makes them soggy. A convection oven takes 10 minutes and dries them out.
