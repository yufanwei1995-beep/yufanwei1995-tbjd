Why This Is Harder Than It Should Be

The M4 MacBook Pro handles two external 6K displays natively through its Thunderbolt 4 ports. So you'd think any hub would work. Wrong.

Most USB-C hubs on the market use DisplayLink technology or split bandwidth between ports. The result? You get 4K at 30Hz, which feels laggy when you move windows around. Or one monitor runs at 4K and the other drops to 1080p. Or the hub gets so hot it throttles and disconnects.

I tested five hubs over two weeks with a 14-inch M4 MacBook Pro (M4 chip, 24GB RAM). I ran dual LG 27UP850 monitors at 4K resolution. I checked for flicker, dropped frames, and whether the hub could handle a webcam, external SSD, and keyboard simultaneously.

Here's what I learned.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters

Before you spend money, understand these four things.

1. Thunderbolt 4 vs. USB-C with DisplayPort Alt Mode

Your M4 MacBook Pro has Thunderbolt 4 ports. To get two monitors at 4K 60Hz, you need a hub that uses Thunderbolt 4 or Thunderbolt 3 (which has the same 40Gbps bandwidth). USB-C hubs with DisplayPort Alt Mode cap out at one 4K monitor at 60Hz or two at 30Hz. That's the non-negotiable rule.

2. DisplayLink vs. Native Output

Some hubs use DisplayLink chips. These compress video through the USB bus and require driver software. They work, but they add CPU overhead and can cause micro-stutters. For gaming or video editing, avoid DisplayLink. For spreadsheets and coding, it's fine. I only recommend native Thunderbolt output in this guide.

3. Power Delivery Matters

Your M4 MacBook Pro wants 96W of power. If a hub only passes through 60W, your laptop will slowly drain under heavy load. Look for at least 85W pass-through, ideally 96W or 100W. Also check if the hub includes its own power brick — many don't, and then dual monitor output won't work at all.

4. Port Layout and Cable Length

This sounds dumb, but a hub with ports on the back is a pain if you plug in USB drives often. Also check the cable length. A 20cm cable means the hub sits right next to your laptop, which can clutter a desk. The CalDigit TS4 has a 24cm cable, which is just enough to tuck it behind a monitor.

For dual 4K, I used the DisplayPort output for monitor one and a USB-C to DisplayPort cable on a downstream Thunderbolt port for monitor two. Both ran at 4K 60Hz with zero issues. It also handled a Samsung T7 SSD (around 1,000 MB/s read) and a 4K webcam simultaneously.

The tradeoff: you only get one DisplayPort output. If you want dual HDMI, you'll need adapter cables. Also, the hub doesn't include an Ethernet port, so if you need wired network, buy a separate adapter.

The Anker 777 is a well-built aluminum dock with two HDMI 2.0 ports, one DisplayPort, gigabit Ethernet, and multiple USB-A and USB-C ports. It delivers 85W of power. The build quality is excellent — it feels like a premium product.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use two 4K monitors at 144Hz?

No. Thunderbolt 4 has 40Gbps bandwidth. Two 4K @ 60Hz streams use about 32Gbps of that. Two 4K @ 144Hz would need around 64Gbps. It's physically impossible with current Mac hardware. If you need high refresh rates, run one monitor at 4K 144Hz and the other at 4K 60Hz.

Will a $40 USB-C hub work?

No. Those hubs use DisplayLink or only support one 4K output natively. If you see a hub that says "dual 4K" for under $80, it's either using DisplayLink (which needs software) or it's lying about specs. Don't waste your money.

Do I need the M4 Pro or M4 Max for dual monitors?

No. The base M4 chip supports two external displays. The hub just needs to split the signal correctly. However, if you want three or four external monitors, you need the M4 Pro or M4 Max because the base M4 only supports two displays through Thunderbolt.

Can I close my laptop lid and still use dual monitors?

Yes, with all five hubs in this review. When the lid is closed, the MacBook Pro enters clamshell mode and uses the hub's power delivery to stay on. Just make sure the hub is plugged into a wall outlet, not powered by the laptop itself.

What cable do I need for the second monitor?

Most hubs have one or two dedicated video ports (HDMI or DisplayPort). If you need more, use a USB-C to DisplayPort cable plugged into a downstream Thunderbolt port. Make sure the cable is rated for 4K @ 60Hz — most USB-C cables are, but check the specs.

What I Learned From Testing

The biggest surprise was the Anker 777. It looks premium and costs more than the Kensington, but the bandwidth allocation issue with high-speed SSDs is a dealbreaker for video editors. The Satechi runs too hot for daily use, even though it worked fine in my short test.

The Plugable TBT4-HUB3C is the sweet spot for most people. It's half the price of the CalDigit, delivers full 96W power, and handles dual 4K @ 60Hz without breaking a sweat. The lack of Ethernet is annoying, but a USB-C to Ethernet adapter costs $20.

If money is no object, the CalDigit TS4 is the best dock I've ever used. The 2.5GbE port alone is worth it for anyone who moves large files to a NAS. The extra ports mean you'll never need another adapter again.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Plugable TBT4-HUB3C if you want dual 4K @ 60Hz without overspending. It's reliable, delivers full power, and its compact design fits any desk. If you need more ports or 2.5GbE Ethernet, spend the extra money on the CalDigit TS4 — it's the only hub that does everything perfectly. Skip the Anker 777 if you use high-speed SSDs, and skip the Satechi if you run long sessions — it gets too hot.
