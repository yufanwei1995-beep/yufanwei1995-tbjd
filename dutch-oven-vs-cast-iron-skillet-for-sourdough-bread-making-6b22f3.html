You've got your starter bubbling, your flour is fresh, and you're ready to bake. Then comes the question that splits the sourdough community: do you bake in a dutch oven or a cast iron skillet?

I've tested both side-by-side for over 200 loaves. The short answer: a dutch oven wins for most bakers. But the skillet has a real place, especially if you're baking batards or want a crisper bottom crust. Here's everything I learned.

What to Look For: The Buyer's Guide

Before we dive into specific products, you need to understand the three things that actually matter for sourdough:

1. Steam Trapping

Sourdough needs steam in the first 15 minutes of baking. That steam keeps the crust soft so the loaf can expand. A dutch oven traps steam naturally with its lid. A skillet can't do this alone — you'll need a metal bowl or a steam pan on the rack below.

2. Thermal Mass

Cast iron holds heat like a champ. When you place dough on a screaming-hot surface, it gets an immediate blast of heat. That creates oven spring — the dramatic rise in the first few minutes. Both dutch ovens and skillets excel here, but thickness matters. A thin skillet will lose heat faster than a thick dutch oven base.

3. Shape and Scoring

Round dutch ovens force you into round boules. A skillet lets you shape a long batard or an oval loaf. If you love those long, rustic loaves from artisan bakeries, the skillet gives you more freedom. You also get better access for scoring — no deep sides to wrestle with.

One more thing: preheating. Both need at least 45 minutes in a 450°F+ oven. Plan ahead or your dough will stick to a lukewarm pan.

My rule of thumb: if you're buying your first piece of cast iron for bread, get the dutch oven. If you already have a skillet and want to try sourdough without new gear, use it — with a steam hack.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bake sourdough in a skillet without a dutch oven?

Yes, but you need steam. Place a shallow pan with 1 cup of boiling water on the rack below your skillet. Spray the dough with water right before it goes in. This mimics the dutch oven's steam trap, though not perfectly.

Why is my bottom crust burnt in a skillet?

Skillets conduct heat directly to the dough's bottom. Drop your oven temp by 25°F, or place a baking sheet on the rack below to deflect some heat. You can also add a layer of cornmeal under the dough.

Is a dutch oven worth the extra money?

If you already have a skillet, no — spend $5 on a metal bowl to use as a cover. If you're starting from scratch, yes. The Lodge dutch oven is only $50 and outperforms every skillet in steam retention.

What about enameled vs bare cast iron?

Bare cast iron (Lodge) gets better oven spring because it has more thermal mass. Enameled (Le Creuset) is easier to clean and won't rust, but it costs 7x more. For bread specifically, bare cast iron wins.

Skip the Le Creuset unless you need a pot that doubles for braising and look great on your stove. The Victoria is a good middle ground if you bake huge loaves, but the handle is a pain.
