What to Look for in a Construction-Grade Rugged Tablet

Buying a rugged tablet for construction work isn't like picking an iPad. You're trading thinness for toughness. Here's what matters most.

Drop and Impact Protection

Look for MIL-STD-810H certification. This military standard tests drops from 4 to 6 feet onto plywood or concrete. Some tablets survive 6-foot drops. Others only 4. The higher the number, the less likely your screen cracks when a ladder tips over.

Ingress Protection (IP Rating)

Construction sites are dusty and wet. An IP65 rating means it's sealed against dust and low-pressure water jets. IP67 means it can survive being submerged in 3 feet of water for 30 minutes. IP68 is even better. For most field work, IP67 is the sweet spot.

Sunlight Readability

Standard tablets max out at 400–500 nits of brightness. That's useless under direct sun. You need 800 nits or more. Some rugged tablets hit 1,200 nits. If you're working outdoors, don't settle for less than 800 nits.

Battery Life

8 hours minimum. 10 to 12 hours is ideal. Hot-swappable batteries let you swap without powering down — critical when you're running Bluebeam or GPS mapping software all day.

Glove Touch and Wet Mode

You'll wear work gloves. The screen must register touches through thick gloves. Wet mode lets you use the tablet in rain without phantom taps. Both are non-negotiable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a standard iPad on a construction site?

Technically yes, but you'll break it within a week. Standard tablets aren't built for drops, dust, or rain. A rugged case helps, but it adds bulk and doesn't match the protection level of a MIL-STD certified device.

Do I really need a 1,000-nit screen?

If you work outdoors, yes. Standard 500-nit screens are unreadable in direct sunlight. 800 nits is the minimum. 1,000 to 1,200 nits is ideal.

What about the Microsoft Surface Pro 9?

The Surface Pro 9 isn't rugged. It has no IP rating and only survives a 2-foot drop. It's fine for an office, but not for a construction site.

How long do rugged tablets last?

With proper care, 3 to 5 years. The Panasonic and Getac models often last 5 years on active sites. Battery replacement is usually needed after 2 to 3 years.
