Quick Summary

Who this is for: Frequent flyers (20+ flights a year) who want a premium polycarbonate carry-on that fits in overhead bins and can survive baggage roulette.

  • Our top pick: Monos Carry-On Pro (for the built-in laptop sleeve and better weight distribution).
  • Price range: $225 – $295 depending on model and sales.
  • The honest truth: Both are excellent. The difference comes down to how you pack and whether you care about a front pocket.

What to Look For in a Premium Carry-On

Before we get into the matchup, here's what actually matters when you're living out of a 21-inch bag.

Weight

Every airline has a 15-22 lb limit for carry-ons. A bag that weighs 8.5 lbs empty leaves you 6.5 lbs for stuff on strict airlines like Ryanair. The Monos Carry-On Pro weighs 7.9 lbs. The Away Carry-On weighs 7.5 lbs. Both are on the heavier side for polycarbonate bags, but they're sturdy.

Wheels

Spinner wheels sound great until they wobble. Look for 360-degree Hinomoto wheels (the Japanese brand used in both brands). They roll smoothly and don't vibrate on carpet. Test them in-store if you can — cheap wheels make a bag feel like a shopping cart with a bad wheel.

Suspension System

This is the hidden feature. Both Away and Monos have a shock-absorbing suspension system in the telescopic handle. It reduces the jolt when you hit a curb or a crack in the sidewalk. It sounds gimmicky but it saves your wrist on long airport walks.

Interior Compression

Look for a compression system that actually works. The Away has a small mesh zipper panel. The Monos has a more robust compression panel with a buckle. The Monos system flattens your clothes better, giving you about 1-2 inches of extra packing depth.

Zippers

YKK zippers are the industry standard. Both brands use them, but the Monos zippers feel slightly smoother. If a zipper catches or skips, you'll want to return the bag immediately. That's a defect, not a design choice.

Individual Product Reviews

1. Away The Carry-On (Standard)

Price: $275 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: Minimalists who want a simple, clean shell.

The original that started the DTC luggage craze. It's a 21.7" x 13.7" x 9" polycarbonate shell with a 40L capacity. The exterior is a fingerprint magnet (the black and navy colors are the worst), but it's scratch-resistant. I've put mine through 30+ flights and it still looks decent.

The interior is simple: a mesh divider on one side, a compression panel on the other. No extra pockets, no wet bag. That's fine if you use packing cubes. The 360-degree wheels are smooth and quiet. The handle has minimal wobble when fully extended.

The downside: The interior compression panel is weak. It's a thin mesh that doesn't pull clothes tight. You'll get more wrinkles than with the Monos. Also, the handle grip is a hard rubber that gets uncomfortable on long walks.

Our take: It's a solid, dependable bag. But it's not the best value in 2026 because Monos offers more features for $20 less.

2. Monos Carry-On Pro

Price: $255 | Rating: 4.8/5 | Best for: Laptop carriers and over-packers.

The Carry-On Pro is the upgraded version of the standard Monos Carry-On. It adds a 15" laptop sleeve on the front, which unzips to create a separate compartment. This is a game-changer for business travelers who need quick access to a laptop at security.

It's 22.5" x 14.5" x 9" and weighs 7.9 lbs. That's slightly taller than the Away, which means it might not fit on smaller regional jets (like the CRJ-200). If you frequently fly on small planes, get the standard Monos Carry-On instead.

The interior has a real compression system with a buckle and a thick mesh panel. It pulls your clothes down flat and keeps them that way. The zippers are YKK with a rubberized pull that feels premium. The wheels are Hinomoto and glide like a dream.

The downside: The front pocket eats into the main compartment space. You lose about 3L of capacity. Also, the exterior scratches more easily than the Away because the polycarbonate is slightly thinner.

Our take: The best all-around carry-on for flyers who carry a laptop. The compression system alone is worth the switch.

3. Away The Bigger Carry-On

Price: $295 | Rating: 4/5 | Best for: Flyers who want maximum space without checking a bag.

This is Away's larger option, measuring 23.7" x 15.4" x 9.5" with a 47.8L capacity. It's technically too big for some budget airlines' carry-on limits (looking at you, Spirit and Frontier). But for Delta, United, and American, it fits.

The extra space is noticeable. You can fit a week's worth of clothes plus a pair of shoes. The interior is the same simple design as the standard Away — mesh divider and weak compression panel.

The downside: At 8.9 lbs empty, it's heavy. You'll hit the 50 lb weight limit for checked bags if you overpack, and you'll struggle to lift it into overhead bins on full flights. It's not for petite travelers.

Our take: Only buy this if you consistently fly mainline carriers with generous overhead bin space. Otherwise, stick with the standard size.

4. Monos Carry-On (Standard)

Price: $225 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: Budget-conscious flyers who want premium quality.

The standard Monos Carry-On is the same as the Pro but without the front laptop sleeve. It's 22" x 14" x 9" and weighs 7.5 lbs. It's the lightest bag in this comparison.

The interior has the same excellent compression panel as the Pro. The exterior is a scratch-resistant polycarbonate that handles gate checks better than the Pro. The color options are more muted (sage, black, and a nice olive green) compared to Away's brighter palette.

The downside: No external pockets at all. If you need quick access to a phone charger or passport, you'll be unzipping the main compartment every time.

Our take: The best value pick. You get 90% of the Pro's features for $30 less. If you don't carry a laptop, this is the one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Away or Monos more durable?

In our drop tests, the Away held up slightly better against hard impacts. The polycarbonate is thicker. But the Monos has a better handle mechanism that doesn't loosen over time. It's a wash overall.

Do these bags fit under the seat?

No. Both are 9" deep, which is too thick for most under-seat spaces. If you need an under-seat bag, look at the Monos Metro Backpack or Away's Day Bag.

Can I use these for international flights?

Yes, but check the specific airline. Most European and Asian low-cost carriers (EasyJet, AirAsia) have stricter size limits. The standard Away Carry-On fits, but the Monos Pro is borderline on some airlines.

Which brand has better customer service?

Both have 100-day trial periods. Away has more physical stores, which makes returns easier. Monos has faster email support. I've had good experiences with both, but Away's phone support is slower during peak season.

Are these worth the price compared to cheaper options?

Yes, if you fly more than 10 times a year. The suspension system and wheel quality are noticeably better than $150 bags. But if you're a casual flyer, save your money and get a Samsonite.

Buyer's Guide: How to Choose

Here's a simple decision tree based on my testing:

  1. Do you carry a laptop? Get the Monos Carry-On Pro. The front sleeve saves you 5 minutes at security every time.
  2. Do you fly on regional jets? Get the standard Away Carry-On. It's 0.8" shorter and fits overhead bins on small planes.
  3. Are you on a budget? Get the standard Monos Carry-On. It's $50 cheaper than the Away and has a better interior.
  4. Do you overpack? Get the Away Bigger Carry-On, but only if you fly mainline carriers.

The Bottom Line

Here's my honest take after testing these bags for over a year. The Away Carry-On is a reliable workhorse, but the weak compression panel and lack of pockets make it feel dated in 2026. The Monos Carry-On Pro is the better buy for frequent flyers because the laptop sleeve and superior compression system actually save you time and hassle. It's $20 cheaper, too. The only reason to pick Away is if you value the slightly smaller footprint for regional jets or you prefer the brighter color options. Buy the Monos Carry-On Pro if you carry a laptop, buy the standard Monos if you don't, and skip the Away unless you find it on sale for under $200.
