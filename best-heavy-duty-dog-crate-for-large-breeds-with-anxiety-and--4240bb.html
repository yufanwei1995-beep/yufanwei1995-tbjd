What to Look for in a Heavy Duty Crate (Buyer's Guide)

Before we get to the reviews, you need to understand what separates a real escape-proof crate from a wire crate with thicker paint. These five factors matter more than anything else.

1. Steel Gauge and Bar Spacing

Standard wire crates use 11-gauge or 12-gauge wire. Heavy duty crates should use 7-gauge or 8-gauge steel — that's roughly double the thickness. For bar spacing, you want 2 inches or less for large breeds. Anything wider and a determined dog can get a paw or snout through to work on the latch. The Impact Crate uses 7-gauge with 2" spacing. The Midwest Ultra in "heavy duty" trim still uses 10-gauge — closer to standard, and it shows.

2. Latch and Door Security

This is where most crates fail. A twist-lock or pressure latch looks secure but a dog that learns to hit it with a paw or nose will pop it open in seconds. You need either a double-locking mechanism (like the ProSelect) or a recessed pin system that's physically impossible to reach from inside (like the Impact). Slide-bolt latches are the worst — I watched a determined Pit Bull open one in 4 minutes flat.

3. Door Style and Hinges

Double doors are standard, but the hinge quality matters. Look for continuous steel piano hinges, not the small stamped hinges you find on wire crates. The double-door design on most heavy duty crates is a weak point — two doors mean two sets of latches to fail. The Ruff Tough Crate solves this with a single massive door that takes up the whole front, eliminating one failure point entirely.

4. Weight and Portability

Here's the honest tradeoff: a truly escape-proof crate weighs 80 to 150 pounds. The Impact High Anxiety weighs 112 pounds. You are not moving this around your house daily. If you need something portable for travel, you'll be sacrificing security. The Petmate Vari Kennel Ultra is the best compromise — it's 45 pounds and secure enough for car travel, but it's not stopping a determined chewer at home.

5. Chew Resistance and Floor Design

Anxious dogs don't just push on doors — they chew the edges, the tray, the corners. A metal pan floor is essential. The plastic trays on standard wire crates get destroyed in a week. The metal floors on the heavy duty options are also easier to clean when your dog has an accident during a panic episode. Look for rounded edges on the floor pan so your dog can't get teeth under a lip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a heavy duty crate hurt my dog's anxiety?

No. A crate that your dog can't escape from actually reduces anxiety over time. The panic comes from the feeling of being trapped AND knowing they can get out. When they learn they can't escape, most dogs eventually settle. That said, you should always pair crate training with behavioral work — the crate is a management tool, not a cure.

How do I size a crate for my large breed?

Your dog should be able to stand up, turn around, and lie down flat. For a 90-pound dog, that's typically a 42" crate. For 100-120 pounds, you need a 48". If between sizes, go up. A crate that's too small adds to anxiety. Measure your dog from nose to tail base and add 4-6 inches.

Can I return a heavy duty crate if my dog still escapes?

Most manufacturers (Impact, Ruff Tough) offer a 30-day return policy. ProSelect has a 90-day guarantee. Keep the box and packaging until you're confident your dog is contained. Also worth noting: Impact offers a lifetime warranty on their crates, which tells you how confident they are.

Is it safe to leave a heavy duty crate outside?

Yes, for short periods. The steel crates are weather-resistant and the Impact has drainage holes in the floor pan. But no crate should be a dog's primary living space — use it for management during high-anxiety times, not as permanent housing.

Will my dog hurt themselves trying to escape a heavy duty crate?

There's a real risk of paw damage, broken teeth, and jaw injuries from chewing on steel. If your dog is a severe chewer, the Ruff Tough Kennel's plastic construction is actually safer on teeth than steel. A dog will eventually stop trying if the crate truly won't give — but it may take a few weeks of frustrated attempts first.
