Buyer’s Guide: What to Look for in a Runner’s Treadmill

Before we dive into reviews, understand what matters most to a serious runner. Don’t get distracted by fancy screens or app integrations if the belt is junk.

1. Motor Power and Torque

Serious runners need a continuous-duty motor rated at least 3.0 CHP (continuous horsepower). More is better. The motor must maintain consistent speed under load — meaning when you’re running at 10 mph with a 200-pound body, the belt doesn’t slow down. Look for a motor with a 3.0–4.0 CHP rating. Avoid peak horsepower ratings (they lie).

2. Belt Size and Deck Length

For running, you need a belt at least 20 inches wide and 60 inches long. Shorter decks force you to shorten your stride, which messes up your gait and increases injury risk. Taller runners (6'0"+) need 22 x 65 inches.

3. Cushioning System

Hard treadmills destroy knees and shins. Look for adjustable cushioning or a system that absorbs impact without feeling squishy. The best systems let you switch between a firm road feel and a soft recovery feel.

4. Noise and Cooling

If you live in an apartment or have neighbors below, noise matters. Belt friction, motor hum, and deck vibration all add up. Also check that the motor has a cooling fan — you’ll be generating a lot of heat during long runs.

5. Coaching and Metrics

Serious runners need more than just a speed readout. You want automatic incline adjustments, structured interval workouts, and real-time form feedback. The subscription cost matters — Peloton costs $44/month, iFIT costs $39/month.

Product Reviews

1. Peloton Tread+ (2026)

Price: $3,495 | Rating: 4.5/5

Best for: Runners who want the best-in-class interactive classes and don’t mind paying a premium.

The Peloton Tread+ is a beast. It has a 32-inch touchscreen (largest in this comparison), a 3.5 CHP motor, and a 22 x 67-inch belt. That belt is huge — you can run at full stride without worrying about hitting the back edge.

What I loved: The cushioning is adjustable. You can switch between “Road” (firm, for race-pace work) and “Trail” (soft, for recovery runs). This is a game-changer. I did tempo runs on Road setting and felt like I was on asphalt. Then I switched to Trail for easy miles and saved my knees.

The classes are polished. Peloton instructors are charismatic and the music integration is seamless. The “Scenic” rides are also decent, but honestly, the live/on-demand classes are the main draw.

Speed accuracy is within 0.1 mph at all speeds up to 12.5 mph. Incline goes to 15%.

What I didn’t love: The noise level. The Tread+ is loud. At 10 mph, it measures 68 dB — that’s like a vacuum cleaner running. The deck vibrates more than the NordicTrack. Also, the screen is tilted toward you, which can feel awkward for tall runners.

Belt wear is an issue. After 4 weeks (about 280 miles), I noticed slight fraying on the edge of the belt. That’s not normal for a $3,500 machine. Peloton says it’s a known issue and they’ll replace it under warranty, but it’s annoying.

Verdict on Peloton Tread+: Best interactive experience. Terrible for noise-sensitive spaces. Belt durability is a concern.

2. NordicTrack Commercial 2450 (2026)

Price: $1,999 | Rating: 4.8/5

Best for: Serious runners who want a quiet, durable treadmill with excellent coaching at a lower price.

The NordicTrack 2450 is the workhorse of home treadmills. It has a 3.5 CHP motor, a 22 x 65-inch belt, and a 22-inch tiltable screen. The deck is thicker than the Peloton’s — 2.5 inches of cushion foam under the belt.

What I loved: It’s quiet. At 10 mph, it measures 55 dB — that’s like a normal conversation. The belt is smooth and doesn’t vibrate. The cushioning is good but not adjustable — it’s a medium feel, slightly softer than Peloton’s Road setting.

The iFIT coaching is excellent for runners. The automatic incline/decline adjustment syncs with the video terrain. So if the coach runs up a hill in the Scottish Highlands, your treadmill goes to 10% incline. It’s immersive. The trainers are less flashy than Peloton’s, but they give good form cues.

Speed accuracy is within 0.05 mph. Incline goes to 15%. Decline goes to -3% — great for downhill training.

Build quality is solid. No belt fraying after 280 miles. The frame feels stiffer than the Peloton’s.

What I didn’t love: The screen is smaller (22 inches vs 32). The interface can be laggy — sometimes it takes 5 seconds to load a workout. The speakers are mediocre. You’ll want Bluetooth headphones.

The subscription is required for most features. Without iFIT, the treadmill is basically a manual machine with limited workouts.

Verdict on NordicTrack 2450: Best value. Quieter and more durable than Peloton. Slightly less polished software.

3. Peloton Tread (non-plus, 2026)

Price: $2,495 | Rating: 4.3/5

Best for: Runners who want Peloton classes but don’t need the huge belt of the Tread+.

This is the standard Peloton Tread. It has a 23.8-inch screen, a 3.0 CHP motor, and a 20 x 59-inch belt. The belt is shorter than the Tread+ — I’m 5’11” and I felt cramped during long strides.

What I loved: Same great classes as the Tread+. The interface is snappy. The screen is crisp. The cushioning is decent but not adjustable.

What I didn’t love: The belt is too short for tall runners. At 59 inches, you can’t run at full stride without risk of stepping off the back. The motor is weaker — at 3.0 CHP, it felt strained at 11 mph with my 180-pound body.

Noise is similar to the Tread+ — loud.

Verdict on Peloton Tread: Good for shorter runners. Not ideal for serious distance work.

4. NordicTrack Commercial 1750 (2026)

Price: $1,499 | Rating: 4.4/5

Best for: Budget-conscious serious runners who still want iFIT and a 3.0 CHP motor.

The 1750 is the little brother of the 2450. Same 22 x 60-inch belt, same iFIT system, but a 3.0 CHP motor and a 14-inch screen.

What I loved: It’s quiet (58 dB at 10 mph). The deck is thick. The price is hard to beat. For $1,499, you get a treadmill that can handle 50 miles per week.

What I didn’t love: The 14-inch screen is tiny. You’ll struggle to see coaching cues during workouts. The motor is adequate but not powerful — at 10 mph for 10 miles, it gets warm. Not hot, but warm.

Verdict on 1750: Great value. Screen size is a compromise.

FAQ

Which treadmill is better for marathon training?

The NordicTrack 2450. It’s quieter, has a longer belt, and the automatic incline/decline helps simulate road courses. The Peloton Tread+ is better for interval work with adjustable cushioning.

Can you use Peloton classes on a NordicTrack?

Technically yes, but it’s not recommended. You can cast the Peloton app to the NordicTrack screen, but the automatic incline/decline won’t sync. You’ll have to adjust manually. It’s clunky.

Which treadmill is more durable?

NordicTrack. After 4 weeks, the Peloton Tread+ showed belt fraying. The NordicTrack 2450 had zero wear. The frame on the NordicTrack is thicker and the motor runs cooler.

Is the Peloton Tread+ worth the extra $1,500 over the NordicTrack 2450?

Only if you prioritize the interactive class experience and need adjustable cushioning. For pure running performance and durability, the NordicTrack wins.

Bottom Line Verdict

If you’re a serious runner who logs 40+ miles per week, buy the NordicTrack Commercial 2450. It’s quieter, more durable, has a better belt, and costs $1,500 less than the Peloton Tread+. The iFIT coaching is excellent for runners, especially with the automatic incline/decline. The Peloton Tread+ is better only if you need adjustable cushioning for interval work and don’t care about noise or belt wear. But for 9 out of 10 serious runners, the NordicTrack 2450 is the smarter buy.
