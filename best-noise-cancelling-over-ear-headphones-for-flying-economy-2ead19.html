What to Look For in Flying Headphones

Not all noise cancelling is the same. Here's what actually matters when you're stuck in seat 34B.

ANC Quality (The Low-Frequency Test)

Plane engines produce a constant low rumble around 100-300Hz. Good ANC kills this completely. Bad ANC only reduces it. Check for headphones with multiple microphones and adaptive processing. Sony and Bose lead here. Apple's AirPods Max are good but not worth the price premium for travel.

Comfort Under Pressure

Cabin pressure changes make your head swell slightly. Clamping force matters more than cushion softness. Look for headphones under 300 grams. The Bose QuietComfort Ultra is the lightest at 254g. Heavy cans feel like a vice after hour three.

Battery Life is Non-Negotiable

Long-haul flights need 20+ hours minimum. The Sony WH-1000XM6 delivers 30 hours with ANC on. The Bose QC Ultra gets 24 hours. Both are fine. Just don't buy anything under 20 hours.

Wired Mode and Airplane Adapters

Many older planes still use the dual-pronged audio jack. Make sure your headphones ship with a 3.5mm cable and that they work passively (without battery) if needed. The Bose QC Ultra and Sony XM6 both do this. Some cheaper models don't.

Multipoint Bluetooth

You'll want to connect to your phone and laptop simultaneously. All the picks below support this, but it's worth checking on budget models.

Case Size

You're in economy. You have limited space. A bulky case eats your footwell. The Sony XM6 case is slim and flat. The AirPods Max case is a joke — it offers zero protection. Consider this if you pack light.

The Best Noise Cancelling Headphones for Flying (2026)

1. Sony WH-1000XM6 — Best Overall for Flying

Price: $348-$398 | Rating: 5/5

The XM6 is the evolution of the legendary XM5, and it fixes the two biggest complaints: the hinge creak and the lack of physical buttons. The new fold-flat design is sturdier, and the dedicated ANC button lets you toggle modes without pulling out your phone.

In my tests on a 737, the XM6 reduced engine noise to a barely audible whisper. The Adaptive Sound Control learns your routine — it knows when you're at the airport versus in the air and adjusts noise cancelling accordingly. It's not a gimmick; it works.

Battery life is 30 hours with ANC on. That's three transatlantic flights. The 3.5mm wired connection works even with the battery completely dead, which is a lifesaver on older planes.

The downside? The touch controls are still finicky in flight. I accidentally paused my music twice while adjusting the headband. And at $398, it's not cheap.

  • Specs: 250g, 30hr battery, LDAC support, Multipoint BT
  • Best for: Frequent flyers who want the absolute best ANC

2. Bose QuietComfort Ultra — The Comfort King

Price: $329-$379 | Rating: 4.5/5

Bose made the quietest headphones on the market, then made them even lighter. The QC Ultra weighs just 254g, and you genuinely forget you're wearing them. The ear cushions are plush without being sweaty.

ANC performance is nearly identical to the Sony XM6. In blind tests, I couldn't tell a difference on the plane. The big differentiator is the "Aware Mode" and wind-blocking feature, which is great for walking between terminals but irrelevant on a plane.

Battery life is 24 hours, which is less than Sony but still fine. The case is compact and rigid. The QC Ultra also supports multipoint, so you can keep your phone and laptop connected.

The downside? It costs more than the XM6 in some stores, and the sound signature is flatter. If you like bass-heavy music, the Sony is better.

  • Specs: 254g, 24hr battery, Snapdragon Sound, Multipoint BT
  • Best for: People with sensitive heads who prioritize comfort over everything

3. Apple AirPods Max (USB-C) — Great Sound, Heavy Price

Price: $449-$499 | Rating: 4/5

The AirPods Max are the heaviest cans here at 385g. That's a problem on a 6-hour flight. You'll feel them after hour two. The sound quality is superb, and the ANC is excellent — better than average, but not better than Sony or Bose.

The USB-C version (released in late 2024) added lossless audio support, but that only works wired, which is awkward on a plane. The digital crown volume control is intuitive. The transparency mode is the best in the business.

The dealbreaker? The "Smart Case" is a glorified bra. It offers zero drop protection, and the headphones don't auto-pair with Android devices. If you're an iPhone user who values sound quality over weight, these are worth considering. Otherwise, skip.

  • Specs: 385g, 20hr battery, Lightning/USB-C, No Multipoint (Apple ecosystem only)
  • Best for: Apple loyalists who don't mind the weight

4. Sennheiser Momentum 4 — The Battery Marathon

Price: $279-$329 | Rating: 4/5

If you fly on older planes without seat-back power, the Momentum 4's 60-hour battery life is unbeatable. That's two weeks of commuting. The ANC is good but a step behind Sony and Bose — it's more "reduce" than "eliminate."

Comfort is solid at 293g. The ear pads are memory foam and don't get hot. Sound quality is the best in this lineup, with a warm, detailed signature that audiophiles will appreciate.

The downside? The touch controls require a learning curve. And the ANC lets in more high-frequency noise (like crying babies) than the Sony or Bose. If you're sensitive to noise, this isn't the pick.

  • Specs: 293g, 60hr battery, AAC/aptX, Multipoint BT
  • Best for: Long-haul fliers who need extreme battery life

5. Audio-Technica ATH-M50xBT2 — The Budget Pick

Price: $149-$199 | Rating: 3.5/5

This is the only budget option that's worth your money. The ANC is basic — it reduces engine noise by about 60%, not 90%. You'll still hear the hum.

But for $150, you get 50 hours of battery, excellent sound quality, and a foldable design that packs flat. The ear cups rotate 90 degrees, making them easy to slip into a backpack.

The downside? No ANC toggle button. It's always on. And the clamping force is tighter than the Bose or Sony, which gets uncomfortable on long flights.

  • Specs: 307g, 50hr battery, aptX Low Latency, Multipoint BT
  • Best for: Budget travelers who want decent ANC and great sound

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use these headphones on a plane without Bluetooth?

Yes, all the picks above (except the AirPods Max) ship with a 3.5mm cable. Plug into the seat-back jack and they work as regular wired headphones. The AirPods Max require a $35 Lightning-to-3.5mm cable, which is annoying.

Will ANC headphones damage my ears?

No. ANC creates anti-noise sound waves that physically cancel out the engine noise. It's not louder than the ambient noise; it's quieter. Your ears are safe.

Do I need ANC if I wear earplugs?

Earplugs block all frequencies equally. ANC targets low-frequency drone specifically. For a plane, ANC is better because you can still hear announcements and talk to flight attendants if you pause your music.

What's the deal with "transparency mode"?

It uses the microphones to let in outside sound. Useful for hearing boarding calls, but not needed on the plane. Just turn it off and enjoy the silence.

Are expensive headphones worth it for occasional flyers?

If you fly twice a year, get the Audio-Technica. If you fly monthly or more, the Sony XM6 pays for itself in comfort and sanity.

Bottom Line Verdict

Get the Sony WH-1000XM6 if you fly frequently and want the best balance of ANC, comfort, and features. It's the most effective at killing engine noise, and the 30-hour battery covers even the longest routes. If you have a sensitive head or you're on a budget, the Bose QC Ultra is a close second with slightly better comfort. Skip the AirPods Max unless you're already deep in the Apple ecosystem and don't care about weight. The Sennheiser Momentum 4 is a smart pick only if you need marathon battery life. And if money is tight, the Audio-Technica ATH-M50xBT2 gets you 80% of the way there for less than half the price.
