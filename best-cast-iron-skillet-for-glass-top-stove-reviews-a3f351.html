Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Not all cast iron is created equal when it comes to glass top stoves. Here's what I learned after testing 12 pans over three weeks.

1. Flatness is Everything

Glass top stoves heat from below. If the pan bottom isn't perfectly flat, you get hot spots and poor contact. Worse, a warped or convex bottom will rock back and forth, scratching the glass. Test at home: place a ruler across the bottom. You should not fit a piece of paper under the center.

2. Weight and Handling

You don't toss food in cast iron. You slide it. On a glass top, you'll frequently lift and reposition the pan. A 12-inch skillet from classic brands weighs 7-8 pounds empty. Add food and you're lifting 10+ pounds. Look for pans under 6 pounds if you have wrist issues or just want easier control.

3. Surface Smoothness

Standard cast iron has a bumpy, pebbled surface. On a gas flame, that's fine. On glass, it creates micro-scratches every time you slide the pan. Machined or polished surfaces (like those from Field Company or Finex) are smooth and glide easily. Budget pick: Lodge's Chef Collection is smoother than their classic line.

4. The "Slide" Test

Here's a trick I use: place the empty pan on the glass and try to slide it with one finger. If it drags or catches, the surface is too rough. If it skates, you're good. This simple test saved me from buying two pans that would have been terrible on glass.

5. Size Matters

Match the pan to your burner. A 12-inch pan on a 7-inch burner heats unevenly. For glass tops, I recommend a 10-inch pan for everyday use — it covers most burners completely and weighs less.

FAQ

Will cast iron scratch my glass top stove?

Only if the pan has a rough bottom or is warped. Smooth-bottomed pans are fine. Always lift the pan to move it — never slide it. That's the real culprit for scratches.

Can I use high heat?

No. Glass tops can crack from rapid temperature changes. Preheat on medium, then increase to medium-high. Never go above medium-high with cast iron on glass.

How do I protect my cooktop?

Use a silicone mat or a paper towel between the pan and glass when not cooking. And always set the pan down gently — don't drop it.

What about the weight — will it break my stove?

No. Glass cooktops are tempered to hold 50+ pounds. A 7-pound pan is nothing. The risk is dropping it, not the weight.

Do I need a special cast iron for glass tops?

No. You just need a flat, smooth-bottomed pan. The ones I tested above have the flattest bottoms I found.
