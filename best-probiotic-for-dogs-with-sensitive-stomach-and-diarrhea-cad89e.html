What to Look for in a Dog Probiotic

Not all probiotics are created equal. Most cheap brands die in the stomach acid before they reach the gut. Here is what matters:

  • Strain specificity: Look for names like Enterococcus faecium SF68 or Bifidobacterium animalis AHC7. These have clinical studies behind them for diarrhea in dogs.
  • CFU count (Colony Forming Units): You want at least 1 billion CFUs per dose. Anything less is a waste of money.
  • Enteric coating or microencapsulation: This protects the bacteria from stomach acid. If the label doesn't mention it, the probiotic may not survive the trip.
  • Prebiotics included: These are food for the good bacteria. Look for FOS or inulin in the ingredient list.
  • Form factor: Powders are easiest to mix into wet food. Chews are convenient but often have lower CFUs. Capsules are good but easy to lose if your dog is picky.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast will a probiotic stop diarrhea?

With a high-quality strain like FortiFlora, you should see firmer stools within 24-48 hours. With cheaper supplements, it can take 5-7 days. If diarrhea lasts more than 72 hours, call your vet.

Can I give my dog human probiotics?

No. Dogs have different gut flora than humans. Human strains like Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG are not effective for canine diarrhea. Stick to veterinary-specific formulas.

Should I use probiotics alongside antibiotics?

Yes, but space them out. Give the antibiotic and probiotic at least 2 hours apart. The antibiotic will kill the good bacteria if given at the same time.

Are there side effects?

Some dogs get gassy for the first few days as the gut microbiome shifts. This is normal. If gas is severe, reduce the dose to half for the first week.
