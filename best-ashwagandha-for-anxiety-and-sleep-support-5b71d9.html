What to Look For in an Ashwagandha Supplement

Not all ashwagandha is created equal. Here's the stuff that actually matters when you're comparing labels.

1. Standardized Extract vs. Raw Powder

Raw root powder is cheap, but the active compounds (withanolides) vary wildly. You want a standardized extract that guarantees a specific percentage of withanolides. Look for KSM-66, Shoden, or Sensoril on the label — these are patented extracts with clinical studies behind them.

2. KSM-66 vs. Sensoril vs. Shoden

  • KSM-66: Full-spectrum root extract. More energizing. Best for daytime anxiety and stress. 600mg is the clinical dose.
  • Sensoril: Leaf and root extract. More sedating. Better for sleep support but can make you feel groggy if taken during the day.
  • Shoden: The strongest — 35% withanolides vs. KSM-66's 5%. Potent but can be too stimulating for some anxious folks.

3. Withanolide Percentage

This is the active compound. You want at least 5% withanolides. KSM-66 has 5%, Shoden has 35%, Sensoril has 8%. More isn't always better — higher percentages can cause stomach upset or jitters in sensitive people.

4. Third-Party Testing

Look for USP, NSF, or ConsumerLab certification. The supplement industry is unregulated, and heavy metal contamination is a real issue with ashwagandha because it's a root that absorbs soil contaminants.

5. Form: Capsules, Gummies, or Tinctures

Capsules are the most consistent. Gummies are convenient but usually have added sugar and lower doses. Tinctures absorb faster but taste terrible and are harder to dose accurately.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does ashwagandha take to work for anxiety?

Most clinical studies show significant effects at 8 weeks, but you'll likely notice subtle changes in 1-2 weeks. The full benefit for sleep typically shows up around week 3-4. Don't expect a Xanax-like effect — this is a slow burn, not a firecracker.

Should I take ashwagandha in the morning or at night?

If you're using KSM-66 (like NutriRise), take it in the morning. It has mildly energizing properties. If you're using Sensoril (like Pure Encapsulations), take it at night. It's more sedating. Taking KSM-66 at night might make it harder to fall asleep.

Can I take ashwagandha every day?

Yes, but most experts recommend cycling — 8 weeks on, 2 weeks off. This prevents tolerance buildup and gives your system a break. It's not habit-forming, but your body does adapt.

What are the side effects?

The most common are stomach upset, nausea, and diarrhea — especially with higher doses or on an empty stomach. Take it with food. Rare side effects include liver issues, so if you have liver problems, talk to your doctor first. Ashwagandha can also interact with thyroid medication and blood pressure meds.

Is ashwagandha safe with other supplements?

Generally yes, but avoid combining it with other sedatives like melatonin or valerian root unless you want to sleep for 12 hours. Also, don't stack it with caffeine — you'll get conflicting signals.
