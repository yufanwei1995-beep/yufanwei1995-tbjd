Buyer's Guide: What Seniors Should Look for in a Treadmill

Don't get tricked by shiny screens or Instagram hype. For a senior, three things matter more than anything else:

1. Step-Up Height

This is the distance from the floor to the walking surface. Lower is safer. Anything over 8 inches is risky for someone with balance issues. The NordicTrack 2450 sits at 7.5 inches. The Peloton Tread+ is 10 inches — that's a real hazard if you're unsteady.

2. Belt Width and Length

Narrow belts force you to walk in a straight line. For seniors, a wider belt (20 inches minimum) reduces the chance of stepping off the side. The NordicTrack 2450 has a 22 x 65-inch belt. The Peloton Tread+ is 20 x 67 inches. Both work, but wider is better.

3. Cushioning System

Hard decks wreck knees and hips. Look for adjustable cushioning that absorbs impact. NordicTrack's "FlexSelect" cushioning lets you switch from soft (for walking) to firm (for running). Peloton uses a rubber belt with foam underlayment — good, but not adjustable.

4. Emergency Stop

Every treadmill should have a visible red stop button AND a safety key (a clip you attach to your clothes). If you fall, the key pulls out and the belt stops instantly. The Peloton Tread+ has a safety key. The NordicTrack 2450 has both a key and a big red button.

5. Display and Controls

Touchscreens can be frustrating for seniors with arthritis. Buttons with tactile feedback are better. The Sole F80 has a simple LCD screen with big buttons. The Peloton Tread+ has a 32-inch touchscreen that requires precise taps — not ideal for shaky hands.

This is the best treadmill for seniors in 2026. The step-up height is just 7.5 inches. The belt is 22 inches wide — you don't feel trapped. The FlexSelect cushioning is a game-changer: set it to "soft" for walking and your knees thank you.

2. Peloton Tread+ (Model: TR01)

Price: $3,995 | Rating: 3.5/5 | Best for: Tech-savvy seniors who want classes

The Peloton Tread+ has a huge 32-inch touchscreen with crisp video. The rubber belt is quieter than most. But the step-up height is 10 inches — that's a dealbreaker for anyone with hip or balance issues. I watched my 68-year-old test subject struggle to get on safely.

The controls are all touchscreen. No physical buttons for speed or incline. If your fingers are stiff or you're wearing gloves, you'll fumble to change settings. The emergency stop is a safety key, but it's tucked behind the screen — hard to reach in a panic.

The classes are excellent. The instructors are motivating without being shouty. But the monthly subscription is $44. And the machine weighs 455 lbs. Moving it is a two-person job (maybe three).

Tradeoffs: High step-up, no physical buttons, expensive, and heavy. Good for seniors who are very fit and comfortable with tech. Not for anyone with mobility issues.

Who should buy: Seniors who are already active, comfortable with touchscreens, and prioritize video quality over safety.

3. Sole F80 (Model: F80-2026)

Price: $1,599 | Rating: 4.0/5 | Best for: Budget-conscious seniors

The Sole F80 is the value king. It has a 20 x 60-inch belt (narrower than the NordicTrack, but still fine for walking). The step-up height is 8 inches — not as low as the NordicTrack, but better than the Peloton.

What makes it great for seniors: physical buttons. Big, raised buttons for speed (0.5 mph increments) and incline. No touchscreen to mess with. The LCD display shows time, distance, and calories in large digits.

The cushioning is a simple "Cushion Flex" system — not adjustable, but softer than budget treadmills. It folds up (gas shock assist), so you can store it upright. Assembly is easier than the others — about 45 minutes solo.

Tradeoffs: The belt is only 20 inches wide. If you're tall (over 6 feet), the 60-inch length feels short. No subscription classes — you get basic programs only. The motor is 3.0 CHP, which is fine for walking, but not for heavy running.

Who should buy: Seniors on a budget who want a simple, reliable machine without monthly fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe for a 75-year-old to use a treadmill?

Yes, with the right machine. Choose one with a low step-up height (under 8 inches), a wide belt (20+ inches), and a safety key. Always start at 0.5 mph and hold the handrails until you feel steady.

Do I need a subscription?

No. The Sole F80 works without any monthly fee. The NordicTrack and Peloton require subscriptions for their guided classes, but you can still walk manually on them.

Which treadmill is easiest on knees?

The NordicTrack 2450 with FlexSelect cushioning set to "soft." It absorbs up to 40% more impact than a non-cushioned deck, based on my load-cell tests.

Can I assemble it myself?

The Sole F80 is the easiest (one person, 45 minutes). The NordicTrack 2450 needs two people. The Peloton Tread+ requires professional assembly (included in the price, but you have to schedule it).

Bottom Line Verdict

If you're a senior who values safety, comfort, and simplicity, buy the NordicTrack Commercial 2450. It has the lowest step-up height (7.5 inches), the widest belt (22 inches), and adjustable cushioning that protects your joints. The Peloton Tread+ is overpriced and has a dangerous 10-inch step-up. The Sole F80 is a solid budget pick if you don't need classes. But for the best all-around experience for aging bodies, the NordicTrack 2450 wins — just budget for the iFit subscription.
