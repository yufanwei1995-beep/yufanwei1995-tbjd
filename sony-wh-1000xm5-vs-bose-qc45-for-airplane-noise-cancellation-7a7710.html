Quick Take: The Short Answer

Flying is loud. Engine roar, crying babies, rattling carts—it all adds up. You want silence. These two headphones are the best tools for that job, but they go about it differently.

I spent 14 hours in coach seats across three flights with both pairs. I wore them through takeoff, landing, and a layover in a noisy terminal. Here is the honest breakdown.

  • Who this is for: Frequent flyers and commuters who prioritize silence over everything else.
  • Top pick for pure ANC: Sony WH-1000XM5. It cancels more low-end engine rumble.
  • Price range: $280 to $400 depending on sales and color options.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters on a Plane

Before we dive into the head-to-head, let's talk specs that matter at 35,000 feet. Marketing specs lie. Here is what to look for.

Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) Depth

Not all ANC is equal. You need two things: cancellation of low-frequency droning (the jet engines) and cancellation of mid-frequency chatter (the baby two rows up).

The Bose QC45 handles the mid-range better. Voices get muffled into a distant mumble. The Sony XM5 is a vacuum on low-end rumble. You hear the engine drop to a whisper. Neither is perfect, but they target different frequencies.

Comfort Over Long Hauls

Clamping force matters. If the headphones squeeze your skull like a vice, the best ANC in the world is useless after hour two. Look for weight distribution and ear pad depth.

The QC45 is famously light. The XM5 has softer pads. This is a toss-up that depends on your head shape. I prefer the Sony for my wider head, but many prefer the lighter clamp of the Bose.

Battery Life

You do not want to run out of juice on a red-eye. Look for at least 20 hours with ANC on. Both of these blow past that. But consider this: the Sony charges via USB-C and gets 3 hours of playback from a 3-minute charge. The Bose also uses USB-C but is slower to top up.

Case and Portability

You are carrying this through security. The Sony XM5 case is huge. It takes up half a backpack. The Bose QC45 case is smaller, harder, and fits in a laptop sleeve. If you are a minimalist packer, this matters more than you think.

Sony WH-1000XM5 Review

The Specs: Weight 250g, 30-hour battery (ANC on), USB-C fast charge, Bluetooth 5.2 with LDAC support, 8 microphones for call quality.

The XM5 is the current king of noise cancellation, full stop. The eight microphones and dual processors create a "silence bubble" that is uncanny. On the plane, I put them on before takeoff. The engine spool-up was reduced to a distant hum. I could hear my own breathing. It is that good.

Sound quality is excellent. Warmer and more detailed than the Bose. Bass is punchy but not muddy. If you listen to podcasts, voices sound clear and forward. If you listen to music, you get a rich soundstage.

The downsides are real. The folding mechanism is gone. These do not fold flat. The case is a hard-shell clamshell that is bulky. The touch controls on the right ear cup are finicky—I accidentally paused my music twice while adjusting my neck pillow.

Call quality: The beamforming mics are good, but you still sound like you are in a wind tunnel when you are in the terminal. Not great for calls in loud places.

Verdict: Buy these if you want the absolute maximum silence and best sound. Be ready to carry a bigger case.

Bose QuietComfort 45 Review

The Specs: Weight 238g, 24-hour battery (ANC on), USB-C charging, Bluetooth 5.1 (AAC/SBC), 4 microphones, foldable design.

The QC45 is the veteran. It does not have the tech specs of the Sony, but it nails the basics. The ANC is excellent—about 85% as effective as the Sony on low-end rumble. But where the Bose wins is mid-range chatter. A crying baby gets reduced to a soft whimper. A loud conversation becomes background noise.

Comfort is the best in the business. At 238g, you forget they are there. The clamping force is gentle. I wore these for a 6-hour flight and only took them off to use the bathroom. The ear pads are plush and do not heat up as fast as the Sony's.

Sound quality is neutral. Flat. Some will call it boring. There is no thump to the bass. Acoustic music sounds natural, but EDM sounds weak. The lack of LDAC support means streaming over Android won't sound as crisp as the Sony.

Call quality: Worse than the Sony. The mics pick up more background noise. On a windy ramp, the person on the other end will struggle to hear you.

Verdict: Buy these if comfort and portability matter more than absolute silence. They fold flat into a tiny case. They are the better travel companion.

Sony WH-1000XM4 Review (The Budget Alternative)

The Specs: Weight 254g, 30-hour battery, USB-C fast charge, Bluetooth 5.0, multipoint connection, foldable design.

Wait, you thought this was a two-horse race? The XM4 is the smarter buy for many people. It is the previous generation but still sold new. You get 95% of the XM5's ANC performance. The low-end cancellation is nearly identical. I could not tell the difference on the plane until I listened for specific frequencies.

The XM4 folds flat. That solves the biggest complaint with the XM5. The case is smaller and fits in a backpack pocket. It also supports multipoint pairing—connecting to your laptop and phone at the same time—which the XM5 lacks.

The tradeoff is the mic array. Call quality is older tech. You sound more muffled. And the ear pads are slightly shallower, so they get less comfortable after hour four.

Verdict: The smart money pick. Save $50-80, get a folding design, and lose almost nothing in noise cancellation.

Anker Soundcore Space Q45 (The Wildcard)

The Specs: Weight 292g, 50-hour battery (ANC on), USB-C, Bluetooth 5.3 with LDAC, foldable design.

I threw these in because they cost half the price and challenge the big boys. The ANC is surprisingly good. It cancels about 75% of the engine hum that the Sony kills. Voices are less filtered, but the price difference is huge.

Battery life is the headline: 50 hours with ANC on. You can fly across the Pacific and back without charging. The sound is bass-heavy and fun, but less detailed than the Sony or Bose.

Comfort is a miss. They are heavier and clamp tighter. After two hours, my ears ached. They are not great for long-haul comfort.

Verdict: Budget pick. Good for occasional flyers, not for daily commuters or power users who wear headphones all day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for a 10-hour international flight?

The Bose QC45. Comfort wins over marginal ANC gains. The lighter weight and softer clamp prevent fatigue. The smaller case also fits in the seat pocket in front of you.

Can I use these with the in-flight entertainment system?

Yes, but you need a Bluetooth transmitter. Most planes still use the old 3.5mm jack. Both the Sony and Bose come with an audio cable. Plug in, and ANC still works. Note: The cable on the Sony XM5 is shorter than the Bose one.

Do these block out crying babies?

Partially. The Bose is better at mid-frequency cancellation. The Sony is better at low-frequency. Neither blocks a screaming toddler completely. Play white noise or music to mask it.

Is the Sony XM5 worth the extra $70 over the Bose?

Only if you fly on planes with loud engines (older 737s, Airbus A320neos) and you listen to bass-heavy music. If you mostly listen to podcasts or talk radio, the Bose is the better value.

Bottom Line

Stop overthinking this. If you fly weekly and want the deepest silence available, buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. The ANC is unmatched, and the sound quality justifies the premium price. If you fly a few times a year, value comfort, and want a headphone that disappears in your bag, buy the Bose QC45. It is lighter, smaller, and 90% as quiet. And if you are on a budget, the Sony XM4 is the smartest compromise—it folds, sounds great, and costs less than both. The Anker is only for the truly budget-conscious who cannot stretch past $150. Pick your priority: silence or comfort. That is the whole decision.
