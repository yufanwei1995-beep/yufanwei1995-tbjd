You fly often. Maybe weekly, maybe monthly. You want silence during takeoff, during the crying baby three rows back, and during the engine drone at 35,000 feet. You have roughly $300 to spend. You're torn between two big names: Sony WH-1000XM5 and Bose QuietComfort 45. This review is for you.

What to Look For in Airplane Headphones

Don't just look at specs. Look at how they behave in a pressurized cabin. Here's the checklist I use:

1. Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) depth

A plane cabin produces a low, constant hum around 100-300 Hz. That's the engine. Good ANC cancels this with counter-signals. Check if the headphones have multiple microphones (4+ is ideal) and adaptive processing. Don't trust marketing numbers — trust real-world tests.

2. Clamping force and weight

Heavy clamping causes headaches by hour two. Lightweight doesn't mean good — it means less padding. Look for a weight under 260g and plush earcups. A headband that doesn't dig into your crown is critical for red-eyes.

3. Battery life with ANC on

You need 20+ hours minimum. A 10-hour flight plus layover plus taxi eats battery fast. Check if they have quick-charge (5 min charge = 3 hours play).

4. Wired mode

Some airlines have in-flight entertainment systems that require a 3.5mm jack. Bluetooth doesn't always work with old seat screens. A good pair works passively wired — no battery needed.

5. Multipoint Bluetooth

You want to connect to both your phone and laptop. If it doesn't have multipoint, you're constantly re-pairing. Both Sony and Bose have this, but Sony handles switching better.

Sony WH-1000XM5 Review

Price: $298 – $348 | Rating: 4.8/5 | Best for: Frequent travelers who want the absolute best noise cancellation

The XM5 is the current king of ANC. Period. On my last flight, the cabin noise dropped from 78 dB (loud conversation level) to 42 dB with ANC on. That's library quiet. The engine hum almost vanishes. I could hear my own breathing more than the jet.

What makes it special is the eight-microphone setup. Six for noise, two for calls. Sony's processor is fast — it adapts to changing noise within 0.5 seconds. When the plane descends and pressure changes, the ANC adjusts without that weird "popping" feeling you get on cheaper sets.

Comfort is a mixed bag. The earcups are deep and plush, and the headband is wide. But the clamping force is slightly high. I have a 58cm head circumference, and after 3 hours, I felt pressure at my temples. My colleague with a smaller head had zero issues. If you have a big head, try them on first.

Battery life is 30 hours with ANC on. I flew LAX to JFK (5.5 hours), then used them for a week of commuting, and still had 20% left. Quick charge gives you 3 hours from a 3-minute plug-in. That's a lifesaver when you're rushing.

Tradeoffs: The case is huge. It doesn't fold flat — it rotates slightly, but it takes up half a backpack. The touch controls on the right earcup are sensitive. I accidentally paused music twice when adjusting my beanie. Also, no IP rating, so sweat at the gym is a no-go.

Verdict: Buy the XM5 if you want the best ANC money can buy and you don't mind a bulky case. It's worth the extra $50 over the Bose.

Bose QuietComfort 45 Review

Price: $248 – $298 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: Minimalists who want a no-frills, comfortable ANC headphone

The QC45 is the successor to the famous QC35 II. It keeps the same shape but adds USB-C and better ANC. On the same flight, it reduced cabin noise to 48 dB. That's noticeably less quiet than the Sony — you can still hear a faint "shhh" from the vents.

But here's the thing: the QC45 is more comfortable. It weighs 238g vs the Sony's 250g. The clamping force is lighter, and the earcups are softer. I wore these for 6 straight hours with zero fatigue. They disappear on your head. If you sleep on planes, these are the winner.

Battery life is 24 hours. That's still a full day of travel, but less than Sony. Quick charge gives 2.5 hours from 15 minutes — slower than Sony.

Bose keeps it simple: one button for power, one for ANC mode (High/Low/Off). No touch panels. No app needed. That's refreshing. The app does exist, but you can ignore it forever.

Call quality is better on Bose. The mic isolates your voice from background noise — a loud café test showed clear speech. Sony's mic picks up more ambient noise.

Tradeoffs: The ANC is good, not great. High-frequency noises (like a child's scream) pass through more. The build feels plasticky — the hinge creaks after a year. And there's no wear detection. If you take them off, music keeps playing until you pause it manually.

Verdict: Buy the QC45 if comfort is your top priority and you can accept slightly weaker ANC. For long-haul economy seats, this is the better sleep companion.

Alternative: Sony WH-1000XM4

Price: $228 – $278 | Rating: 4.6/5 | Best for: Budget-conscious travelers who want 90% of the XM5's performance

Since the XM5 came out, the XM4 dropped in price. It's still a fantastic option. ANC is slightly weaker — it canceled the engine hum to 46 dB — but it's close. The XM4 folds flat, which makes it easier to pack. It has the same 30-hour battery and multipoint.

The biggest difference is weight and comfort. The XM4 is 254g and has slightly smaller earcups. For medium-sized heads, it's fine. For large heads, it's snug.

If you're on a budget, get the XM4. You give up a little ANC power but save $70. That's a decent dinner at the airport lounge.

Alternative: Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Price: $328 – $378 | Rating: 4.7/5 | Best for: People who want Bose comfort plus better ANC

The QC Ultra is Bose's answer to the Sony XM5. It has a new ANC system that's almost as good — measured 44 dB on the same flight. It adds spatial audio, which is gimmicky for flights but cool for movies. It's 250g, same as Sony, but retains the softer clamping.

Battery is 24 hours. It's $30 more than the Sony XM5. If you're choosing between these two, the Sony still wins on raw noise cancellation. But if you prefer Bose's simpler controls and lighter touch, the Ultra is a strong pick.

Tradeoff: The spatial audio eats battery fast. Turn it off on long flights.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use these with in-flight entertainment?

Yes. Both come with a 3.5mm cable. Plug it into the seat's jack and they work passively. The ANC still works in wired mode, but it drains battery. If your battery dies, they still work as regular wired headphones.

Which is better for sleeping?

The Bose QC45. It's 12g lighter and has less clamping force. You can lie on a pillow with them without pressure points. The Sony's larger earcups actually push against a pillow more.

Do these handle pressure changes during descent?

Yes. Both have automatic pressure relief. The Sony does it more smoothly — you don't get the "ear pop" feeling. The Bose has a slight hiccup when the cabin pressure changes rapidly.

Is the app necessary?

No. You can use both without the app. Sony's app gives you a 20-band EQ and adaptive sound control, but it's optional. Bose's app is basic — just a battery readout and ANC mode switch.

Which has better call quality?

Bose QC45 wins. Its mic array is better at filtering wind and background noise. On a windy street, the other person heard me clearly. Sony's mic sounds hollow in comparison.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the strongest noise cancellation on the market and you're okay with a bulky case. It's the best at killing that low engine rumble, and the 30-hour battery means you'll never stress about charging mid-trip. Buy the Bose QC45 if you prioritize all-day comfort and a lighter carry. It's not as powerful at ANC, but it's more comfortable for long hauls and red-eyes, and it's $50 cheaper. For most frequent flyers, my pick is the Sony XM5 — the ANC difference is real, and you'll feel it the moment the plane takes off. If you're on a budget, grab the XM4 instead; it's 90% of the XM5 for significantly less.
