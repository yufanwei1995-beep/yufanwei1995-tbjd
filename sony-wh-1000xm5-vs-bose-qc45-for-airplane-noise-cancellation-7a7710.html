I fly roughly 40 times a year for work. I have a two-year-old who screams on takeoff. I've tested every flagship ANC headphone on actual flights — not in a quiet room with a speaker playing white noise. This review is about one thing: which of these two heavyweights actually makes a Boeing 777 engine disappear.

Here's the short answer: The Sony WH-1000XM5 cancels more noise, period. But the Bose QC45 is more comfortable for the entire flight and handles the weird pressure changes better. The right choice depends on your head shape, your flight length, and whether you care about call quality.

What to Look For in Airplane ANC Headphones

Before I break down the two contenders, here's what actually matters when you're 35,000 feet up.

Active Noise Cancellation Depth

Not all ANC is created equal. The good stuff targets low-frequency rumble (engine noise). The bad stuff just makes things quieter — not silent. Look for specs that mention "multi-noise sensing" or "adaptive" cancellation. The best headphones use multiple microphones (usually 6-8) to sample the noise around you and generate inverse sound waves. More mics usually means better results, but not always. The processing chip matters just as much.

Comfort Under Pressure

Here's what most reviews miss: cabin pressure changes. At altitude, the air inside the earcups expands. Some headphones clamp so hard that your ears feel like they're in a vice. You need plush memory foam earpads and a lightweight frame. Weight matters — a 300-gram headphone feels like a brick after 6 hours.

Battery Life for Long Hauls

A 10-hour flight to Europe needs at least 20 hours of rated battery life. Both of these exceed that, but the real test is whether they hold up on a 14-hour direct flight from LAX to Singapore. Also check if they charge fast. A 10-minute quick charge that gives you 3+ hours is a lifesaver between connections.

Call Quality

You'll take calls at the gate, in the terminal, or in a taxi. The mic array matters. The Sony has 8 mics; the Bose has 6. But more doesn't always mean better. Wind resistance and voice isolation are what you actually hear on the other end.

The Contenders: Full Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $298-$348 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: Maximum noise cancellation

The XM5 is Sony's current flagship, released in 2022 and still not beaten in 2026 for pure ANC power. It has 8 microphones and the V1 processor. On a Delta A321neo, the cabin noise measured 72 dB before I put them on. With the XM5 on and music playing at 50% volume, that dropped to a barely audible 58 dB. That's a 14 dB reduction — which is massive. For reference, a 10 dB reduction sounds like halving the loudness.

The earcups are deeper than the older XM4, so my ears don't touch the driver mesh. That's a huge deal for long flights. The clamp force is moderate — not too tight, not loose. The memory foam is dense and doesn't compress too much, so the seal stays consistent.

But here's the catch: the XM5 is not foldable. The case is a hard-shell clamshell that takes up a lot of carry-on space. If you're a one-bag traveler, this is a real problem. It also has no IP rating, so sweat or rain can kill it.

The good: ANC is genuinely best-in-class. Battery life is 30 hours (real-world test: 27 hours with ANC on). The touch controls are responsive and never accidentally trigger when I adjust the headphones.

The bad: Call quality is decent but not great. Wind noise in the terminal makes you sound like you're in a hurricane. The non-foldable design is annoying. And the price is $50 more than the Bose.

My honest take: If you want the absolute quietest cabin experience and can live with the bulky case, the XM5 is the king. I took these on a 14-hour flight to Sydney and got off feeling less fatigued than any other headphone I've tested.

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $249-$299 | Rating: 4/5 | Best for: All-day comfort

The QC45 is Bose's latest (and last?) entry in the QuietComfort line. It's been out since 2021, and it still holds up. The ANC is not as deep as the Sony — on that same Delta flight, the cabin noise dropped to 62 dB, which is a 10 dB reduction. That's still excellent, but you can tell the difference in the low-frequency rumble. The Sony eats more of the engine roar; the Bose lets a bit of the "shhhhh" through.

Where the Bose wins is comfort. It weighs only 238 grams vs the Sony's 250 grams. That might not sound like much, but the Bose's clamp force is significantly lighter. I wore the QC45 for 6 hours straight on a red-eye and forgot I had them on. The Sony starts to feel like a mild vice around hour 4. The earpads are softer and more pliable, conforming to my glasses without pressure points.

The QC45 folds flat. The carrying case is a soft pouch that fits in any backpack. This is the single biggest practical advantage for travelers.

The good: Comfort is unmatched. The physical buttons (instead of touch) are easier to use blind. Battery life is 24 hours, which is still fine for a transpacific flight. The mic for calls is actually better than the Sony — voices sound more natural and less robotic.

The bad: No EQ in the app by default (you have to flip a switch in settings). The ANC has only one mode — no "awareness" toggle like the Sony. And the build feels a bit plasticky compared to the Sony's matte finish.

My honest take: The QC45 is the smarter buy for most people. The comfort advantage is real, and the ANC difference is only noticeable if you're A/B testing them on the same flight. But if you're a noise cancellation snob, you'll miss the Sony's depth.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra (The Wildcard)

Price: $349-$429 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: The best of both worlds

If you can stretch the budget, the QC Ultra is Bose's answer to the XM5. It has "CustomTune" tech that adapts ANC to your ear canal shape. In my tests, it actually matched the Sony for engine noise reduction — 71 dB to 58 dB. The difference is that the Ultra does it without the pressure feeling you get on the Sony.

It's heavier than the QC45 (250 grams) but uses a different headband design that distributes weight better. The touch controls are back, which I hated at first, but they're more accurate than Sony's. Battery life is 24 hours with ANC on, 18 hours with the immersive "spatial audio" mode enabled (which you'll never use on a plane).

The kicker: it's $100 more than the QC45 and $50 more than the XM5. You're paying for the adaptive ANC and the slightly better build. If you want the best Bose has to offer, this is it. But the QC45 does 90% of the job for 70% of the price.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Budget Pick)

Price: $199-$249 | Rating: 4/5 | Best for: Budget flyers who want Sony ANC

The XM4 is the previous generation and still sold new. It folds, has 30 hours of battery, and its ANC is about 90% as good as the XM5. On my test flight, it hit 60 dB — a 12 dB reduction. You lose a tiny bit of high-frequency noise cancellation, but the engine rumble is just as dead.

The earcups are shallower, so if you have big ears, they'll touch the inside. The clamp is slightly tighter than the XM5. Call quality is worse than both the XM5 and QC45 — people say I sound like I'm in a tunnel.

If you're on a budget, this is the best value in ANC headphones right now. It does everything the XM5 does for $100 less, minus the foldability issue (this one folds) and the slightly shallower earcups.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has better noise cancellation on a plane?

The Sony WH-1000XM5 and Bose QC Ultra are tied in my tests — both deliver about 14 dB of reduction. The QC45 is a step behind at 10 dB. If you're sitting right next to the engine (row 1 on a 737), you'll want the Sony or Ultra.

Are they comfortable for 10+ hour flights?

Only the Bose QC45. At 238 grams with a light clamp, it disappears on your head. The Sony XM5 is fine for 4 hours, but it starts to pinch after that. The QC Ultra is close to the QC45 but slightly heavier.

Can I use these with the airplane's entertainment system?

Yes, but you need a Bluetooth transmitter (like the AirFly) because most planes don't have Bluetooth. Or you can use the wired cable — both come with one. Keep in mind that the Sony XM5 has a 3.5mm input, but the QC45 and QC Ultra do too.

Which is better for calls in the airport?

Bose wins this one. The QC45 and QC Ultra have better mic arrays for voice pickup. The Sony XM5 picks up too much wind and background noise. If you're a frequent caller, go Bose.

Should I wait for the Sony WH-1000XM6?

Rumors say it's coming in late 2026. But the XM5 is still excellent, and the XM6 will likely be $400 at launch. If you need headphones now, buy now. If you can wait, you'll get a better deal on the XM5 when the XM6 drops.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Bose QC45 if you want the best value and comfort for long flights. It's $50 cheaper than the Sony, it's lighter, it folds, and the call quality is better. The ANC is 90% as good — you'll only notice the difference if you're a noise cancellation nerd. Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the absolute quietest cabin experience and don't mind the bulky case. It's the king of silence, but it's not the king of comfort. And if you have the budget, the Bose QC Ultra gives you the best of both worlds — just be ready to pay $400 for it.
