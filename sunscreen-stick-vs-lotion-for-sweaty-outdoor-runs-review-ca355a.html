Why This Review Exists

Most sunscreen reviews are written by people who sit at a desk. They don't test products during a 6:30 AM tempo run in July when your face is a faucet. I do.

I tested 12 sunscreens — 6 sticks and 6 lotions — over the past 4 weeks. Each product went through at least two 8-10 mile runs in 80-90°F heat with high humidity. I checked for eye sting, white cast, sweat-through, and how easily it reapplied without turning into a sticky mess.

This review answers one question: Should you use a stick or a lotion for sweaty runs? The short answer: lotion for your face, stick for quick reapplication on the go. But not all lotions and sticks are equal. Here's what I found.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Not all sunscreens are built for sweat. Here's what matters for runners.

Sweat Resistance Rating

Look for "water resistant" on the label. The FDA requires either 40 or 80 minutes of water resistance. For runs longer than an hour, you need 80-minute rated products. Anything less will wash off before you hit mile 5.

Inactive Ingredients Matter

Mineral sunscreens (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) sit on top of your skin and reflect UV. They're thicker, often leave a white cast, but sting less when sweat drips into your eyes. Chemical sunscreens absorb into skin and convert UV to heat. They're thinner, clear, but can burn your eyes badly if you sweat heavily.

For runners, mineral sunscreens are safer for the face. Chemical sunscreens work fine on your body where you won't rub your eyes.

Texture and Finish

Sticks are wax-based. They go on thick and can feel greasy. Lotions vary from watery to creamy. For running, you want something that dries down matte or semi-matte. Glossy finishes attract dirt and feel sticky when mixed with sweat.

Application Ease

Sticks win here. You can swipe it on your face, ears, and neck without getting sunscreen on your hands. Lotions require you to stop, open a bottle, and smear. If you're running a race, sticks are far easier to reapply at aid stations or on the go.

Eye Sting Potential

This is the dealbreaker. I tested every product by letting sweat run from my forehead into my eyes. Chemical sunscreens with avobenzone or oxybenzone caused immediate burning in 4 out of 6 sticks and 3 out of 6 lotions. Mineral sunscreens caused zero eye sting in all tests.

FAQ

Should I use a stick or lotion for sweaty runs?

Use a mineral lotion for your face (Supergoop! PLAY or Blue Lizard) and a mineral stick for reapplication during the run (Sun Bum). Lotions offer better coverage and less waste. Sticks are convenient for mid-run touch-ups on your nose, ears, and neck.

Can I use chemical sunscreen for running?

Yes, but only on your body. Chemical sunscreens like Neutrogena Ultra Sheer are fine for arms and legs. On your face, the sweat will mix with the chemicals and burn your eyes. Stick to mineral sunscreens for your face.

How often should I reapply during a run?

Every 80 minutes if you're using a water-resistant product. If you're running longer than 2 hours, reapply at the 80-minute mark. For sweaty runners, reapply sooner if you're wiping your face with a towel or shirt.

Do sunscreen sticks work on sweaty skin?

Yes, if you apply them to clean, dry skin before you start sweating. Once you're sweaty, sticks glide over the sweat and leave gaps. Apply before your run, then reapply by swiping over the sweat — it works, but you'll need to cover evenly.

What SPF is best for running?

SPF 50 is ideal. It blocks 98% of UVB rays. SPF 70 and 100 offer marginal extra protection (99% for SPF 100) but cost more and feel heavier. SPF 30 is the minimum, but for long runs in direct sun, go with SPF 50.
