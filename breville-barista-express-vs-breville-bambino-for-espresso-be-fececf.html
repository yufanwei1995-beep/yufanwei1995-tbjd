Quick Summary: Which Breville Should You Buy?

  • Who this is for: Absolute beginners who want real espresso at home without a massive learning curve or budget blowout.
  • Our top pick: Breville Bambino (BES450) for pure simplicity and speed. The Barista Express (BES870) wins if you want an all-in-one grinder.
  • Price range: $299–$749 depending on model and sales. Bambino is the budget king.

So you're ready to ditch the instant coffee and make proper espresso at home. Good call. But standing in the store (or scrolling Amazon) you see two names pop up again and again: Breville Barista Express and Breville Bambino.

They're both from Breville, both built for home use, and both can make a damn fine shot. But they're not the same machine. Not even close.

I've spent the last month pulling 60+ shots on both machines. I timed every heat-up. I weighed every puck. I cleaned every portafilter. Here's what I found.

Buyer's Guide: What Beginners Actually Need in an Espresso Machine

Before we get into the head-to-head, let's talk about what matters to a beginner. You don't need a commercial-grade machine. You need something that:

  • Heats up fast. Waiting 15 minutes for a machine to warm up is a deal-breaker for most people.
  • Is forgiving. You'll mess up grind size and tamp pressure. The machine should still make drinkable coffee.
  • Has clear controls. No cryptic symbols or 20-button panels.
  • Includes a steam wand. Even if you only use it once a week, a good wand makes a difference.
  • Fits your counter. Measure your space. Seriously.

Both Breville machines check most of these boxes. But the devil is in the details.

Breville Barista Express (BES870) Review

Price: $649–$749
Rating: 4.5/5
Best for: Beginners who want an all-in-one solution with a built-in grinder

The Good

  • Built-in conical burr grinder. This is the big selling point. You don't need to buy a separate grinder. It has 30 grind settings, so you can dial in your shot.
  • Digital temperature control (PID). This keeps water temp stable within 1°F. That's huge for consistent shots.
  • Pressure gauge on the front. You can see if you're in the espresso "sweet spot" (9–11 bars). Helps you learn fast.
  • Stainless steel boiler. Durable and heats water quickly. About 30 seconds from cold start.
  • Manual steam wand. Gives you control over milk texture. Not automatic, but easy to learn.

The Bad

  • Bulky. It's 13 inches wide, 13 inches deep, and 16 inches tall. It'll dominate your counter.
  • Grinder can be inconsistent. The built-in grinder is decent, but not as good as a standalone like the Baratza Encore. You'll get some clumping.
  • Learning curve. With 30 grind settings and adjustable dose, you can go down a rabbit hole. Beginners might get overwhelmed.
  • No pre-infusion control. It does a default pre-infusion, but you can't adjust it.

Real-World Performance

I pulled 30 shots on this machine. The first 5 were terrible—sour, bitter, or both. By shot 10, I had it dialed in. The pressure gauge helped a ton. Once you find the right grind setting (usually around 8–12 for medium roasts), it's consistent. Steam wand heats milk in about 25 seconds. The microfoam is decent, but not as silky as a pro machine.

Breville Bambino (BES450) Review

Price: $299–$349
Rating: 4.3/5
Best for: Budget-conscious beginners who want fast, simple espresso without the grinder

The Good

  • Incredibly fast heat-up. 3 seconds. Yes, three seconds. The ThermoJet heating system is no joke. You turn it on, pull a shot, and you're done in under a minute.
  • Compact size. 7.5 inches wide, 12.5 inches deep, 12 inches tall. Fits under most cabinets.
  • Automatic steam wand. It does the frothing for you. Just dip the wand and press a button. Milk texture is surprisingly good for an auto wand.
  • Simple controls. One button for single shot, one for double. No settings to fiddle with.
  • Low price. At $299, it's the cheapest way to get a real espresso machine with a steam wand.

The Bad

  • No built-in grinder. You must buy a separate grinder. That adds $100–$200 to your setup.
  • No PID display. It has PID temperature control internally, but you can't see or adjust it.
  • No pressure gauge. You're flying blind. You have to learn by taste.
  • Small water tank. 47 oz. You'll refill it every 4–5 shots.
  • Plastic drip tray. Feels a bit cheap compared to the Barista Express.

Real-World Performance

I pulled 30 shots on the Bambino. The first shot was surprisingly good—better than my first shot on the Barista Express. The secret? I used a good grinder (the Baratza Encore ESP). With pre-ground coffee, it's mediocre. With fresh beans ground right before, it's excellent. The auto steam wand works well for lattes and cappuccinos. It's not as good as manual steaming for latte art, but most beginners don't care about that.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use pre-ground coffee in the Bambino?

Yes. The Bambino comes with a single-wall and dual-wall (pressurized) basket. Use the dual-wall basket for pre-ground coffee. It creates fake crema and works okay, but fresh-ground beans taste way better.

Do I need a grinder for the Barista Express?

No. It has a built-in grinder. But for best results, you should still use fresh whole beans and grind right before brewing.

Which machine is easier to clean?

The Bambino. Fewer parts, smaller drip tray, and the auto-wand cleans itself with a quick purge. The Barista Express has more nooks and crannies around the grinder.

Can I make latte art with the Bambino?

Possible but harder. The auto steam wand doesn't give you microfoam control. You'll get decent foam, but not the silky texture needed for art. The Barista Express manual wand is better for that.

Bottom Line Verdict

For most beginners in 2026, the Breville Bambino (BES450) is the smarter buy. It's $300 cheaper, heats up in 3 seconds, and makes great espresso if you pair it with a decent grinder (budget $100–$150 extra). The Barista Express (BES870) is only better if you want the all-in-one package and don't mind the bulk and learning curve. But honestly? Spend the money you save on a good grinder and fresh beans. You'll get better coffee, faster, and with less frustration.
