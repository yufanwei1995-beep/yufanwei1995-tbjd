What to Look For in an Anti-Frizz Serum

Not all serums are created equal. Here’s what actually matters when you’re shopping for humid weather.

Silicones vs. Oils

Silicones (like dimethicone) coat the hair shaft and create a barrier against moisture in the air. They work instantly and give that sleek look. Oils (like argan or jojoba) penetrate the hair but don't provide the same level of humidity protection. For serious humidity, you want a serum with silicones near the top of the ingredient list. Just know that you'll need a clarifying shampoo once a month to prevent buildup.

Weight and Texture

Curly hair is fragile. A serum that's too heavy will weigh down your curls and make them look stringy. Look for lightweight, water-based formulas. If it feels greasy on your fingers, it will feel greasy on your curls.

Hold Factor

Some serums offer a light flexible hold, while others are more like a gel-serum hybrid. In 80%+ humidity, you need some level of hold to keep the curl clump intact. A serum with zero hold won't do much when the dew point is high.

Fragrance

This sounds minor, but a strong perfume can clash with your other products. Also, sensitive scalps can react badly. Stick to serums with mild, natural scents unless you love the smell.

How to Apply Serums for Best Results

Application is 50% of the battle. Here’s the method that worked best in our tests:

  1. Apply to soaking wet hair, right after you turn off the shower.
  2. Use a small amount. Start with a dime-sized amount. You can always add more.
  3. Rub the serum between your palms to warm it up, then "praying hands" it over your hair.
  4. Scrunch your curls upward to encourage curl clumping.
  5. Do not touch your hair while it air-dries or diffuses. Touching it creates frizz.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a serum and a gel together?

Yes. Use a light serum first to seal the cuticle, then a gel on top to lock the curl. This is the "LOC" method (Liquid, Oil, Cream). The serum acts as the oil layer.

How often should I clarify my hair if I use silicone serums?

Once every two weeks. Silicones build up and block moisture from getting in. If your curls feel dry even when wet, you need to clarify.

Is humectant or anti-humectant better?

For curly hair, look for "anti-humectant" formulas. Humectants like glycerin attract water from the air, which makes frizz worse in humid conditions. Anti-humectants repel water.
