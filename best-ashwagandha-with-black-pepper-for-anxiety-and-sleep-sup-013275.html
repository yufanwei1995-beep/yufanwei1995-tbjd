What to Look For: The Buyer's Guide

Before you buy anything, understand these four things. They make or break the supplement.

1. The Extract Type Matters More Than You Think

There are two main types of ashwagandha extract: KSM-66 and Sensoril. KSM-66 is a root-only extract with high withanolide content (the active compound). It's better for daytime energy and stress reduction. Sensoril is a leaf-and-root extract that tends to be more sedating — better for sleep. Look for either, but know what you're getting. If a product just says "ashwagandha root powder" without a standardized extract, it's probably weak.

2. Black Pepper: KSM-66 Doesn't Need It, But Most Others Do

Here's the tricky part. KSM-66 is already highly bioavailable on its own. But many brands add BioPerine (a patented black pepper extract) to regular ashwagandha to boost absorption. If you're buying a non-KSM-66 product, you need the black pepper. If you're buying KSM-66, the pepper is a bonus but not critical.

3. Withanolide Percentage

Check the label. You want at least 5% withanolides. Some premium extracts hit 10%. Anything less is a waste of money. If the label doesn't list the percentage, move on.

4. Third-Party Testing

Supplements are not regulated by the FDA. Look for brands that use third-party testing (like NSF or USP) or at least publish Certificate of Analysis (CoA) on their website. This proves what's actually in the bottle matches the label.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long until I feel the effects?

Most people feel a difference in 7-14 days. Some feel it sooner, some need 4 weeks. If you feel nothing after 30 days, the dose is too low or the extract is weak.

Can I take ashwagandha with my other medications?

Maybe, but talk to your doctor first. Ashwagandha can interact with thyroid medication, blood pressure drugs, and sedatives. It can also lower blood sugar, so diabetics should be cautious.

Is it safe to take every day?

Yes, for up to 3 months. After that, take a 2-week break to reset your tolerance. Long-term daily use without breaks can reduce effectiveness.

What's the best time to take it?

For anxiety during the day, take it in the morning with breakfast. For sleep, take it 30-60 minutes before bed. If you're using a KSM-66 product, morning is better — it's energizing. If you're using Sensoril, night is better — it's sedating.

Does black pepper really make a difference?

Yes, for regular ashwagandha extracts. Piperine (the active compound in black pepper) inhibits certain enzymes in your gut that break down withanolides before they absorb. Without it, you lose up to 50% of the active compounds. KSM-66 is the exception — it's already optimized for absorption.
