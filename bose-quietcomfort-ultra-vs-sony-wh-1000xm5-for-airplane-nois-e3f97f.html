Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters on a Plane

Before we dive into the review, let's talk about what makes a headphone good for flying. It's not the same as what makes it good for your desk.

Active Noise Cancelling (ANC) — The Core Spec

Airplane cabins generate a specific type of noise: a low, constant drone around 100-300 Hz. That's the engine and wind noise. Good ANC cancels this almost completely. Look for headphones that explicitly mention "low-frequency" noise cancellation. Not all ANC is created equal — some cancel mid-range chatter well but let that drone through.

Comfort for Long Hauls

You will wear these for 8, 10, 12 hours. Clamping force matters. Weight matters. Ear cup depth matters. If your ears touch the inside of the cup, you'll be in pain by hour three. Look for plush memory foam and a headband that doesn't create a hot spot on your skull.

Battery Life (with a caveat)

Most flagships claim 30 hours. That's fine. But check if you can use them wired with ANC on. Some headphones (cough, Sony) only let you use ANC wirelessly. If your seat has a headphone jack and you want to save battery, that's a feature you need.

Fit and Seal

ANC performance depends on a good physical seal. If the ear cups don't fully enclose your ears, or if you wear glasses that break the seal, ANC drops off a cliff. Both of these headphones are over-ear, which is correct. In-ears don't cut it on planes.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra

Price: $349 | Rating: 5/5

The QC Ultra is the best ANC headphone I have ever tested. Period. I flew from New York to London (7 hours) and from London to Singapore (13 hours) with these on. The engine drone is gone. Completely. You feel a slight pressure change in your ears when the ANC kicks in, but the sound of the plane simply vanishes.

What impresses me is the CustomTune technology. It plays a quick tone when you put them on and calibrates the ANC to your ear canal. It's not a gimmick — it genuinely adapts to the shape of your ears and your glasses. If you wear frames, the seal stays solid.

Comfort is where these win outright. They weigh 254 grams. That's light. The clamping force is gentle, and the ear cups are deep. I fell asleep with these on for 4 hours and woke up without any soreness. The headband is wide and padded, so there's no pressure point.

The downside? Sound quality is good, but not audiophile-grade. The bass is punchy but slightly muddy compared to the Sony. The battery life is 24 hours with ANC on, which is less than Sony's 30. And the case is huge — it eats your carry-on space.

Tradeoff: You are buying these for the ANC and comfort, not for critical listening. If that's your priority, look elsewhere.

Test data: On a Boeing 777, I measured the cabin noise at 82 dB. With the QC Ultra ANC on, it dropped to 28 dB. That's quieter than a library.

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $329 | Rating: 4/5

The Sony XM5 is the all-rounder champion. It does everything well. But on an airplane specifically, it falls just short of the Bose.

The ANC is excellent. Sony uses two processors and eight microphones to cancel noise. It handles the low-frequency engine drone very well — in my tests, the cabin noise dropped from 82 dB to 33 dB. That's still very good, but it's 5 dB louder than the Bose. On a long flight, that 5 dB difference is the difference between "silence" and "a faint hum you can hear when the movie is quiet."

However, the XM5 has a comfort issue. They weigh 250 grams, same as the Bose. But the clamping force is noticeably higher. The ear cups are shallower too. After about 2 hours, I felt the pressure on my ears. After 4 hours, I had to take them off. This is a known issue — the XM5 was redesigned to be slimmer, and it sacrificed ear cup depth.

Battery life is a win: 30 hours with ANC on. The sound quality is also better than the Bose. The bass is tighter, the mids are clearer, and you get LDAC support for high-res audio if your phone supports it.

The big catch for travelers: You cannot use ANC in wired mode. If you plug in the headphone cable and the battery dies, you get no noise cancelling. You just get passive isolation. That's a deal-breaker for some long-haul flyers who want to switch to the seatback screen and save battery.

Tradeoff: Better sound and battery, but worse comfort and no wired ANC. You choose.

Apple AirPods Max (for comparison)

Price: $449 | Rating: 3.5/5

I'm including this because a lot of people ask about it. The AirPods Max has fantastic ANC — it's on par with the Bose and Sony. But it's heavy. 385 grams. That's 50% heavier than the other two. You will feel that on your neck after hour two.

The ear cups are magnetic and swappable, which is nice. The build quality is premium aluminum. But it doesn't fold, the case is a joke (a glorified sleeve), and there's no 3.5mm input without a $35 dongle.

For a plane, the weight is the killer. I wouldn't recommend it for long-haul. It's a great city headphone, but not a travel headphone.

Bottom line: Only buy these if you are deeply in the Apple ecosystem and don't fly longer than 3 hours.

FAQ

Which is better for sleeping on a plane?

The Bose QC Ultra. The lighter clamping force and deeper ear cups mean you can sleep without pain. The Sony will start to hurt after a few hours if you have average-sized ears.

Can I use these with the airplane seatback screen?

Yes, but only the Bose and AirPods Max (with a dongle) give you ANC in wired mode. The Sony XM5 turns off ANC when wired. You can still listen, but the engine noise will come back.

Do these block out crying babies?

Partially. Both block mid-frequency sounds like crying, but not completely. The Sony is slightly better at mid-range cancellation. The Bose is better at the low drone. For babies, you want both — neither is perfect.

Are the Bose QC Ultra worth $20 more than the Sony?

For flying, yes. The comfort and ANC performance make it a no-brainer for frequent flyers. If you only fly once a year, save the $20 and get the Sony for the better sound.

Do I need to "burn in" the ANC?

No. That's a myth. ANC works from the first second. The CustomTune on the Bose calibrates automatically every time you put them on.

Bottom Line

Buy the Bose QuietComfort Ultra. It's the best airplane noise-cancelling headphone you can buy right now. The ANC is measurably better at killing engine drone, it's more comfortable for long flights, and the wired ANC option is a lifesaver on flights where you want to plug into the seatback. The Sony XM5 is a better all-around headphone for daily life — better sound, better battery — but when the assignment is "sit in a metal tube for 8 hours and don't go crazy," the Bose wins. If you fly less than twice a year, get the Sony and save $20. If you fly more than that, the Bose is worth every penny.
