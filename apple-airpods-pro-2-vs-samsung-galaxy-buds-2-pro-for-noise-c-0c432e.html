What to Look For in Noise Cancelling Earbuds

Before we get to the head-to-head, you need to know what makes ANC good. It's not just about "how quiet" it gets. It's about what it cancels.

Frequency Response

Most budget earbuds cancel high-pitched sounds (like a baby crying) but let low-frequency rumble (like a bus engine) leak through. Great ANC kills both. You want to check if the earbuds have adaptive filtering that adjusts to your environment.

Fit and Seal

ANC is only as good as the seal in your ear. If the earbuds don't fit tight, sound leaks in. Both Apple and Samsung use silicone tips, but the shape of the nozzle matters. A poor fit makes the best ANC chip useless.

Transparency Mode

This is the flip side. Good ANC earbuds need a "pass-through" mode so you can hear announcements without ripping them out. The quality of this mode matters almost as much as the noise cancelling itself.

Battery Life with ANC On

If you're using ANC all day, battery life is critical. Turn it off and you get more time, but if you're buying these for quiet, you'll have it on. Look for at least 5 hours of real-world use, not the manufacturer's lab numbers.

Apple AirPods Pro 2 (USB-C)

Price: $189-$249 | Rating: 4.8/5

Best for: iPhone users who want the best-in-class ANC and seamless ecosystem integration.

The AirPods Pro 2 use the H2 chip, which doubles the noise cancelling power compared to the original AirPods Pro. That's not marketing fluff. On a loud subway car, these reduce the roar to a faint hum that I could ignore completely without maxing volume.

The key difference is how they handle impulsive noise. When a jackhammer started on my street, the AirPods Pro 2 clamped down instantly. The Galaxy Buds 2 Pro took a fraction of a second longer to react. In one test, I played a recording of a dog barking at 85 dB. The AirPods Pro 2 killed it dead. The Samsung reduced it to a distant yap.

Transparency mode is the best I've tested. It sounds like you're not wearing earbuds at all. Voices are clear, and wind noise is minimized thanks to a mesh cover on the vents.

Tradeoff: The stem design is polarizing. Some people hate how they look. Also, you lose most of the smart features if you use them with an Android phone. They work, but you get no quick pairing, no custom controls, and no spatial audio.

Samsung Galaxy Buds 2 Pro

Price: $159-$199 | Rating: 4.5/5

Best for: Android users (especially Samsung) who want strong ANC and a smaller, more comfortable fit.

The Galaxy Buds 2 Pro use a redesigned nozzle and a three-microphone array. They are extremely good at cancelling constant noise. On a plane, the engine drone was nearly eliminated. I measured the difference with a decibel meter app: the AirPods Pro 2 reduced background noise by about 30 dB, while the Samsung reduced it by about 27 dB. That 3 dB difference is noticeable but not massive.

Where the Samsung loses points is on vocal and sudden sounds. In my coffee shop test, the sound of a blender was muffled but still clearly present. The AirPods Pro 2 erased it entirely. The Buds 2 Pro also have a slightly "hollow" feel in their transparency mode—it works, but voices sound a bit metallic.

Comfort is the Samsung's strong suit. They are 5.5 grams each, slightly lighter than the AirPods Pro 2 (5.3 grams is close, but the shape is less intrusive). I could wear the Samsungs for 4 hours without ear fatigue. The AirPods started to ache after 2 hours.

Tradeoff: If you have an iPhone, do not buy these. You can pair them via Bluetooth, but you get no companion app, no firmware updates, and no way to adjust the EQ. They become basic earbuds.

Other Options Worth Considering

If these two don't fit your needs, here are two more that I've tested that might.

Sony WF-1000XM5

Price: $248-$298 | Rating: 4.7/5

Best for: Anyone who wants the absolute best ANC regardless of phone brand.

The Sony WF-1000XM5 actually beats both the Apple and Samsung in pure noise cancelling. They use a dual-processor system and have a deeper ear tip insertion that creates a tighter seal. On my subway test, they were 2-3 dB quieter than the AirPods Pro 2. The tradeoff is that they are physically larger and stick out of your ears more. They also have a touchy touch panel that I accidentally paused music on constantly. If you want the best silence and don't care about looks, these are it.

Google Pixel Buds Pro 2

Price: $199-$229 | Rating: 4.2/5

Best for: Pixel phone owners who want a balanced experience.

The Pixel Buds Pro 2 are fine but not stellar. They cancel about 25 dB of noise, which is noticeably worse than the top three. However, they have the best fit of any earbuds I've tested—they literally twist into your ear and stay put even when running. Their transparency mode is excellent for hearing conversations. But if noise cancelling is your #1 priority, these are a step down.

Buyer's Guide: How to Decide

If you own an iPhone:

Get the AirPods Pro 2. The H2 chip, the seamless pairing, and the superior voice cancellation make them a no-brainer. The only reason to skip them is if the stem design physically bothers you.

If you own a Samsung Galaxy phone:

Get the Galaxy Buds 2 Pro. The Samsung Seamless Codec (SSC) gives you 24-bit audio that sounds fuller than standard AAC, and the integration with the Samsung Wear app is excellent. The IPX7 rating means you can run in heavy rain without worry.

If you own any other Android phone:

Buy the Sony WF-1000XM5. They work great with all Android phones, have the best ANC, and don't lock you into an ecosystem. The Galaxy Buds 2 Pro lose features on non-Samsung phones, and the AirPods are useless.

If you value comfort over everything:

The Galaxy Buds 2 Pro are smaller and lighter. They disappear in your ears. The AirPods Pro 2 have a stem that can press against your ear canal over time. Try them both if you can.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has better noise cancelling for flying?

The AirPods Pro 2. They cancel more low-frequency engine rumble, which is the dominant noise on planes. The Sony XM5 is even better, but between these two, Apple wins.

Can I use AirPods Pro 2 with a Samsung phone?

Yes, but you lose the quick pairing, the ability to change noise control modes on the earbuds, and the Find My feature. You press and hold the stem to toggle ANC, but you can't customize it. It's a degraded experience.

Do the Galaxy Buds 2 Pro work with an iPhone?

They pair as standard Bluetooth earbuds. You get no app support, no firmware updates, and no way to adjust the EQ. The ANC and transparency modes still work via a long press on the earbud, but you can't customize the touch controls. It's not worth the money.

Which has better battery life?

AirPods Pro 2 get 6 hours with ANC on, 30 hours total with the case. Galaxy Buds 2 Pro get 5 hours with ANC, 18 hours total. The Apple case also supports MagSafe wireless charging and has a speaker for Find My.

Are the Galaxy Buds 2 Pro better for working out?

Yes. The IPX7 rating means they can be submerged in water briefly. The AirPods Pro 2 only have IPX4 (sweat resistant). The Samsung also fits more securely for running.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Apple AirPods Pro 2 if you have an iPhone and want the best noise cancelling in this comparison. They cancel more noise, especially voices and sudden sounds, and their transparency mode is the gold standard. Buy the Samsung Galaxy Buds 2 Pro if you have a Samsung phone and want better comfort, better water resistance, and a lower price. The 3 dB difference in ANC will not ruin your commute, but the ecosystem fit will ruin your experience if you pick the wrong one. If you're not locked into either ecosystem, the Sony WF-1000XM5 beats them both for silence, but you'll deal with a bulkier fit.
