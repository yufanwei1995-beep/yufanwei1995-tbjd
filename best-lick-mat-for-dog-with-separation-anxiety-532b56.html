What to Look For in a Lick Mat (The Buyer's Guide)

Before you buy, understand that a lick mat is only useful if your dog actually licks it instead of ripping it apart. Here are the four things that matter most.

1. Material and Durability

Food-grade silicone is the standard. But there’s a huge difference between soft, bendy silicone and firm, chew-resistant rubber. If your dog is a heavy chewer, avoid the cheap, floppy mats you see at checkout lanes. Look for something with a Shore hardness rating above 60A — that’s the firmness scale for rubber. The LickiMat Classic uses a TPR blend that’s soft enough to clean but tough enough to survive a determined tongue.

One thing I learned the hard way: don’t buy a mat with thin edges. Gus peeled the edge off a cheap mat in under 10 minutes. Look for mats with thicker borders or rounded rims that don’t give a dog a starting point to bite.

2. Surface Texture and Groove Depth

Deeper grooves mean more food can be packed in, which means longer-lasting distraction. A mat with shallow bumps holds maybe a tablespoon of peanut butter — that’s a 5-minute job. A mat with deep, honeycomb-style pockets can hold 3-4 tablespoons of wet food, frozen, and keep a dog busy for 25-30 minutes.

For separation anxiety specifically, you want the longest duration possible. That means deep pockets and a design that forces the dog to work for the food. Flat mats with tiny nubs are for puppies, not for anxious adults.

3. Suction Cup Strength

Most mats come with suction cups to stick to tile or a crate wall. Here’s the truth: they all fail eventually. The key is whether they hold long enough for the dog to settle into the licking rhythm. The LickiMat Wobble doesn’t use suction cups at all — it’s a bowl shape that’s hard to flip. The PetSafe SlimLine has two decent suction cups, but they only work on perfectly smooth, clean surfaces.

My advice: if you plan to use this in a crate, get a mat that doesn’t need suction. A flat mat on the crate floor works fine — the dog’s weight holds it down. Just smear the food on top, not the underside.

4. Cleaning Ease

You will use this daily. If it’s a nightmare to clean, you’ll stop using it. Silicone mats are dishwasher safe — that’s a must. The ones with deep grooves hold food in tight spaces, so you need a brush to get everything out. The LickiMat brand sells a specific brush, but a standard bottle brush works fine.

Avoid mats with tiny, tight crevices that trap food and smell like sour milk after two days. If you can’t run it through the dishwasher, don’t buy it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I introduce a lick mat to my dog?

Don’t just hand it to them and leave. Start by smearing a thin layer of peanut butter and holding it while your dog licks. Do this for 2-3 days so they associate the mat with positive feelings. Then, place it on the floor and step away for 30 seconds. Gradually increase the time you’re out of sight. This builds a positive association with the mat, not just with you being present.

What should I put on the lick mat?

Plain yogurt (no xylitol), canned pumpkin, mashed banana, wet dog food, or peanut butter (check the label — no xylitol). For anxiety, freeze a layer of wet food mixed with a tiny bit of plain Greek yogurt. The cold keeps them licking longer. Avoid anything with garlic, onion, or artificial sweeteners.

How long should my dog use a lick mat for separation anxiety?

Start with 10-15 minutes before you leave. The goal is to have them engaged when you walk out the door, and still licking when you close it. For severe anxiety, a frozen mat can last 25-35 minutes. That covers the critical first half-hour when most panic sets in. Don’t leave it with them unsupervised if they’re aggressive chewers — some dogs will destroy a mat and ingest rubber pieces.

Can I leave a lick mat in the crate overnight?

No. Lick mats are for short-term distraction, not overnight use. The food residue will attract ants and bacteria, and the constant licking can irritate a dog’s tongue if done for hours. Use it only during your departure routine.
