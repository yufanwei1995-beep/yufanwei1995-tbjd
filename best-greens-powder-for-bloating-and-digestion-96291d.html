What to Look For in a Greens Powder for Digestion

Not all greens are created equal. Here is what separates a good digestion powder from a useless one.

1. Fiber Content (The Bloating Paradox)

You need fiber to move things along, but too much too fast causes gas. Look for 5-8 grams of fiber per serving. Anything over 10g will likely make you worse. Check the label for organic acacia fiber or flaxseed — these are gentle on the gut.

2. Digestive Enzymes

Enzymes break down food so your stomach doesn’t have to work as hard. Look for protease, amylase, and lipase on the label. You want at least 500mg of a digestive enzyme blend per serving. If the label doesn’t list specific enzymes, skip it.

3. Probiotics (CFUs Matter, But Not How You Think)

Most greens add probiotics. The issue is shelf stability. Look for spore-based probiotics like Bacillus subtilis or Bacillus coagulans. These survive the digestive tract. Refrigerated strains in a powder that sits on a shelf for months are mostly dead by the time you drink it.

4. FODMAP Sensitivity

If you are sensitive to FODMAPs, check the ingredients. Many greens powders contain inulin or chicory root — these are prebiotics that feed gut bacteria but cause massive bloating in sensitive people. If you know you have IBS, avoid these entirely.

5. Artificial Sweeteners

Stevia is fine. Sucralose, aspartame, and sugar alcohols are not. They ferment in the gut and cause gas. Some brands use stevia leaf extract — that’s natural and safe. Anything else, put the tub down.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast do greens powders reduce bloating?

Most people see a difference in 3-7 days. If you have severe bloating from FODMAPs, it might take up to two weeks. The enzymes work immediately to break down food, but the probiotic benefits take time to build up in your gut.

Should I take greens powder in the morning or at night?

Morning is best. Your stomach is empty, so the enzymes and probiotics absorb better. Taking it at night can cause gas because the fiber ferments while you sleep. Drink it 20 minutes before breakfast.

Can greens powders replace vegetables?

No. They are a supplement, not a substitute. You still need whole vegetables for their fiber and phytonutrients. Greens powders are a safety net for days when your diet is not perfect.

Do greens powders cause bloating?

Some do. Most powders contain inulin or chicory root as prebiotic fiber. If you are sensitive to FODMAPs, these will make you bloated. If you have IBS, choose a powder with acacia fiber instead — like Transparent Labs or Kaged.

Are expensive greens powders worth it?

Not always. AG1 is triple the price of Kaged but only marginally better. The extra cost goes to marketing and sourcing. Look at the label, not the price tag. If the enzymes and probiotics are there, you are good.
