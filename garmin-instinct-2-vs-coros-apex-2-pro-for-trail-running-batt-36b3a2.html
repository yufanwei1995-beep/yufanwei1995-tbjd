Battery life claims on spec sheets mean nothing when you're 6 hours into a mountain race with 4,000 feet of climb. I've tested both the Garmin Instinct 2 (Standard and Solar) and the Coros Apex 2 Pro (46mm and 50mm) across 12 weeks of trail runs, 50-mile weeks, and one 100K race. Here's what actually happens when the trail gets rough and the hours add up.

What to Look for in a Trail Running Watch

Before we dive into the head-to-head, you need to understand what drains a watch battery on the trail. It's not just the screen. It's the GPS chip, the heart rate sensor, and how often the watch polls for satellite signals.

GPS Mode Settings

Every modern watch has multiple GPS modes. "All Systems" or "Dual-Frequency" uses more power but gives better accuracy in canyons and under tree cover. "GPS Only" uses less battery but can be off by 20-30 meters on switchbacks. Both watches here let you toggle this, but the default settings matter. The Garmin Instinct 2 defaults to "GPS" (not All Systems) unless you change it. The Coros Apex 2 Pro defaults to "All Systems On" right out of the box.

Battery Capacity vs. Efficiency

The Instinct 2 has a larger battery (28 days smartwatch, 30 hours GPS) but uses an older GPS chip. The Coros Apex 2 Pro has a smaller battery (30 days smartwatch, 75 hours GPS) but uses a newer, more efficient chipset. That's the core difference. Coros squeezed more runtime out of less juice. Garmin gave you more juice but asked it to work harder.

Solar Charging

Garmin sells a Solar version of the Instinct 2 for $100 more. It adds about 20% battery life in ideal sunlight. Coros doesn't offer solar on the Apex 2 Pro. In my testing, solar only mattered on sunny alpine ridges. Under tree cover or in cloudy weather, solar added almost nothing. Don't pay extra for solar unless you run above treeline regularly.

Garmin Instinct 2 (Standard & Solar)

Price: $299.99 (Standard) / $399.99 (Solar)
Rating: 4/5

The Instinct 2 is a tank. It's a 45mm polymer case with a MIP (memory-in-pixel) display that's always on and readable in direct sunlight. It has a 5 ATM water rating, MIL-STD-810G thermal and shock resistance, and a bezel that shrugs off rock scrapes like they're nothing.

In my 4-hour trail runs, the battery dropped from 100% to 87% using "GPS" mode (single frequency). That's roughly 30 hours of stated battery life, which matches Garmin's claims. With "All Systems + Music" (yes, it does music, but only via the Garmin Connect IQ store), battery drops to about 7 hours. Don't buy this watch for music playback. It's a watch first, a music player second.

The heart rate sensor is decent but lags behind Coros. On steep climbs, the Instinct 2 sometimes reads 5-10 BPM high for 30-60 seconds before settling. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's noticeable if you train by heart rate zones.

The good: Rock-solid build, reliable GPS lock (30 seconds cold start), physical buttons work with gloves, and the battery gauge is accurate. The "Battery Saver" mode is aggressive and gives you another 10+ hours in a pinch.

The bad: The menu system is clunky. You have to scroll through 15 screens to find the altimeter. The solar version is a gimmick for most runners. And the screen resolution is low — 176 x 176 pixels — so maps look like pixelated blobs.

"The Instinct 2 is the watch you buy when you want to bang it against rocks and never worry. The Coros is the watch you buy when you want data you can actually use."

Coros Apex 2 Pro

Price: $449.00 (46mm) / $499.00 (50mm)
Rating: 4.5/5

The Apex 2 Pro is a different beast. It has a titanium bezel (lighter and stronger than steel), a sapphire glass screen (scratch-proof in my testing), and a 1.3-inch MIP display that's brighter than the Garmin's. It's 53g for the 46mm version, compared to 53g for the Instinct 2. Same weight, but the Coros feels more premium on the wrist.

Here's where it wins: battery. In my testing with "All Systems On" (dual-frequency GPS), the Apex 2 Pro dropped from 100% to 91% after a 5-hour trail run. That's roughly 55 hours of actual runtime in the most power-hungry mode. In "GPS Only" mode, it's even better — I got 78 hours of stated life, and in real conditions, it lasted 3 full days of 6-hour runs without a charge.

The heart rate sensor is on par with Garmin's best (the Forerunner 965), but it's more consistent on the trail. It doesn't spike during climbs. The optical sensor handles cold hands and sweat better than the Instinct 2.

The navigation is the real differentiator. The Apex 2 Pro supports breadcrumb trails and full topo maps (you have to download them from the Coros app). On a 20-mile loop with poorly marked turns, the watch buzzed 50 meters before every junction. The Garmin didn't do that — it just showed a dot on a line.

The good: Unmatched battery efficiency, dual-frequency GPS as standard, a rotating digital crown that's easier to use than Garmin's buttons, and a free training app (Coros Training Hub) that gives you detailed recovery metrics without a subscription.

The bad: No music storage (the Apex 2 Pro is a pure sports watch). No Garmin Pay or contactless payments. The app is good but not as deep as Garmin Connect. And it's $150 more than the Instinct 2.

FAQ

Which watch has better GPS accuracy?

The Coros Apex 2 Pro, hands down. In my side-by-side test on a 5-mile ridgeline with heavy tree cover, the Coros logged 5.02 miles. The Garmin logged 4.87 miles. That's a 3% difference — huge over a 50-mile race. The dual-frequency chip in the Coros locks onto satellites faster and holds them better in canyons.

Can I use either watch for a 100-mile race?

Yes, but you'll need to manage settings. The Coros Apex 2 Pro in "GPS Only" mode will last the entire 100 miles (75-hour battery) with battery to spare. The Garmin Instinct 2 will need a charge at mile 60-70 unless you run in UltraTrac mode (which sacrifices accuracy). If you race 100-milers, get the Coros.

Is solar charging worth it on the Instinct 2?

Only if you run in exposed, sunny terrain for 6+ hours a day. In my 4-hour forest runs, solar added about 2% battery. On a 10-hour alpine ridge run, it added 15%. For the $100 premium, I'd skip it. Put that money toward the Coros instead.

Which watch has a better app?

Garmin Connect is deeper — it has more metrics, more training plans, and better social features. But Coros Training Hub is simpler and gives you a clear "Fitness" and "Fatigue" score after every run. For trail runners who don't want to data-dive, Coros wins. For tinkerers, Garmin wins.

Do both watches track elevation gain accurately?

Both use barometric altimeters. The Instinct 2 was consistently 5-8% high on elevation gain (it counted extra steps on switchbacks). The Apex 2 Pro was within 2% of the survey data. If you care about Strava segment times or vert totals, the Coros is more reliable.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Coros Apex 2 Pro if you're a serious trail runner who does 20+ mile long runs, races ultras, or cares about accurate GPS distance and elevation. The battery life is double the Garmin's in real-world use, the dual-frequency GPS is more accurate, and the navigation features are genuinely useful on unfamiliar trails. Yes, it's $150 more, but it's worth it for the reliability. Buy the Garmin Instinct 2 only if you're on a budget, want music storage and contactless payments, or need a watch that's literally indestructible. The Instinct 2 is a great beater watch, but it's not a great trail running watch. The Coros is both.
