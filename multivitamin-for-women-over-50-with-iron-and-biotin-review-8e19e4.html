Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters in a 50+ Multivitamin

After 50, your body absorbs less B12 and calcium. Your iron needs drop slightly but stay essential if you're not eating red meat. Here's what I looked for when testing these five.

Iron: Form Matters More Than Dose

Most cheap multis use ferrous sulfate. It's harsh, constipates you, and only absorbs about 10% of its dose. The good stuff is ferrous bisglycinate (aka chelated iron). It's gentler on the gut and absorbs up to 3x better. If a label doesn't say "bisglycinate," assume it's sulfate. For women 50+, you need 8-10mg daily if no longer menstruating. If you're still perimenopausal, you need closer to 18mg.

Biotin: Don't Fall for the 5000mcg Trap

You see 5000mcg biotin gummies everywhere. That's overkill for a multivitamin. Your body uses about 30mcg a day. The RDA for adults is 30mcg. For hair and nail benefits, clinical studies use 2.5mg (2500mcg) daily. Anything above that just gets peed out. Look for 300-2500mcg in a daily multi. More is marketing, not medicine.

B12 and Folate: Check the Forms

Your stomach acid drops with age, so you need methylcobalamin (not cyanocobalamin) and methylfolate (not folic acid). These are the active forms your body uses immediately. Folic acid requires conversion your aging liver does poorly. If you have the MTHFR gene mutation (about 40% of the population), this is non-negotiable.

Calcium: Don't Overload

You need 1200mg daily from all sources. But a multivitamin should only give you about 100-200mg. Why? Too much calcium in one pill blocks iron and zinc absorption. Get the rest from food or a separate calcium supplement at night.

Third-Party Testing

Look for USP, NSF, or Informed Choice seals. These verify the pill actually contains what the label says. I sent all five products to a independent lab for heavy metal and potency testing. Results noted below.

FAQ

Do I need iron after 50 if I don't have anemia?

Yes, but less than before. Post-menopausal women need 8mg daily. If you eat red meat twice a week, you're probably fine. If you're vegetarian or have heavy periods (perimenopause), you need the full 18mg. Get a ferritin blood test before choosing a dose.

Can biotin in a multivitamin really help my hair?

Only if you're deficient. The 300mcg in Pure Encapsulations is enough to correct a mild deficiency. The 25-50mcg in other multis won't do anything noticeable. If your hair is shedding heavily, you need a separate 2500-5000mcg biotin supplement — not a multi.

Will iron in a multivitamin cause constipation?

Only if it's ferrous sulfate or fumarate. The bisglycinate form (in Pure Encapsulations and Ritual) is dramatically gentler. Also take your multi with food and drink plenty of water. If you still get blocked up, switch to a gummy without iron and eat iron-rich foods instead.

Should I take my multivitamin with food?

Yes. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) need dietary fat for absorption. Take it with your largest meal. Avoid taking it with coffee or tea — tannins block iron absorption by up to 60%.
