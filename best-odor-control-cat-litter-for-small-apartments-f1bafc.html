Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Small Spaces

Before you buy anything, understand these five factors. They matter more in a small apartment than anywhere else.

1. Clumping Speed

Slow clumps break apart when you scoop. That leaves urine residue in the box, which is the #1 source of ammonia smell. Look for litters that clump in under 10 seconds. Hard clumps that hold their shape mean you remove the smell source completely, every time.

2. Dust Levels

In a tiny apartment, dust doesn't dissipate. It settles on your bed, your kitchen counter, and your coffee mug. Low-dust litters are non-negotiable if you have allergies or value clean surfaces. Most clay litters are dusty. Premium clays and plant-based options are better.

3. Scent vs. Unscented

Strong perfumes in litter don't eliminate odor — they mix with it. The result is a weird lavender-ammonia combo that's often worse than the original smell. In a small space, that's amplified. I recommend unscented or lightly scented litters. If you want fragrance, look for ones using activated charcoal or natural enzymes, not artificial perfume.

4. Tracking

Every cat tracks litter. In a big house, you can sweep once a day. In a small apartment, litter ends up in your bed. Look for larger, heavier granules or litters specifically designed to minimize tracking. Pellet-style litters track the least but often have worse odor control.

5. Weight and Disposal

You'll be carrying litter up stairs and hauling it to the trash. Heavy clumping litters make for heavy bags. Also consider flushable options if your building allows it and you don't have septic. This is a practical concern apartment dwellers often overlook.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I scoop in a small apartment?

Daily. Non-negotiable. In a small space, even 24 hours of urine sitting in a box creates noticeable ammonia. Daily scooping keeps the smell away and extends the life of your litter.

Should I use scented or unscented litter?

Unscented, unless you specifically like the scent. Artificial perfumes mix with ammonia and create a worse smell. Activated charcoal or baking soda-based litters control odor without adding fragrance.

How often do I need to fully change the litter?

Every 2-3 weeks for clumping litter with daily scooping. Some premium litters like Boxiecat can go a month. If you smell ammonia even after scooping, it's time for a full change.

Can I use a covered litter box to control smell?

A covered box traps smell inside, but it also traps moisture, which makes the litter break down faster. You'll need to clean it more often. And some cats hate covered boxes. Try an open box with a high back wall instead.
