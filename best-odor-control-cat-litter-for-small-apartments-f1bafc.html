What to Look For in Apartment Cat Litter

Not all litters are built the same. Here’s what matters most in a small space:

Odor Control Technology

Clay litters use activated carbon or baking soda to trap ammonia. Silica gel litters (crystal) absorb urine and let water evaporate, which kills odor at the source. Plant-based litters rely on natural enzymes. In my testing, ammonia lock is the #1 factor — if the litter can’t neutralize urine smell within 30 seconds, it fails in an apartment.

Dust Levels

In a small space, dust doesn’t just settle on the floor — it gets into your bed, your food, and your lungs. Tilt the bag and see if a cloud forms. Clumping clay is the dustiest. Crystals are nearly dust-free. Plant-based litters vary wildly.

Clumping Strength

Weak clumps break apart when you scoop, leaving urine-soaked litter at the bottom of the box. That means you’re doing a full dump-and-clean every 3 days instead of every 10. Strong clumps lock odor in a solid mass you can remove in one scoop.

Weight and Tracking

Heavy litter (like traditional clay) stays in the box better but is a pain to carry up stairs. Lightweight litters track everywhere — you’ll be sweeping litter dust off your kitchen floor daily. Crystals track the least but feel weird under some cats’ paws.

Scent vs. Unscented

Fragranced litters mask odor with perfume. In a small apartment, that perfume mixes with ammonia and creates a worse smell. Unscented litters that actually neutralize odor are the better choice. If you want fragrance, look for natural plant-based scents (like pine) rather than synthetic "ocean breeze."

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I change the litter in a small apartment?

With clumping clay, scoop daily and do a full dump-and-clean every 2-3 weeks. With crystal litter like Pretty Litter, scoop solids daily and change the entire box every 3-4 weeks. With plant-based litters, scoop daily and change every 2 weeks — they break down faster.

Does baking soda really help with odor?

Yes, but only if it’s mixed into the litter itself. Sprinkling baking soda on top of the litter doesn’t do much because it doesn’t reach the urine at the bottom. Look for litters that list baking soda or charcoal as an ingredient.

What’s the best way to reduce tracking in an apartment?

Use a litter mat outside the box — a textured one, not a flat one. Also, place the box on a hard surface, not carpet. Carpet holds litter dust and makes the smell worse.

Is scented or unscented better for small spaces?

Unscented wins in apartments. Fragrance mixed with ammonia creates a chemical smell that’s worse than the original odor. If your litter has a strong scent, you’re probably masking a problem, not solving it.

Can I flush cat litter in an apartment?

Only if the litter is specifically labeled flushable (like tuft + paw). Never flush clay or silica litter — it clogs pipes and damages plumbing. Even flushable litter should be checked against your building’s regulations.
