Buyer's Guide: What to Look For in a Clean Whey

Before we get into the head-to-head, you need to know what separates a quality protein powder from overpriced sand. Here are the four things I check on every label.

Protein Source and Quality

Whey isolate is filtered more than concentrate, meaning less lactose and fat. Both brands use isolate as the base, but the sourcing matters. Look for grass-fed cows and cross-flow microfiltration. That process keeps the protein intact without harsh chemicals.

Third-Party Testing

This is non-negotiable. If a brand doesn't pay for independent lab testing, they're asking you to trust their word. Transparent Labs uses Informed Choice. Legion uses Labdoor and has their own third-party certification. Both are solid, but the scope of testing differs.

Artificial Sweeteners and Additives

This is where most brands fail. Sucralose, acesulfame potassium, and artificial dyes are cheap and common. Both Transparent Labs and Legion avoid these, but they use different natural sweeteners. That affects taste and aftertaste significantly.

Price Per Serving

Don't just look at the tub price. Divide it by the number of servings. A $60 tub with 30 servings is $2 per scoop. A $50 tub with 20 servings is $2.50 per scoop. Always do the math before buying.

Transparent Labs Whey Isolate

Rating: 4.7/5

Transparent Labs keeps things simple. The ingredient list is short: grass-fed whey protein isolate, cocoa powder (in chocolate flavors), natural flavoring, and stevia. That's it. No artificial colors, no gums, no digestive enzymes trying to mask a cheap protein source.

Each serving delivers 28 grams of protein, 1 gram of fat, and 2 grams of carbs. The amino acid profile is complete, and you get about 6.5 grams of BCAAs per scoop.

I tested the Chocolate Peanut Butter flavor. It mixes well in a shaker bottle with no clumps. The texture is light, not thick or chalky. In water, it tastes like a melted milkshake. In milk, it's almost dessert-like.

The one downside is the stevia aftertaste. Some people taste it more than others. My training partner hates it. I barely notice it. If you're sensitive to stevia, order a single-serve pack before committing to a full tub.

Transparent Labs is Informed Choice certified, meaning every batch is tested for banned substances and heavy metals. That's important if you compete in tested sports.

Legion Athletics Whey+

Rating: 4.3/5

Legion's Whey+ is their isolate and concentrate blend. It's 100% grass-fed whey isolate, but they add a small amount of concentrate to improve texture and taste. That means slightly more lactose, but the protein per serving stays high at 22 grams.

The label is clean. No artificial sweeteners. They use stevia and monk fruit for sweetness. The flavors are natural, and the ingredient list is transparent — no proprietary blends hiding anything.

I tried the French Vanilla flavor. It's sweeter than Transparent Labs, and the monk fruit helps mask any stevia bitterness. The texture is creamier, which makes it better in oatmeal or baking. In plain water, it's still smooth but slightly thicker than Transparent Labs.

Legion is Labdoor certified and tested for heavy metals and banned substances. They also offer a money-back guarantee if you don't like the taste. That's a nice safety net if you're hesitant.

The downside: the concentrate means a tiny bit more carbs and fat per serving. It's negligible for most people, but if you're strictly counting macros, Transparent Labs edges it out.

Flavor Comparison: Which Tastes Better?

Taste is subjective, but I gathered opinions from five people. Here's the breakdown:

  • Chocolate: Transparent Labs wins. Deeper cocoa flavor, less sweet.
  • Vanilla: Legion wins. Creamier and more dessert-like.
  • Fruit flavors: Legion has more options, but Transparent Labs tastes more natural.
  • Mixability: Both mix clean with no clumps in a shaker.

If you want something that tastes like a treat, go Legion. If you want something that tastes like real food without the fake sugar vibe, go Transparent Labs.

Price and Value Comparison

Here's where things get interesting. Both brands are premium priced, but the value differs.

Transparent Labs sells a 30-serving tub for around $59. That's about $1.97 per serving. Legion sells a 28-serving tub for around $55, which is about $1.96 per serving. Nearly identical.

But Transparent Labs gives you 28 grams of protein per serving. Legion gives you 22. That means you're paying about 7 cents per gram of protein with Transparent Labs, and about 9 cents per gram with Legion. Transparent Labs is the better deal if you're tracking cost per gram of protein.

Legion frequently runs discounts on their website, especially for first-time buyers. If you catch a sale, the price gap narrows. Transparent Labs rarely discounts, but they have a subscription option that saves you 10%.

Who Should Buy Which?

Buy Transparent Labs if:

  • You want the purest whey isolate with zero concentrate
  • You compete in tested sports and need Informed Choice certification
  • You prefer chocolate flavors over vanilla
  • You want the highest protein per serving

Buy Legion if:

  • You're sensitive to stevia's bitter aftertaste
  • You want a creamier texture for baking or oatmeal
  • You want a money-back guarantee
  • You prefer vanilla or fruit flavors

Frequently Asked Questions

Are both proteins gluten-free?

Yes. Both are gluten-free and contain no artificial colors or flavors.

Can I use these for weight loss?

Yes. Both are low in carbs and fat, making them easy to fit into a calorie deficit. Transparent Labs has slightly fewer calories per serving (120 vs 130).

Which is better for lactose-intolerant people?

Transparent Labs is better. It's a pure isolate with almost no lactose. Legion includes a small amount of concentrate, which adds a bit more lactose.

Do these mix well in water?

Both mix fine in water. Transparent Labs is thinner, Legion is slightly creamier. Neither leaves clumps.

Are these proteins tested for banned substances?

Yes. Transparent Labs is Informed Choice certified. Legion is Labdoor certified. Both test every batch for heavy metals and banned substances.

Bottom Line

Both Transparent Labs and Legion Athletics make excellent clean whey proteins. You can't go wrong with either. But if you force me to pick one, it's Transparent Labs Whey Isolate. It gives you more protein per serving, a cleaner ingredient list with zero concentrate, and a better cost per gram of protein. The stevia aftertaste is the only knock, and it's minor. If you're sensitive to stevia or you want a creamier texture and a money-back guarantee, grab Legion Whey+ instead. Either way, you're avoiding the artificial junk that fills most protein powders on the market.
