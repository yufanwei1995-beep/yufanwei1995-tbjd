Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before we get to the reviews, here's what actually matters when you're lifting heavy in a small space.

Weight Range and Increments

Most "adjustable" dumbbells top out at 50 or 60 pounds per hand. That's fine for curls, but it's useless for squats, rows, or bench presses if you're past a beginner level. You want at least 80–90 pounds per hand for lower body work. The 200-pound total means you're looking at 100 lbs per dumbbell, which is a serious load.

Also check the increment size. Some models jump by 10 pounds, which makes progressive overload a pain. Look for 5-pound increments or less in the critical 10–50 pound range.

Footprint and Storage

In an apartment, floor space is gold. A 6-foot barbell rack is out. You need something that sits in a corner or under a bed. Measure your space before you buy. Some models, like the PowerBlock, are compact cubes. Others, like the NÜOBELL, look like traditional dumbbells but take up more room per pound.

Floor Protection

This is the dealbreaker for renters. Dropping a 100-pound dumbbell on hardwood will cost you your security deposit. Look for rubber-coated plates or a design that doesn't have sharp metal edges. Also budget for a dumbbell mat — a 6x4-foot rubber mat runs about $50 and is non-negotiable.

Speed of Adjustment

If it takes 30 seconds to change weights, you'll skip the change and do the wrong weight. The best designs let you swap weights in under 5 seconds. This matters most for circuit training or supersets.

Build Quality and Warranty

You're paying for a tool that will take a beating. Check the warranty. A 5-year warranty is the minimum. Look for metal gears and locking mechanisms, not plastic parts that will crack under load.

The tradeoff: It's a great starter dumbbell, but you'll outgrow it. If you're already lifting 60+ pounds for squats, skip it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drop these dumbbells on my apartment floor?

No. Even with rubber coating, dropping a heavy dumbbell will damage floors and annoy neighbors. Always use a thick rubber mat and lower the weight under control. If you're doing Olympic lifts, you need a different setup entirely.

Are adjustable dumbbells safe?

Yes, if you lock the mechanism properly. The biggest risk is a loose plate falling off mid-lift. Always double-check the lock before lifting. The models above all have secure locking systems.

How much floor space do I need?

About 4x6 feet for a workout area, plus a small corner for storage. All the dumbbells on this list fit in a 2x2-foot footprint when stored.

What weight should I buy?

If you can afford it, get the highest weight you might need in the next 2-3 years. Buying a 50-pound pair and then upgrading to 90 pounds costs more in the long run. Start with what you can lift now, but leave room to grow.

Do I need a bench?

For a full-body routine, yes. An adjustable bench (around $150) lets you do presses, rows, and flyes. You don't need a full rack, but a bench is essential for pressing movements.

If you absolutely need the full 200 pounds, the SMRTFT is the only option, but you'll pay a premium for that extra 20 pounds. Skip the Bowflex unless you're a pure beginner — you'll outgrow it in six months.
