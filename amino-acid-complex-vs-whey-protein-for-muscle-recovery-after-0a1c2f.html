Buyer's Guide: What to Look For When Buying

Before we dive into specific products, here is what actually matters when you are over 50 and shopping for recovery supplements.

Leucine: The Master Switch

Leucine is the amino acid that triggers muscle protein synthesis (MPS). As you age, your body becomes resistant to this signal. You need 2.5 to 3 grams of leucine per serving to reliably kickstart MPS. If a product has less than that, you're basically drinking expensive flavored water.

Total Protein vs. Isolated Amino Acids

Whey protein contains all nine essential amino acids, plus non-essential ones. It's a complete food. An amino acid complex is typically just the essential amino acids (EAAs) or branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs). Your gut has to do less work with isolated amino acids because they're already broken down, but you miss out on the extra calories and satiety that whole protein provides.

Digestive Tolerance

Lactose intolerance becomes more common with age. Whey concentrate has more lactose than whey isolate. If you get bloated after shakes, look for an isolate or hydrolysate, or switch to an amino acid complex which is naturally lactose-free.

Third-Party Testing

Your kidneys and liver aren't what they used to be. You don't want to ingest heavy metals or banned substances. Buy from brands that use third-party testing like Informed Choice or NSF Certified for Sport.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take both amino acids and whey protein?

Yes, but it's usually redundant. If you take a whey shake, you're already getting all the EAAs and BCAAs you need. Adding an amino acid complex on top is just extra calories and cost. The only exception is if you take BCAAs during a fasted workout and save whey for after. This is a niche strategy and not necessary for most people.

Is whey protein safe for my kidneys at 50+?

For healthy kidneys, yes. The old myth that high protein damages kidneys has been debunked for healthy individuals. However, if you have pre-existing kidney disease, talk to your doctor. The general rule is 1.2 to 1.6 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight is safe and beneficial for older adults.

Why do I get bloated from whey but not amino acids?

Lactose intolerance. Whey concentrate contains lactose. Amino acid complexes do not. If you get bloated, switch to a pure whey isolate (like Transparent Labs) or a plant-based EAA product. Your gut will thank you.

When should I take them for best recovery?

Within 30-60 minutes after your workout. That's when your muscles are most receptive to nutrients. If you train fasted, take amino acids immediately after or even during the workout. If you train fed, a whey shake post-workout is perfect.

Does creatine help more than these?

Creatine is the most studied supplement for muscle recovery and strength. It works differently than protein. We recommend taking 5g of creatine monohydrate daily in addition to your protein or amino acids. They are not competing; they are complementary.
