What to Look For: The Buyer's Guide

Before you spend a dime, measure your setup. Grab a tape measure. Know your desk depth, your monitor size, and how much stuff you keep on your work surface. Here's what actually matters:

Footprint and Clearance

A converter sits on your existing desk. That means it eats vertical space when lowered. If you're 5'4" like my wife, a converter that's 4 inches tall when collapsed might feel too high for typing. Full desks let you set the minimum height to fit your chair perfectly. Measure your seated elbow height — that's your target for the keyboard tray or desk surface.

Weight Capacity and Monitor Setup

Most converters handle 20–35 pounds. That's fine for one or two monitors. But if you're running a heavy 32-inch monitor on a gas-spring arm, you'll need a heavy-duty converter like the Ergotron LX or just bite the bullet on a full desk. Full desks typically support 150–300 pounds, so they're the better choice if you have a full tower PC, speakers, and a printer on top.

Stability at Height

Here's the tradeoff no one mentions: converters wobble more at max height because they're perched on top of another surface. A full desk with a C-frame or T-frame base is much more stable when raised. If you're a heavy typer or use a drawing tablet, stability matters more than anything. I've spilled coffee twice with a converter at full extension. Never happened with my Uplift V2.

Cable Management

Converters are a cable nightmare. You have to route cords from your desk, up the converter arm, and into your devices. Most have a small channel, but it's cramped. Full desks have wide cable trays underneath. If you hate zip ties and velcro wraps, factor that in.

Adjustability Range

Full desks typically adjust from 25 to 50 inches. Converters add about 15–20 inches on top of your desk height. So if your desk is 29 inches tall, a converter tops out around 45–49 inches. For someone over 6 feet tall, that might not be enough. Check the specs. The FlexiSpot M2B goes from 4.7 to 19.7 inches of added height — that's a solid range.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a converter work on any desk?

No. You need a desk that's at least 24 inches deep and can handle the clamp pressure. Particle board desks under 1 inch thick are risky — the clamps can crack them. Solid wood or heavy MDF is fine. Also, make sure your desk doesn't have a lip or edge trim that prevents the clamp from seating properly.

How much space do I need for a full standing desk?

You need about 2 feet of depth and at least 40 inches of width for a comfortable setup. Also, you need 6–8 inches of clearance behind the desk for cables and the motor unit. If your room is narrower than 6 feet, a full desk will feel cramped. A converter is the better call.

Is a converter less stable than a full desk?

Yes, almost always. A converter adds height on top of a desk, creating a lever arm that magnifies wobble. Full desks with a single central column or C-frame are far more stable at standing height. If you're doing fine motor work like drawing or soldering, go with a full desk.

Can I use a laptop on a converter?

Yes, but I recommend an external monitor and keyboard. Otherwise, you'll be looking down at a 13-inch screen, which defeats the ergonomic purpose. The FlexiSpot M2B and Ergotron WorkFit-T both have separate keyboard trays that help with this.

Do I need to worry about my floor?

Full desks can dent hardwood floors under heavy loads. Use a floor mat. Converters are lighter, so they're less of a concern. Also, check the weight of the full desk itself — the Uplift V2 weighs about 90 pounds in the box. Make sure you can get it up the stairs.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy a converter if you already have a sturdy desk, work with a single monitor, and your floor space is tight. The FlexiSpot M2B is my top pick — it's well-built, reasonably priced, and fits most setups. Buy a full desk if you have the floor space, run a heavy multi-monitor rig, or hate the wobble. The Uplift V2 is the best investment for long-term comfort and stability, even if it costs three times as much. If you're on a strict budget, the Mount-It! MI-7920 will get you standing today, but plan to upgrade it within a year or two when the cheap parts start to wear.
