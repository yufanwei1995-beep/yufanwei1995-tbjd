Who This Is For

You want one Bluetooth speaker that lives in your beach bag all summer. You don't want to baby it. You don't want to worry about sand in the ports or a wave sneaking up on your towel. You also want it loud enough that people three coolers down can hear the bass drop.

This comparison is for anyone who has stared at both speakers in a store and wondered: Do I pay more for the Sonos brand, or do I get the JBL that everyone swears by? I've spent the last month taking both to actual beach parties, pool days, and one very sandy tailgate. Here's what I learned.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters at the Beach

Before we dive into the two main contenders, here's what you need to check before buying any outdoor speaker.

IP Rating Isn't Everything

Both speakers have an IP67 rating. That means they're dust-tight and can survive being submerged in a meter of water for 30 minutes. That's great. But here's the thing nobody tells you: salt water is not the same as fresh water. Salt corrodes the metal mesh and the charging contacts. Rinse your speaker with fresh water after every beach trip. Do this even if the spec sheet says it's waterproof. Your future self will thank you.

Battery Life vs. Volume

A speaker that lasts 12 hours at 30% volume is useless if you can't hear it over the waves. Look at the real-world battery life at 70%+ volume. That's what matters for a party. Most manufacturers list battery at low volume. I always test at realistic listening levels.

Weight and Portability

You're carrying this in a bag with sunscreen, towels, and snacks. Every ounce counts. But too light means cheap plastic. Find the sweet spot between rugged and portable.

Sound Profile

Outdoors, you lose all the bass reflection you get from walls. A speaker that sounds decent in your kitchen might sound thin and weak on a beach. Look for speakers with passive radiators or dedicated woofers. You need actual air movement.

Charging Port Protection

Sand gets everywhere. If the charging port cover is flimsy, you'll find sand inside it by the end of the season. Look for snug, rubberized covers that actually click shut.

Sonos Roam 2 Review

Price: $169–$179 | Rating: 3.5/5

The Sonos Roam 2 is the smartest portable speaker I've ever tested. It has Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and voice assistant support. It seamlessly integrates with your existing Sonos system at home. That's a killer feature if you're already in the Sonos ecosystem.

But here's where it falls apart at the beach: battery life is only 10 hours, and that's at moderate volume. Crank it up to party levels and you'll be lucky to get 6–7 hours. That's not enough for a full afternoon and evening. It's also quieter than the JBL — max volume is noticeably lower. In a beach environment with wind and waves, that's a problem.

The sound quality is excellent for its size. It has a balanced, detailed sound that's more refined than the JBL. But it lacks the punchy bass that makes people dance. The Roam 2 is a precision instrument. The JBL is a party cannon.

Durability is where the Roam 2 worries me. The fabric mesh feels premium, but it's more fragile than the JBL's rugged grille. I dropped it on sand from waist height and it survived, but I didn't feel confident doing it. The charging contacts are exposed on the bottom. Salt water and sand will find those.

Verdict on Sonos: Buy this if you want a smart speaker that you occasionally take outdoors. Don't buy it as your dedicated beach speaker.

JBL Flip 6 Review

Price: $129–$149 | Rating: 4.5/5

The JBL Flip 6 is the workhorse of the portable speaker world. It's been through everything. It's survived drops, dunkings, and years of abuse. The fabric and rubber construction feels bulletproof. The passive radiators on the ends are exposed, which looks odd, but they're actually recessed enough to survive most falls.

Battery life is a solid 12 hours at moderate volume. At party volume (80%+), you'll get 7–8 hours. That's better than the Sonos. It also gets loud. The Flip 6 has a dedicated tweeter and a racetrack-shaped woofer. It pushes serious air for its size. In open air, it maintains bass response better than any speaker in this class.

The sound profile is V-shaped — boosted bass and treble. That's perfect for outdoors. It cuts through ambient noise. It's not as detailed or accurate as the Sonos, but you won't care when everyone's dancing.

Here's the real kicker: the JBL is cheaper. You're paying less money for a louder, longer-lasting, more rugged speaker. The only thing you lose is smart features and Wi-Fi connectivity.

Verdict on JBL: This is the beach party champion. It's simple, loud, and tough.

Honorable Mention: Ultimate Ears BOOM 4

Price: $149–$169 | Rating: 4/5

The UE BOOM 4 is the third option you should consider. It has a cylindrical design that delivers true 360-degree sound. That's genuinely useful at a beach party where people are spread out. It's also rated IP67 and floats if you drop it in the water. The JBL and Sonos sink.

Battery life is 15 hours, which beats both. But it's heavier and bulkier than the other two. The sound is good but not as punchy as the JBL. The bass is adequate but doesn't hit as hard.

Best for: Pool parties where floating capability and 360-degree coverage matter more than max volume.

FAQ

Can I pair two JBL Flip 6 speakers together?

Yes. JBL has PartyBoost. You can link two Flip 6 speakers for true stereo sound. You can also connect them to other compatible JBL speakers. It works reliably within about 30 feet.

Does the Sonos Roam 2 work without Wi-Fi?

Yes, it has Bluetooth. But you lose the smart features and multi-room audio. At the beach, you'll just be using it as a dumb Bluetooth speaker anyway.

How do I clean sand out of the ports?

Use a soft, dry toothbrush. Gently brush out the charging port and any crevices. Do not use compressed air — that can push sand deeper into the speaker. After a beach day, rinse the whole speaker with fresh water (yes, even the ports — they're sealed), then let it dry completely before charging.

Which speaker is better for calls on the beach?

Neither is great. The Sonos has better microphone quality, but wind noise ruins it anyway. Just use your phone for calls.

Is the JBL Flip 6 worth the upgrade over the Flip 5?

Yes. The Flip 6 adds a separate tweeter for clearer highs. It's a noticeable difference in loud environments. If you already have a Flip 5, it's worth upgrading when it dies. Don't rush out to buy one if your current speaker works.

Bottom Line

Buy the JBL Flip 6. It's cheaper, louder, lasts longer, and takes more abuse than the Sonos Roam 2. The Sonos is a better speaker in terms of audio fidelity and smart features, but those qualities don't matter when you're fighting wind, waves, and a crowd of people yelling over the music. The JBL is built for exactly this scenario. It's the speaker I grab when I head to the beach, and I have no hesitation recommending you do the same. If you absolutely need Wi-Fi and voice control, get the Sonos — but understand you're paying a premium for features that will be useless in the sand.
