Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Sensitive Skin

Before you buy, understand these 4 things. They'll save you from a week of flaking.

1. Concentration Isn't Everything

Marketing pushes 1% retinol like it's the holy grail. For sensitive skin, 0.1% to 0.3% is plenty. The studies showing anti-aging benefits used concentrations as low as 0.1%. You're better off with a lower dose you can actually use daily than a strong one that makes you quit after 3 days.

2. Look for Encapsulated or Micro-Delivery Systems

Encapsulated retinol releases slowly into the skin. It bypasses the surface irritation phase and converts to retinoic acid deeper in the dermis. CeraVe and Olay both use this tech. It's not a gimmick—it's the difference between tingly and tolerable.

3. Buffer Ingredients Matter More Than the Retinol

Check the label for niacinamide, ceramides, or squalane. These calm inflammation while retinol does the work. A bare-bones retinol serum with no soothing agents is a red flag for sensitive skin.

4. Skip the Fragrance and Dyes

Perfume is the #1 cause of contact dermatitis in skincare. If the ingredient list has "parfum" or "fragrance" anywhere, put it back. You want a serum that smells like nothing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I use retinol if I have sensitive skin?

Start with 1 night per week for 2 weeks. Then go to 2 nights for 2 weeks. Only after 4 weeks of no irritation, bump to every other night. Don't rush this. The goal is consistent use, not aggressive use.

Can I use retinol with vitamin C?

Not at the same time. Vitamin C is acidic, retinol is alkaline—they cancel each other out. Use vitamin C in the morning, retinol at night. If you must use both at night, wait 20 minutes between applications.

Do I need to wear sunscreen if I use retinol?

Absolutely. Retinol thins the stratum corneum (top layer) temporarily, making you more UV-sensitive. SPF 30+ every morning is non-negotiable. If you skip sunscreen, you'll undo all the anti-aging work.

What's the difference between retinol and retinyl palmitate?

Retinol is 20 times stronger. Retinyl palmitate is an ester that converts even slower—it's barely effective but virtually non-irritating. For sensitive skin, retinol is the sweet spot. Retinyl palmitate is a waste of money if you want real results.
