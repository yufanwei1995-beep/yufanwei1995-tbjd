Why This Choice Matters More Than You Think

In a small kitchen, every inch counts. A countertop microwave eats up 1.5 to 2 square feet of precious workspace. A drawer microwave slides under your counter, giving you back that space for food prep. But that convenience comes with a hefty price tag and some real usability quirks.

I've tested both types in my own kitchen remodels and in client projects. Here's what actually matters when you're making this call.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Space and Layout

Measure your counter depth first. Most countertop microwaves are 15–18 inches deep. If you have shallow counters (under 20 inches), a drawer unit might be your only option to avoid a massive appliance jutting out. Drawer microwaves need a cutout that's about 24 inches wide and 18 inches tall, plus clearance for ventilation.

Installation Complexity

Countertop units: plug in and go. Drawer units: require a licensed electrician to run a dedicated 15-amp circuit, and you'll need a carpenter to build the cutout. That's an extra $300–$500 in labor on top of the unit price. If you're not doing a full remodel, stick with countertop.

Usability and Ergonomics

Drawer microwaves slide open like a drawer. You place food on the flat bottom and slide it shut. Sounds great, but there's a catch: you have to bend down to see inside, and hot liquid spills are harder to avoid since you can't just lift the bowl out straight up. Countertop units sit at eye level, which is easier for most people.

Power and Size

Most countertop microwaves range from 700 to 1,200 watts. Drawer units are typically 1,000 watts. For a small kitchen, 900 watts is the sweet spot — it heats evenly without nuking your food. Anything under 700 watts is too weak for anything beyond reheating coffee.

FAQ

Can I install a drawer microwave myself?

Unless you're comfortable with electrical work and cabinet modifications, no. You need a dedicated circuit and a precise cutout. Hiring a pro costs $300–$500. Countertop units need zero installation.

Are drawer microwaves harder to clean?

Yes. The flat bottom is easy, but the back and side corners are hard to reach. You'll need a long-handled brush or a vacuum attachment. Countertop units let you remove the glass turntable and wipe everything down in seconds.

Do drawer microwaves heat food differently?

No, the heating technology is the same. The only difference is ergonomics — you're reaching down instead of across. Some people find it easier to stir food in a drawer because you can see the bottom of the bowl without lifting it.

Will a drawer microwave increase my home's resale value?

Maybe. If you're doing a full kitchen remodel with high-end appliances, it looks impressive. But if you're just swapping one appliance, buyers won't pay a premium for it. Countertop microwaves are expected in most kitchens.
