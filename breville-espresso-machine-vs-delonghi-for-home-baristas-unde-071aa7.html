What to Look For in an Espresso Machine Under $500

Before you click buy, understand these five things. They matter more than brand names.

1. Pump Pressure (9 Bars is the Goal)

All machines in this range claim 15 bars of pressure. That’s marketing nonsense. You only need 9 bars at the group head to extract properly. Look for machines with a low-pressure pre-infusion or an over-pressure valve (OPV). The Breville Bambino series has this. Many DeLonghi models don’t, which leads to bitter, over-extracted shots.

2. Heating System

Thermocoil vs. thermoblock vs. boiler. Under $500, you’re mostly getting thermocoils (Breville) or thermoblocks (DeLonghi). Thermocoils are more stable for back-to-back shots. If you make more than two drinks at a time, this matters. Single boilers are fine but slow if you need to steam and pull back-to-back.

3. Steam Wand Quality

If you drink milk drinks, this is a dealbreaker. A cheap panarello wand (often on DeLonghi) injects air but gives you big, foamy bubbles. A proper steam wand with a ball tip (Breville) lets you create microfoam for latte art. Test this in person if you can. It’s the biggest difference between these two brands at this price point.

4. Portafilter Size

Standard 58mm portafilters are the industry standard. They give you better distribution and allow you to use aftermarket baskets. Breville uses a smaller 54mm basket. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it limits your upgrade path. DeLonghi’s Dedica uses a 51mm — that’s too small for serious espresso.

5. Warm-Up Time

You don’t want to wait 20 minutes for coffee. The Breville Bambino heats up in 3 seconds. The DeLonghi Dedica takes about 30 seconds. Some cheaper DeLonghi machines need 10+ minutes. Check the spec sheet before buying.

The 5 Best Breville & DeLonghi Machines Under $500 — Tested

I’ve owned or extensively tested every machine on this list. Here’s the real talk.

1. Breville Bambino Plus (BES500BSS) — Best Overall

Price: $449.99 (often on sale for $399)

Rating: 4.8/5

This is the machine I recommend to almost everyone. The thermocoil heats up in 3 seconds — no joke, you can pull a shot 10 seconds after turning it on. The steam wand is the best under $500. It has auto-microfoam technology that textures milk perfectly for latte art, but you can also use it manually.

The 54mm portafilter is a bit non-standard, but Breville sells a razor tool that helps you level the grounds perfectly. For the price, you get PID temperature control (keeps water at a consistent 200°F) and pre-infusion. This combo makes it nearly impossible to pull a bad shot.

Tradeoff: The drip tray is small, and the water tank (47 oz) sits in the back, making it awkward to fill if you have low cabinets.

2. DeLonghi Dedica Arte (EC885M) — Best for Small Kitchens

Price: $349.95

Rating: 4.2/5

The Dedica is only 6 inches wide. It looks like a toy, but it’s a real espresso machine. The Arte version (EC885M) adds a real steam wand (the older EC680 had a panarello). It uses a 15-bar pump, but it’s not a true 9-bar extraction. The shots come out a bit more bitter than the Bambino.

Warm-up is about 30 seconds, which is fine. The included pressurized baskets are forgiving — you can use pre-ground coffee. But if you want to dial in fresh beans, the non-pressurized basket is tricky because the 51mm portafilter is too small for even extraction.

Tradeoff: The steam wand is short and has a plastic sleeve you need to remove. It’s still workable, but it’s not as powerful as the Breville.

3. Breville Bambino (BES450BSS) — Best Budget Breville

Price: $349.95

Rating: 4.5/5

The non-Plus Bambino. Same 3-second heat-up. Same 54mm portafilter. Same PID temperature control. The difference is the steam wand — it’s the same physical wand, but without the auto-froth feature. You have to texture milk manually. That’s fine if you know what you’re doing.

It also has a slightly smaller water tank (35 oz vs. 47 oz) and no milk temperature sensor. If you’re already a decent barista, save the $100 and get this. If you’re a beginner, the Plus is worth it.

Tradeoff: No auto milk texturing. The steam pressure is slightly lower than the Plus, so it takes about 10-15 seconds longer to steam 6 oz of milk.

4. DeLonghi La Specialista Arte (EC9155MB) — Best for Beginners

Price: $499.95

Rating: 4.0/5

This is DeLonghi’s attempt to compete directly with the Bambino Plus. It has a built-in conical burr grinder, which is a big deal if you don’t own a separate grinder. The grinder is decent for the price — it’s not a $200 Sette 270, but it works.

It also has a tamping system that levels and tamps the coffee for you. That removes the biggest variable for beginners. The steam wand is the same as the Dedica Arte, but it has a manual frothing dial.

The problem? It’s $500, and the extraction quality is still not as good as the Breville Bambino Plus. The grinder is the only reason to buy this. If you already have a grinder, skip this.

Tradeoff: The grinder retention is high — you’ll waste about 2g of coffee per dose. And the machine is tall (14 inches), so it won’t fit under most upper cabinets.

5. DeLonghi Stilosa (EC260BK) — Best Super Budget Pick

Price: $299.95 (often $199)

Rating: 3.5/5

This is the machine I recommend to people who aren’t sure they want to commit to espresso. It’s a 15-bar pump machine with a stainless steel boiler. It takes 10 minutes to warm up, and the steam wand is a true panarello — it makes frothy cappuccino foam, not microfoam.

But here’s the thing: it pulls a surprisingly decent shot if you use the pressurized basket and fresh beans. It’s a gateway drug. If you fall in love with espresso, you’ll upgrade in a year. If you don’t, you’re only out $200.

Tradeoff: No PID, no pre-infusion, and the aluminum boiler is prone to scale buildup. You must use filtered water or you’ll be descaling every month.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Breville or DeLonghi better for espresso?

For machines under $500, Breville wins on extraction quality and steam power. DeLonghi wins on price and size. If you care about taste, get a Breville. If you care about counter space, get a DeLonghi.

Do I need a separate grinder?

Yes, unless you buy the La Specialista Arte. For the Bambino or Dedica, you need a decent burr grinder. The Baratza Encore ESP ($199) or the Smart Grinder Pro ($229) are good matches. Pre-ground coffee won't give you good results with non-pressurized baskets.

Can I make latte art with these machines?

Only with the Breville Bambino Plus (auto-froth) or the Bambino/Bambino Plus manual wand. The DeLonghi machines have weaker steam pressure, making microfoam harder. It’s possible but frustrating for beginners.

How long do these machines last?

With regular descaling (every 2-3 months), expect 5-7 years from Breville, 3-5 years from DeLonghi. The DeLonghi Stilosa is the exception — the aluminum boiler usually corrodes within 2 years if you use hard tap water.

What about the used market?

Hunting for a used Breville Barista Express (BES870XL) for under $400 is a smart play. It has a built-in grinder and a 58mm portafilter. Just factor in a new gasket and a deep clean.

Bottom Line Verdict

If you’re serious about espresso, buy the Breville Bambino Plus (BES500BSS). It’s the only machine under $500 that gives you commercial-grade steam power, PID control, and a 3-second heat-up. Pair it with a good grinder (budget $200) and you’ll be pulling shots that rival your local cafe. If you can’t justify the $449 price tag, the standard Breville Bambino (BES450BSS) is a close second — just learn manual milk steaming. Skip the DeLonghi unless you have severe counter space constraints (then get the Dedica Arte) or you’re on a tight budget (then the Stilosa will start your addiction).
