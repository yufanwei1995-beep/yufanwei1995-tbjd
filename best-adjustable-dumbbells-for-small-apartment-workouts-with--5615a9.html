Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before you drop serious cash, understand the three mechanics of adjustable dumbbells. This determines if you'll actually use them or if they'll become a $500 coat rack.

1. Adjustment Mechanism (Speed vs. Safety)

There are two main types. The dial system (Bowflex, NÜOBELL) lets you turn a knob to select weight. It's fast—about 3 seconds to change. The cradle/tray system (PowerBlock, Marcy) requires you to slide the dumbbell onto a base plate and pull pins. It's slightly slower but often cheaper and more robust. Avoid cheap twist-lock mechanisms that loosen during reps; they're a safety hazard.

2. Weight Range and Increments

Most sets go from 5 lbs to 52.5 lbs. That's fine for beginners and most intermediate lifters. If you're advanced, look at the NÜOBELL 80 or PowerBlock Elite EXP, which go up to 80 lbs. Check the increments: 5 lb jumps are standard, but 2.5 lb increments (like the Bowflex 1090) are better for progressive overload on lateral raises.

3. Stand Design and Footprint

The included stand is a huge factor. You want a tray that holds the dumbbells securely but doesn't require a PhD to align. Measure your space. A typical stand is 22" W x 18" D. The Bowflex stand has a 24" footprint. The NÜOBELL stand is wider to prevent tipping. Ensure the stand has rubber feet to avoid scratching hardwood floors.

4. Durability & Noise

Metal-on-metal clanking is a nightmare in an apartment with thin walls. Look for dumbbells with nylon or plastic bushing inserts (like the NÜOBELL) to dampen noise. Also, check the weight plates—are they fully enclosed or exposed? Exposed plates (PowerBlock) are more likely to dent if you drop them.

5. Warranty

Moving parts break. Bowflex offers a 10-year frame warranty, 1-year parts. PowerBlock offers 10-year full warranty. NÜOBELL has a 3-year. Stick with a company that backs their product.

Product Reviews

1. Bowflex SelectTech 552 with Stand (Our Top Pick)

This is the gold standard. The 552 adjusts from 5 to 52.5 lbs in 2.5 lb increments for the first 25 lbs, then 5 lb increments after. That granularity is a game-changer for rotator cuff work and delt raises. The dial mechanism is smooth and quick. The stand is sturdy and doubles as a storage tray that keeps the dumbbells off the floor.

I tested these for heavy rows and bicep curls. The plates are fully enclosed in a plastic/metal housing, so they're quiet—no metal-on-metal clang. The grip is knurled but not aggressive, which is good for high-rep work.

The downside: The length of the handle stays the same regardless of weight, which means at 5 lbs, the plates are far from your hand, making the dumbbell feel awkwardly long. Also, the maximum 52.5 lbs is limiting for serious deadlifts.

  • Weight Range: 5-52.5 lbs
  • Increments: 2.5 lbs (up to 25 lbs), then 5 lbs
  • Footprint: 24" x 18"
Verdict: The best all-around choice for most people. Fast, quiet, and reliable.

2. NÜOBELL 80 Adjustable Dumbbells (Premium/Luxury)

If you have the budget, these are the Ferrari of adjustable dumbbells. They go up to 80 lbs and use a magnetic dial system that feels premium. The plates are contoured and sit closer to the hand, so they feel like fixed dumbbells—a massive advantage over the Bowflex. The weight range is perfect for progressive overload without buying a second set.

The stand is heavy-duty with a wide base, so it doesn't tip if you set them down aggressively. The adjustment is fast: select the weight, lift, and go. The build quality is exceptional; the nylon bushings mean zero rattle.

The downside: The price. It's nearly double the Bowflex. Also, the magnetic dial can be finicky if you're sweaty—you need dry hands to grip it. And at 80 lbs, the handle is long, which can be awkward for close-grip presses.

  • Weight Range: 5-80 lbs

Verdict: Worth the premium if you lift heavy and hate the "toy" feel of other adjustables.

3. PowerBlock Elite EXP (Best Budget)

PowerBlock has been making these since the 90s. The EXP model goes from 5 to 50 lbs (expandable to 70 with expansion kits). The mechanism uses a pin that you slide into a cradle. It's not as fast as a dial, but it's bombproof. The unique design nests the plates inside each other, so the footprint is super narrow—about half the width of the Bowflex. This is key for tiny apartments.

The stand is minimal but functional. The weights are fully exposed steel, which looks industrial. They're a bit clunky for exercises like lateral raises because the plates are wide. But for squats, presses, and rows, they're fantastic.

The downside: They're noisy. You'll hear the plates shift slightly during reps. The pin mechanism requires two hands to adjust, which breaks your flow during supersets. And the max weight without expansion is only 50 lbs.

  • Weight Range: 5-50 lbs (exp. to 70)
  • Increments: 5 lbs
  • Footprint: 16" x 18" (narrowest)
Verdict: The best value. Durable, compact, and half the price of the NÜOBELL.

4. Bowflex SelectTech 1090 (High-Capacity)

This is the big brother to the 552. It goes from 10 to 90 lbs in 5 lb increments. If you're a strong lifter, this is the only dial-based option that gets you to heavy weight. The mechanism is the same smooth dial system as the 552. The stand is larger and heavier to accommodate the extra mass.

The big problem? The length. At 90 lbs, the dumbbell is over 20 inches long. It's impossible to do certain exercises like close-grip presses or good mornings without hitting your thighs. The plates also stick out far from the handle at lower weights, which makes the balance feel off.

The downside: It's expensive and bulky. The 90 lb setting is unwieldy. You also need a lot of grip strength to hold the thick handle at high weights.

  • Weight Range: 10-90 lbs
  • Increments: 5 lbs
  • Footprint: 28" x 22" (largest)
Verdict: Only buy this if you're already lifting heavy and can't progress with the 552s.

5. Marcy Adjustable Dumbbells with Stand (Entry-Level)

Marcy offers the cheapest complete set—around $150. It uses a simple pin-and-plate system. You slide the dumbbell onto a stand, pull a pin, and slide plates off. It's functional but slow. The plates are plastic-coated, which protects your floors. The weight range is 5 to 25 lbs per dumbbell, which is fine for beginners and upper-body toning.

The downside: The build quality is noticeably lower. The plastic coating chips over time. The adjustment is clunky. And the max weight is too light for any lower-body work. This is for someone who just wants light resistance for home workouts, not serious strength training.

  • Weight Range: 5-25 lbs

  • Footprint: 20" x 16"
Verdict: Fine for beginners, but you'll outgrow them in a month.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are adjustable dumbbells safe to drop?

No. The internal mechanisms are sensitive. Dropping them will break the dial or bend the pins. If you're training to failure on heavy sets, use a spotter or do controlled negatives.

Will the stand scratch my floors?

Most stands come with rubber feet. But if you have hardwood, buy a cheap rubber mat (like a horse stall mat) to place under the stand. It also prevents the dumbbells from sliding when you set them down.

Can I do deadlifts with these?

Yes, but only up to the max weight. At 52.5 lbs, you'll outgrow them quickly for deadlifts. For most apartment dwellers, 50 lbs is enough for Romanian deadlifts and goblet squats.

How much space do I really need?

You need the stand footprint plus about 3 feet of clearance on each side for lifting. The PowerBlock is the most space-efficient, but the NÜOBELL's wide base is more stable.

If you're a heavier lifter who needs 80 lbs and has the cash, spring for the NÜOBELL 80—it's the only one that feels like a real dumbbell. And if you're on a strict budget, the PowerBlock Elite EXP is a solid, compact workhorse. Skip the Marcy unless you're just starting out and don't mind replacing it in six months.
