What to Look For in a Calming Bed for a Chewer

You can't just buy any bed that says "calming." You need a specific combination of features. Here's what matters.

1. Chew Resistance is Non-Negotiable

If your dog chews, a standard fluffy bed is a ticking time bomb. Look for these specific materials:

  • Ballistic Nylon or Cordura: These are the same materials used in military gear. They resist punctures and tears far better than standard polyester. Ripstop nylon is a good sign.
  • Reinforced Stitching: Double or triple-stitched seams are critical. Chewers always attack the seams first.
  • Zipper Guards: A zipper is a weak point. Look for beds where the zipper is tucked under a fabric flap or is metal and heavy-duty.

2. The "Calming" Factor

A calming bed works by mimicking a den. This lowers your dog's heart rate and stress levels. You have two main options:

  • Donut/Bolster Beds: The raised rim gives your dog something to rest their head on and creates a sense of security. Good for dogs that like to curl up.
  • Cave/Hooded Beds: These fully enclose your dog. They are excellent for extremely anxious dogs that like to hide. However, they are often less durable because they have more fabric.

For a chewer, a donut bed is usually safer. Cave beds have more seams and edges to attack.

3. Washability

Anxious dogs drool, shed, and sometimes have accidents. If the bed isn't machine washable, it will smell terrible within a week. Look for beds with removable, machine-washable covers. For chewers, a solid, wipeable surface is a massive plus.

4. Size and Weight

You are buying for a small dog. A bed that is too big won't provide the snug, secure feeling they need. Measure your dog from nose to tail base and add 4-6 inches. That's your target bed diameter. Also, consider the weight. A lightweight bed is easy for a dog to drag around and "fluff" before settling, which is good. But it's also easier for them to throw around aggressively.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: My dog chews because of separation anxiety. Will a bed help?

A: A bed addresses the symptom, not the cause. A calming bed provides a safe space, which can reduce anxiety, but you should also work on training and possibly consult a vet for severe cases. Products like a Thundershirt or calming pheromone diffusers can be used in conjunction with the bed.

Q: Can I just give my dog a metal bowl and a towel instead?

A: You could, but it won't offer the joint support or the specific "den-like" security that a bolsters provides. A towel on the floor is cold and flat. It doesn't help a dog feel protected. The raised sides of a bed are crucial for their mental state.

Q: What's the best way to introduce a new bed to a chewer?

A: Don't just toss it on the floor. Put a t-shirt you've worn (unwashed) in the bed. Your scent is calming. Reward your dog with treats for lying in it. Never leave them alone with it for the first few days until you are sure they won't destroy it.

Q: Is an elevated bed really better for chewing?

A: Yes. It's not about the material being stronger (though it often is). It's about physics. A flat bed has no corners to grab and pull. An elevated bed is taught, making it very hard for a dog to get their teeth under the fabric. This is why the K9 Ballistics and Coolaroo work so well.

If your dog is a true, dedicated chewer who has destroyed multiple beds, stop wasting money on plush options. Buy the K9 Ballistics The Chew Proof Elevated Dog Bed.
