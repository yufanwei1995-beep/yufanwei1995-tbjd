What to Look For Before You Buy

Not all solar motion lights are the same. I've installed over a dozen in the last few years, and the specs that matter most are often buried in the fine print. Here's what separates a keeper from a return.

Lumens vs. Actual Brightness

Lumens tell you total light output, but the design of the reflector and the angle of the LEDs matter just as much. A 1000-lumen light with a narrow 90-degree beam will blind you up close but leave the far corner dark. Look for a beam angle of at least 120 degrees for corner coverage. Also, check if the light has multiple LED panels you can angle independently. That's a game-changer for aiming light exactly where you need it.

Battery Size and Type

The battery is the heart of the system. Standard AA NiMH batteries are cheap to replace but hold less charge. Lithium-ion packs (usually 18650 cells) hold more energy and last longer per charge. A 2000mAh battery will power a 500-lumen light for about 8 hours on a full charge. Anything less than 1500mAh in a bright light means you'll get maybe 4-5 hours — not enough for a full summer night. Check if the battery is replaceable. If it's sealed, the whole unit dies when the battery does.

Solar Panel Efficiency

Bigger isn't always better, but a 5.5V/6W panel will charge a battery much faster than a 3W panel, especially on cloudy days. Look for polycrystalline or monocrystalline panels — they're more efficient than amorphous ones. The panel should also be separate from the light head on a cord (at least 10 feet). This lets you put the panel in full sun while the light points into a shady corner. Fixed panels on the light itself are fine for sunny spots only.

Motion Sensor Range and Angle

Most sensors claim 10-30 feet of range. In reality, the angle matters more. A 120-degree sensor will catch someone walking from the side. A 90-degree sensor only catches someone walking directly toward it. For a corner, you want the widest angle possible. Also check the detection zone — some sensors have a dead spot directly below the light. Adjustable heads help you point the sensor down to cover that gap.

IP Rating and Build Quality

IP65 means fully dust-tight and protected from water jets. IP44 means splash-proof but not sealed. For outdoor use, get at least IP65. The housing material matters too — die-cast aluminum dissipates heat and lasts years. Cheap plastic housings crack in UV after two summers. Look for a metal housing with a tempered glass lens, not polycarbonate.

Light Modes

The three main modes are: Motion-only (light turns on when triggered), Dusk-to-dawn (stays on all night at low brightness), and Smart dual-brightness (dim at night, bright when motion is detected). The dual-brightness mode is the most useful — you get constant ambient light plus a bright burst when someone approaches. Some lights also have a timer function to shut off after 30 or 60 seconds. That saves battery.

Installation Ease

You'll need to mount the light at 8-10 feet high for the sensor to work properly. Check if the bracket allows for angle adjustment after mounting. Most come with screws and anchors, but if you're mounting to vinyl siding, you'll need special anchors. A swivel mount is essential for aiming the light downward into a corner.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do solar motion lights last?

Most quality units last 3-5 years. The battery is the first thing to fail. If the battery is replaceable, you can extend the life. The LEDs themselves are rated for 50,000 hours — they'll outlast the battery and the housing.

Do solar lights work in winter?

Yes, but they charge slower. In winter, you get fewer hours of sunlight and the sun is lower in the sky. Expect about 4-5 hours of light instead of 10-12. If you live in a snowy area, clear snow off the panel. Some units struggle to charge when the panel is covered.

How high should I mount the light?

Between 8 and 10 feet. Any higher and the motion sensor's detection zone gets too far from the ground. Any lower and the sensor can't catch approaching people — they'll be in the dead spot below the light.

Can I use these indoors?

Technically yes, but the solar panel needs to be outdoors. Most units have a separate panel on a cord, so you could mount the light inside a garage or shed and run the panel outside. Just know the motion sensor may trigger from small animals or moving objects.

What's the difference between PIR and radar sensors?

PIR (passive infrared) sensors detect heat. They're the standard in solar lights — cheap, reliable, and they don't emit any radiation. Radar sensors detect movement and aren't fooled by heat, but they use more battery. All five lights here use PIR sensors.
