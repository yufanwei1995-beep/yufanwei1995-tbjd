What to Look For in a Budget Carry-On

Before we dive in, you need to know what actually matters when you're spending under $50. Most cheap bags fail in the same three places.

1. Exact Dimensions Matter More Than You Think

International budget airlines are ruthless. Ryanair's limit is 55 x 40 x 20 cm. EasyJet is slightly larger. If your bag is 56 cm, you're checking it at the gate and paying a fee that costs more than the bag itself. Look for a bag that is at least 0.5 inches smaller than the limit in every dimension. Wheels and handles count. I measure everything with the wheels and handle extended.

2. Weight is Your Enemy

Most international airlines have a 7kg (15.4 lbs) carry-on limit. A cheap bag that weighs 7 pounds empty leaves you almost no room for clothes. Look for bags under 5.5 pounds. That's the sweet spot for budget hardside cases.

3. Zippers and Wheels Are the Weak Points

You're not getting YKK zippers at this price. But you can get bags with reinforced corners and basic inline skate wheels that won't shatter on impact. Avoid bags with exposed zippers on the main compartment if you can. Look for a recessed zipper design that's less likely to snag.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I really get a good carry-on for under $50?

Yes, but you have to manage your expectations. You're getting a bag that will last 2-3 years of occasional travel, not a lifetime warranty bag. The Amazon Basics and Travelpro are the only ones I'd trust for more than a handful of flights.

What's the most important spec for international travel?

Dimensions, hands down. If your bag is 1 inch too big, you're paying a gate-check fee that's $60-$100. That's more than the bag costs. Always buy a bag that is smaller than the strictest airline you might fly.

Do I need 4 wheels or 2 wheels?

Four-wheel spinners are better for airports. They roll upright next to you. Two-wheel bags are better if you're walking on rough ground or cobblestones. For international travel, spinners are the way to go. Just make sure the wheels are inset into the shell so they don't add to the height.

Hardside or softside for budget bags?

Hardside protects electronics better and is easier to clean. But cheap hardside shells crack. Softside bags are more forgiving when you overpack. For under $50, I'd go hardside only if you're willing to accept it might get a crack after a few flights. The Amazon Basics is the only hardside that held up well in testing.

What's the weight limit for international carry-ons?

Most Asian and European airlines have a 7kg (15.4 lbs) limit. US airlines usually don't have a weight limit for carry-ons. If you're flying internationally, weigh your bag at home. A bag that weighs 6 pounds empty leaves you only 9 pounds of clothes, which is about 3-4 outfits.

Bottom Line Verdict

If you're flying any airline with strict size limits like Ryanair, EasyJet, or Wizz Air, buy the Amazon Basics 20-Inch Hardside Spinner. It fits the sizer, weighs just over 5 pounds, and costs around $45. The handle is a bit wobbly, but it won't let you down on a weekend trip to Paris or a week in Tokyo. If you're only flying major carriers like British Airways or Delta and you want the lightest bag possible, the Travelpro Maxlite 5 is worth the extra $5. Skip the Rockland—it's too small and poorly built. And don't buy the Coolife unless you're flying EasyJet only and need the TSA lock. For the price of a single checked bag fee, you get a carry-on that saves you money on every future flight.
