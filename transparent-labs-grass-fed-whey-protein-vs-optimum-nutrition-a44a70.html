Buyer's Guide: What to Look For When You're Lactose Intolerant

Not all whey is created equal. Here's the breakdown of what actually matters for your gut.

Whey Concentrate vs. Isolate vs. Hydrolysate

Whey concentrate (WPC) is the cheapest form. It contains 70-80% protein, but the rest is mostly lactose and fat. If you're sensitive, this is your enemy. Whey isolate (WPI) goes through extra processing to strip out lactose and fat, pushing protein up to 90%+. Most isolates have less than 1 gram of lactose per serving. That's usually tolerable. Hydrolysate is pre-digested—broken down into smaller peptides. It's the easiest on the stomach but the priciest.

Rule of thumb: If you're lactose intolerant, buy isolate. Period. Don't let marketing fool you with "ultra-filtered" concentrate claims. Check the label for "isolate" as the first ingredient.

Check the Actual Lactose Content

Most brands don't list lactose grams. You have to do math. A standard serving of isolate has 0.5-1.5 grams of lactose. Concentrate has 3-5 grams. For reference, a glass of milk has about 12 grams. Most people with lactose intolerance can handle up to 2 grams in a single sitting without issues. So, look for products with under 1 gram per scoop.

Added Enzymes

Some brands sneak in lactase—the enzyme you're missing. That's a game-changer. If a product has lactase, even a concentrate might sit fine with you. Both of our main contenders don't add it, but it's a feature worth hunting for.

Artificial Sweeteners and Fillers

Guess what causes bloating more than lactose? Sucralose and maltodextrin. These fake sugars ferment in your gut and cause the exact same symptoms. If you're sensitive, stick to stevia or monk fruit. That's a big reason why Transparent Labs wins for many people.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drink whey if I'm lactose intolerant?

Yes, if you choose an isolate. The extra filtration removes most of the lactose. Stick to products with under 1 gram per scoop, and you'll likely be fine.

Is Transparent Labs worth the extra money?

If you're sensitive, yes. The clean ingredient list (no sucralose, no gums) is just as important as the low lactose. You're paying for peace of mind.

Why does Optimum Nutrition cause bloating if it has isolate?

Because it's a blend. The concentrate portion brings lactose back, and the sucralose can ferment in your gut. It's not pure isolate.

What about hydrolyzed whey?

It's the safest bet. Dymatize ISO100 is hydrolyzed, which means it's pre-digested. Your body doesn't have to work hard to break it down, reducing the chance of stomach upset.

Bottom Line Verdict
