Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Before you buy, understand what matters for photo editing.

Screen Quality

This is the most important spec. You need at least 100% sRGB coverage. 72% NTSC or 100% DCI-P3 is better. Look for OLED or IPS panels. TN panels are garbage for photo work.

Processor

Any modern Intel Core i5, AMD Ryzen 5, or Apple M-series chip works for basic editing. Raw files from 24MP+ cameras need more power. Avoid Celeron, Pentium, or older i3 chips.

RAM

16GB minimum. 8GB works for light edits but chokes on large files. 32GB is overkill for most.

Storage

512GB SSD minimum. You'll fill 256GB fast with Raw files and apps. External drives help, but internal speed matters for Lightroom catalogs.

Color Accuracy

Check reviews for Delta E values. Under 2 is excellent. Under 3 is good. Over 3 means colors shift.

FAQ

Can a $600 laptop really edit photos?

Yes, if you choose carefully. The Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5 at $599 works for 24MP files. Avoid anything with 8GB RAM if you shoot 45MP or larger.

Is the iPad Pro better than a laptop for photo editing?

For pure editing speed and screen quality, yes. The M4 chip and OLED display are unmatched. But the laptop wins on file management, full Photoshop, and price.

Do I need a dedicated GPU for photo editing?

Not for Lightroom or basic Photoshop. GPU helps with AI features and heavy filters but isn't required. Integrated graphics in Ryzen 5 or Intel Core i5 are fine.

Can I use an iPad Pro as my only editing device?

Yes, if you work in Lightroom Mobile or Affinity Photo. You'll struggle with complex Photoshop files. External storage is mandatory for Raw files.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the ASUS Vivobook S 14 OLED if you want a laptop that handles photo editing without compromise at $749. The OLED screen, 16GB RAM, and fast Ryzen 5 processor make it the best value.
