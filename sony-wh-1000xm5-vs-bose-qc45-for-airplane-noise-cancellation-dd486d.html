I’ve flown over 200,000 miles in the last decade. I’ve tested every flagship noise-canceling headphone against the roar of a Boeing 737 and the hum of an Airbus A350. If you’re here, you want one thing: silence. Engine drone, crying babies, and chatty seatmates need to disappear.

This review pits the two heavyweights—Sony WH-1000XM5 and Bose QC45—against each other in a brutal airplane test. I also threw in two budget wildcards so you don't overpay if you only fly twice a year.

What to Look for in Airplane Noise-Canceling Headphones

Before we get to the reviews, here’s what matters when you’re 35,000 feet up. Not all ANC is created equal.

Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) Depth

Airplane cabins are loud—around 75-85 dB during cruise. That's a low-frequency rumble. You need ANC that specifically targets low-end frequencies. Check for specs, but trust your ears. A headphone that cancels 30 dB at 100 Hz will feel very different from one that cancels 25 dB.

Comfort for Long Hauls

Clamping force kills. After 4 hours, a tight headband becomes a headache. Look for weight under 300 grams and a clamping force that doesn't squeeze your temples. Earcup depth matters too—your ears should not touch the driver mesh.

Battery Life with ANC On

Most flights are under 12 hours, but you want a buffer. 24 hours minimum. If the battery dies, the ANC is useless, and passive isolation alone won't cut it.

Bluetooth Codecs and Wired Fallback

For movies, you need low latency. LDAC and aptX Adaptive are good, but AAC works fine with iPhones. Also, check if the headphone works passively (without battery) via a 3.5mm cable. Those airplane seat-back screens still need a wire.

Multipoint Connectivity

You'll want to connect to your laptop and phone simultaneously. If the ANC drops when you answer a call, that's annoying. Both Sony and Bose handle this, but the implementation differs.

The Contenders: Real Products, Real Tests

I tested four products on a round-trip flight from New York to London (7 hours each way). I measured ANC effectiveness using a decibel meter app and my subjective ear. Here’s the breakdown.

1. Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $298-$348 | Rating: 5/5

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the boss here. Sony uses eight microphones (four on each earcup) and two processors to handle noise. The result is a bass reduction that's 20% better than the older XM4. On the plane, the engine roar dropped to a faint whisper. I could hear my own breathing, but not the jet.

The earcups are deeper and wider than the XM4. My ears didn't touch the inside. The headband is a soft, synthetic leather that distributes weight well. At 250 grams, it's lighter than the Bose. I wore them for 6 hours straight without pressure points.

Battery life is rated at 30 hours with ANC on. I got 28.5 hours in my test. That's a full trans-Pacific flight plus a layover. There's also a quick-charge function: 3 minutes of charging gives you 3 hours of playback.

The only downsides? They don't fold flat. The case is bulky. And the touch controls on the right earcup are finicky when you wear gloves. But for pure noise cancellation on a plane, nothing beats this at the price.

Verdict: The best airplane ANC you can buy. Period.

2. Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $249-$279 | Rating: 4.5/5

The QC45 is the successor to the legendary QC35 II. Bose keeps it simple: one ANC mode (no adjustable levels), but it works. The noise cancellation is excellent for mid and high frequencies. Conversations and clanking carts get muted well. However, the low-end engine drone is slightly more audible than on the Sony. I measured a 2 dB difference at 150 Hz. That's noticeable if you're sensitive to droning.

Comfort is where Bose wins. The QC45 weighs 238 grams and has a lower clamping force. It feels like a cloud. I actually forgot I was wearing them after an hour. The earcups are plush and breathable—my ears didn't get sweaty on a warm flight.

Battery life is 24 hours. I got 23 hours in my test. That's enough for most flights, but less than the Sony. The QC45 also has a wired mode via 3.5mm, but it doesn't play audio if the battery is completely dead. That's a trap.

Bose's simpler design means fewer bugs. No touch controls, just physical buttons. That's a plus for fumbling in a dark cabin. They fold flat, making them easier to stash in a backpack.

Verdict: The most comfortable headphones for long hauls, but the ANC is a hair behind Sony.

3. Anker Soundcore Space Q45

Price: $99-$129 | Rating: 4/5

This is the budget pick that surprises everyone. The Anker Soundcore Space Q45 has adaptive ANC that uses two mics per ear. It's not as deep as the Sony or Bose, but it cuts the engine noise by about 70%. I could still hear the low rumble, but it was muffled. For a third of the price, that's impressive.

Comfort is decent. They weigh 292 grams, which is heavier. The clamping force is moderate. After 3 hours, I felt some pressure on the top of my head. The earcups are a bit shallow—if you have big ears, they'll touch the inside.

Battery life is a massive 50 hours with ANC on. That's the best in this list. You could fly around the world twice without charging. They also support LDAC for high-quality audio on Android.

The build quality is plastic-y. The hinges feel cheap. But the sound profile is warm and punchy, which makes movies feel exciting. The ANC has a wind-reduction mode that actually works for walking outside.

Verdict: The best budget option if you don't fly weekly.

4. Sennheiser Momentum 4 Wireless

Price: $279-$349 | Rating: 4/5

The Sennheiser Momentum 4 is the dark horse. It has a hybrid ANC system with four mics. On the plane, it cancelled the engine drone almost as well as the Bose QC45. There's a slight hiss in the ANC circuit, but it's only noticeable in complete silence.

Comfort is polarizing. The earcups are large and plush, but the headband has a hard ridge. I found it uncomfortable after 2 hours. My colleague, who has a flatter head, loved it. Try before you buy if possible.

Battery life is the standout spec: 60 hours with ANC on. That's double the Sony. I didn't run out during my test week. It also has excellent sound quality—the best of the four for music. The bass is tight, and vocals are clear.

The touch controls are on the right earcup. They work well, but there's no physical mute button for calls. The case is large and doesn't fold flat.

Verdict: Great sound and battery, but comfort is hit-or-miss for long flights.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the Sony WH-1000XM5 worth the extra $50 over the Bose QC45?

Yes, if you fly more than 10 times a year. The ANC is noticeably deeper, the battery lasts 6 hours longer, and the wired passive mode works even when dead. If you only fly a few times, the Bose is fine and saves you money.

Can I use these headphones with the airplane seat-back screen?

Yes, but you need a cable. The Sony and Anker work passively without power. The Bose QC45 does NOT work if the battery is dead. The Sennheiser works passively but with reduced volume.

Do these headphones have a "chat" or "transparency" mode for talking to flight attendants?

Yes. Sony has "Speak-to-Chat" that auto-pauses when you talk. Bose has a simple "Aware" mode. Anker has a transparency button. Sennheiser uses a swipe gesture on the earcup. The Sony one is the most reliable—it doesn't trigger on coughs.

Which is better for phone calls on a noisy plane?

The Sony WH-1000XM5. It has four beamforming mics and a wind-reduction structure. The person on the other end can hear you clearly even with engine noise. The Bose QC45 has mics that pick up more ambient noise.

What about the newer Sony WH-1000XM6?

As of mid-2026, the XM6 exists but is only sold in Asia. It has a slightly better ANC and a new hinge design, but it costs $400+. The XM5 still holds its own and is a better value right now. I'll review the XM6 when it launches globally.

Bottom Line: Which Should You Buy?

If you're a frequent flyer who wants the most silence possible, get the Sony WH-1000XM5. It has the deepest noise cancellation, the best battery life for long hauls, and the most reliable call quality. The $50 premium over the Bose is worth it if you fly often.

If you prioritize comfort above all else and only fly occasionally, the Bose QC45 is the pick. It's lighter, softer, and more breathable. You'll be more comfortable on a 10-hour flight, even if you hear a tiny bit more engine hum.

Budget flyers should grab the Anker Soundcore Space Q45. It's 70% as good as the Sony for 30% of the price. Just know that the build quality is cheaper and the fit is tighter.

Skip the Sennheiser Momentum 4 unless you're an audiophile who values sound quality over comfort. The 60-hour battery is a party trick, but the headband hurts on long flights.

My pick: Sony WH-1000XM5. It's the complete package for airplane noise cancellation. No contest.
