Quick Summary

  • Who this is for: Home cooks who want one appliance that pressure cooks AND air fries without taking up two counter spots.
  • Our top pick: Instant Pot Duo Crisp 8qt — better pressure cooking, more even crisping, and a built-in air fryer lid that doesn't cost extra.
  • Price range: $129-$199 for the Duo Crisp, $99-$149 for the Ninja Foodi lid (plus you need a compatible Ninja base).

You want crispy wings and tender pot roast without owning three appliances. I get it. I've spent the last month cooking identical meals side-by-side in the Instant Pot Duo Crisp and the Ninja Foodi Air Fryer Lid. Here's the honest breakdown so you don't waste $200 on the wrong one.

What to Look For Before Buying

Before you click "add to cart," understand these four things. They'll make or break your experience.

1. Does It Actually Pressure Cook AND Air Fry Well?

Some combo units nail one function and phone in the other. The Ninja Foodi lid only works on specific Ninja pressure cooker bases. The Duo Crisp has its own dedicated base and lid. Test both functions — don't assume they're equal.

2. Air Fryer Lid vs Built-In Air Fryer

The Ninja Foodi lid is a separate attachment that replaces your standard lid. It's cheaper if you already own a Ninja base. The Duo Crisp has a built-in air fryer lid with a heating element and fan permanently attached. No swapping parts mid-cook.

3. Capacity and Footprint

An 8-quart Duo Crisp takes up serious counter space (about 14" x 14"). The Ninja lid adds height but no width if you have the base. Think about where this lives before you buy.

4. Ease of Cleaning

Air fryer lids get greasy. The Duo Crisp's lid has a removable inner ring and the basket is dishwasher safe. The Ninja Foodi lid has a non-stick surface that scratches easily if you're rough with it. Neither is "self-cleaning," so budget 10 minutes per use for wiping down.

Instant Pot Duo Crisp 8qt (Model: 140-3011-01)

Price: $149-$199 | Rating: 5/5 | Best for: Most families

The Duo Crisp is a full 11-in-1 pressure cooker with a detachable air fryer lid. I cooked a 5-pound chicken in 35 minutes flat — pressure cook for 25, air crisp for 10. The skin came out shatteringly crisp, and the meat was fall-off-the-bone tender.

The air fryer basket is 6 quarts, which handles a full batch of fries for four people. The fan is powerful — I got even browning on frozen mozzarella sticks without rotating them mid-cook. That's rare in this category.

Tradeoff: It's bulky. The 8qt model weighs 23 pounds and you'll need dedicated shelf space. The lid doesn't store on the base when you're pressure cooking — you have to rest it somewhere. And the yogurt function is finicky; the temperature swings by 2-3 degrees.

Bottom line: If you want one appliance that does everything well, this is it. The pressure cooking is faster than my stovetop, and the air frying beats my standalone toaster oven.

Ninja Foodi Air Fryer Lid (Model: 6-in-1 for 6.5qt & 8qt)

Price: $99-$149 | Rating: 3.5/5 | Best for: Ninja base owners, budget buyers

This is a smart idea: take your existing Ninja pressure cooker and turn it into an air fryer by swapping the lid. The lid has a heating element, fan, and a crisper basket that drops into your existing pot. It's lightweight (4.5 pounds) and stores in a drawer.

In testing, the Ninja lid browned chicken thighs nicely, but the fan is weaker than the Duo Crisp's. I had to shake the basket every 7 minutes for even crisping on tater tots. The "Max Crisp" setting helps, but it runs hot — I burned one batch of fries at 400°F for 12 minutes.

Tradeoff: The lid only works on Ninja bases (not Instant Pot or off-brands). If you have a Ninja Foodi pressure cooker already, this is a cheap upgrade. If you're starting from scratch, you're paying for a base AND a lid — same cost as the Duo Crisp, but with worse performance.

Bottom line: A good value for existing Ninja owners. A poor choice for new buyers because you'll end up with a less capable system for the same money.

Instant Pot Duo Crisp 6.5qt (Model: 140-3010-01)

Price: $129-$169 | Rating: 4/5 | Best for: Couples, small kitchens

Same features as the 8qt but smaller. The 6.5qt air fryer basket holds a 4-pound chicken or a single layer of wings for two. It's 4 pounds lighter and 2 inches narrower, which makes a real difference on cramped countertops.

I found the 6.5qt heats up faster than the 8qt — about 3 minutes quicker to pressure. But you lose the ability to cook a large roast or meal-prep batches. If you're cooking for more than two people regularly, skip this size.

Tradeoff: The smaller basket means you'll cook in batches for anything more than a snack. The air fryer lid is the same design as the 8qt, but the smaller pot means the fan hits a higher percentage of the food surface. Slightly better crisping, actually.

Bottom line: The sweet spot for singles, couples, or students. Same quality as the 8qt, just less capacity.

Ninja Foodi 6-in-1 6.5qt with Air Fryer Lid (Model: OP301)

Price: $179-$229 | Rating: 4/5 | Best for: People who want a sealed system

This is the full Ninja kit — base, pressure lid, and air fryer lid all in one box. It's the direct competitor to the Duo Crisp. I tested it side-by-side, and here's the deal: the Ninja pressure cooks slightly faster (about 2 minutes on a 5-pound roast), but the Duo Crisp air fries better.

The Ninja's "TenderCrisp" feature is genuinely cool — you pressure cook, then air fry without opening the pot. I made a whole chicken in 30 minutes and the skin was crispy while the breast stayed juicy. The Duo Crisp can't do that; you have to manually switch lids.

Tradeoff: The Ninja's air fryer lid has a fixed heat element that doesn't reach the edges of the pot. Food near the walls gets less crisp. You'll need to shake or flip more often. Also, the non-stick coating on the inner pot is delicate — I already see micro-scratches after a month of use.

Bottom line: If you value the one-pot "crisp without opening" feature, this is the better system. But the Duo Crisp is more forgiving and easier to clean.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the Ninja Foodi lid on an Instant Pot?

No. The Ninja lid's locking mechanism is proprietary and only fits Ninja bases. Don't try to force it — you'll damage both pieces.

Is the air fryer lid worth it over a standalone air fryer?

If you already own a pressure cooker, yes. You save counter space and money. If you're buying new, get a combo unit like the Duo Crisp — it's the same price as buying a separate pressure cooker and air fryer.

How long does it take to air fry frozen fries in these?

In the Duo Crisp, 14-16 minutes at 400°F with a shake at the halfway point. In the Ninja lid, 16-18 minutes with two shakes. The Duo Crisp's stronger fan makes the difference.

Do these replace a microwave?

No. They're slower for reheating. Keep your microwave for leftovers and use these for real cooking.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Instant Pot Duo Crisp 8qt if you want the best all-around performance. It pressure cooks faster, air fries more evenly, and costs less than the Ninja full kit. The Ninja Foodi lid only makes sense if you already own a compatible Ninja base — in that case, it's a cheap upgrade. The Duo Crisp 6.5qt is the pick for smaller households. Skip the Ninja 6-in-1 full kit unless the "TenderCrisp" one-pot feature is a must-have; the Duo Crisp beats it on crisping quality and durability for less money.
