What to Look For (Buyer's Guide)

Not all ashwagandha is the same. Here's what actually matters when comparing products.

1. Standardized Extract vs. Raw Powder

Raw powder is cheap but inconsistent. You want a standardized extract with guaranteed levels of withanolides (the active compounds). Look for 5% withanolides or higher. KSM-66 and Sensoril are the two most clinically studied extracts — both are good, but they work slightly differently.

2. Piperine Content

Black pepper extract (piperine) is what makes the ashwagandha absorb better. But you need the right amount — 5–10 mg of piperine per serving is the sweet spot. Too little does nothing. Too much can cause stomach upset. BioPerine is the patented, most reliable form.

3. Root vs. Leaf Extract

KSM-66 is made from root only — good for stress and sleep. Sensoril is made from root and leaf — slightly more sedating, better for sleep specifically. Both work. Just know the difference.

4. Third-Party Testing

Supplements aren't tightly regulated. Look for brands that use third-party labs (USP, NSF, or at least a "tested by" seal on the label). Avoid proprietary blends that don't list exact amounts.

5. Form: Capsule, Gummy, or Powder

Capsules are easiest to dose consistently. Powders are cheaper but taste bitter — and you have to measure them yourself. Gummies are convenient but usually have less extract and more sugar. Stick with capsules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does black pepper actually make ashwagandha work better?

Yes. Piperine (the active compound in black pepper) inhibits certain liver enzymes that break down ashwagandha's withanolides. That means more of the active compounds reach your bloodstream. Studies show absorption can increase by up to 2000%. Just make sure the product uses BioPerine or a standardized 5 mg dose — random black pepper powder isn't as reliable.

How long until I feel the effects?

Most people feel a difference in 3–7 days. But cortisol levels take time to rebalance — full effects for sleep often take 2–4 weeks. Be consistent. Take it at the same time daily.

Morning or night?

For sleep and anxiety, take it with your evening meal. The piperine needs fat for absorption, and taking it at night supports the body's natural cortisol wind-down. If you also want daytime calm, split the dose — morning and night.

Can I take it with other supplements?

Yes, but avoid taking it with sedatives, thyroid medication, or blood sugar meds without asking your doctor. Ashwagandha can lower blood sugar and thyroid hormone levels. Also, don't take it with other herbs that cause drowsiness (like valerian or melatonin) unless you want to be knocked out.

What's the difference between KSM-66 and Sensoril?

KSM-66 is root-only, higher dose (600 mg), more energizing, and better for daytime stress. Sensoril is root + leaf, lower dose (125 mg), more sedating, and better for sleep. Both are clinically studied. Pick based on your main goal.
