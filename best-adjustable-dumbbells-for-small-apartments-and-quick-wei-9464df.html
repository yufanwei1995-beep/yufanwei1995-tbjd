What to Look For in Adjustable Dumbbells

Before we dive into specific models, you need to know what separates a good adjustable dumbbell from a frustrating paperweight. Here are the four things that matter most in a small apartment.

1. Adjustment Speed

This is the whole point. If it takes you 30 seconds to change weights, you'll skip dropsets and waste half your workout. The best systems change weight in under 5 seconds. The worst require you to unscrew collars and fiddle with plates—avoid those entirely.

2. Footprint and Storage

Measure your space before you buy. A 50 lb pair of dumbbells takes up about 24 inches of shelf space. If you're storing them under a couch or in a closet, check the length and height when seated in the cradle. Some models like the Nuobell stand upright, which helps.

3. Weight Range and Increments

Most apartment users are fine with 5 to 50 lbs per dumbbell. But check the increments. The Bowflex jumps 5 lbs at a time—fine for curls, annoying for lateral raises. The PowerBlock goes in 2.5 lb increments, which is much better for progressive overload on smaller muscle groups.

4. Noise and Durability

Your downstairs neighbors will hear metal clanking. Plate-based systems like PowerBlock are quieter than selector-style dumbbells. Also, look for metal gears and cradles. Cheap plastic adjustment mechanisms break within a year. Read the warranty—most good brands offer at least 2 years.

Our Top 5 Picks for Small Apartments

1. Bowflex SelectTech 552

The most famous adjustable dumbbell in the world, and for good reason. Each dumbbell adjusts from 5 to 52.5 lbs in 2.5 lb increments. The twist dial mechanism is simple: rotate the dial on the end, set it to your weight, and lift. It takes about 3 seconds per dumbbell.

The good: The weight range covers 90% of what most people need. The cradle doubles as a storage tray, so it fits neatly on a shelf. The adjustment mechanism is bulletproof—I've had mine for 4 years with zero issues. At 17.5 inches long, they fit in most spaces.

The bad: They're long. At 17.5 inches, they feel awkward for overhead presses if you have narrow shoulders. The 5 lb increments are too big for tiny deltoid work. And the plastic dial feels cheap, even though it holds up.

The verdict: If you want a set-and-forget solution that just works, get the Bowflex. It's the safest choice for beginners and intermediates.

2. PowerBlock Elite EXP

These are the opposite of the Bowflex. Instead of a dial, you pull a magnetic pin and slide the weight selector. Each dumbbell goes from 5 to 50 lbs in 2.5 lb increments. The weight changes are nearly instant—under 2 seconds.

The good: The 2.5 lb increments are a game-changer for isolation exercises. The compact design is a perfect cube (about 8x8x8 inches), so they store incredibly well. They're also the quietest option we tested—no metal clanking, just a soft click.

The bad: The shape takes getting used to. The square design means the center of gravity is different than a traditional dumbbell. Some exercises, like renegade rows, are awkward because the handles are shorter. And expansion kits cost extra if you want to go beyond 50 lbs.

The verdict: Get the PowerBlock if you care about precise weight increments and have limited shelf space. The learning curve is worth it.

3. Nuobell 80

The premium option. Each dumbbell adjusts from 5 to 80 lbs in 5 lb increments via a twist dial. The entire dumbbell expands and contracts as you change weight—no cradle required. This is the most space-efficient design on the market.

The good: When fully contracted, they're only 13 inches long. That's 4.5 inches shorter than the Bowflex. The weight changes are fast and satisfying—about 3 seconds. The metal build quality is excellent; these feel like professional gym equipment, not home workout toys.

The bad: The 5 lb increments limit your progression on small muscles. At 80 lbs each, they're pricey—expect to pay over $600. And if you drop them, the internal mechanism can jam. You must be disciplined about setting them down gently.

The verdict: The Nuobell is the best-looking and most compact option. If budget isn't a concern and you want to future-proof your setup, this is it.

4. Ativafit Quick Adjustable Dumbbell

The budget pick. This is a clone of the Bowflex design, but at roughly half the price. Each dumbbell goes from 5 to 50 lbs in 5 lb increments. The twist dial works the same way, and the cradle doubles as storage.

The good: For $200, you get 90% of the Bowflex functionality. The weight range is solid for most home users. The build quality is decent—the plastic dial feels a bit flimsy, but it works.

The bad: The 5 lb increments are the same issue as the Bowflex. The weight plates rattle slightly when you lift. And the warranty is only 1 year, which tells you something about durability expectations. I've seen reports of dials cracking after 18 months of heavy use.

The verdict: If you're on a tight budget and need a functional pair of adjustable dumbbells, the Ativafit works. Just know you're trading long-term durability for upfront savings.

5. Yes4All Adjustable Dumbbells with Cradle

This is a traditional plate-loaded system, but with a twist. The dumbbells come with a cradle that holds the plates, so you slide plates on and off a threaded bar. Each dumbbell goes from 5 to 50 lbs in 5 lb increments.

The good: It's the most durable option on this list—there's nothing to break. The price is unbeatable, often under $150. And because plates are separate, you can buy additional plates to increase the weight later.

The bad: Adjustment speed is terrible. You have to unscrew the collar, slide plates off, slide new ones on, and re-tighten. This takes 30 to 60 seconds per dumbbell. If you're doing circuit training, this is a dealbreaker.

The verdict: Only consider this if you never change weights mid-workout. It's a good starter set, but it fails the core requirement of this review: quick weight changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are adjustable dumbbells safe for apartments?

Yes, but be smart about it. Place a rubber mat underneath to protect floors and muffle noise. Avoid dropping them—the internal mechanisms in dial-based systems can jam. The PowerBlock is the safest for apartments because it has no exposed plates that can clang together.

How much space do I really need?

You need about 2 feet of shelf space for the dumbbells and cradle. Add another 6 feet of floor space for exercises like rows and presses. A 4x6 foot corner of a room is enough for most workouts.

Can I do every exercise with these?

No. You can't do heavy deadlifts or barbell squats with 50 lb dumbbells. But you can do all upper body work, lunges, goblet squats, and Romanian deadlifts. For most apartment users, that's enough.

What about warranty and support?

Bowflex offers a 2-year warranty. PowerBlock offers 10 years on the frame. Nuobell offers 2 years. Ativafit offers 1 year. If longevity matters, PowerBlock is the clear winner.

Bottom Line

For most apartment dwellers, the Bowflex SelectTech 552 is the best choice. It balances speed, weight range, and price better than any other option. If you have a bigger budget and want something more compact, get the Nuobell 80. If precise increments matter more than anything, go with the PowerBlock Elite EXP. Skip the Ativafit unless you're desperate to save money, and skip the Yes4All unless you never change weights mid-session.
