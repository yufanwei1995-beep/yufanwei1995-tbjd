Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Call Quality

Before you spend money, understand that "good call quality" is not about how you hear the other person. It's about how they hear you. Here are the three things we test that matter most:

1. Microphone Array and Beamforming

Every modern earbud has multiple mics. The trick is how the chip processes the sound. Beamforming focuses on your mouth and rejects sounds from the sides. If you type on a mechanical keyboard, you need aggressive beamforming. If you sit near an AC vent, you need a mic that doesn't clip on loud noises.

2. Wind and Background Noise Reduction

Offices have HVAC systems that rumble, and conference rooms have fans. The best earbuds use a bone-conduction sensor or an extra mesh covering on the mic to physically block wind. Software can't fix a hardware flaw. We test with a desk fan at 3 feet and a white noise machine at 70dB.

3. Voice Tone vs. Voice Clarity

Some earbuds make you sound "crisp" but robotic. Others sound warm but get lost in noise. The ideal is a neutral tone with high intelligibility. We record our voices and play them back to a panel of five listeners who rate naturalness and clarity separately.

The Two Contenders: Full Reviews

Apple AirPods Pro 2 (USB-C, 2025 Model)

Price: $209-$249 | Rating: 4.8/5

The AirPods Pro 2 are the gold standard for iPhone users. The H2 chip does heavy lifting, but the real magic is the voice-processing algorithm. In our office test with a loud HVAC and a coworker talking 6 feet away, the caller said my voice was "clear, like I was in a quiet room."

Wind handling is stellar. The mesh on the mic is fine enough to block most gusts. We walked outside with a 15 mph breeze and the caller heard zero wind noise. That's rare.

The downside? Battery life is 6 hours with ANC on, which is okay but not great. And if you're on Android, you lose the seamless pairing and the "Conversation Awareness" feature that automatically lowers volume when you speak. The tone is slightly bright, so if you have a very deep voice, it might sound a bit thin.

Key Spec: 6 hours talk time (ANC on), IP54 rated, bone-conduction mic for voice pickup.

Samsung Galaxy Buds 2 Pro

Price: $179-$219 | Rating: 4.2/5

The Galaxy Buds 2 Pro are excellent for Samsung users. They have a more robust sound profile for music, but for calls, they fall slightly behind Apple. In the same office test, my caller said there was "a slight echo" and that my voice sounded "a bit tinny."

The good news is background noise rejection is aggressive. A keyboard clatter at full speed was almost completely silenced. The bad news is that aggression sometimes cuts off the starts of my words. If you're a fast talker, you'll notice clipped syllables.

The fit is more secure than the AirPods, which is great for walking. But the mic is more sensitive to wind. A 10 mph breeze caused a "whooshing" sound that the caller found annoying. Battery life is similar at 5 hours of talk time.

Key Spec: 5 hours talk time (ANC on), IPX7 water resistance, 3 mics with high SNR.

Sony WF-1000XM5 (The Dark Horse)

Price: $199-$248 | Rating: 4.0/5

You didn't ask about these, but you should consider them. Sony's XM5 has the best ANC on the market and a new "AI noise reduction" for calls. In our tests, it reduced background voices better than both Apple and Samsung. However, the voice tone is a bit muffled — callers said I sounded "like I was in a tunnel" compared to the AirPods.

The mic wind handling is mediocre. It's not bad, but it's not class-leading. If you're in a completely static office with no windows open, the XM5 is a solid choice. If you move around, skip it.

Key Spec: 5.5 hours talk time, 8 mics total, AI-based wind reduction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do AirPods Pro 2 work well with Android for calls?

Yes, but you lose the "Conversation Awareness" and voice isolation features that are baked into iOS. The basic mic works fine, but you won't get the full noise-reduction benefit. If you're on Android, the Galaxy Buds 2 Pro are the better match.

Why does my voice sound robotic on Zoom with either earbud?

That's usually a Bluetooth codec issue. Both earbuds default to AAC, which is fine for music but not for voice. In the Bluetooth settings, try switching to "Call Only" mode or disabling "HD Voice" in your phone's settings. This forces a more stable connection.

Which has better battery for back-to-back meetings?

The AirPods Pro 2 win with 6 hours. But if you're in a marathon of 4-hour calls, all three will need a case recharge. The AirPods case gives you 24 extra hours, the Samsung case gives 18.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the Apple AirPods Pro 2 if you live in the Apple ecosystem and take calls in an office with HVAC noise or windows. The bone-conduction mic and wind mesh are simply better for voice pickup. Buy the Samsung Galaxy Buds 2 Pro if you're on Android and want a more secure fit — just accept the slightly tinny tone and watch out for gusts of wind. If you hate both, the Sony XM5 is a fine third option, but only for static environments. For most people, the AirPods Pro 2 are the clearest, most reliable call companion in 2026.
