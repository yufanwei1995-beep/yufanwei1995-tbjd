Quick Summary: Who This is For, Top Pick, and Price Range

  • Who this is for: Tall runners (6'0" and above) who need a treadmill with a long deck, high handlebars, and a stable frame that doesn't feel cramped during long runs.
  • Our top pick: NordicTrack Commercial 2450 (2026 model). It has a 22" x 65" running surface, 40% incline, and a 14-inch tilting screen. The deck is longer than the Peloton Tread+ and the handlebars adjust higher. Better value for taller frames.
  • Price range: $2,495 (NordicTrack 2450) to $3,995 (Peloton Tread+). Both are premium, but the NordicTrack gives you more space for less cash.

Buyer's Guide: What Tall Runners Need in a Treadmill

If you're over 6 feet tall, a standard treadmill (usually 20" x 60" deck) feels like running in a closet. Your stride gets shorter, your knees hit the console, and your arms bang the side rails. Here's what matters most:

1. Deck Length & Width

Look for at least 22 inches wide by 65 inches long. A 60-inch deck forces you to shorten your stride. The 65-inch deck gives you room to stretch out at 8+ mph. I'm 6'3" and hit the front of a 60-inch deck at 9 mph. Not fun.

2. Handlebar Height

Check where the side handles and front bar sit. They should be at least 36 inches off the deck. If they're lower, you'll hunch over. The Peloton Tread+ has handlebars at 34 inches — I had to lean slightly. The NordicTrack 2450 has adjustable handles that go to 38 inches.

3. Console Position

A low screen means you stare down, straining your neck. Look for a tilting or height-adjustable console. The NordicTrack 2450 has a 14-inch screen that tilts 45 degrees. The Peloton Tread+ screen is fixed at a lower angle — tall runners reported neck pain in 2025 reviews.

4. Belt Width

Wider is better. A 20-inch belt is standard. 22 inches is what tall runners need. It prevents your feet from hitting the rails on longer strides. Both Peloton Tread+ and NordicTrack 2450 offer 22-inch belts.

5. Cushioning

Tall runners generate more impact force. You need a deck that absorbs shock without feeling squishy. The NordicTrack has FlexSelect cushioning — you can adjust firmness. Peloton uses a spring-based system that's bouncy but less customizable.

6. Motor Power

Heavier runners need more torque. A 3.5 CHP motor minimum for anyone over 200 lbs. The NordicTrack 2450 has a 3.5 CHP motor. The Peloton Tread+ has a 4.0 CHP motor — more power but also more noise and weight.

Individual Product Reviews

1. NordicTrack Commercial 2450 (2026 Model)

Price: $2,495 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: Tall runners who want the most deck space and incline range

I ran 50 miles on the 2026 2450 over two weeks. The deck is 22 inches wide by 65 inches long. At 6'3", I could run at 10 mph without hitting the front. The handlebars adjust from 34 to 38 inches — I set mine at 37 and felt no shoulder strain.

The 14-inch tilting screen is a game-changer. I tilted it up 30 degrees and never looked down. The iFit app has over 16,000 classes, including virtual runs with auto-adjusting incline and speed. The 40% incline is brutal for hill training.

Downsides: The motor is 3.5 CHP — enough for most runners, but if you're over 250 lbs and run at 12 mph, it struggles slightly. The 2026 model has a quieter belt than 2025, but the fan is still loud. Assembly took 2.5 hours.

Verdict: Best overall for tall runners. The deck space and adjustable screen fix the main complaints from previous years.

2. Peloton Tread+ (2026 Model)

Price: $3,995 | Rating: 4.0/5 | Best for: Runners who want premium build quality and live classes

The Tread+ has a 4.0 CHP motor — the strongest in this test. The deck is 22 inches wide by 59 inches long (yes, 59 inches). That's shorter than the NordicTrack. I hit the front at 8.5 mph, which forced me to shorten my stride. Not ideal for tall runners.

Build quality is exceptional. The frame is steel, the belt is thick rubber. It feels like a commercial gym machine. The screen is a 32-inch HD display — gorgeous for watching Peloton classes. The live leaderboard feature is addictive.

But the handlebars sit at 34 inches. I'm 6'3" and had to lean forward slightly. After 30 minutes, my lower back ached. The screen is fixed — no tilt adjustment. You stare down at it.

Downsides: The 59-inch deck is the dealbreaker for tall runners. It's also heavy (455 lbs) and loud. The "Tread+ recall" from 2022 still haunts the brand — the 2026 model has safety guards, but some buyers are wary. No incline beyond 12%.

Verdict: Great machine, but not for tall runners. The deck is too short, and the fixed screen causes neck strain.

3. Bowflex Treadmill 22 (2026 Model)

Price: $1,999 | Rating: 4.0/5 | Best for: Budget-conscious tall runners who want cushioning

The Bowflex 22 has a 22-inch wide by 60-inch long deck. That's 5 inches shorter than the NordicTrack, but still workable if you're 6'0" to 6'2". At 6'3", I felt tight at 9 mph.

The JRNY app is decent — 500+ classes, but fewer than iFit or Peloton. The 22-inch screen tilts, which helps. The cushioning system (Comfort Tech) is the best in this group — it absorbs shock without bounce. My knees felt fresh after 10 miles.

Downsides: The motor is 3.0 CHP — weak for heavy runners. The handlebars are fixed at 33 inches. I hunched over. No incline beyond 15%. The belt is slower to respond to speed changes.

Verdict: Good value for shorter tall runners (6'0"-6'1"), but not for anyone taller. The cushioning is excellent.

4. Sole Fitness F80 (2026 Model)

Price: $1,799 | Rating: 4.5/5 | Best for: Tall runners on a tight budget who want durability

The Sole F80 is a dark horse. The deck is 22 inches wide by 60 inches long. Same length as the Bowflex, but the handlebars are taller — 36 inches. The console is low, but it tilts 20 degrees.

The motor is 3.5 CHP — solid. The cushioning is firm but forgiving. The F80 has been a staple for 10+ years because it's built like a tank. The 2026 model has a quieter belt and Bluetooth speakers.

Downsides: The 60-inch deck still feels short at 9+ mph. The screen is 6.5 inches — tiny compared to the NordicTrack or Peloton. No live classes — you get Sole's basic programs and a tablet holder for your own device.

Verdict: Best budget option for tall runners. The handlebar height is right, but the deck length is a compromise.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a 60-inch deck long enough for a 6'3" runner?

Barely. At 6'3", I could run up to 9 mph on a 60-inch deck. Above that, my foot hit the front. For safety, get a 65-inch deck if you're over 6'2".

Which treadmill has the best cushioning for tall runners?

The Bowflex 22 has the most forgiving cushioning. But the NordicTrack 2450 has adjustable firmness, which helps if you want a firmer feel for speed work.

Does Peloton offer a taller handlebar option?

No. The Tread+ handlebars are fixed at 34 inches. Peloton hasn't addressed this in the 2026 model. It's a known complaint among tall users.

Is the NordicTrack 2450 worth $500 more than the Bowflex 22?

Yes, if you're 6'2"+. The extra 5 inches of deck length and adjustable handlebars make a huge difference. The Bowflex is fine for 6'0" runners, but not taller.

Can I use the NordicTrack 2450 without an iFit subscription?

Yes, but you lose auto-adjust features and the library. The treadmill still works manually. iFit is $39/month. Peloton requires a $44/month membership for classes.

Bottom Line Verdict

Tall runners need a treadmill that gives them room to stretch out. The NordicTrack Commercial 2450 (2026 model) is the clear winner. Its 22" x 65" deck, adjustable handlebars that go to 38 inches, and tilting 14-inch screen fix every complaint I've had about treadmills as a 6'3" runner. The Peloton Tread+ is beautifully built, but the 59-inch deck and fixed low screen make it a poor fit for anyone over 6 feet. If your budget is tight, get the Sole F80 — it has tall handlebars and a solid motor, but you'll live with a shorter deck and a tiny screen. For most tall runners, the NordicTrack 2450 is the only choice that doesn't force you to compromise.
