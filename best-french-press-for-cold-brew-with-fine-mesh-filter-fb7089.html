Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters in a Cold Brew French Press

Before you click buy, understand these four things. They make or break the cold brew experience.

1. Mesh Micron Rating (The Non-Negotiable)

Standard presses use a single-layer mesh around 150-200 microns. That's fine for hot coffee, useless for cold brew. Cold steeping extracts more fines (tiny coffee particles) because the water sits for 12-18 hours. You need a mesh at or below 60 microns. The Espro P7 uses a double micro-filter rated at 55 microns. The Frieling uses a triple-layered mesh at 50 microns. Both work. Anything above 100 microns will let sludge through.

2. Plunger Seal Quality

Cold brew doesn't need pressure like hot coffee does, but a cheap plunger seal will still leak grounds around the edges. Look for a silicone seal that hugs the glass wall tightly. The Espro and Frieling both have excellent seals. The Mueller has a decent one but it's not as snug. Test this before you commit to a 14-hour steep.

3. Glass vs. Stainless Steel

Borosilicate glass is fine for cold brew. It's non-reactive and won't hold flavors. But it's fragile. If you're clumsy or plan to travel with your press, get a stainless steel model like the Frieling. It's double-walled, keeps your brew cold for hours, and won't shatter in the sink. The tradeoff is you can't see the brew strength through the metal.

4. Capacity and Pour Spout Design

Cold brew concentrate is strong. You'll dilute it 1:1 or 1:2 with water or milk. A 34oz press yields about 24oz of concentrate, enough for 4-6 drinks. Also check the spout. A sharp, narrow spout gives you controlled pouring. Wide spouts drip down the side of the carafe and make a mess.

FAQ

Can I use any French press for cold brew?

Technically yes, but you'll get gritty coffee. Standard presses have mesh holes around 150-200 microns. Cold brew steeps for 12+ hours, which extracts more fine particles. Those particles pass right through the standard mesh. You need a fine-mesh press (60 microns or less) for smooth cold brew.

How long should I steep cold brew in a French press?

12-18 hours at room temperature, or 18-24 hours in the fridge. Shorter steeps give you a milder brew; longer steeps get more extraction but risk bitterness. I found 14 hours at room temp is the sweet spot for most beans. If you use a coarse grind, you can go longer without bitterness.

Is cold brew concentrate stronger than regular coffee?

Yes. Cold brew concentrate is typically 2-3 times stronger than regular drip coffee. You should dilute it with water, milk, or ice at a 1:1 or 1:2 ratio. If you drink it straight, you'll get a massive caffeine hit and a very intense flavor.

What grind size should I use?

Coarse, like breadcrumbs. If you use a fine grind, the mesh will clog and you'll get a bitter, over-extracted mess. A coarse grind allows water to flow through the grounds evenly during the long steep.
