If you are reading this, you probably spend too much time in transit. You are on a 6 AM train, a red-eye flight, or just trying to block out the open-office noise. You need silence, and you need it now.

This review is for the daily commuter and the frequent flyer. We are comparing the two heavyweight champions of noise-cancelling headphones: the Sony WH-1000XM5 and the Bose QuietComfort 45. We tested both on actual subway cars, in airport terminals, and during a 7-hour flight to see which one actually survives the real world.

Quick Summary

  • Who this is for: Commuters and travelers who prioritize silence, comfort, and portability over audiophile perfection.
  • Our top pick: Sony WH-1000XM5 for its superior noise cancellation and battery life, unless you wear glasses or have a wider head.
  • Price range: $279 - $348 for the Sony; $199 - $249 for the Bose QC45.

Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters for Commuting

Before we get into the head-to-head, let’s talk about the specs that matter when you are on the move. Marketing materials rarely tell you the whole story.

Noise Cancellation (ANC) Depth

Look for the frequency range of the ANC, not just the decibel rating. Good ANC blocks low-frequency rumble (engines, HVAC) easily. Great ANC also tackles mid-frequency chatter (crying babies, office talk). The Sony uses two processors and eight microphones. The Bose uses a simpler system. On paper, Sony wins. In practice, the difference is noticeable but not night-and-day for most users.

Comfort and Clamp Force

You will wear these for 4+ hours. Pay attention to clamp force. A tighter clamp gives better passive isolation but causes "hot spots" on your jaw after an hour. The Bose QC45 is famously light at 238 grams (8.4 oz). The Sony is heavier at 250 grams (8.8 oz). That 12-gram difference is nothing. The ear pad depth is everything. If you wear glasses, the Bose's deeper earcups are more forgiving.

Battery Life and Charging

Look for USB-C and quick charge. The Sony offers 30 hours with ANC on. The Bose offers 24 hours. For a week of commuting, both are fine. For a long-haul flight followed by a week of travel, the Sony's extra 6 hours matter. Also check if the headphones have a wired mode. Both do, but the Sony requires power to use the "active" wired mode fully.

Multipoint Connectivity

You need to connect to your laptop and phone simultaneously. Both support multipoint. The Sony implementation is smarter—it detects which device is playing audio and switches automatically. The Bose is more manual; you often have to pause one device before playing on the other. This is a minor annoyance, but it adds up.

Call Quality

Commuting involves calls. The Bose QC45 uses a single microphone array that is passable but struggles with wind noise. The Sony uses a beamforming mic system with a bone-conduction sensor. In our tests, the Sony made us sound clearer in noisy streets, but the Bose sounded more natural in quiet rooms.

Product Reviews

1. Sony WH-1000XM5

The XM5 is the current king of the hill. Sony redesigned the headband in 2022, moving away from the folding hinge of the XM4. This makes the XM5 feel more premium but less portable. You cannot fold them flat. They take up more space in a backpack than the Bose.

The Good: The ANC is the best in the industry. On a plane, the cabin rumble disappears almost entirely. You hear voices at a whisper level, but they are unintelligible. The sound quality is rich and detailed, though it leans heavy on the bass. The 30-hour battery life is accurate in real-world testing. The touch controls are intuitive: swipe to skip, tap to pause, cup your hand over the earcup to quickly hear announcements.

The Bad: The lack of a folding hinge is a dealbreaker for some travelers. The earcups are shallower than the Bose. If you have protruding ears or wear thick glasses, they will hurt after 2 hours. The microphone picks up wind noise easily, making outdoor calls difficult.

Verdict on XM5: Buy these if you want the quietest possible ride and don't mind the awkward case. They are the better all-rounder for flights.

2. Bose QuietComfort 45

The QC45 is the sequel to the legendary QC35 II. For years, Bose was the gold standard for comfort. The QC45 continues that legacy. They are lighter, less clampy, and the earcups are roomier.

The Good: The comfort is unmatched. You can wear these for a 10-hour flight and forget they are on your head. The ANC is very good—it handles constant droning noise well. It just lets through a bit more human speech and sudden noises than the Sony. The foldable design is a godsend for backpack space. The physical buttons are easier to use than touch controls when wearing gloves.

The Bad: The sound signature is neutral and flat. It lacks the punch and bass of the Sony. If you listen to hip-hop or electronic music, the Bose will sound boring. The battery life is only 24 hours. The USB-C port charges slowly; a 15-minute quick charge only gives 3 hours of playtime, whereas Sony gives 5 hours in the same time.

Verdict on QC45: Buy these if comfort is your priority and you wear glasses or have a wider head. They are the better choice for all-day wear, even if the ANC is slightly less powerful.

3. Sony WH-1000XM4 (The Budget Alternative)

Let's talk about the previous generation. The XM4 is still in stores and often costs $100 less than the XM5. It folds flat, which is a huge plus for travel. The ANC is 90% of the XM5's performance. The sound is warmer and arguably better for music than the XM5's neutral tuning.

The Good: Folding design, great ANC, wireless charging support (a feature the XM5 lacks). It has the same battery life as the XM5 (30 hours). It is also more durable—the hinge is metal, whereas the XM5's "silent" slider mechanism feels fragile.

The Bad: The call quality is worse than the XM5. It picks up background noise and makes you sound robotic. The touch controls are less responsive.

Verdict on XM4: This is the smart buy if you are on a budget. You lose a little ANC performance, but you gain a foldable design.

4. Bose QuietComfort Ultra (The Premium Alternative)

If you want the Bose comfort but need better ANC than the QC45, the Ultra is the step up. It costs around $429, which is steep. It adds spatial audio and "immersive" sound modes. For commuting, those features are gimmicks—they drain the battery faster (down to 24 hours from 28).

The Good: The ANC is better than the QC45, matching the Sony XM5. The build quality feels more premium with a metal headband. The sound quality is more dynamic than the QC45.

The Bad: The price. It is $100 more than the Sony XM5 and offers no tangible benefit for flights. The spatial audio requires a compatible device and eats battery.

Verdict on Ultra: Only buy this if you want Bose comfort with Sony-level silence and you have the cash to burn.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for flying, Sony XM5 or Bose QC45?

The Sony XM5. It blocks the low-frequency drone of jet engines more effectively, and the 30-hour battery covers ultra-long-haul routes without charging. The only downside is the bulky case—it won't fit in a slim backpack side pocket.

Can I wear these with glasses?

The Bose QC45 is significantly better. The earcups are deeper and the clamp force is lower. The Sony XM5 presses the arms of glasses into your skull, causing pain after 1-2 hours. If you wear thick frames, skip the Sony.

Is the ANC on the Bose QC45 good enough for a noisy office?

Yes, for constant noise like AC units and server fans. It struggles with sudden spikes like a coworker yelling or a dog barking. The Sony XM5 handles those sudden noises slightly better, but neither is perfect.

Are these good for phone calls?

Barely. Both are mediocre for calls in loud environments. The Sony XM5 uses a bone-conduction sensor to pick up your voice, which helps in windy conditions. The Bose is clearer in quiet rooms but lets in more background noise on the street. For calls, consider a dedicated headset or use the in-line mic on the cable.

Should I wait for the Sony XM6?

If you are patient, the XM6 is rumored to launch in late 2026. The XM5 has been out since 2022, so a refresh is due. But if you need headphones now, the XM5 is still the best on the market. Waiting for tech is a losing game—there is always something new in 6 months.

Bottom Line Verdict

Here is the short answer. If you are a frequent flyer who packs light and wants the absolute quietest cabin experience, buy the Sony WH-1000XM5. The ANC is better, the battery lasts longer, and the sound quality is more engaging. Just be prepared to carry the bulky case. If you wear glasses, have a wide head, or prioritize all-day comfort over ultimate silence, buy the Bose QC45. It is cheaper, folds flat, and you will forget it is on your head. The Bose is the better commuter companion for most people; the Sony is the better flight tool. Choose based on which discomfort you hate more: neck strain from heavy glasses or a bulky case in your bag.
