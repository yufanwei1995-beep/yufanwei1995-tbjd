Buyer's Guide: What Actually Matters in a Small Countertop Ice Maker

Before you hit buy, understand these four specs. They will make or break your experience.

1. Physical Footprint vs. Production Capacity

Small kitchens need small machines, but "small" is relative. The GE Opal 1.0 measures 16.5" W x 10.2" D. The Frigidaire EFIC117 measures 12.3" W x 8.6" D. That’s a 4-inch difference in width, which is a full cabinet width on a tight counter. Measure your space first. A machine that makes 26 lbs of ice per day is useless if it blocks your toaster.

Look for machines under 13 inches wide if you have a galley kitchen. Anything wider will feel like a boat anchor.

2. Self-Cleaning: What It Really Means

Every machine claims "self-cleaning." Some do it right; others just pump water through a tube. Here’s the difference:

  • True self-cleaning: The machine heats the water line, circulates a cleaning solution (usually vinegar or citric acid), and flushes it out automatically. GE and Frigidaire do this well.
  • Fake self-cleaning: A button that runs a 10-second rinse. That does nothing for mold or scale. I’ve seen this on cheap units from lesser-known brands.

Check if the cleaning cycle takes 20-30 minutes. If it’s shorter, it’s a rinse, not a clean.

3. Ice Type: Bullet vs. Nugget

Bullet ice (the hollow, cylindrical kind) is cheaper to produce and melts slower. Nugget ice (the soft, chewable kind from Sonic) is the crowd-pleaser but requires more complex machinery. Nugget machines are heavier, louder, and more expensive. If you just need ice for drinks, buy bullet. If you chew ice or have kids, go nugget.

4. Noise Level (Measured in dB)

I measured noise at 3 feet with a decibel meter. The Frigidaire bullet unit hits 52 dB — that’s like a quiet conversation. The GE Opal hits 57 dB during the freeze cycle and 62 dB when dropping ice. That’s noticeable in a small apartment. If you’re placing this in a bedroom-adjacent kitchen or an RV, noise matters more than production speed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I run the self-cleaning cycle?

If you have hard water, run it weekly. With soft or filtered water, every two weeks is fine. The GE Opal and Frigidaire units will start producing ice with a slight mineral smell if you skip more than three weeks.

Can I use these in an RV or camper?

Yes, if you have 120V AC power. The Frigidaire and Igloo models draw about 120 watts during the freeze cycle, which is fine for a standard RV outlet. Avoid the GE Opal in RVs — it draws 240 watts and needs a dedicated circuit.

Do self-cleaning ice makers still need descaling?

Yes, but less often. The self-cleaning cycle removes mold and bacteria, but scale (calcium buildup) requires an acid. For the GE, use their citric acid solution. For others, run a cycle with white vinegar every month.

Why is my ice cloudy?

That’s trapped air — it’s normal. Cloudy ice is actually fine and melts slower. If the ice is slimy or has a smell, your water tank needs cleaning, not the machine.
