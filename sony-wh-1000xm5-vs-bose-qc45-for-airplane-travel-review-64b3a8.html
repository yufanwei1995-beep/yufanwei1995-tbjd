Quick Verdict

  • Who this is for: Frequent flyers who need silence on planes, not just bass-heavy music.
  • Our top pick: Sony WH-1000XM5 — better ANC against low-frequency engine drone, superior call quality, and 30-hour battery.
  • Price range: Sony typically $298-$348; Bose QC45 usually $249-$329 depending on sales.

I fly about 80,000 miles a year. Mostly transatlantic. That means 7+ hours of jet engine roar, crying babies, and the guy behind me who watches TikTok without headphones. I tested the Sony WH-1000XM5 and Bose QuietComfort 45 on actual flights — not just in a quiet room. Here's what matters.

What to Look For in Airplane Headphones

Before we dive into the two heavyweights, you need to know what actually matters at 35,000 feet.

  • ANC strength (specifically low-frequency): Jet engines produce a constant low rumble around 100-400 Hz. Good ANC kills this. Mids and highs (voices, clinks) are easier to block. If a headphone only handles chatter, it'll fail on a plane.
  • Comfort for 6+ hours: Clamping force and ear pad material matter more than weight. A 250g headphone that pinches is worse than a 300g one that doesn't.
  • Battery life with ANC on: Don't care about ANC-off battery. You'll never use it on a plane. Look for 20+ hours minimum.
  • Multipoint Bluetooth: You need to switch between your phone (music) and laptop (movie) without fiddling with settings.
  • Wired option: Some airlines still have seatback screens with headphone jacks. If the ANC dies mid-flight, a 3.5mm cable is your backup.
  • Case size: Overhead bins are tight. A hard case that fits in your personal item bag is a lifesaver.

The Contenders

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $298-$348 | Weight: 250g | Battery: 30 hrs (ANC on) | Bluetooth: 5.2, multipoint

The XM5 is Sony's flagship. It uses eight microphones and dual processors — that's overkill for most people, but exactly what you want when sitting next to a 777 engine. The ANC is tuned specifically to combat low-frequency noise. On my last flight from JFK to LHR, the cabin noise dropped to a faint whisper. I could hear my own breathing over the engine. That's impressive.

The sound profile is warm and detailed. Bass is punchy but not muddy. Vocals are clear. If you listen to podcasts, voices sound natural, not tinny. The touch controls on the right earcup are intuitive — swipe to skip, tap to pause. They work 95% of the time, which is better than most.

The bad: The XM5 doesn't fold. It's a bulky case, about the size of a small loaf of bread. That's annoying in a full backpack. Also, the earpads are protein leather — they get warm after 3 hours. And there's no IP rating, so don't wear them in heavy rain.

Call quality: This is where Sony beats Bose. The beamforming mics pick up your voice clearly, even with cabin noise. I called my wife from the airport lounge with a vacuum running nearby — she said I sounded like I was in a quiet room.

Verdict: Best-in-class ANC and battery. The folding issue is real, but if silence is your priority, this wins.

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $249-$329 | Weight: 238g | Battery: 24 hrs (ANC on) | Bluetooth: 5.1, multipoint

The QC45 is Bose's answer to the XM5. It's lighter, it folds flat, and the clamping force is gentler. After 6 hours, my ears didn't hurt. That's rare. The earcups are deep — my ears don't touch the inside, which is a problem I have with Sony.

ANC-wise, it's excellent but not equal. The Bose kills high-frequency noise (voices, clatter) better than Sony. But against that low jet-engine hum, the Sony is noticeably quieter. On the same flight, I switched mid-trip. The difference was like turning a fan from high to medium. Not night and day, but audible.

Sound quality is more neutral. If you like flat, accurate audio for classical or acoustic, you'll prefer Bose. If you want thumping bass for hip-hop, Sony wins. Neither is bad — just different.

The bad: The QC45 has a design flaw — the "aware mode" button is easy to hit accidentally when adjusting the headband. I've paused my music three times just by moving them on my head. Also, call quality is mediocre. My wife said I sounded like I was in a tunnel with wind noise. It's usable, but not great.

Verdict: More comfortable for marathon sessions and packs smaller. But ANC is a step behind Sony, and the mic is worse.

One More Option: Bose QC Ultra

Price: $329-$379 | Weight: 254g | Battery: 24 hrs (ANC on) | Bluetooth: 5.3, multipoint

If you're willing to spend more, the QC Ultra is Bose's current flagship. It has "immersive audio" (their version of spatial audio) and better ANC than the QC45 — closer to Sony's XM5. It also folds, which gives it an edge over Sony in portability. But it's $30-50 more expensive and the immersive audio is a gimmick that eats battery. Skip it unless you find it on sale for under $330.

Real-World Flight Test: What Actually Happened

I flew Boston to London (6.5 hours) and London to Dubai (7 hours) with both. Here's the raw data:

  • Engine noise: Sony reduced it to a barely-perceptible hum. Bose left a soft "whoosh" that I noticed when music paused.
  • Movie dialogue: Both were clear, but I had to raise volume by 15% on Bose to match Sony's clarity.
  • Battery anxiety: After a full day of flights and layovers (14 hours total listening), Sony showed 60% left. Bose showed 45%. Both survive a marathon day, but Sony has more margin.
  • Sleeping: I'm a side sleeper. Neither is comfortable for laying your head against a window. The earcups are too bulky. Bring a neck pillow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use these on a plane without Bluetooth?

Yes. Both include a 3.5mm audio cable that plugs into the seatback screen. But note: ANC still works in wired mode, but it uses battery. If you're on a 10-hour flight with a dead battery, you're listening to the engine. Charge before you board.

Which is better for small heads?

Bose QC45. The clamping force is lighter and the headband has more adjustability. Sony's clamp is tighter, which is good for seal (better ANC) but bad for pressure points.

Will these fit in a personal item bag?

Bose QC45 — yes, easily. The folded case is about the size of a large wallet. Sony XM5 — only if your bag is roomy. The case is roughly 7x8x3 inches. It fits in a backpack but eats space.

Do I need the app?

Both have apps, but you don't need them. Sony's app has a 20-band EQ that's useful if you want to tweak bass. Bose's app is barebones — just firmware updates. Skip both until you need to update.

Buyer's Guide: Which Should You Actually Buy?

Here's the honest breakdown:

  • Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if: You fly long-haul (5+ hours), you want the quietest possible cabin experience, and you don't mind a bulky case. The call quality alone is worth the premium if you take work calls during layovers.
  • Buy the Bose QC45 if: You prioritize comfort over everything, you fly shorter routes (under 3 hours), or you're on a budget. The lighter weight and foldable design make it better for daily commutes and weekend trips.
  • Skip both if: You're strictly a wired listener or you need headphones for sports (neither has water resistance). Look at the Sony WH-CH720N or Anker Soundcore Q45 for cheaper options.

Final Verdict

For airplane travel, the Sony WH-1000XM5 is the better purchase. The ANC difference on low-frequency engine noise is the single most important factor on a flight, and Sony wins that test clearly. Yes, the case is annoying and the earpads get warm. But when you're sitting in seat 34B next to a crying infant, you'll appreciate the extra silence. If you're a budget traveler or you only fly a few times a year, the Bose QC45 is a solid second — just don't expect it to match Sony's noise cancellation or call quality.
