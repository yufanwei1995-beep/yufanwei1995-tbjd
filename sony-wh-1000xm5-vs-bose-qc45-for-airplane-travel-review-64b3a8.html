What to Look For in Airplane Headphones

Before we get into the nitty-gritty, here is what actually matters when you are stuck in seat 34B for six hours.

Active Noise Canceling (ANC) Depth

This is the main event. You need ANC that kills low-frequency rumble — that's the jet engine hum. But you also need it to handle mid-frequency noise like chatter and the clatter of the drink cart. Both of these headphones do this well, but they do it differently. Sony uses eight microphones to sample noise. Bose uses four. More mics don't always mean better, but in this case, Sony's processing is more aggressive.

Comfort and Clamp Force

A heavy headphone is a nightmare by hour three. The Sony XM5 weighs 250 grams. The Bose QC45 weighs 238 grams. That 12-gram difference is noticeable over time. But weight isn't everything. Clamp force matters more. The QC45 has a lighter clamp. The XM5 grips a bit tighter, which actually helps with ANC but can fatigue your jaw after a few hours. If you wear glasses, the QC45 is more forgiving on your temples.

Battery Life and Charging

You want a headphone that lasts an entire flight plus your layover. The Sony XM5 claims 30 hours with ANC on. The Bose QC45 claims 24 hours. In my tests, the Sony hits about 28 hours. The Bose hits about 22. Both have quick-charge features: 3 minutes of charging gives you 3 hours on the Sony, and 15 minutes gives you 2.5 hours on the Bose. The Sony is clearly better here.

Wired and Bluetooth Flexibility

Some airlines still have those old seat-back screens with the 3.5mm jack. Both headphones include a cable. But here is the catch: the Sony XM5 has a USB-C to 3.5mm cable. The Bose QC45 has a standard 3.5mm to 3.5mm cable. If you lose the Sony's special cable, you are out of luck. The Bose cable is easy to replace anywhere. Also, the Sony XM5 does not work in wired mode when the battery is completely dead. The Bose QC45 does. That is a huge point for the Bose.

Call Quality and Microphones

You might need to take a work call during a layover. The Sony XM5 has a vastly better microphone array. It uses bone conduction technology and AI-based noise reduction. The person on the other end will hear your voice, not the airport PA system. The Bose QC45 microphone is passable but sounds hollow and picks up more background noise. If you take calls regularly, the Sony wins by a mile.

Individual Product Reviews

Sony WH-1000XM5

Price: $298-$348

Rating: 4.5/5

Best for: Frequent travelers who want the best possible noise canceling and music quality.

The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the king of silence. Period. The ANC is so good that it makes a plane full of crying babies sound like a library. The eight-microphone system is overkill in a good way. It actively cancels out the rumble of the engines, the hiss of the air vents, and even the muffled chatter from three rows away.

The sound quality is warmer and more detailed than the Bose. Bass is punchy but not muddy. Vocals are clear. If you listen to podcasts, voices sound natural. If you listen to hip-hop or EDM, the low-end thump is satisfying without drowning out the mids.

Comfort is where the Sony stumbles slightly. The clamp force is a bit stronger than the Bose. I have a medium-sized head, and after about three hours, I felt a slight pressure around my ears. With glasses, the pressure is more pronounced. It is not a dealbreaker, but it is a real consideration.

The battery life is excellent. I flew from New York to London, used them for the entire 7-hour flight, and still had 70% battery left. The quick charge is genuinely useful when you are rushing between gates.

Tradeoffs: The proprietary USB-C to 3.5mm cable is a pain. The headphones are slightly bulky and do not fold flat like the Bose. The touch controls on the right cup are sensitive — I accidentally paused my music a few times when adjusting the headphones.

Bose QuietComfort 45

Price: $249-$329

Rating: 4/5

Best for: Comfort-first travelers, glasses wearers, and people who need a reliable wired backup.

The Bose QC45 is the comfort king. The ear cushions are plush, the clamp force is gentle, and the 238-gram weight disappears on your head. I wore these for a 10-hour flight to Tokyo and forgot I had them on. That is the highest praise I can give.

The ANC is good but not as deep as the Sony. It completely kills the jet engine rumble, but you will hear the occasional high-pitched cry or the sharp clink of a glass bottle. It is not distracting, but it is there. In a quiet room, the ANC hiss is very low, almost imperceptible. The Sony has a slightly more "silent" feel.

Sound quality is neutral. The Bose signature sound is flat and accurate. Bass is present but not boomy. If you like a "flat" studio sound, you will love these. If you want bass that makes your ears tickle, you will be disappointed.

The battery life is adequate at 22 hours real-world. You will get through a transatlantic flight and a layover, but you will be charging them before you land on the way back. The big win here is the standard 3.5mm cable. Any airline with a seat-back screen will work, and if you lose the cable, any cheap pair of earbuds' cable will work.

Tradeoffs: The microphone is mediocre. Callers complain about background noise. The ANC is not as aggressive as the Sony. The sound lacks the punch that most consumers want. The charging port is USB-C but the cable is short.

Sony WH-1000XM4 (Budget Alternative)

Price: $248-$298

Rating: 4.5/5

Best for: Budget-conscious travelers who want 95% of the XM5's performance.

The XM4 is the previous generation, but it is still a fantastic headphone. It folds flat, which is a huge advantage for packing. The ANC is nearly as good as the XM5 — maybe 90% as effective. The sound profile is slightly warmer and bassier than the XM5. Some people actually prefer the XM4's sound.

Battery life is 30 hours with ANC on. It uses a standard USB-C to USB-C cable for wired mode, which is more common than the XM5's proprietary cable. The touch controls are less sensitive than the XM5, which is a good thing.

Tradeoffs: The microphone is worse than the XM5. The fit is similar to the XM5 — tight-ish clamp. It is older, so you might find it at a steep discount.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use these on an airplane with the seat-back screen?

Yes, both work with a wired connection. The Bose QC45 is easier because it uses a standard 3.5mm cable. The Sony XM5 requires its special USB-C to 3.5mm cable, which is easy to lose. Also, the Sony XM5 will not work wired if the battery is completely dead. The Bose will.

Which has better noise canceling for the actual engine noise?

The Sony WH-1000XM5. It cancels about 3-4 decibels more low-frequency noise than the Bose. That is the difference between hearing a faint hum and hearing nothing. On a plane, the Sony is noticeably quieter.

Are these comfortable for a 10-hour flight?

The Bose QC45 is the safest bet. They are lighter and have a gentler clamp. The Sony XM5 is fine for most people, but if you have a larger head or wear thick glasses, you will feel pressure around hour four.

Do I need to buy a Bluetooth adapter for the airplane entertainment system?

Not for these two. Both come with wired cables. But if you want to use Bluetooth with the seat-back screen, you need a separate transmitter like the AirFly. I recommend just using the cable.

Which is better for taking phone calls in the airport?

The Sony XM5, no contest. The microphone array is vastly superior. The person on the other end hears your voice clearly, even in a noisy terminal. The Bose QC45 sounds like you are talking from a tin can.

My Final Take

Buy the Sony WH-1000XM5 if you want the best noise canceling on the market, excellent sound, and a great microphone. The battery life is better, the ANC is deeper, and the overall experience feels more premium. The only real downside is the proprietary cable and the slightly tighter fit.

Buy the Bose QC45 if comfort is your absolute priority. If you wear glasses, if you have a sensitive head, or if you just want something you can forget you are wearing, the Bose wins. The standard cable is a lifesaver, and the flat-folding design packs better.

Bottom line: For most travelers, the Sony XM5 is the better headphone. But the Bose QC45 is the better travel companion for a specific group of people. If you are on the fence, try them both on. Your head will tell you which one to buy.
