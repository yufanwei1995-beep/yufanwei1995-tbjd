If you've bleached, colored, or heat-styled your hair into a fragile mess, you've probably heard two names: K18 and Olaplex. Both promise to fix broken bonds. Both cost a lot. But they work completely differently. This article breaks down exactly how each one works, who should buy which, and whether you even need either one.

Quick Summary

  • Who this is for: Anyone with chemically processed, heat-damaged, or over-brushed hair that snaps or feels like straw.
  • Our top pick: K18 Leave-In Molecular Repair Mask — it fixes the root cause faster and works with any shampoo.
  • Price range: $30 to $75 depending on size and where you buy.

Buyer's Guide: What to Look For in a Bond Repair Treatment

Before spending $40 on a tiny tube, understand the three things that separate a good bond repair from a gimmick.

1. Active Ingredient and Mechanism

Olaplex uses a single molecule called bis-aminopropyl diglycol dimaleate. It finds broken disulfide bonds (the ones that hold hair together) and reconnects them. It works best when applied during chemical services, but the No.3 at-home version still helps.

K18 uses a peptide complex that mimics the natural keratin structure in your hair. Instead of patching bonds from the outside, it penetrates the cortex and rebuilds the entire keratin chain from the inside out. That's a fundamental difference.

2. How It's Applied

Olaplex No.3 is a pre-shampoo treatment. You apply it to damp hair, wait 10 minutes (or longer), then rinse and shampoo. It's an extra step that takes time.

K18 is a leave-in. You shampoo, towel-dry, apply 4-6 pumps, wait 4 minutes, then style as usual. No rinsing. Faster and simpler for most people.

3. Long-Term Results vs Quick Fix

Olaplex gives a noticeable improvement after the first use but requires consistent weekly application. K18 claims to deliver results after just one 4-minute treatment, with the full effect visible after 4-6 consecutive washes. Both are maintenance products — neither is a permanent cure.

Key insight: K18 works on the structure, Olaplex works on the bonds. If your hair is severely damaged, K18 addresses more than just the broken sulfur links.

Product Reviews

1. K18 Leave-In Molecular Repair Mask

This is the one that changed the game. The K18 mask (50ml sells for around $75, 15ml for $30) is a leave-in peptide treatment that doesn't require a waiting period or a rinse. You literally just apply it after washing and move on with your day.

What it does: The biomimetic peptides are small enough to penetrate the hair cortex. Once inside, they rebuild the keratin structure that was destroyed by bleach or chemical relaxers. It doesn't coat the hair like a silicone-heavy serum; it actually changes the internal structure.

Real results: After one use on my heavily highlighted hair, the snap factor dropped by about 50%. After four washes, my hair felt thicker and looked noticeably less frizzy. The ends, which were see-through and split, looked denser. That took about two weeks.

Trade-offs: It's pricey. A 50ml bottle lasts about 6-8 weeks if you use it every wash, which is the recommended frequency. It can also make hair feel slightly stiff if you use too much — less is more. And if your hair is protein-sensitive, it might feel a bit crunchy.

Best for: People with moderate to severe damage who want visible results in under a week without adding extra steps to their routine.

2. Olaplex No.3 Hair Perfector

The OG bond builder. Olaplex No.3 (100ml retails around $30) is a treatment you apply before shampooing. It uses that single active ingredient to reconnect broken disulfide bonds.

What it does: It's excellent at preventing damage during chemical services and maintaining hair between salon visits. The formula is watery and easy to distribute. You leave it on for at least 10 minutes, but many stylists recommend leaving it on for an hour for severe damage.

Real results: My hair felt smoother immediately after the first use — no doubt. But the effect was more cosmetic than structural. The strength improvement was there, but it took about 8 weeks of weekly use to match what K18 did in two weeks. It also didn't fix the thin, wispy ends as well.

Trade-offs: The application process is a hassle — wet hair, apply, wait, rinse, then shampoo, then condition. That's three extra steps. It also doesn't play well with sulfate-heavy shampoos; you need a sulfate-free shampoo to keep the bonds intact, which is an additional cost.

Best for: People with mild damage, or those who get regular salon treatments and want to maintain the results at home. Also great for preventing damage before it happens.

3. Olaplex No.8 Bond Intense Moisture Mask

This is the in-shower version of No.3, but with added moisture. It's a 100ml tube for about $28. It's a rinse-out mask you leave on for 10 minutes.

What it does: It combines the bond-repairing molecule from No.3 with a blend of oils and ceramides for hydration. It's a two-in-one — bond repair and deep conditioner.

Real results: Good for dry, brittle hair that needs immediate slip and softness. The moisture boost is real, and it detangles like a dream. But it's not as potent as No.3 for structural repair. It's more of a conditioner with bond-repair benefits.

Trade-offs: It's a maintenance step, not a rescue treatment. If your hair is breaking off, this won't fix it. You also need to use it regularly, and the results fade quickly once you stop.

Best for: Those who want soft, manageable hair and don't have severe breakage. A good weekly substitute for your regular conditioner.

4. K18 Molecular Repair Hair Oil

K18 expanded its line with a finishing oil (30ml for about $48). It's a leave-in oil that contains a smaller dose of the same peptide complex.

What it does: It seals the cuticle, adds shine, and provides heat protection up to 230°C/450°F. You apply it to damp or dry hair before styling.

Real results: It's a nice finishing product, but it's not a replacement for the mask. The peptide dose is too low to make a structural difference. I use it as a heat protectant and frizz tamer — and it does both well. The shine is legit, without greasiness.

Trade-offs: It's expensive for an oil, and the repair benefits are minimal. If you already own the mask, this is optional. If you're on a budget, skip it and buy the mask in the larger size.

Best for: People who want a daily heat protectant with a hint of bond-repair peptides.

5. Olaplex No.0 Intensive Bond Building Treatment

No.0 is a water-based primer (150ml, about $30) designed to be used before No.3. It preps the hair by swelling the cuticle so the bond-repair molecule in No.3 can penetrate deeper.

What it does: It's a booster, not a standalone treatment. You spray it on damp hair, wait 10 minutes, then apply No.3 on top.

Real results: Using them together is more effective than No.3 alone — a 2-step ritual that takes about 20 minutes. The added benefit is real but marginal. If you're already using No.3, this adds maybe 15% more repair.

Trade-offs: It's another product, another step, and another $30. The bottle runs out fast — I got about 6 uses. That's $5 per wash just for the primer.

Best for: Obsessive Olaplex users who want maximum bond repair and don't mind a 20-minute ritual twice a week.

FAQ

Can you use K18 and Olaplex together?

Yes, but don't use them on the same wash day. The molecules can compete for the same binding sites. Use K18 one day and Olaplex No.3 another day. Many people alternate weekly.

How often should you use K18?

For the first 4-6 washes, use it every time you shampoo. After that, once a week for maintenance. Overuse can lead to protein overload, which makes hair stiff and brittle.

Is Olaplex No.3 a conditioner?

No. It's a pre-treatment. It doesn't moisturize. You still need a separate conditioner after rinsing it out.

Which is better for bleached hair?

K18. Bleach destroys the entire keratin structure, not just the disulfide bonds. K18 rebuilds that structure. Olaplex is better for preventing damage during the bleaching process itself.

Bottom Line Verdict

Buy the K18 Leave-In Molecular Repair Mask if your hair is visibly damaged — breaking off, stretching, or feeling like cotton candy when wet. It's faster, easier, and produces deeper structural repair than Olaplex No.3. It costs more upfront, but you use less product and see results in two weeks instead of two months. Buy Olaplex No.3 if your hair is only mildly damaged or if you get regular salon treatments and want to keep the bonds strong between visits. It's cheaper and works best as a preventative maintenance tool. If you're on the fence, start with the small 15ml K18 ($30) — if it doesn't impress you in a week, you're only out the cost of a takeout dinner.
