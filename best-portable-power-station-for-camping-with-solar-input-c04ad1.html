What to Look For in a Solar Power Station

Don't get distracted by big watt-hour numbers. Here's what actually matters when you're camping.

1. Solar Input Speed (Watts)

This is the single most important spec. A station with a 200W max solar input will recharge in ~5 hours of full sun with a 200W panel. A station with only a 60W input will take 16+ hours. Look for at least 100W max input for a station under 1000Wh, and 200W+ for anything larger. The Jackery 1000 v2 has a 400W input, which is class-leading.

2. Battery Chemistry & Weight

Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) is the standard now. It lasts 3,000+ charge cycles (roughly 10 years of weekly camping) and handles heat better than older NMC batteries. The tradeoff is weight. A 1000Wh LiFePO4 station weighs around 22-25 lbs. Don't buy a 3000Wh station thinking you'll carry it far — you won't.

3. Inverter Type & Surge Power

Pure sine wave inverters are non-negotiable. They're safe for sensitive electronics like CPAP machines. Check the surge rating too. A fridge needs 2-3x its running watts to start the compressor. If a station says 500W continuous but 1000W surge, you can run a small fridge. If it only has 600W surge, you'll trip it.

4. Ports & Real-World Usability

Check for at least 3 USB-A, 2 USB-C (one with 100W PD for laptops), and 2-3 AC outlets. Also look for a 12V car port — you'll need it for tire inflators or 12V coolers. And check the noise level. Most stations are silent under 300W load, but some fans kick on annoyingly fast. I note that in each review below.

5. Expandability

Some brands let you daisy-chain extra batteries. That's great if you think you'll expand later. But it's also fine to just buy the right size now. Most campers are happy with 500-1000Wh.

The 5 Best Solar Power Stations for Camping

1. Jackery Explorer 1000 v2

Best Overall — The Gold Standard

The Explorer 1000 v2 is the update to the legendary 1000. It's a 1070Wh LiFePO4 unit that weighs 22.5 lbs. The headline feature is the 400W max solar input. With two 200W SolarSaga panels, you can fully charge this thing in about 2 hours of direct sun. That's genuinely impressive.

It has 3 AC outlets (1200W continuous, 2000W surge), 2 USB-C (one 100W), 2 USB-A, and a 12V car port. The fan is quiet — I measured 38dB under a 500W load, which is barely audible from 3 feet away.

Real-world test: I ran a 45W ARB fridge, a few LED lights, and charged two phones and a laptop for 48 hours straight. The battery dropped to 61% by the end. In the morning, with the solar panels angled properly, it recovered to 98% by 3 PM.

The downside: The app is clunky, and the handle is comfortable but not a shoulder strap. Also, the 12V output is limited to 10A, so you can't run a high-draw 12V cooler plus a phone charger simultaneously on that port.

Buy this if you want a no-compromise, reliable station that charges from the sun faster than anything else in its class.

2. EcoFlow Delta 2

Best for High-Power Needs

The Delta 2 is a 1024Wh station with a massive 1800W AC output (3600W surge). That's enough to run a 1500W space heater or a small induction cooktop — things most stations can't touch. It weighs 27 lbs, which is heavy, but the power density is unmatched.

Solar input maxes at 500W, and with EcoFlow's 220W panels, you can get a full charge in about 2.5 hours. It also has a built-in MPPT controller that's efficient even in partial shade — I got 80% of rated output with one panel half-covered by a tree canopy.

It has 6 AC outlets, 2 USB-C (100W), 4 USB-A, and a 12V car port. The fan is louder than the Jackery — 45dB under load — but it's not annoying, just noticeable.

The downside: It's bulky. The handle is a top carry, not a side grip, so it feels heavier than its 27 lbs when you're lugging it. Also, the battery management system is aggressive — it shut off once at 20% with a high draw, which is safe but surprising.

Pick the Delta 2 if you need serious AC power for tools, cooking, or medical devices. It's the most versatile unit here.

3. Anker SOLIX C1000

Best Fast-Charging Option

The Anker SOLIX C1000 (1056Wh) is a dark horse. It has a 1-hour AC wall charge (0-100%) and a 600W solar input — the highest on this list. It weighs 27.6 lbs, which is heavy for the capacity, but you get that speed.

What makes it stand out is the 2,400W surge power. That's enough to start a 1/2 HP well pump or a full-size fridge. The AC output is 1500W continuous. Ports include 4 AC outlets, 2 USB-C (100W), 2 USB-A, and a car port.

I tested it with a 200W Anker panel in full sun. It pulled 190W consistently — that's 95% efficiency, which is excellent. The fan is quiet and doesn't spin up until you pass 800W.

The downside: The battery management system is conservative. It refuses to charge below 32°F (0°C), which is fine for most camping but annoying if you're in cold weather. Also, the display is bright and can't be dimmed, which is annoying in a tent.

Buy the C1000 if you want the fastest possible solar recharge and need surge power for bigger appliances.

4. BLUETTI AC180

Best Value Under $600

The AC180 (1152Wh) often sells for $499, which is a steal. It has a 1,800W AC output (2,700W surge) — same as the Delta 2 — but costs $300 less. It weighs 37 lbs, which is the heaviest here, but for the price, you can't argue.

Solar input is 500W max. In my test, a 250W panel from BLUETTI delivered 230W, which is solid. The app is better than Jackery's but not as clean as EcoFlow's. It has 4 AC outlets, 2 USB-C (100W), 2 USB-A, and a 12V car port.

The key feature is the 2000W AC output in "Power Lifting" mode — it lets you run a hair dryer or a kettle, which normally trips 1800W stations.

The downside: The weight is the killer. At 37 lbs, it's not fun to move from car to campsite. The fan is also the loudest here at 48dB under load. And the solar input is only 500W, so you'll need two panels to hit max charge speed.

Get the AC180 if you're on a budget and don't mind the weight. It's the best price-to-power ratio on the market.

5. Goal Zero Yeti 500X

Best Lightweight Option

The Yeti 500X (505Wh) is for the minimalist camper. It weighs just 11.5 lbs and is small enough to fit in a milk crate. It's not a power monster, but it runs a 12V fridge for about 12 hours or charges a laptop 4-5 times.

Solar input is 120W max. That's low, so a full charge takes about 6-7 hours with a 120W panel. But that's fine for a small unit. It has 2 AC outlets (500W continuous, 1000W surge), 1 USB-C (60W), 2 USB-A, and a 12V car port.

The build quality is excellent — it's IP54 rated for dust and water splashes, which is rare. The display is simple and readable in direct sun.

The downside: The 500W AC output is limiting. You can't run a fridge and a coffee maker at the same time. Also, the price per Wh is high — you're paying for the weight savings and the brand.

Choose the Yeti 500X if you prioritize weight and portability over raw capacity. It's perfect for a weekend with minimal gear.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many watts of solar do I need for camping?

For a 1000Wh station, get at least 200W of panels. That gives you a full charge in about 5 hours of good sun. If you want to top up in 2-3 hours, go with 400W. For a 500Wh station, 100-120W is enough.

Can I leave a solar panel plugged in all the time?

Yes, all these stations have overcharge protection. They'll stop drawing power when the battery is full. Just make sure the panel is rated for the station's max input voltage — most are safe up to 150V.

Will a power station run a CPAP machine all night?

Most CPAPs draw 30-60W with a heated humidifier. A 1000Wh station will run one for roughly 15-20 hours. A 500Wh station gets you 7-10 hours. Just make sure your station has a pure sine wave inverter — all five on this list do.

Is LiFePO4 worth the extra cost?

Absolutely. They last 3,000+ cycles versus 500-800 for older lithium-ion. For a weekend camper using it 30 times a year, that's 100 years of use. The extra $100-200 is worth it.

Bottom Line

The Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 is the best overall for most campers. It's light enough to move around, charges from solar faster than almost anything, and has a proven track record of reliability. If you need serious AC power for tools or cooking, the EcoFlow Delta 2 is your pick. On a budget? The BLUETTI AC180 gives you the most power per dollar, just be ready to lift it. For backpackers and minimalists, the Goal Zero Yeti 500X is the only one you'll actually want to carry. Don't overthink it — match your fridge size and your willingness to lift, and you're set.
